## 1. Three oath days (IR-08-01)

The policy was launched in **2014**, on the **first day** of the government. At Prime Minister Modi’s oath, the leaders of the **South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC)** were invited. He called that unprecedented. The first day was spent on the neighbours.

**SAARC is only for South Asia.** Members he named: **Afghanistan, Pakistan, Maldives, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, India**. Two neighbours are outside it. **China** is **East Asia**. **Myanmar** is **Southeast Asia**. That regional lock stays **IR-06**. The **Prime Minister of Pakistan, Nawaz Sharif**, was among the guests. His brother **Shehbaz Sharif** is Prime Minister now.

**2015:** Modi made a surprise visit to **Lahore**. The two sides decided to open peace talks. Pakistan did not reciprocate. It kept sponsoring terrorism.

**2016** is the turning point, the **Uri** attack. From then, talks and terror cannot go together, trade and terror cannot go together, and blood and water cannot flow together. The water line is why the Indus treaty is suspended. The treaty file stays **IR-01**.

**2019**, second oath: **Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation (BIMSTEC)** leaders, not SAARC. The point was to keep Pakistan out. Pakistan is in SAARC and not in BIMSTEC, because BIMSTEC is the Bay of Bengal, not the whole of South Asia. **Seven** members, as he listed them: **Sri Lanka, India, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand**. He again said “technological” while expanding the name. The name is **Technical**, already locked.

**2024**, third oath: **seven** neighbouring leaders, named at the bottom of handout page 2. He listed **Sri Lanka, Maldives, Seychelles, Bangladesh, Mauritius, Nepal, and Bhutan**. That guest list is not the BIMSTEC membership. Seychelles and Mauritius are in; Myanmar and Thailand are not. Pakistan is still out.

**SAARC has jammed.** **Dr S. Jaishankar** calls it a jammed vehicle. The **last SAARC summit was in 2014**. None since. Pakistan’s disruption is the reason he gave. India has therefore given more weight to BIMSTEC.

The sheet puts **2016** beside SAARC. He was clear, and he repeated it: the **2014** oath was the SAARC invitation, including Nawaz Sharif, and **2014** is also the last summit. **2016** is Uri, and the start of the new Pakistan policy. Lock the spoken years.

## 2. The Doval Doctrine (IR-08-02)

**Ajit Doval**, the **National Security Advisor (NSA)**, is the name on the Pakistan file. Pakistan does not understand the language of peace, so the policy since **2016** is what he called **defensive offence**: offence is the best form of defence. If Pakistan sponsors a terror attack, India answers **militarily** and hits the part of Pakistani territory used for that attack. He called this the new military normal. It is consistent since 2016.

Three cases, in the order he taught them.

1. **2016, Uri**, then the **surgical strikes**. Indian forces went into **Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK)** and hit terror **launch pads**. The sheet writes **LoC** beside this operation. That LoC is the **Line of Control**. It is not the credit line in the next section.
2. **2019, Pulwama**, then the **Balakot** strike. The **Indian Air Force** destroyed a terrorist camp at **Balakot**, inside Pakistani territory.
3. **Last year, the Pahalgam attack**, then **Operation Sindoor**. Camps at **nine** places, deep inside Pakistani territory. Modi has said the operation is **continuous**: the next sponsored attack gets the same answer. The purpose was the **proxy infrastructure**, not a full-scale war. The nine-camp picture and the “this is hard power” line stay **IR-07**.

The same three sentences sit under the doctrine: talks and terror cannot go together, trade and terror cannot go together, blood and water cannot flow together.

## 3. How the help is paid for (IR-08-03)

Besides the oath-day signal, the government has helped neighbours in a crisis and with money. During COVID, free vaccines went under **Vaccine Maitri** (that scheme stays **IR-06**). The sheet also writes **Sanjeevani** beside free medicines. The audio only said free medicines under an operation whose name did not come through. Lock the sheet word, and do not invent a machinery for it.

Three instruments.

| Instrument | What it is | The number he gave |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Line of Credit (LoC)** | A **loan**. The neighbour pays it back **with interest**. | India charges about **1 to 2 per cent**. China charges a commercial rate, **more than 6 per cent**, which is how a poor borrower falls into the debt trap. |
| **Grant** | **Aid**. It is **not** returned. Not a loan, not a debt. | No separate figure. |
| **Currency Swap Agreement (CSA)** | For a neighbour in a **balance of payments** crisis: foreign-exchange reserves too thin to pay for imports. | The neighbour deposits **local currency** with the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**. RBI gives the **equivalent in US dollars**, to be reversed later. **RBI carries the risk** — if that local currency falls further, the loss is India’s. |

**Maldives:** a currency swap of **750 million US dollars**, signed recently. He walked the units: **1 billion = 1,000 million**, so 750 million is **less than 1 billion**. India’s foreign-exchange reserves are **more than 700 billion US dollars**, which is why India can take that risk for a small neighbour.

**Sri Lanka, 2022:** a financial package of **4 billion US dollars**. He first said 2018, then corrected himself to **2022**. A package **can** hold all three instruments — swap, line of credit, and grant — and this one did. The collapse had two causes he had already used: the **pandemic**, and **China’s debt trap**. The economic crisis set off a political crisis the same year. That 2022 picture stays **IR-07**; the **4 billion** and the three-part package are this class.

After the package, Sri Lanka **cancelled Chinese contracts** for power plants on islands in the **north**, about **50 km from Chennai**, and gave the work to Indian companies. He did not name the islands. The lesson he drew: criticism of the **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)** does not counter it. A poor neighbour needs the investment from somewhere. India has to be the alternative.

## 4. Jaishankar’s definition, and where it parts from Gujral (IR-08-04)

On handout page 3, **Dr S. Jaishankar** defines the policy. India is the biggest country in the region, so India takes the lead, goes the extra mile, stays **non-reciprocal**, stays generous, and **invests**. Investment is what makes neighbours sensitive to India’s **core security concerns**. He named two.

1. The rising **presence of China** in the neighbourhood.
2. Neighbouring territory must not be used to **sponsor terrorism**.

He also split this policy from the **Gujral Doctrine**. Gujral was for the **entire** region. **Neighbourhood First does not include Pakistan.** Pakistan has the doctrine in section 2.

Non-reciprocity itself — give, and do not wait for the favour to come back — stays **IR-07**.

## 5. What gets in the way (IR-08-05)

**China.** BRI investments are meant, in his line, to ring-fence India in the Indian Ocean. He walked the same pearls the China note already holds: Southeast Asia, Myanmar, Bangladesh, **Hambantota**, the Maldives, **Gwadar**, a naval base at **Djibouti**, plus **CPEC** and **CMEC**. Do not rebuild that list here. The new local fact is the cancelled northern Sri Lankan power contracts, above. A neighbour that is made to feel insecure **tilts to China** to balance India. That tilt was yesterday’s external balancing.

**Connectivity.** Without a trade route there is no rise in the volume of trade. China is rebuilding connectivity by reviving the old Silk Route through BRI. India has to build the routes too.

**SAARC**, already above: last summit **2014**, a jammed vehicle, so BIMSTEC carries more of the policy.

**Radicalisation, and anti-India politics.** India wants neighbours that are **democratic, stable, and secular**. A radical neighbour becomes a breeding ground. He placed the rise in **Bangladesh**, in **Afghanistan** after the Taliban’s return, in **Pakistan**, and in the **Maldives**.

The anti-India speeches are, in his account, an **election** device. A development campaign is hard, because politicians fail to deliver. The shortcut is emotion: **identity** (religion, language, caste, region) and **nationalism**. Nationalism needs an enemy. **Donald Trump** was the outside example — “Make America Great Again,” with Russia and China cast as enemies, and a later attempt to normalise with China. Neighbours already feel the **big-brother** insecurity, because the power gap is so large. Desperate politicians use that insecurity.

**Pakistan** is where he said it is most common. The country is still **feudal**: **zamindars** holding thousands of acres, **no land reforms** of the Indian kind, so “still living in 1947.” Generals and those landlords work together. The elected politicians are puppets. It is a **pseudo-democracy** — that category stays **IR-07**. The economic picture he added: always near a payments crisis, the Prime Minister “with a begging bowl” at the **International Monetary Fund (IMF)**. In an election the speech becomes: poverty, hunger, and corruption are not the problem; **India** is. The same device, he said, has been used by **K. P. Sharma Oli** in Nepal (former Prime Minister; he was Prime Minister last year), by the **Rajapaksa** brothers in Sri Lanka (**Mahinda** and **Gotabaya**), by **Mohamed Muizzu** in the Maldives, and most recently in Bangladesh.

**Bangladesh.** **Jamaat-e-Islami** fought the recent election on two methods: **radical religious propaganda**, which he tied to attacks on secular values and on **Hindu minority** rights, and **anti-India** speeches. The party is **sponsored by Pakistan’s Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI)**. It was also part of the **2024** student movement that forced **Sheikh Hasina** out. Hasina was the pro-India Prime Minister. Jamaat **lost**. The **Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP)** won, led by **Tarique Rahman**, who is Prime Minister now. Yesterday’s audio did not land that personal name. Lock it here.

**Maldives, 2023.** Two candidates. **Ibrahim Mohamed Solih**, president from **2018**, pro-India, policy name **India First**. **Mohamed Muizzu**, then pro-China, slogan **“India Out”**: Indian military presence had grown under Solih and was violating Maldivian sovereignty, so the forces should go. The hashtag ran on social media. **Muizzu won.** Once in office the policy flipped. He came to the **2024** oath, invited Modi and gave him a large welcome, and India signed the **750 million** dollar swap. He now does what he judges to be the Maldives’ interest, which is not to become, in the phrase used in class, “a suit of China,” and not to be the next Sri Lanka. Election lines and the policy after the election are different things.

India’s practice, which he defended with the Bangladesh case: **do not answer** the campaign abuse in the same language. Answering gives the desperate candidate importance and does permanent damage. Jamaat lost. India is now trying to engage the **Tarique Rahman** government.

**Instability**, the last challenge, and mostly a restatement of yesterday: a civil war in Sri Lanka in the past; a civil war **now in Myanmar**; **Nepal’s Gen Z** protests last year; Bangladesh **2024**, when Hasina was forced to resign.

## 6. The way forward he dictated (IR-08-06)

These three sentences were **not** in the printed handout. He dictated them onto the empty space, and they are on notebook pages 2 and 3.

1. **Do not pick favourites.** Engage **every** political party, the one in office and the one in opposition. A favourite creates a binary: everyone else gangs up on India. **Sheikh Hasina** is the case. She leads the **Awami League**, she is the daughter of **Sheikh Mujibur Rahman**, and she was Prime Minister from **2009 to 2024**. India invested in her alone. The other parties knew she was the Indian favourite. Do not burn the bridge to today’s opposition; they can be tomorrow’s government. His mirror: foreign leaders who meet Modi also meet **Rahul Gandhi**, as Leader of the Opposition, because Indian governments change.
2. **Stand up for what the people of the neighbour actually want** — jobs, development, growth — and make the small neighbours **part of India’s growth story**. A pro-India public then outlasts any one government. Sri Lanka is the recent proof. The **4 billion** package, against a crisis he blamed on China’s debt trap, left people thankful. In the **recent Sri Lankan election, no candidate used an anti-India line**, because the politicians knew it would lose votes.
3. **Deliver.** Announce less, finish on time. A road promised in **five** years and built in **twenty-five** cannot compete with China, which he said finishes a promised five-year port in **three**. The standing example is the **India–Myanmar–Thailand (IMT) Trilateral Highway**, still not complete, deadlines missed.

Under that, a formula he said belongs in **every** IR answer on how to strengthen a relationship. Globalisation needs all three levels, not government talks alone.

| Level | Meaning | How, in this class |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **G2G** | Government to government | Visits, negotiations, agreements. Necessary, and not enough. |
| **P2P** | People to people | **Culture** (which is also soft power), **sport**, **education** (scholarships, student exchanges), **tourism**. |
| **B2B** | Business to business | **Free trade agreements (FTAs)** and **connectivity**, so the volume of trade can rise. |

The answer shape he stopped to teach: when the question says **examine, analyse, evaluate, critically examine**, or **comment**, describe the thing, then its strength, then its weakness, and close with a **way forward**. A civil-service answer is not “China is too strong, so India can never answer.” The close has to be constructive. That is why this sitting was built as idea, then strength, then challenge, then these steps.

## 7. What India wants in Afghanistan, and the graveyard (IR-08-07)

Afghanistan is the first bilateral. The neighbour that would serve India is **stable, peaceful, united, democratic**, and **free of outside influence** — he named **Pakistan** and **China**. Those words, he said, are alien there. Afghanistan is among the most unstable countries, and it is called the **Graveyard of Empires** because powerful states have tried to dominate it and failed: the **British** in the **Anglo-Afghan wars**, the **Soviet Union** in the Cold War, and **America** recently. The country was destroyed in the process.

The old affinity, before that wreck, was historical. The **Kushan** empire reached Afghanistan and Central Asia. Medieval invaders came **from** Central Asia and Afghanistan. **Maharaja Ranjit Singh**’s empire reached Afghanistan. In the national movement, **Abdul Ghaffar Khan** took part; he is remembered as the **Frontier Gandhi**.

Handout page 5 is a **forty-year** chronology. He started at **1979**.

**1979.** The **USSR** invaded, to install a **communist** government. The map was different: the five Central Asian republics were still Soviet until **1991**, so the USSR and Afghanistan **shared a border**. The five, in his mnemonic: **two T’s** — **Tajikistan, Turkmenistan**; **two K’s** — **Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan**; **one U** — **Uzbekistan**. Afghanistan was described as the **soft underbelly** of the Soviet Union.

The United States wanted to stop a communist expansion and could not fight the USSR directly. The reason is nuclear deterrence, which stays **IR-03**. So it was a **proxy war**, run through **Pakistan**. The ISI used radical religious propaganda: Islam was under threat, and the Soviet Union was an enemy of Islam. That produced an army of **Afghan Mujahideen**. A **mujahid** is a person willing to fight for the religion; he said **deen** means religion. Training, weapons, and money came through Pakistan, and the money and weapons were American. Pakistan was the agent on the ground. The war ran **ten years, 1979 to 1989**. In **1989** the Soviet Union withdrew. Disintegration, and the end of the Cold War, he placed **later, in 1991**. Do not merge the withdrawal year and the collapse year. America had blocked the communist expansion. Afghan problems, he said, had just started: the young men had been radicalised, armed, and left proud of having beaten a superpower.

They wanted a mujahideen government and strict Islamic rule. They could not agree on a leader. **Afghanistan’s diversity is ethnic, not a single community that shares a mosque.** Ethnicity, in this class, is culture, language, history, geography, and custom — not religion alone. The same religion does not make one community, and one community need not share one religion. His Indian picture of that second point was the **Punjabi** community: Hindu, Sikh, and, across the border, Muslim.

**Civil war, 1989 to 1996.** In the **north**, the prominent groups are **Tajiks, Uzbeks, and Hazaras**. They formed the **Northern Alliance**, supported by **India and Iran**. India had a defence agreement with **Tajikistan** and ran **Indian Air Force** bases there, used to support the Alliance. The sheet names two: **Farkhor**, then **Ayni**. The audio slurred both; lock the sheet.

In the **south**, on the Pakistan border, the **Pashtun** community, **Pashto**-speaking. From Pashtun students in **madrasas** came the **Taliban**. A **talib** is a student in a madrasa. Pakistan radicalised them. The Taliban was a **proxy of Pakistan**.

**1996.** The Taliban defeated the Northern Alliance and formed a government. India gave it **no recognition** and kept supporting the Alliance. Only **three** states recognised that government: **Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates (UAE)**.

Afghan territory was then used to harbour terrorism against India. An Indian Airlines aircraft, **IC-814**, was hijacked and taken to **Kandahar**, which was the Taliban’s military headquarters. The Taliban sympathised with the hijackers. India released **Masood Azhar**, then in a **Jammu** jail. After his release he formed **Jaish-e-Mohammed (JeM)**. The **1999** date of the hijacking stays **IR-07**; this class said “the 90s” and added the prisoner and the organisation.

The Taliban of that government: an **ultra-conservative, radical, political and militant** organisation of **Pashtuns**, with the object of enforcing **Sharia**. Sharia, as he defined it, is law inferred from Islamic scripture — the **divine theory of law**, as against a man-made constitution. India’s opening words are **“We, the people.”** Under that rule, **women’s education and employment were banned**, and a woman could not leave the house without a male family member. **Minority** rights: the **Bamiyan Buddha**, which he called the highest rock statue of the Buddha and a **UNESCO** world-heritage site, was **bombed**. **Human** rights: a thief’s **limbs** were cut off in public; a heinous crime was punished by public **beheading**. This government lasted until **2001**.

## 8. The twenty-year war, and Taliban 2.0 (IR-08-08)

**2001.** The **11 September** attacks, by **Al-Qaeda** under **Osama bin Laden**. About **3,000** Americans died; he said **2,977** to be precise. Highest American deaths on a single day since the Second World War, since **Pearl Harbor**. America declared a **war on terror**. **US-led NATO** forces attacked Afghanistan. The codename is **Operation Enduring Freedom (OEF)**. The attack was on Afghanistan because the Taliban had sheltered Al-Qaeda and bin Laden, so Afghan territory was being used against the United States. The Taliban was thrown out of **Kabul**. The stated next goal was a **stable democracy**, so the territory would not be used for terrorism and rights would not be violated.

Afghanistan adopted a democratic constitution. The first election was **2004**: **Hamid Karzai**. He was **re-elected in 2009**. The constitution allows **only two terms**, so he could not contest **2014**.

**2014:** **Ashraf Ghani** against **Abdullah Abdullah**. Ghani won. Abdullah said the election was rigged and refused the result. The United States brokered a compromise: **Ghani as President**, Abdullah as **chief executive**, a **National Unity Government**. The sheet’s letters beside this are cramped; he said National Unity Government. **2019** repeated the crisis. Ghani won again, Abdullah again refused, and the two held **parallel oaths**. America again put Ghani in the presidency and accommodated Abdullah.

India, he said, was delighted. No relationship with the 1996 Taliban; an excellent one with the democratic government. Ghani was a friend.

The war still lasted **twenty years, 2001 to 2021**, because the Taliban **insurgency** never accepted the democratic government. They called it a puppet installed by the United States, and they kept fighting to retake Kabul and restore Sharia. America knew that a troop withdrawal would collapse the democracy. It helped build the **Afghan National Security Forces (ANSF)** to represent that government. **India and the United States** trained them and gave them weapons.

**Osama was not in Afghanistan.** In **2011**, after ten years, the **Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)** found him in **Pakistan**, in **Abbottabad**, in a large compound **in front of Pakistan’s military academy**, living with his wives and children — not in a cave. He compared that to a man living across the road from the **Indian Military Academy (IMA), Dehradun**: not possible without the government’s knowledge. The United States did **not** tell Pakistan. It had lost trust. A **surgical strike** killed him. **Barack Obama** announced it in a night press conference. Pakistan denied knowing. He called 2011 a turning point in South Asia: the US–Pakistan relationship deteriorated, America **stopped military aid**, Pakistan’s economic trouble deepened, and Pakistan **tilted to China**, joined the BRI, and took Chinese investment. From 2011, India and the United States became **defence partners** and **strategic partners**. India’s line to Washington, as he put it: you now see the pain we have been describing.

The war’s cost, as he stated it: **six trillion dollars** — he set that against India’s economy at **four trillion**, already on **IR-07** — and the lives of **hundreds of thousands** of American soldiers, paid by taxpayers who had already been through the financial crisis of **2008–09**. Public opinion turned. A democracy is accountable, so from 2011 America wanted an **exit**. Obama tried to end the war and failed.

**January 2017:** **Donald Trump** took the oath. He once said “2007” in the same breath; the oath year is **2017**. American practice, as he taught it: elections in **November**, oath in **January**, a **four-year** term, and if the President dies the **Vice President** serves the rest. There is **no** fresh election in the middle. Ending the Afghan war was the first promise. In **2017 and 2018** he still used Obama’s method: peace talks **between the Taliban and the democratic government**, so that a deal would let America leave. **India’s stand: Afghan-led and Afghan-owned.** The democratic government should lead, because India wanted the talks to strengthen the democracy. The Taliban refused to sign with a government they called illegal.

In **2018** Trump telephoned the Taliban and opened **direct** talks, **bypassing** Kabul. The talks were in **Doha, Qatar** — the **Doha** talks. He called them the last nail for the democratic government: more legitimacy for the Taliban, none of the role India had asked for.

**February 2020:** a **US–Taliban peace deal**. America agreed to leave. The withdrawal would be **phased** — a **15-month** timeline, not overnight — and **conditional**. Two conditions. First, **intra-Afghan negotiations**: talk to the other communities, the **Tajiks, Uzbeks, and Hazaras**, even if not to Ghani. Second, a promise **never again to sponsor terrorism**. The Taliban accepted both. He called it a **deal of convenience**. Both sides wanted the Americans gone. As the troops left, the democracy weakened and the Taliban grew.

Trump expected the timing to win the **November 2020** election. He lost. The reason in this class was **COVID**, a subject off the manifesto, after he had run on ending the war, an anti-China line, an anti-immigrant line, and a wall on the Mexican border. He then behaved, in the comparison used here, like Abdullah: the election was not free and fair, and his supporters protested at the **Capitol**. **Joe Biden** took the oath in **January 2021** and said the same deal would be carried out.

**15 August 2021.** The Taliban **recaptured Kabul**. This is **Taliban 2.0** — the second Taliban government. The first was **1996**. Democracy collapsed “like a house of cards.” The **ANSF surrendered**. Weapons India and America had supplied are now with the Taliban. **Ashraf Ghani fled.** American troops were still at the **Kabul airport**, catching the last flights, while the rest of the city was already Taliban. A twenty-year war, **six trillion dollars**, to replace the Taliban with the Taliban. He read that as **declining American hegemony**. **Vladimir Putin**, he said, watched, and attacked **Ukraine** a few weeks later. A further aside — America failing in **Iran**, with Russian and Chinese support — was not developed.

India has given **Taliban 2.0 no official recognition**. His phrase: back to **1996**.

The shape of the new government is on **handout page 17**, which is not in these four sheets. ### Lecture — locks

1. **2014 oath = SAARC leaders, including Nawaz Sharif.** **2015** Lahore. **2016 Uri** is the turn: talks, trade, and “blood and water” with terror. **2019 oath = BIMSTEC**, seven Bay states, Pakistan out. **2024 oath = seven neighbours**, a list that includes **Seychelles and Mauritius** and is not the BIMSTEC list. **Last SAARC summit: 2014.** The sheet’s **2016** beside SAARC is the Uri year, not the invitation.
2. **Doval Doctrine, since 2016: defensive offence.** Uri → surgical strikes into **PoK** (sheet: **Line of Control**). Pulwama → **Balakot**. Pahalgam → **Operation Sindoor**, **nine** places, a **continuous** operation against proxy infrastructure. The nine-camp hard-power line stays **IR-07**.
3. **Line of Credit** ≈ **1–2%**, against China’s **more than 6%**. A **grant** is not repaid. A **currency swap** is dollars from the **RBI** against local currency, and the RBI holds the risk. **Maldives: 750 million dollars.** Reserves **more than 700 billion.** **Sri Lanka 2022: 4 billion**, swap plus credit plus grant. He corrected **2018 → 2022**. Northern power plants, **~50 km from Chennai**, moved from Chinese to Indian companies. Islands not named.
4. **Jaishankar:** lead, extra mile, non-reciprocal, generous, invest — so neighbours respect two core concerns, **China’s presence** and **terrorism from their soil**. **Gujral covered the whole region. This policy excludes Pakistan.**
5. Election anti-India lines are a device. **Jamaat-e-Islami**, backed by the **ISI**, lost. **BNP** won; **Tarique Rahman** is Prime Minister. **2023 Maldives:** Solih’s **India First** lost to Muizzu’s **India Out**; Muizzu then came to the 2024 oath and took the **750 million** swap. Do not answer campaign abuse in kind.
6. Way forward, dictated: no favourites (Hasina, **Awami League**, **2009–2024**); stand with the neighbour’s public (Sri Lanka’s recent election had **no** anti-India line); deliver on time (**IMT** highway still unfinished; **5 years** slipping to **25**, while China finishes a five-year job in **three**). Formula: **G2G, P2P, B2B**.
