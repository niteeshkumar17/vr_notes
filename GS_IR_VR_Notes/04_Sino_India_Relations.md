## 0. Why China (IR-04-01)

Four reasons on the board:

1. **Largest neighbour** — long-running **border dispute** still live. Vajpayee: we can choose friends, **not** neighbours.
2. **Rising power** — already **number two**; world’s **second-largest economy**; world’s **largest military**; competing with the United States in **artificial intelligence (AI)** / quantum. Trump talk of a **G2** (US–China).
3. **Largest trading partner** and **largest source of import** — Indian economy is heavily import-dependent on China. Class lock for **2025–26: bilateral trade crossed US$151 billion**. Trade is **heavily in China’s favour** (deficit detail = **18 Sep, IR-05-06**).
4. **Recurrent theme in Indian foreign policy (IFP)** — the proverbial **elephant in the room**. Neighbours, big powers, **United Nations (UN) / BRICS** — China pops up. Big **security challenge** for years to come.

Sheet list for the course: (1) importance of China; (2) brief history; (3) **three Cs** — cooperation, competition, conflict; (4) major irritants — **border dispute** and **Sino–Pak nexus**. Competition as a separate block is thin today; conflict lives in border + Pakistan.

**Civilizational states:** India and China are not only two nations with two territories — they are two of the world’s ancient civilizations (roots **> 5,000 years**). Iran is also called one. Ancient traffic: **Faxian / Xuanzang** to India; **Buddhism** India → China via the **Silk Route** through Central Asia; India got **paper** and **silk**. GS lock from here is **post-Independence**.

## 1. Three phases (IR-04-01)

| Phase | Years | Class lock |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **I** | **1950–1962** | Friendship → war |
| **II** | **1962–1991** | Freeze, then Rajiv reset |
| **III** | **1991 onwards** | Three Cs |

**Why 1950:** **1949** Mao’s communist revolution. India was the **first non-socialist-bloc** country to **recognise communist China** and established **diplomatic ties in 1950**. (Cold War: India in neither American nor Soviet bloc.)

### Phase I — Hindi–Chini Bhai-Bhai, then the drop

Initial years: friendship and ideological convergence. Slogan **Hindi–Chini Bhai-Bhai** = Indians and Chinese are **brothers**, not mere neighbours.

**Panchsheel agreement, 1954** — five principles to guide the relation (full list already IR-01; do not restudy): mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty; mutual non-interference; mutual non-aggression; equality and mutual benefit; peaceful coexistence.

**Two factors** that wrecked the phase: **Tibet** and the **border**.

**Tibet as buffer (1947):** independent in class terms — own **currency, passport, legal system**. A **buffer state** = territory that separates two countries so they do not fight. Mao sent the army; **by 1950 Tibet annexed**. Buffer gone. India was **not happy** (cultural links snapped) but **was not in a position** to stop it.

By the **same 1954 Panchsheel**, India recognised Tibet as the **Tibet Autonomous Region** of China. China had promised **autonomy** (internal affairs). It did not. **1959** revolt, brutally suppressed. **Dalai Lama** (political + religious head) fled; India gave **political asylum**. China still calls him a **separatist** and read the asylum as **interference in internal affairs**.

**Border:** India inherited an **unsettled** Sino-Indian border from the British. From **1955**, Chinese maps showed large Indian tracts as China — **Aksai Chin** (Ladakh) and what was then **North-East Frontier Agency (NEFA)**, now **Arunachal Pradesh**. Talks found no common ground → **1962 war**. China inflicted a **crushing defeat**. Lowest point. Blow to India’s prestige as a Non-Aligned leader. Nehru had not imagined the *bhai* would stab him in the back.

### Phase II — no ambassador, then Rajiv–Deng

**1962–1976: no ambassador-level ties.** Restored **1976**. The ambassador sent was **Dr K.R. Narayanan** — later **President** of India (sheet *KK* = same person).

**1979:** External Affairs Minister **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** visited — **no breakthrough**.

**1988:** Prime Minister **Rajiv Gandhi** — **landmark**. First Indian PM in China in **~30 years** (last was Nehru). **Deng Xiaoping** rolled out the red carpet. Deng: the **21st century could be an Asian century** if the two Asian giants — **elephant and dragon** — come together. (19th = Europe; 20th = US.)

**Dual-track policy** (still live): (1) keep talking **resolution of the border**; (2) **do not let the border stop** ties in other areas — economic, cultural, scientific. Deng had opened China in **1978**; India **Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (LPG) in 1991**.

### Phase III — 1991 onwards

**1991** = LPG **and** disintegration of the **Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)**. Relation characterised by **cooperation, competition, conflict**.

## 2. Cooperation — bilateral, regional, global (IR-04-02)

**Since Rajiv Gandhi’s 1988 visit**, India and China have cooperated at **bilateral, regional and global** levels.

### Bilateral — political

High-level visits both ways. **Narendra Modi** and **Xi Jinping** have met multiple times this decade:

| When | Where | Class lock |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **2019** | **Chennai / Mahabalipuram** (sheet *Chennai*) | Informal summit |
| **Oct 2024** | **BRICS Kazan** | Reset attempt |
| **Aug 2025** | **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Tianjin** | Reset attempt |
| **Sep 2026** | **BRICS New Delhi** | Xi in India after **7 years** |

The **~5-year** meeting gap was the **Eastern Ladakh standoff** (still on). Kazan + Tianjin were attempts to **reset** ties **adversely impacted** by that standoff. Dual-track is back: Kailash–Manasarovar yatra restarted; **direct flights**; **business visas**.

**BRICS New Delhi (class, same 18th summit as CA-260906 — no extra Day-1):** both agreed to **continue improving ties**; declared they were **partners and not rivals**. India: **for good ties, peace at the border is necessary**. Relation should rest on **three mutuals — respect, sensitivity, interest**. Both: **long-term strategic perspective**. Former ambassador line: Eastern Ladakh took the relation **10 steps back**; Modi–Xi have taken **3 steps** forward — still **net negative**.

### Bilateral — economic

China has overtaken the US as **largest trading partner**. **Bilateral trade crossed US$151 billion in 2025–26.** China = **largest source of import**. Deficit = **18 Sep, IR-05-06** (~US$112 billion).

### Bilateral — border (names today; mechanism in §5)

Cooperation through **border agreements** and the **Special Representative (SR)** mechanism — even while the border is a **major irritant**.

### Regional

**SCO** mechanism. **Founding members of BRICS** and of two financial institutions: **Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)** and **New Development Bank (NDB)** (also called the BRICS Bank). NDB newspaper extras stay on IR-03 / CA-260906.

### Global

**Climate change:** greater **Western** responsibility to cut **greenhouse gas (GHG)** emissions and to provide **climate finance** for the **Global South**. India and China accept they are top emitters and will cut, but the **West polluted first**.

**Reform of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank** — still **Western-dominated**. (UN class lock stays: World Bank President appointed by the **US President**; IMF cannot move without the US as largest shareholder.) Modi at BRICS: from a **pyramid of privilege** to a **platform of cooperation** = multilateralism, not unilateralism.

## 3. Border as colonial legacy; LoC vs LAC; three sectors (IR-04-03)

Wherever the British left, they left a **permanent dispute** (India–Pakistan, India–China, Israel–Palestine). Three names for the Sino-Indian line:

| Year | Person | Lock |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **1865** | **Johnson** | Surveyed **Aksai Chin**; concluded it was part of the princely state of **Jammu and Kashmir**. **India favours the Johnson line.** |
| **1893** | **McDonald / McDonnell** (**British** ambassador **to** China — **not** a Chinese ambassador) | Map showed Aksai Chin as **China**. **China favours this line.** Britishers **did not resolve the ambiguity**. |
| **1913–14** | **Sir Henry McMahon**, Simla Conference | Aim: demarcate **Assam–Tibet**. Chinese **boycotted** (said talks must be British–Chinese, not British–Tibetan). McMahon ignored them, pushed the line, produced the **McMahon Line**. China: **colonial imposition**, does **not** recognise it; claims **entire Arunachal** as **Zangnan / South Tibet**. |

After **1857**, princely states under British **paramountcy** (foreign + defence = British). J&K was a princely state; once it joined India, Johnson’s Aksai Chin came with it — that is India’s case.

### Line of Control (LoC) vs Line of Actual Control (LAC)

| | **LoC** | **LAC** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Where | **Jammu and Kashmir** only — Indian Kashmir vs **Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK)** | **Entire Sino–India border** |
| Paper | **Demarcated** on ground **and** map | **Not demarcated** |
| Basis | Two India–Pakistan agreements: **1949** and **1972** | **Perception** |

Class classroom: each side patrols to *its* idea of the line; perceptions **overlap** → “get out of my territory.” Soldiers are taught to **fight**, not chat. In matching-perception sectors they exchange sweets; in overlapping sectors they clash.

**Claim line vs LoC vs LAC vs China-occupied Kashmir:** India’s **official map** claims the whole former princely state. In reality India controls only part. **PoK** is the only **direct land link Pakistan–China**. If PoK were under Indian physical control, **Afghanistan** would be a neighbour (access to Central Asia). **China-occupied Kashmir** on the class map = **Aksai Chin + Shaksgam**. (UN world-map row is already CA-260908 — no extra Day-1.)

### Three sectors

| Sector | Indian geography | Dispute |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Western** | **Ladakh** | India: China illegally occupies **Aksai Chin** (since **1962**) and **Shaksgam Valley** (**Pakistan gifted to China, 1963** — “neighbour’s flower-pot”). |
| **Middle / Central** | **Uttarakhand + Himachal Pradesh** | **Least** disputed. Class examples: **Barahoti** (Uttarakhand); some Himachal pockets. |
| **Eastern** | **Sikkim + Arunachal Pradesh** | China claims **~90,000 km²** — **entire Arunachal as South Tibet**. Chinese **place-name lists**; objection to PM at **Sela Tunnel**; **stapled visas** for J&K and Arunachal travellers; **Shanghai airport** detainee whose passport said birthplace Arunachal; **no visa** to an Arunachal Indian Administrative Service officer (“he is coming from China”). |

**Stapled visa** = extra page, stamped, **taken away** — no passport record of the China visit. Signal: China does **not** treat J&K / Arunachal as integral India.

**One-China policy (class):** India recognised **Tibet** (1954) and **Taiwan** as part of China. **Since 2010** — **16 years** — India has **not repeated** “I support One China” in any **joint statement**. Foreign Secretary last year: “our position is well known.” **2026 Modi–Xi:** the **Chinese** statement claimed India repeated Tibet/Taiwan-as-China; **India’s statement had no such line** — reply to stapled visas.

Most disputed = **eastern and western**. Middle = least; **early harvest** of delimitation is **most likely** there.

## 4. Importance of Tibet and the Dalai Lama issue (IR-04-04)

Handout box: **Tibet lies at the heart of the Sino–India border dispute.** Three reasons.

### Political

Buffer removed (India unhappy). Asylum to Dalai Lama (China unhappy). China presents a **distorted** history (“Tibet always ours”); class: Tibet has been **in and out** of Chinese control; in a medieval stretch Tibet was the stronger. When a **centre weakens, peripheries assert** (Mughal analogy).

### Religious

**Dalai Lama** = Tibetan for **ocean of wisdom**; was **political and religious** head (Vatican / Pope analogy). Tibetan Buddhism = **Vajrayana** (tantric); believes in **reincarnation**. Hierarchy: **Dalai Lama = No. 1**; **Panchen Lama = No. 2**; each recognises the other’s incarnation. Current Dalai Lama is the **14th**.

**1995:** Dalai Lama recognised a boy as the next Panchen Lama; the boy was **kidnapped the next day**; China installed its own Panchen Lama (who praises Beijing). China accused of **severe human-rights violations** in Tibet and of **diluting the Tibetan character** by settling **non-Tibetans**.

**Dalai Lama (last year, 90th birthday in class):** next Dalai Lama will be appointed by a **trust he has set up** and will be born in the **“free world”** (outside China). **China rejected that** — **only the Chinese government** has authority to appoint the next Dalai Lama. Experts: a **battle for control of Himalayan Buddhism**. **India’s stand on succession will significantly determine** the shape of that battle. Class: India should **not** let China decide.

**Tawang** (Arunachal): cultural centre of Tibetan Buddhism; birthplace of the **6th Dalai Lama**. China wants Tawang **in any water / border settlement**. India: Tawang is India.

### Strategic

Tibet is to China what **Odisha** is to India — a **mineral** store. **~40% of China’s minerals** from Tibet. **Water tower of Asia.** Three Indian rivers rise in Tibet: **Indus, Satluj, Brahmaputra**. China is building a **mega-dam on the Brahmaputra** (water-as-weapon fear) — **full river block = 18 Sep, IR-05-07**. Bullet-train / infrastructure up to the Arunachal border = faster **military mobilisation**. In a war, class: **China has the upper hand** on speed.

**Mao — five fingers of Tibet:** if the **palm** is Tibet, the fingers are **Ladakh, Bhutan, Sikkim, Nepal, Arunachal**.

| Finger | Class “if we control…” |
|:---|:---|
| **Ladakh** | via West Pakistan, access to the **Arabian Sea** |
| **Bhutan** | via East Pakistan, same sea logic |
| **Sikkim** | cut the **Siliguri / Chicken’s Neck** corridor (mainland–Northeast) |
| **Nepal** | strike into the heartland (**Uttar Pradesh / Bihar**) |
| **Arunachal** | entire Northeast under the grip |

That is why Mao moved on Tibet in **1950**.

## 5. Border agreements, WMCC, SR (IR-04-05)

**Long-term goal:** peaceful **resolution**. **Short-term:** keep peace on an undemarcated, perception-based line.

**1993:** maintain **peace and tranquillity** (first agreement after Rajiv).  
**1996:** military **confidence-building** — **no heavy troops / heavy weapons** at the border.  
**Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination (WMCC):** officers’ group, **includes military**, **headed by a diplomat** — India’s lead is **Joint Secretary (East Asia)**. Comes in when local commanders cannot clear a tension.

**Border protocols (class):** no patrolling party **carries weapons**; if two parties meet, **raise the banner and go back**; **no tailing**; local commanders first, then WMCC.

**Special Representative (SR) mechanism, 2003:** a cabinet-ranked person each side, **one-to-one**, for **final settlement**. India: **National Security Advisor (NSA) Ajit Doval**. China: **Wang Yi** (also Foreign Minister; class nickname **Silver Fox**).

**25th round — Beijing, August 2026** (Wang Yi–Doval):

- two groups under **WMCC** for **“early and substantial harvest of boundary delimitation”**;
- **two additional border meeting points** and **two hotlines** between militaries in the **eastern and middle** sectors (western hotlines already exist);
- next SR round in **India, 2027**.

**Delimitation = on the map; demarcation = on the ground** (Radcliffe analogy).

**2005 — three stages of settlement** (still the ladder):

1. **Political parameters and guiding principles** — **done 2005**. Criteria: e.g. if there is a **settled population**, the other side will **not insist** on that tract; **package** covering all three sectors; historical sensitivity. (Class warning: China building **artificial villages** on the contested border to game the “settled population” criterion.)
2. **Technical agreement** — **ongoing since 2005**. How to use a mountain / a river as the line.
3. **Delimitation and demarcation** — not yet.

Early harvest: sector **not named**; **middle** is the least disputed, so **most probable**.

## 6. Eastern Ladakh standoff (IR-04-06)

Still on. Three questions: **what / why / efforts**.

### What (from 2020, Covid lockdown)

Chinese troops came **several kilometres inside** Indian territory in Eastern Ladakh, **pitched camps**, blocked Indian patrols — both in sectors **with** a perception gap **and** in sectors **without** one. Class scale: **~50,000** Chinese with **heavy weapons**; India matched with **~50,000**. **~1 lakh** still on the line with tanks / artillery / missiles. This **broke 1993 and 1996**.

**Pangong Tso:** **one-third** India, **two-thirds** China; **perception differs**. **Galwan:** class lock — **no difference in perception**.

### Why

Experts, several reasons; class stress:

1. **Article 370 (2019)** — J&K split into two **Union Territories** (greater Centre). Home Minister in Parliament: we will take back **PoK and Aksai Chin**. China criticised.
2. **Quad** (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue: **India, United States, Japan, Australia**). China calls it **Asian NATO**. Reminder: India is the **only Quad country with a direct border** with China — “I can poke you.”
3. **Most important in class: the Darbuk–Shyok–Daulat Beg Oldie (DS–DBO) all-weather road** after **two decades**. **Daulat Beg Oldie (DBO)** sits near **Karakoram Pass**, the **Aksai Chin highway** (1950s), and **overlooks the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)**. 24×7 tanks/guns to DBO = **power-projection** China did not like.

India is trying to **close the infrastructure gap** (still huge): **Sela Tunnel** (Arunachal; China objected to Modi’s inauguration); **Vibrant Villages** border scheme; Indian mobile signal now where Chinese towers used to win.

### Efforts — three terms (do not swap)

| Term | Class |
|:---|:---|
| **Disengagement** | Troops **separate by a few km**; a **buffer** so they do not stand eyeball-to-eyeball. **He can still be in your territory.** |
| **De-escalation** | **Bring the tension down.** |
| **De-induction** | **Remove troops and weapons** to peacetime locations; each side in its territory; **free patrol** of old patrolling points. |

India activated **WMCC** and **commander-level talks** (Western sector, many rounds), plus PM / External Affairs / Defence / NSA tracks.

**What has happened:** **disengagement at all friction points.** First at **Galwan Valley, June 2020**. **Buffer is on Indian territory**; Chinese still sit on the far side; **local shepherds cannot graze** in the buffer.

**Galwan fight:** a patrol led by **Colonel Santosh Babu** went to verify Chinese pull-back (protocol: **unarmed**). Chinese still there → clash. **20 Indian soldiers** died. **First deaths on the LAC in 45 years** — last death **1975**. Before that: broken jaws/bones, **no deaths**. Class Chinese figure **~35**. This is why close troops can blow into a war — **next class will reuse Galwan**.

**What India wants:** **de-escalation + de-induction** and **status quo ante April 2020** (*status quo ante* / *jata stithi*): Chinese out, old patrol restored. Dual-track continues (yatra, flights, SR) even while the line is not settled.

**Xi** = most powerful Chinese leader after Mao; ideas of **Mao, Deng and Xi** are in the Chinese constitution.

## 7. Sino–Pakistan nexus (IR-04-07)

Will **not** be retaught in the Pakistan lectures — “recall lecture 2 / this class.”

**Nature:** not a free lunch — **transactional**. Pakistan: China is an **all-weather ally**. Basis: **common enmity with India** + **mutual benefit**. Pakistani poetry on the sheet: higher than the Himalaya, deeper than the Indian Ocean, sweeter than honey.

**Implications for India:**

1. **Territorial** — **Shaksgam** to China; **CPEC** runs **Xinjiang → PoK → Gwadar** (Balochistan). Class: CPEC is a **BRI** arm; China pumping **> US$62 billion** (~**20% of Pakistan’s GDP**) into roads, pipelines, dams. Fulfils Mao’s **Arabian Sea** finger. **Gwadar** as a future **Chinese naval base** threatens the Indian Ocean. India **boycotts BRI / rejects CPEC** because it **violates territorial integrity**. Same infrastructure can **resupply Pakistan in a war**. China building a road in Shaksgam that **threatens India’s position at Siachen**. **BRI full treatment = 18 Sep, IR-05.**
2. **Two-front war** — fighting **China and Pakistan at once** stretches resources; Indian doctrine must live with the possibility. **1965:** Chinese **second-front** talk was **rhetoric**. **1971:** might have been real had India not signed the **Indo–Soviet Treaty, 1971** (deterred US **and** China). USSR is gone; Russia is **no match**.
3. **Military and nuclear** — **Pokhran 1974** raised Pakistani insecurity (“we will eat grass but make the bomb”). **China helped Pakistan to nuclear parity.** Parity then licensed **proxy war** (state-sponsored terrorism) — **thousand cuts** because Pakistan cannot win an outright war. China also **military supplies** that **narrow India’s conventional edge**.
4. **Support over terrorism** — China is Pakistan’s **UN Security Council diplomatic shield**. Modi: Pakistan is the **mothership of terrorism**; Xi: Pakistan is a **victim**. China has **blocked** Indian bids to list Pakistan-based individuals/groups. Class terror pair: **13 December 2001** Parliament; **26 November 2008** Mumbai. **Operation Sindoor:** one Indian general — **one front, three adversaries** — **Pakistan + China + Turkey** (Chinese technicians, satellite intelligence; Turkish drones). Extra on the Sindoor / PRAHAAR cluster, **no extra Day-1**.

**Low-cost Chinese strategy:** prop Pakistan to **box India inside South Asia**.

**What should India do:** **strengthen itself** — **economic, military, technological** — and keep **good ties with the West**. Class pegs: indigenous **Sudarshan Chakra** air defence; **S-400** (Russia) in the Pakistan air threat; **German** submarines, **French Rafale**; **US** still **indispensable** (even under Trump) — **Maverick** anti-tank; **Pax Silica** (US grouping vs China’s semiconductor choke — already IR-01). **BRICS must not be painted anti-Western.**

### Update — 17 September 2026 (*The Hindu* — Pak–China Boundary Joint Commission)

**Press Trust of India.** First meeting of the **Pakistan–China Boundary Joint Commission** in **Islamabad**. India **rejected** it (clip: **Wednesday**).

**Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) spokesman Randhir Jaiswal:** the body has **no legal basis** to decide arrangements on **Indian territory under illegal occupation**. **“There is no boundary between Pakistan and China.”** India rejects the so-called Joint Commission as **without any legal basis**.

**Already in this class — do not restudy:** Shaksgam **1963** gift; **China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)** through **Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK)**; India **rejects CPEC** as a sovereignty violation.

**New locks**

- India has **never recognised** the so-called **China–Pakistan Boundary Agreement of 1963**.
- Entire **Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh** “**have been, are and will always remain integral and inalienable parts of India**.”

## 9. Why India refused BRI (IR-05-01)

India needs infrastructure (roads, ports; Delhi Metro was built with Japanese money; **Sagarmala** is India’s own port-capacity project). China offered that money through **BRI**. India still said no.

**Cost–benefit:** the **cost of joining was greater than the benefit**. The benefit let go was infrastructure investment.

**Two costs**

1. **China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)** passes through **Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK)**. Joining BRI would have meant accepting Pakistan’s control of that territory. Since **1947** India has said Kashmir is India’s and the occupation is illegal. (The 17 Sep rejection of the Pak–China Boundary Joint Commission stays on **IR-04-07**.)
2. BRI is not only roads. Through it China raises its influence in **South Asia**, which India treats as its **backyard**. Realism in class: **power is a zero-sum game** — one side up, the other down.

## 10. What BRI is (IR-05-01)

| Lock | Class line |
|:---|:---|
| What | Massive infrastructure project of **President Xi Jinping**, launched **2013** |
| Scale | Investment of **more than US$3.5 trillion** in **more than 100 countries** |
| Land | **Silk Road Economic Belt (SREB)** — roads, highways, digital connectivity, oil and gas pipelines. On the map: central China to **Rotterdam** (Netherlands) |
| Sea | **Maritime Silk Road (MSR)** — ports and other coastal infrastructure. China’s eastern coast to Europe |
| Name | Both revive the ancient **Silk Road** (China in the east, Europe in the west; silk was the export). Buddhism’s road to China is already Part 1 |

China has also extended BRI toward the Americas. Do not expand that into a third corridor.

**Malacca Dilemma.** Chinese oil from West Asia and Africa crosses the Indian Ocean and the **Strait of Malacca**. China fears the United States can block that lifeline. Two branches were drawn to reach the Indian Ocean without that choke. A 22 Sep *Hindu* piece uses **Malacca–Singapore** as a **straits regime** for **Hormuz** — that is a different Malacca, on `GS_IR_VR_Notes/01_Basics_of_International_Relations.md`. **No extra Day-1.**

| Branch | Port he named | What it does |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CPEC** | **Gwadar** | Oil and gas come ashore; pipelines run to China’s interior |
| **China–Myanmar Economic Corridor (CMEC)** | **Kyaukpyu** | Same idea, Bay of Bengal side |

He tied both to two of Mao’s **five fingers** (the fingers themselves stay Part 1).

### Impact on India

- **Kashmir policy** would have had to bend (CPEC / PoK).
- **South Asia:** in 13 years every neighbour except **Bhutan** joined. Named: Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Nepal. Money buys influence. **Tarique Rahman** broke the habit that a new Bangladesh prime minister comes to India first; he went to **China**.
- **Africa:** India is the maritime neighbour; China is not, but BRI money has cut that advantage. Chinese mines and processing plants dominate the **critical-mineral** supply chain (technology, semiconductors). Using that dominance to punish others is **weaponisation of economic dependence**. Last year China restricted **rare-earth magnets** to India. Those magnets go into **electric vehicles (EVs)**. Without them the EV shift, and the climate target tied to it, stalls.

### India’s objections

| # | Objection | Class example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | Violates **territorial integrity and sovereignty** | CPEC through PoK. You cannot join BRI and reject CPEC. |
| 2 | **Debt-trap diplomacy** | **Hambantota** (Sri Lanka). Loan terms still not public (**no transparency**). When Sri Lanka could not repay, the port went to China on a **99-year lease**. |
| 3 | Lacks **transparency, inclusivity, sustainability** | Inclusivity: Chinese engineers and labour, so local jobs are thin. **More than 90%** of **Gwadar** revenue goes to China, not a 50–50 split. Sustainability: **Myitsone** dam on the **Irrawaddy** in Myanmar — stopped by the elected government for the damage, cleared by the military government; most of the power goes to China. |

## 11. What India offered instead (IR-05-02)

China’s economy is about **five times** India’s. India cannot outspend BRI. With like-minded partners it has pushed other corridors. (He used the **Monroe Doctrine, 1823** — the Americas as a US sphere — only as the backyard analogy. Do not drill it as an India fact.)

| Step | With whom | Lock |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Asia–Africa Growth Corridor (AAGC)** | **Japan** | Japan: money and technology. India: experience in Africa. Aim: connect Africa with South Asia and Southeast Asia. Both are hit by China’s supply-chain grip. |
| **International North–South Transport Corridor (INSTC)** | **Russia and Iran**, idea floated **2000** | Mumbai–Suez–**St Petersburg** takes **45 days** and is exposed to West Asia (Houthis on the Red Sea). INSTC: St Petersburg by road and rail to the **Caspian**, across the sea into Iran, then **Bandar Abbas** to India. About **26 days**, and **40%** more efficient. If Suez is shut, the fallback is the Cape of Good Hope, which adds time. Putin’s India visit: they agreed to build the **missing links**. |
| **India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC)** | **United States** and West Asian partners, floated at India’s **G20, 2023** | India–Europe via West Asia, shorter than Suez. Partners he named: **UAE, Saudi Arabia**, and **Haifa** in Israel. Meant to check BRI in West Asia. It needs Israel–Saudi ties. After the **2023** Hamas attack and the Gaza war, Saudi Arabia’s line was: no normalisation until Palestine is addressed. Class: no permanent friends; interest is permanent, so the corridor can still move. |
| **Chabahar** | **Iran** | India’s answer to **Gwadar**, and India’s gateway to Afghanistan. Being tied to INSTC. The two ports are **less than 200 km** apart. Needs workable ties with **both Iran and the US**. The project is under **US sanctions**. |

## 12. Indian Ocean: pearls, diamonds, MAHASAGAR (IR-05-03)

Indo-Pacific is the arena. In the **Indian Ocean Region (IOR)** the expert labels are:

| | China | India |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Label | **String of Pearls** | **Necklace of Diamonds** |
| Coined | **2004**, US firm **Booz Allen Hamilton** | **2011**, former Foreign Secretary **Lalit Mansingh** |
| Meaning | Encirclement of India by access to bases | Counter-encirclement |
| Status | **Not** an official Chinese policy. A Chinese official will deny the name. | **Not** an official Indian policy. An Indian official will deny the name. |

**Official** Indian policy: **SAGAR (2015)** — **Security and Growth for All in the Region** — upgraded to **MAHASAGAR** — **Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions**. Sagar = sea, mahasagar = ocean: a larger version. Under it India helps with coastal infrastructure, surveillance, the **blue economy** (ocean fisheries and the like), and **humanitarian assistance and disaster relief (HADR)**, and presents itself as a **net security provider**. He cited help after **Cyclone Ditwah** in Sri Lanka and after flash floods in Nepal.

Necklace of Diamonds and MAHASAGAR are **complementary**: diamonds are access to bases; MAHASAGAR fills the other gaps. UPSC has asked String of Pearls, so the unofficial name is writable.

**Pearls he named:** **Coco Island** (just north of the Andamans), **Kyaukpyu**, **Chittagong**, **Hambantota**, **Malé**, **Gwadar**, and **Djibouti** — China’s **first overseas military base**, and it is **operational**.

**Diamonds he named:** **Chabahar** (the answer to Gwadar), **Duqm** (Oman), **Assumption Island** (Seychelles), **Agalega** (Mauritius — India has built a runway there), **Sabang**, **Changi**, plus partners **Vietnam, Japan**, and the **Philippines** (India has given the **BrahMos** supersonic cruise missile; the name joins the **Brahmaputra** and Russia’s **Moskva**).

**Why the pearls hurt — three ways:** security, **energy** (India is a net oil-and-gas importer from West Asia and Africa), and **economic** lifelines (most trade moves by sea). Pulses from **Mozambique and Tanzania**; palm oil from **Indonesia and Malaysia**.

China calls the ships **research vessels**. India calls them **spy ships**: they map the ocean floor for submarines, and they watch missile tests. **December 2024:** a Chinese ship sat in the Bay of Bengal for an announced test from **APJ Abdul Kalam Island (Odisha)**; India **deferred**; the ship was back on the new date.

**Yuan Wang 5**, **August 2022**, docked at **Hambantota** despite India and the US. Snooping range he gave: **700 km** — covering **Sriharikota**, Kalam Island, **Eastern Naval Command at Visakhapatnam**, and **Kudankulam**. Sri Lanka was in crisis and needed **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** money, which would not move without China. Since then Sri Lanka has been more careful not to anger India.

Navy comparison he gave, without ship names: India has **two** aircraft carriers and is getting a **third**; **two** nuclear submarines and a **third** coming. China’s third carrier is larger than India’s and has some technology only the US also has.

## 13. UNCLOS, then salami slicing (IR-05-04)

**1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).** Three provisions for this class:

| Zone | Lock |
|:---|:---|
| **Territorial sea** | **12 nautical miles** from the coast. Sovereignty and Indian law run only this far. Beyond that, the water is international. |
| **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)** | **200 nautical miles** from the coast (**the 12 are inside the 200**). Only that country may take the sea’s resources — **living** (fisheries) and **non-living** (oil and gas). |
| **Disputes** | **Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA)**. **2014:** India–Bangladesh maritime case. India **lost some area**, could have appealed, and **did not** — it wanted the relationship with **Sheikh Hasina**. |

How two close coasts (India–Sri Lanka, Oman–Iran) split an EEZ is a **geography** question. He did not lock a continental-shelf mileage.

**Salami slicing:** China takes territory in thin pieces, so the other side does not go to war over any one slice, and over time the slice is a meal. He placed it in **Doklam** (Bhutan), **Eastern Ladakh**, and the **South China Sea**.

## 14. South China Sea (IR-05-05)

**Nature:** a **maritime** dispute between China and the neighbours on that sea — **Philippines, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei**, and **Taiwan**.

| Year | What |
|:---|:---|
| **2009** | China published a map with an imaginary **U-shaped nine-dash line** and claimed about **90%** of the South China Sea |
| **2013** | A **tenth dash**, to take in **Taiwan** |
| Islands named | **Paracel**, **Scarborough Shoal**, **Spratly**. Taken bit by bit from Vietnam and the Philippines in particular |
| **2013 / 2016** | Philippines took China to the **PCA in 2013**. The **2016** verdict: the nine-dash line is **illegal** under UNCLOS. China called the verdict illegal and **sped up militarisation**, including **artificial bases** |

**US line:** the sea is **not a Chinese lake**; it is an international water body; **freedom of navigation and overflight**. US ships and aircraft go close to the islands. China pays fishermen to **park** vessels and not fish — he said **250 to 300** vessels — to show the islands are occupied. China’s historical claim (we held them in antiquity) was rejected in class by the Akhand Bharat comparison: using ancient empires as title would hurt India’s own neighbours.

**Why it matters**

| | World | India |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Trade | **One-third** of global shipping, trade of **more than US$3.5 trillion**. Whoever controls the sea can disrupt it. | **55%** of India’s **Indo-Pacific** trade (US West Coast, Japan, South Korea) passes through it |
| Resources | Oil, gas, fisheries | **Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC)** was exploring in **Vietnam’s EEZ**, with Vietnam’s permission. China said leave. India refused. |

**India’s stand**

| | Pre-Galwan | Post-Galwan (June 2020) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Posture | **Neutral.** Did not take sides. Did not want to antagonise China. Asked the coastal states to write a **code of conduct** among themselves. | **More assertive.** South China Sea is a **primary** interest (it had been secondary). Tit for tat: if China pokes India’s backyard, India can do the same in China’s, with partners. |
| Words India then used | — | **Not a Chinese lake.** Part of the **global commons**. China must follow UNCLOS (he called it the **constitution of the sea**) and the **PCA** verdict. No use of force to settle disputes — **without naming Taiwan** — and settlement under the **UN Charter** and international law. |

**Steps after Galwan:** ties upgraded with **Vietnam, Philippines and Japan** (strategic partners). Both presidents were in India last year; on the day the Philippines president was here, the two navies exercised in Philippine waters. With the **Quad**, India says it wants a **free, open and inclusive Indo-Pacific**.

**Indo-Pacific Maritime Domain Awareness (IPMDA):** a Quad project. Satellites watch **dark ships** — vessels that **switch off the radio transponder** (like covering a number plate) and take fish inside the **Philippines EEZ**, which under UNCLOS belong only to the Philippines. India’s node is the **Information Fusion Centre in Gurugram**. **Malabar** alternates: one year the Indian Ocean, the next the Pacific. China watches those exercises from what it treats as its backyard. When the Japanese prime minister was in India, China’s line was that India–Japan ties should not target a third country.

## 15. Economic ties (IR-05-06)

Colonial pattern, with China in Britain’s place: India sends **raw materials** and buys **finished goods**. China has overtaken the US as the **largest trading partner** and is the **largest source of imports** (the **US$151 billion** total for **2025–26** is already Part 1). The total hides the tilt.

**Trade deficit** = imports minus exports.

| | Figure he locked |
|:---|:---|
| Exports to China | about **US$20 billion** |
| Imports from China | about **US$132 billion** |
| **Deficit** | about **US$112 billion** — the first time it crossed **US$100 billion** |
| Comparison | India’s annual defence budget is about **US$80 billion**. China takes in more than that just by trading with India. |

The sheet’s **US$152 billion** is the same total-trade ballpark as the **US$151 billion** of Part 1. The deficit sum is **132 − 20 = 112**.

Imports are especially **electronics and pharmaceuticals**. Export industries also buy **intermediate** inputs from China.

**Weaponisation, last year** (and again when Modi met Xi at **Tianjin**): China squeezed

| Item | Why it hurt |
|:---|:---|
| **Diammonium phosphate (DAP)** | Kharif, including rice |
| **Rare-earth magnets** | EVs |
| **Tunnel boring machines (TBM)** | Infrastructure, including the bullet-train project |
| **Critical minerals** | The wider supply chain |

**After Galwan**, India had tightened the tap: Chinese **foreign direct investment (FDI)** lost **automatic** approval (case by case); **Atmanirbhar / vocal for local**; Chinese firms kept out of infrastructure projects. At the **BRICS** summit in **New Delhi** the two sides decided to **normalise economic ties further** — some of those curbs eased (business **visas**, **direct flights**, a more open FDI line; he cited a **Vivo** deal with an Indian firm that was allowed). He called the easing **temporary**. India still knows the vulnerability.

**What India should do** (his list, not a government white paper)

1. **Greater market access** in China, including fewer **non-tariff barriers (NTBs)** — quality rules and quotas, not only tariffs — and sell **manufactured** goods, not only raw materials.
2. **Manufacturing** deep enough to matter in global supply chains. Use **free trade agreements (FTAs)**: he named the **EU** deal (he called it the mother of all of them), the **UK** deal, and the negotiation with the **US**.
3. Reforms in **land, labour, agriculture and tax**, so investors do not prefer **Vietnam**. After the **2018** US–China trade war, firms leaving China went to Vietnam, not India. **NITI Aayog** is the think tank he named on taking **China Plus One** seriously.
4. **Infrastructure.** Ship **turnaround**: **3 days** in China, **10 days** in India. In the time India handles one ship, China handles three.

## 16. Brahmaputra, then congagement (IR-05-07)

The three Tibetan rivers are already Part 1. The one that matters most is the **Brahmaputra** — lifeline of the Northeast and of Bangladesh, where it is the **Jamuna**.

**Medog** project, which China calls the **project of the century**:

| Lock | Figure |
|:---|:---|
| Power | **60 gigawatt (GW)** |
| Investment | **more than US$167 billion** — he called it the largest single infrastructure investment in the world |
| Scale | About **three times** the **Three Gorges**; **60 GW** would cover **Great Britain’s** electricity need for a year |
| China’s reply to India | Concerns dismissed |

**Four worries**

1. **No river-water treaty** (unlike the **Indus Waters Treaty** with Pakistan and the **Ganga** treaty with Bangladesh). There is only a **data-sharing** arrangement for the Brahmaputra, **April to October**, and India **pays**. When the border is tense, China has **stopped** the data.
2. **Poor record** as an upstream state. Dams on the **Mekong** have cut flows to **Cambodia and Vietnam**.
3. **Not transparent.** First no dam, then a few (**Zangmu** has started), then a series, now the project of the century.
4. The dams can be a **weapon**: store water in a dry year, or release it and make an artificial flood. The Himalaya is still rising (Indian plate under the Eurasian plate). A big quake could burst a dam downstream. China says the engineering is safe.

**India’s buffer, not a cure:** the **Upper Siang** dam in **Arunachal Pradesh**, **11 GW**. The **Siang** is the river’s name after the **Yarlung Tsangpo** enters India; after the **Dibang** joins, it is the Brahmaputra. The dam is meant to **mitigate** (reduce, not remove) a sudden Chinese release. Locals are protesting displacement. He said a newspaper report on that protest was out the day of class; the clip’s detail is the protest, not a new official figure.

**Long-term policy.** In a period of global uncertainty (Trump’s tariffs), India has been **normalising** with China, especially on the economy. Experts call that **strategic hedging** — cutting risk. They also say lasting peace is a **structural impossibility** while China’s attitude holds. Four reasons on the sheet:

1. Unresolved **border**
2. **Sino–Pakistan** collaboration against India
3. China’s effort to **undercut India in South Asia**
4. Friction over **Dalai Lama succession**

Hence **congagement** = **containment + engagement**.

| Contain | Engage |
|:---|:---|
| Pearls checked by the **Necklace** and **MAHASAGAR** | **BRICS** (Xi did come to Delhi) |
| BRI checked by **IMEC** and the other corridors | Economic normalisation, where it helps |

## 17. India–Russia, introduction only (IR-05-08)

A friend in need. **Time-tested.** In hard moments Russia (and before it the USSR) has bailed India out. The **1971** treaty as a deterrent to the US and China is already Part 1.

| Why Russia still matters | Lock |
|:---|:---|
| **P5** | Has used the **veto** for India. An Indian **UN Security Council** seat needs every P5, including Russia. |
| Defence | **Largest supplier.** About **60%** of India’s inventory is of **Russian or Soviet** origin. |
| Energy | **Largest oil supplier** as well. |

**Two phases**

**Soviet, 1947–1991.** Three pillars:

1. **Defence** — the West would not sell. **MiG-21**, retired last year after **six decades**.
2. **Economic and technical help** — **Bhilai** steel plant; payment in **rupees** when India had no dollars; **Aryabhata**; **Rakesh Sharma** on a Soviet craft.
3. **Geostrategic convergence** in South Asia: **India + USSR versus US–China–Pakistan**.

When the USSR collapsed, India lost its biggest backer. Both successors spent the 1990s looking at the **United States**, the only superpower, and **1991** is also **LPG**. Ties dipped.

**Post-Soviet, 1991 onwards**

| Year | Lock |
|:---|:---|
| **2000** | Putin in India. Raised to a **strategic partnership**. |
| **2010** | Raised again to a **special and privileged strategic partnership**. Qualitative — “a very special friend” — not a new treaty text he dictated. |
| Mechanisms | **Annual summit** (one year Delhi, the next Moscow) and **2+2** (foreign and defence ministers). India has a 2+2 with the **Quad countries and with Russia**, which is why he called Russia a close partner. |
| Recent summits | **2024** Modi to Russia. **2025** Putin to India. **2026** is Modi’s turn to go. |

Defence is still the **core** pillar. Economic ties are being pushed and are full of constraints — that mechanism is **next class**.

No permanent friends. In this phase Russia has moved closer to **China**. Until Trump’s second term India had moved closer to the US. He pointed at a US bill: the **top five** importers of Russian oil and gas would face sanctions, and India is the **number 2** importer of Russian oil. That tariff threat sits over the relationship. Since **2000** India has had three prime ministers (**Vajpayee, Manmohan Singh, Modi**); Russia has had **Putin**.

## 18. Part 2 — UPSC Quick Recall

1. India needed the infrastructure and still refused **BRI (2013, Xi, &gt;US$3.5 tn, &gt;100 countries)** because **cost &gt; benefit**. **CPEC / PoK** would have rewritten the Kashmir line. South Asia is the backyard; power is zero-sum. **Bhutan** is the neighbour that stayed out.
2. **SREB** = land (to Rotterdam). **MSR** = sea. **Malacca Dilemma** → **CPEC / Gwadar** and **CMEC / Kyaukpyu**. Objections: sovereignty, **debt trap** (**Hambantota, 99 years**), and the lack of **transparency, inclusivity, sustainability**. **Gwadar revenue &gt;90%** to China. **Myitsone** on the Irrawaddy.
3. Counters: **AAGC** (Japan), **INSTC** (**2000**; **45 → ~26 days**, **40%**), **IMEC** (**G20 2023**), **Chabahar** (**&lt;200 km** from Gwadar). China’s economy ≈ **5×** India’s.
4. **String of Pearls, 2004, Booz Allen Hamilton.** **Necklace of Diamonds, 2011, Lalit Mansingh.** Both **unofficial**. Official line: **SAGAR 2015** → **MAHASAGAR**. Pearls hit **security, energy, economy**. **Djibouti** = first overseas Chinese base. **Yuan Wang 5, Aug 2022, 700 km.**
5. **UNCLOS 1982:** **12 nm** sovereignty, **200 nm EEZ**, disputes to the **PCA** (**India–Bangladesh 2014**). **Salami slicing** = Doklam, Eastern Ladakh, South China Sea.
6. **Nine-dash line, 2009, ~90%.** Tenth dash **2013** (Taiwan). **PCA 2016: illegal.** Pre-Galwan **neutral**. Post-Galwan: **not a Chinese lake**, UNCLOS + PCA. **One-third** of shipping, **&gt;US$3.5 tn**. India: **55%** of Indo-Pacific trade. **IPMDA** / **dark ships** / Fusion Centre **Gurugram**.
7. Deficit **~US$112 bn ≈ 132 − 20**. Total trade **~US$151 bn** is not the deficit. Defence budget **~US$80 bn**. Weaponisation: **DAP, rare-earth magnets, TBM, critical minerals**. Turnaround **3 days vs 10**.
8. **Medog: 60 GW, &gt;US$167 bn**, about **3× Three Gorges**. No water treaty; data **April–October**, and it stops when the border is hot. **Upper Siang, 11 GW**, mitigates. **Congagement** = contain (IMEC, necklace) + engage (BRICS, economy). Four blocks: border, Sino-Pak, South Asia, Dalai Lama succession.
9. Russia: time-tested, **P5 veto**, **~60%** of the inventory, largest oil supplier. **2000** strategic; **2010** special and privileged; **2+2** plus annual summit. **2024** Modi, **2025** Putin, **2026** Modi’s turn. Defence-as-core is next class.
