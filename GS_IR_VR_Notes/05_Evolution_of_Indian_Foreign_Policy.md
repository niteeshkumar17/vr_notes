## 1. Optimistic non-alignment, 1946–62 (IR-06-01)

**Jawaharlal Nehru.** India refused to be a **camp follower**: neither the **North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO)** nor the Warsaw Pact. That refusal is the policy of **non-alignment**.

Three gains.

1. **Strategic autonomy.** The ability to run an independent foreign policy without outside interference. It is the foundational principle of Indian foreign policy. Join a military alliance and you must **bandwagon**: blindly follow the power that has promised you security. NATO members bandwagon the United States, because there are no free lunches. Bandwagoning is the opposite of strategic autonomy. India has never joined a military alliance.
2. **Leadership of the Global South.** The poor, post-colonial, third world — mostly in the southern hemisphere — is the **Global South**. The rich world, Europe and North America, is the **Global North**. Those states launched **NAM**. Nehru was a founding father, and a poor country that had just left colonial rule still took a leadership role.
3. **Best of both worlds.** A post-colonial state had scarce resources and did not want them spent on the Cold War. It still needed foreign money and foreign technology. Non-alignment let India take both from the American camp and from the Soviet camp. In the 1950s, while basic and capital-intensive industry and hydroelectric projects were going up, help came from the United States, Britain, France, **and** the Soviet Union. He pointed to the NCERT on Indian economic development.

**NAM is more than neutrality.** You can call it neutral in the sense of staying out of the two camps. Calling it *only* neutrality does not do it justice. It was a **principled distance** from both.

The mistake was not the policy. The policy was excellent. India became **too optimistic** while carrying it out. The message to the world was peace, cooperation, and the land of Gandhi. The false step was to assume that others would therefore be peaceful with India. International politics is anarchy: self-help, survival of the fittest. A polite deer is still eaten. National security was taken for granted, and **China attacked in 1962**. The war itself stays on the China note. The lesson here is why India was surprised: idealism was asked to do the work of security.

**Nehru was an idealist.** Idealism, as he restated it: states should cooperate, take a multilateral path, respect international law and human rights, and seek **collective security** through an organisation such as the United Nations, instead of balance of power, an arms race, and alliances.

**Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel was a realist.** First Home Minister and first Deputy Prime Minister. Realism puts national security first, and security comes from power. He wrote to Nehru not to trust China: China had a record of grabbing land, Hindi-Chini-bhai-bhai and the **1954** friendship (Panchsheel) would not hold, and India should be ready for a war. The notebook dates one letter **27 July 1950**. The audio did not dictate the day. Patel died before 1962. The attack came as he had warned.

## 2. Realism and recovery, 1962–71 (IR-06-02)

The 1962 defeat ended the optimism. India started treating national security as the first job and tried to recover the war losses.

**1965.** Pakistan attacked, reading India as weak: the China defeat was fresh, and **Nehru had died in 1964**. Pakistan underestimated **Lal Bahadur Shastri**. India had already begun to rebuild. The reply held. Indian forces reached the outskirts of **Lahore**, capital of Pakistan’s Punjab, and a ceasefire came before the city was taken. His slogan in that war was **Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan**. The audio heard “Jawahar”.

## 3. Regional power, 1971–91 (IR-06-03)

A **regional power** is the strongest state in its own region. India’s region is **South Asia**, south of the Himalayas. **China is East Asia, not South Asia.** For most of these twenty years the Prime Minister was **Indira Gandhi**. She was not her father’s idealist. Her decisions were realist.

**Friendship treaty with the USSR, 1971.** The West called India a hypocrite: non-aligned, and still signing with the Soviet Union. America described India as **Team B of the USSR**. She answered that India had not joined the Warsaw Pact, so it was still non-aligned. A friendship treaty is not a military alliance. Only an alliance carries an obligation to guarantee security, and only an alliance forces bandwagoning. It was an open secret, though, that if America threatened India the Soviet Union would help. That is what **neutralised the American threat** in the December 1971 war. America did not jump in, because a Soviet jump would have made the war too expensive.

The victory was not an ordinary one. **East Pakistan** became **Bangladesh**. The map of South Asia changed and Pakistan was weakened. On **16 December 1971**, General **A. A. K. Niazi** of the East Pakistan army signed the surrender before Lieutenant General **Jagjit Singh Aurora**. **More than 90,000** Pakistani soldiers and officers became prisoners of war. The army chief was General **Sam Manekshaw**.

**1974, Pokhran.** India was the first country in South Asia to test. Nuclear technology is **dual-use**. Civilian use is power, and he named the **Kudankulam** plants in Tamil Nadu. Military use is a weapon. The 1974 test was officially a **peaceful nuclear explosion**: civilian only. The West did not believe it. India did **not** declare a weapon in 1974. The weapon test is **1998**, same place, under **Atal Bihari Vajpayee**. That distinction is the new lock. The bare fact “tested twice” is already on the world-order note.

### Why Bangladesh is not the Sri Lanka case

He answered a question before the later phases, and the sheet puts it with 1971.

Pakistan is built on the **two-nation theory**: separate nations for separate religious communities. India rejected that. India is unity in diversity, and **sarva dharma sambhava** — equal respect and protection for every religion. Endorsing a Tamil separation in Sri Lanka would have invited the same demand at home.

Inside Pakistan, power sits with **Punjabi Muslims**. He said more than **90 per cent** of Pakistan’s prime ministers and army chiefs have been Punjabi Muslims. The Prime Minister then was **Shahbaz Sharif**, a Punjabi. The army chief then was also a Punjabi. He did not name the chief. Sindhis, Baloch, and — before 1971 — Bengali Muslims were left out.

In the **1971** election, Sheikh **Mujibur Rahman**’s **Awami League** won the most seats, because East Pakistan had more people and more seats than the west. He should have become Prime Minister. He was jailed instead. Bengali Muslims then launched the **Mukti Bahini** movement (mukti = liberation). The audio glossed “Bahini” as Bangla land. Do not memorise that gloss. The **East Pakistan army** answered with **Operation Searchlight**: millions targeted, hundreds of thousands killed. About **15 million** refugees entered India. Pakistan then opened the war on India’s western border, thinking India would stay out of the east. India was ready, and went into the east as well.

Bangladesh was not made on religion. Both sides were Muslim. It was made on **Bengali** ethnicity and language. The 1971 constitution made it a **secular** republic, with Mujib as Prime Minister. India also had the geography: East Pakistan had no land link to West Pakistan, and India enclosed it on three sides. The population as a whole wanted out. That is not the Sri Lanka problem.

### Sri Lanka, and why the phase ends in a failure (IR-06-04)

In the **1980s** Sri Lanka had a civil war. **Sinhala** majority, mostly Buddhist, against the **Tamil** minority in the **Northern Province**. Tamils were treated as second-class. They formed the **Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE)**. Eelam means a Tamil homeland. They wanted a separate state, by violence, on the Bangladesh analogy. India was sympathetic to the Tamils and wanted the discrimination to end. India did **not** want Sri Lanka broken, and did **not** endorse the LTTE’s methods.

**Rajiv Gandhi.** The **Indo–Sri Lanka Accord, 1987.** Sri Lanka would pass the **13th Amendment**: more **land, police and financial** powers to the Northern Province, a kind of autonomy he compared with the old Article 370. Sri Lanka’s condition: India must help end the civil war first. The LTTE rejected the accord. It wanted separation, not autonomy.

India sent the **Indian Peacekeeping Force (IPKF)** in **1987** for **Operation Pawan**. Three jobs: disarm the LTTE, take the **Jaffna** peninsula, end the civil war. It failed. The force fought about **three years**. About **1,200** Indian soldiers were martyred. A later sentence in the audio said 500. The sheet and the first figure are **1,200**. In **1990** the IPKF left without ending the war. Soon after, the LTTE assassinated Rajiv Gandhi. He did not give the year of the assassination. The war ended in **2009**, when Sri Lankan forces killed **Velupillai Prabhakaran** and defeated the LTTE.

The 13th Amendment is **still not implemented**. The bargain was implementation in return for disarming the LTTE, and India did not disarm them. India’s line today is still that Sri Lanka should implement it, and give the Northern Province those three powers.

**Lesson.** Do not send your armed forces to settle another country’s internal quarrel. He was not saying India should stay silent on world issues. He was saying do not try to rebuild someone else’s state with your army. Power can destroy an enemy. Building a stable country is harder. The United States failed in Vietnam, and failed in Afghanistan from **2001 to 2021** (the aim was a stable democracy, not the destruction of the country; the Taliban still rules). India failed in Sri Lanka. Failure there does not mean India was the weaker state. India was there to end a civil war, not to smash the island.

## 4. The 1990s: the friend lost (IR-06-05)

The Cold War ended. India’s friend, the USSR, disintegrated into **15** countries. Pakistan’s friend, the United States, won. Bipolar became **unipolar**. American hegemony was bad news for India, which had been closer to the Soviet Union and was still called Team B.

Changes, to live in that world:

- **Economic policy, 1991.** **Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation (LPG)**, under **P. V. Narasimha Rao** and **Manmohan Singh**. A closed economy opened. The United States favoured that model, so the opening was also a **tilt towards America**. Foreign direct investment and foreign institutional investment came in, much of it American. The same tilt pulled India closer to **Japan** and **Israel**, who sit in the American camp. Japan has a military alliance with the United States. He said that today the Japan and Israel relationships are even stronger than the American one, and he quoted **Benjamin Netanyahu**: the India–Israel relationship is a marriage made in heaven. The start was the 1990s.
- **Look East, 1992.** Southeast Asia was growing. Narasimha Rao launched **Look East** for a cordial relationship with **Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)** countries. The present government renamed it **Act East**.
- **Strategic autonomy under hegemony.** China was already a nuclear-weapon state, and the border was disputed. During the Cold War the Soviet friendship was read as a shield: the USSR would not let China attack. That shield was gone. One answer was to join an American military alliance and accept bandwagoning. India refused. The other answer was to become strong enough alone. In **1998**, under Vajpayee, India became a nuclear-weapon state and gained **nuclear deterrence**. He said China can no longer dare a war. Nine states have the bomb (already IR-03). Very few of the nine have a **triad**: the ability to launch from **land, air, and underwater**. **North Korea does not.** **India and China do.** A triad makes deterrence more credible. He did not list the others who lack it.

**Submarine-launched ballistic missile (SLBM).** The underwater leg. Submarines began as German **U-boats** in the Second World War, for stealth. Diesel and electric boats are noisy. **Sonar** reads the sound. He used **200 kilometres** as the classroom range at which a noisy boat can be found. A **nuclear-powered** boat replaces that engine with a small reactor and is much harder to hear. A nuclear-powered boat that can also fire a ballistic missile is the sharpest version. India has one. He named **Arihant**. The commission year is not this class.

## 5. Balancing power, and multi-alignment (IR-06-06)

**Phase 5** is the first fourteen years of the century, **2000–2014**: Vajpayee, then Manmohan Singh for ten years. **Phase 6** is **2014 onwards**, Narendra Modi. He taught them together because they overlap. Manmohan Singh started the policy. Modi intensified it.

By 2000 India was more confident: a nuclear-weapon state, and one of the fastest-growing economies after 1991. The world was leaving unipolarity. American hegemony was declining. A multipolar order was forming. India chose friendly relations with **several** rising centres at once, including centres that hate each other.

| Pair of rivals | India keeps both |
|:---|:---|
| United States, and Russia / Iran / China | Relations with the United States kept growing: the **nuclear deal** under Manmohan Singh; strategic and defence partnership under Modi; a trade deal he said was being worked on |
| Arab world (he named **Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar** — oil and gas) and **Israel** | Both. The Arab side’s quarrel with Israel is Palestine |
| **Iran** and the Arab states, especially Saudi Arabia | Both. He called it the Shia–Sunni divide. The West Asia class will open it |
| **Japan**, and Russia / China | Japan treats both as rivals |
| **European Union** and Russia | He said India has recently signed “the mother of all trade deals” with the EU. He did not give the date |

You cannot put Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Modi in one room. India meets them separately: a BRICS summit on one side, a Quad summit on the other. That skill is what he means by a **balancing power**. Do not put every egg in one basket.

**America’s China-containment initiatives, which India has joined**

- **Quad.**
- **Pax Silica** — the American answer to China’s hold on critical-mineral supply chains. Already **IR-01**. No extra Day-1.
- **Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment (PGII)**, launched by the **G7**, led by the United States, because criticising BRI is useless if China is the one writing the cheques. **IMEC** (India–Middle East–Europe Corridor) is one PGII project. India is in both. The China–Pakistan Economic Corridor is a BRI project, already on the China note. He said India and America have both boycotted BRI.

**China- and Russia-led initiatives, which India has also joined**

- **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)**, led by China and Russia, aimed at American hegemony.
- **BRICS Plus**, where China and Russia lead the push against American economic hegemony, including de-dollarisation. The **New Development Bank (NDB)** is their alternative to the American-dominated International Monetary Fund and World Bank. The bank’s own locks stay on the world-order note.
- **Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)**. He inverted the middle words. China is the biggest shareholder. **India is the second.** AIIB money is used for BRI, which India has boycotted. The reason he gave is strategic, not tactical: tactical is a short aim, strategic is a long one. If these become the financial institutions of the next twenty or thirty years, India wants a seat in them now.

India is in every camp. He said India is the **only** country with simultaneous membership of **SCO, the Quad and BRICS**. That is possible because of strategic autonomy. One day a Quad meeting discusses how to answer China. The next day an SCO or BRICS meeting discusses how to answer American hegemony.

**Non-alignment** meant equal **distance**. **Multi-alignment**, Jaishankar’s word for the present policy, means equal **closeness**. A military alliance is a marriage: NATO members are committed to the United States, Japan is under the American **nuclear umbrella** (Japan has no bomb; America has promised to use its own if Japan is attacked). You cannot keep the other relationships. India therefore stays with **strategic partnerships** and **defence partnerships**, which are a high degree of friendship and are still not an alliance. Those two types were opened in the previous class. He also named a recent **Makkah Defence Pact** as another alliance. He did not name its members.

**AUKUS** is the alliance of **Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States**. Australia is to receive nuclear-powered submarines. Join it and you take that camp’s foreign-policy line.

**Strategic hedging.** The word is borrowed from investment: spread the money so one crash does not take all of it. His examples of separate assets were shares, crypto, property, gold, silver, and a bank fixed deposit. In foreign policy it means: do not over-rely on one partner in a world that is **neither fully unipolar nor fully multipolar**. Nobody can say who will dominate in twenty or thirty years. America, in his line, is a **declining** hegemon, failing in West Asia, and Donald Trump changes position too often to be the single basket. Varma Sir used “hedging” for the China normalisation (`04_Sino_India_Relations.md`). This use is the wider one: many partners, not one. Both stay.

America criticises the policy because it wants India to bandwagon, and India has refused. The job of the policy is India’s interest, not Washington’s approval.

### Energetic diplomacy, from 2014 (IR-06-07)

Jaishankar’s phrase for the Modi years: new energy, and an attempt to look like a **global power**, not only a regional one. The world accepts that only if India takes on global problems.

- **2023:** India hosted the **G20**. Under that presidency the **African Union** became a G20 member. His argument: if the European Union is in, the poorer Global South should have a voice too.
- **This year** India hosted the BRICS summit. That summit is already on the China note (12–13 September 2026). No extra Day-1.
- Founding member of the **International Solar Alliance**, headquarters **Gurugram**. A role in the **Paris** climate agreement. During the pandemic, free vaccines under **Vaccine Maitri**. A vocal line against terrorism.
- India has also started giving money to Global South countries for their growth, and calls itself the **voice of the Global South**.
- India **supports multipolarity**. America wants to keep unipolarity. India’s addition: a multipolar world is not possible without a **multipolar Asia**. One country, China, should not be left to dominate the continent.

A friendly relationship does not mean agreement on everything. America and Canada differ. India and Russia differ. India and China do economic business and still have disputes. He described India–China as **three Cs: conflict, cooperation and competition**. A later slip said “contribution”. Lock the three he defined. Varma Sir’s word for the same relationship is **congagement**. Do not merge the two labels.

### Lecture — locks

1. **1946–62** is optimistic non-alignment: autonomy, leadership of the Global South through NAM, and money plus technology from **both** camps. The policy was sound. Taking security for granted is what 1962 punished. Patel was the realist in that argument.
2. **1962–71** is realism and recovery. Pakistan struck in **1965** after Nehru’s death in **1964**. Shastri’s army reached the edge of Lahore.
3. **1971–91**, Indira Gandhi, regional power in **South Asia** (China is East Asia). The **1971** treaty neutralised America and was **not** the Warsaw Pact. **16 December**: Niazi, Aurora, more than **90,000** prisoners. **1974** was a peaceful explosion. **1998** was the weapon.
4. **IPKF, 1987–90**, Operation Pawan, failed. The **13th Amendment** (land, police, finance for the Northern Province) is still not in force. About **1,200** soldiers. Prabhakaran was killed in **2009**. Do not send an army to rebuild someone else’s state.
5. Bangladesh was ethnic and linguistic, not religious: Awami League, Searchlight, **15 million** refugees, a secular republic. East Pakistan had no land link to the west.
6. The **1990s**: India’s friend lost, Pakistan’s friend won. **LPG 1991**, Look East **1992** (now Act East), and **1998** as the substitute for an American alliance. Triad = land, air, underwater. North Korea has no triad. Arihant is the underwater example.
7. **2000–14** and **2014 onwards** were taught as one policy: a balancing power, **multi-alignment** (equal closeness). India is in Quad and PGII/IMEC, and in SCO, BRICS and AIIB, where India is the **second** largest shareholder. The only simultaneous **SCO + Quad + BRICS** member, in his line. Hedging means not one basket. Energetic diplomacy, the African Union into the G20 in **2023**, and a multipolar Asia.
