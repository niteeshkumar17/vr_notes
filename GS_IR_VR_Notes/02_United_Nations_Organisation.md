## 1. Origin of the United Nations (IR-02-01)

**United Nations Organisation (UNO / UN)** = international organisation formed at the **end of the Second World War**.

**Four fundamental objectives** (class order):

| # | Objective | How (class) |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | Maintain **international peace and security** | **Collective security** = *all for one and one for all*; punish the aggressor. Protect future generations from the “scourge of war.” |
| 2 | Uphold respect for **international law** | — |
| 3 | Protect **human rights (HR)** | Rights available **by virtue of being born as a human** — inalienable; race / sex / tribe do not matter. |
| 4 | Ensure **progress** of humanity | Development, later widened (Human Development Index, Sustainable Development Goals). |

**Blueprint:** **San Francisco Conference, April–June 1945**. **50** founding members → **193** members now.

**Came into existence: 24 October 1945** = **UN Day** (UN’s birthday).

**India** was a **founding member** among the 50 — still a **British colony** in 1945; the British allowed it. India was also a founding member of the **League of Nations**.

**Predecessor:** **League of Nations, 1919**, after the **First World War**. It could not stop the Second World War. Framers of the UN tried to fix those weaknesses.

**Human rights as a 1945 idea:** the world saw the **Holocaust** — systematic killing of **6 million Jews**. Aristotle (class): *without laws and order, humans are the worst of animals.* Homework movie: **Schindler’s List**.

### 2.1 United Nations Security Council (UNSC)

**Executive.** Primary organ for **international peace and security**. Decisions are **legally binding** on all members.

### 2.2 United Nations General Assembly (UNGA)

**Not** the legislature (it does not make binding law). **Deliberative** body — discussion of issues of international importance; passes **resolutions** that are **recommendations**. Sometimes called the **World Parliament**.

**Most democratic organ:** **one country, one vote, one value** — United States and Nepal weigh the same. All **193** members sit here.

### 2.3 International Court of Justice (ICJ)

**Judicial** wing. Detail in §5.

### 2.4 Secretariat

UN **bureaucracy** — services the principal organs. Headed by the **Secretary-General (SG)** = **public face** of the UN.

- Appointed by the **General Assembly** on the **recommendation of the Security Council**.
- Term: **5 years**; can serve **more than one** 5-year term.
- Current: **António Guterres** (Portugal). **January 2027** — a new SG; class: most probably from **Latin America**.
- **Shashi Tharoor** contested; candidature **not supported by the United States**.

### 2.5 Trusteeship Council

Set up to manage **11 trust territories** (many ex-colonies of Germany). By **1994** they had become independent or merged. **No work since 1994.**

**Reform suggestion:** give it **global commons** — a resource that belongs to all humanity. Class examples: **outer space**, **international waters / seas**.

### 2.6 Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)

Manages the UN’s **economic and social** work. **54 members**, elected by the General Assembly. **Supervises specialised agencies**.

## 4. Security Council: membership, voting, actions (IR-02-04, IR-02-05)

Three things to learn: **membership**, **voting**, **actions**.

### 4.1 Membership

**15** members = **Permanent Five (P5)** + **10 non-permanent**.

**P5 (always there):** **United States, United Kingdom, Russia, China, France** — victorious powers of the Second World War. They designed an **exalted** status for themselves.

**Non-permanent:** elected by the **General Assembly** for a **2-year term**, on a **regional** basis. A sitting non-permanent member is **not eligible for immediate re-election**.

**India:** non-permanent member **8 times**; now wants a **permanent** seat.

**Current Asia seat:** **Pakistan** is a non-permanent member; tenure ends **31 December 2026** — so Pakistan **cannot** seek immediate re-election. India is competing for the next Asia non-permanent seat; **Tajikistan** is also in the race (class: Prime Minister’s Central Asia visit).

### 4.2 Voting on **substantive** matters

Substantive = important = **maintenance of international peace and security**. Rules sit in the **UN Charter** (constitution of the UN).

Two conditions:

1. **9 out of 15** must say yes.
2. **No negative vote by a P5** = **veto**.

The word **veto is not in the Charter**. The Charter says the Council cannot decide without the **concurrence of the P5**.

**Three options** for any member (permanent or not): **yes / no / abstain**.

**Abstention by a P5 is not a veto.**

| Class example | What happened |
|:---|:---|
| Ukraine ceasefire, **14–1**, the “no” is **Russia** | Condition 1 met (14 ≥ 9); condition 2 fails → **veto → end of the matter** |
| **Trump Gaza Peace Plan**, November 2025 | Passed; **Russia and China abstained** (P5 abstention ≠ veto). Binding on all members |
| History of **US** vetoes | Class: **about half** used to protect **Israel** |
| **Soviet Union, 1971** | Veto to protect **India’s interest on Jammu and Kashmir** |

<p style="text-align:center;"><em><strong>Figure:</strong> Chapter VI talks; Chapter VII hits; “Chapter 6½” is a UN invention sitting in between.</em></p>

**Chapter VI — peaceful resolution of disputes:** negotiation, **arbitration**, mediation.

**India–Pakistan / Jammu and Kashmir:** India took the matter to the Security Council. Council invoked Chapter VI and appointed **United Nations Commission for India and Pakistan (UNCIP)** to mediate. **Ceasefire 1 January 1949**; under UN military observers the **ceasefire line** was drawn — later the **Line of Control (LoC)**.

**Chapter VII — coercive:** authorise **use of force**, **economic sanctions**, **arms ban**.

**North Korea:** first use of **collective security**, **1950**. North Korean troops had taken **Seoul**. An international force under the **UN flag**, **led by the United States**, pushed them back. **North and South Korea are still technically at war** (no peace treaty). Boundary: **38th parallel** — one of the most heavily militarised lines. Later: sanctions and arms bans over the **nuclear** programme. Leader: **Kim Jong-un**.

**“Chapter 6½” — United Nations Peacekeeping Missions (UNPKM):** a Secretary-General’s phrase. **Midway** between VI and VII. **The word “peacekeeping” is not in the Charter** — an **innovation**. Troops (**blue helmets**, mixed nationalities) go **with the parties’ consent** to hold a ceasefire. They may use force **only in self-defence**. Class: **Colonel Sofiya Qureshi** (army brief after Operation Sindoor) has served on a UN peacekeeping mission from India.

## 5. Three courts in The Hague (IR-02-06)

Newspaper will throw three names. **All three sit in The Hague.** Decisions of all three are **legally binding** on the parties they bind — **none has its own enforcement police**. Weight = **international legitimacy**. For the ICJ, enforcement sits with the **Security Council** (and can be **vetoed**).

| | **ICJ** | **PCA** | **ICC** |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Full name | International Court of Justice | Permanent Court of Arbitration | International Criminal Court |
| UN body? | **Yes** | **No** | **No** |
| Age | Principal organ, 1945 | **Oldest** international court — **Hague Conventions 1899 and 1907** | **Newest** — **Rome Statute 1998**, court from **2002** |
| Who can approach | **Only governments** | **Governments and private parties** | Prosecutes **individuals** |
| Job | Settle **disputes** + **advisory** opinions to UN organs | Arbitration (class: **United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS)** cases) | Four crimes (below) |
| Binding? | Dispute judgment **legally binding**; **advice non-binding** | Binding on parties | Binding on members / situations it covers |
| Enforcement | **No.** Power is with the **Security Council** | No own enforcement | No own army |

### 5.1 ICJ jurisdiction — two doors

Country A can drag B only if:

1. **Voluntary jurisdiction** — the other party **also agrees** (A vs B).  
   **1984 United States vs Nicaragua** (Central America). Both went; ICJ found **against the US**; US **refused** to comply; ICJ **cannot punish**; Nicaragua went to the Security Council; the **US vetoed**.

2. **Compulsory jurisdiction** — a **treaty** both signed says ICJ will settle disputes under that treaty.  
   **December 2023: South Africa vs Israel** on the **1948 Genocide Convention** (in force **1951**; motto *never again* after the Holocaust). Both are parties → Israel **had to appear**. Class figure used by South Africa: **72,000+** killed in Gaza as genocide. Judgment, when it comes, is binding; enforcement still needs the Council.

Homework: **Kulbhushan Jadhav** — India invoked **compulsory** ICJ jurisdiction after Pakistan’s military court death sentence.

### 5.2 PCA — India–Bangladesh 2014

**2014 India–Bangladesh maritime** (not land) boundary — **UNCLOS**. PCA decision **binding**. India **lost some maritime space**, could have appealed, **chose not to** (good ties; **Sheikh Hasina** then). Land boundary is the **100th Constitutional Amendment**, not this case.

Class also: a **PCA** verdict on the **Indus Waters Treaty** a few days before this lecture — **India rejected** it (India did not participate). Comes back in the Pakistan lecture.

South China Sea / UNCLOS promised in **Lecture 3**.

### 5.3 ICC — four crimes, who is out

Prosecutes an **individual** if his own government will not, for:

1. **War crimes**  
2. **Genocide**  
3. **Crimes against humanity**  
4. **Crime of aggression**

**124** countries have signed. **Not members:** **United States, Russia, China, India, Israel**.

**Arrest warrants** (class, last year) against **Vladimir Putin** and the **Israeli Prime Minister** — they are not members, but the alleged crimes were on the territory of **members** (**Ukraine**, **Palestine**). If the ICC asks India to arrest Putin: India says **the decision does not apply — India is not a member**.

**Rodrigo Duterte** (former Philippines President) on trial at The Hague for **crimes against humanity** (anti-drug campaign; many civilians died).

## 6. Assessment — first three weak, fourth strong (IR-02-07)

Judge the UN on the **four founding objectives**. Class verdict after ~80 years: **poor on peace, law, and human rights**; **impressive on progress**.

### 6.1 Peace and security

**Supporters:** no **Third World War**. The League failed that test.

**Critics:** that is **not** the UN’s achievement. Cold War stayed cold because of the **balance of terror** / **Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD)** — **nuclear deterrence**. **1962 Cuban Missile Crisis** — President **John F. Kennedy** chose diplomacy over a first strike (class: no winners in a nuclear war; Hiroshima–Nagasaki).

**Veto paralysis — Cold War:** **1960s Vietnam** (aggressor: US); **1979 Afghanistan** (aggressor: Soviet Union). UN did not rescue either.

**Veto paralysis — after the Cold War:** **Gaza (2023–)** and **Ukraine (2022–)**. UN has not punished **Israel** or **Russia** (US / Russian veto).

### 6.2 International law

**2003 Iraq:** US sought Council authorisation to invade on **weapons of mass destruction (WMD)** charges against **Saddam Hussein**. **Russia, France, China** were not convinced. US invaded **unilaterally** — **Kofi Annan**: violation of international law (only the Council can authorise force, as in **Korea 1950**). **No WMD found.** Class: real aim was **oil**. **Matsyanyaya** — big fish eats the small fish (US / Iraq); UN as bystander.

Class current illustrations of the same “might is right”: **Venezuela**; **Strait of Hormuz** / **UNCLOS** freedom of navigation.

## 7. Reform of the UN (IR-02-08)

Many reforms need an **amendment of the Charter**.

**Two steps (class):**

1. Approved by **two-thirds of the General Assembly** — **128 of 193**.
2. Approved by the **parliaments of two-thirds of members, including all P5**.

**No amendment against the wish of any P5.** That is a **virtual veto** on the Charter itself.

**Three privileges that make P5 a VIP club:** always present; **veto** on Council decisions; **veto** on Charter amendment. The Council is **not** “one member, one vote, one value.”

**Four pillars of reform (sheet):**

1. **UNSC** — fix the **democratic deficit** (membership + veto). Suggestions: **abolish the veto** (no P5 will agree) or, more practical, **majority among P5** (class: **3–2** instead of unanimity).
2. **UNGA** — **binding recommendations** on some matters (give it teeth).
3. **Financial reform** — more money so operations do not shrink.
4. **Quick / good deployment ability** — send troops **fast**; every second of conflict costs lives.

**External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar (class):** P5 membership is **frozen in the world of 1945**. Britain and France are declining; the **sun has set** on the empire. Missing: class’s **3rd / 4th / 6th** largest economies (**Germany, Japan, India**). P5 is a **predominantly white club** (only **China** is not). **Europe** (smallest continent) has **three** permanent seats (Russia, France, UK). **Asia** has **one**. **Africa** and **Latin America** have **zero**.

### 7.1 G4 vs Coffee Club / Uniting for Consensus (UFC)

| | **G4** | **Coffee Club / UFC** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Who | **Japan, India, Brazil, Germany** — four **aspirants** for a permanent seat | Regional **rivals**: **Italy** (vs Germany), **Argentina** (vs Brazil), **Pakistan** (vs India), **South Korea** (vs Japan). Sheet also: **Spain**. Official name **Uniting for Consensus**. Nickname from the **Italian** ambassador calling rivals for **coffee** (also **Cappuccino Club**) |
| Size | **15 → 24 or 26** | **15 → 25** (10 new) |
| New seats | **6 new permanent** + **4 or 5 non-permanent** | **Only non-permanent or semi-permanent** — **no new permanent**, **no veto** |
| How the 6 P split (G4) | **2 Asia** (India, Japan); **2 Africa** (three claimants: **South Africa, Nigeria, Egypt**); **1 Latin America** (Brazil); **1 Europe** (Germany) | — |
| Veto | Undemocratic — **abolish**; **but if veto stays, G4 wants it too** | Forget veto |

**Semi-permanent (Coffee Club):** a member that can be **re-elected for consecutive terms** — today’s rule **blocks** immediate re-election.

**Kite-string point:** members **hold the string**. Weaknesses are **designed** — nobody gives an organisation more power than they will accept (accountability / privacy analogy: Article 21).

## 8. India and the UN (IR-02-08)

Line from class: **either you are on the table or you are on the menu.** India learned that in **1948** on **Jammu and Kashmir** — others decided. India wants to **hold the string**.

Two grounds for a **permanent seat**:

### 8.1 Credentials

- World’s **largest democracy** and **largest population** — a permanent seat = **one-sixth of humanity** at the table; cuts the democratic deficit.
- **6th largest economy.**
- **Responsible nuclear-weapon state** — class: unlike Pakistan, India has **not sold** nuclear technology; non-proliferation record is **impeccable**.
- **Rich civilisational legacy** — **Buddha, Ashoka, Gandhi**; shelter to the persecuted (**Parsis** from Iran; **Maharaja of Nawanagar** took in Polish children in the Second World War — streets in Poland named after him). **21 June** International Yoga Day.

### 8.2 Rich contribution

- **Founding member** (and of the League).
- Always pushed **multilateralism** over unilateralism; strengthen the UN.
- One of the **largest contributors** to **United Nations Peacekeeping Forces (UNPKF)** — **blue helmets**; **first country to send an all-women contingent**; lives lost in the line of duty. This year: **Major Abhilasha Barak**, UN Mission in **Lebanon**, **UN Gender Advocate Award**. **Colonel Sofiya Qureshi** also a peacekeeper.
- **Voice of the Global South** (ex-colonies / developing countries): **decolonisation**; **neo-colonialism** (class: Venezuela, rename games, Greenland); **apartheid** (**Nelson Mandela** — Bharat Ratna); **nuclear disarmament** (even as a nuclear-weapon state); **climate finance** (rich countries polluted first; they fund adaptation / mitigation in the South).

### 8.3 Challenges to India’s bid

1. **China** — the one P5 that has **not** supported India. Other **four P5 have**. **Shyam Saran** (ex-Foreign Secretary): China sees India as an **inferior power** and will not allow **power parity**. Class line: aspirations have hit the **Great Wall**. China’s Coffee Club friend: **Pakistan**.
2. **Coffee Club** — can block the **two-thirds** in the General Assembly.
3. **Status-quo bias among P5** — even supporters know **nothing moves without China**; cheap to “support” India on paper.

**East vs West** inside the Council: **China + Russia** vs **United States**. They do not want a **pro-Western** new permanent (they **rejected Japan** — **G7**). Gaza / Ukraine votes split the same way.

**BRICS** (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa — summit **in India next week**, class): demand **reform of the International Monetary Fund (IMF)** and the **World Bank** (UN specialised-family money bodies). **West dominates:** World Bank President is **always appointed by the US President**; IMF cannot move without the **US** (largest shareholder). India and China want voice matching weight.

**If the UN does not reform**, legitimacy may shift to **G20 / BRICS**. Reform so the UN reflects **2026 power**, not **1945**.

**What India should do (class close):** stay invested; keep **diplomatic** work to build opinion. Next class: **India–China**. Sources enough for GS: **class + handouts + Yellow Book + current affairs**.
