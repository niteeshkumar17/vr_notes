## Section A: Monetary Policy vs Fiscal Policy — The Two Pillars

| | Monetary Policy | Fiscal Policy |
|---|---|---|
| **Hindi Name** | *Mudra Niti* (मुद्रा नीति) | *Rajkoshiya Niti* (राजकोषीय नीति) |

| **Governed By** | **RBI** | **Government** (Central + State) |

### Budget — Historical Context
- **Pre-2000:** Budget presented at **5:00 PM** — convenient for Britishers to print and return same day; no legal basis for this timing
- **Year 2000 (Atal Bihari Vajpayee Govt):** Changed to **11:00 AM** — convenient for Indians
- **Pre-2017:** Budget presented in **March**
- **2017 onwards (Modi Govt):** Shifted to **1st week of February** (specifically 1st February)

### B.1 Revenue Receipts

> **Definition — Three Key Characteristics:**
> 1. They **do NOT create liability** for the government
> 2. They are **NOT obtained through selling of government property**
> 3. *(Implicit)* They are **short-term/recurring** in nature

Revenue Receipts are divided into:

#### (i) Tax Revenue
- **Direct Tax** — Personal Income Tax (PIT), Corporate Tax, MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax), DDT (Dividend Distribution Tax), CGT (Capital Gains Tax), STT (Securities Transaction Tax)
- **Indirect Tax** — GST (on most goods & services), VAT (on 7 excluded items)

#### (ii) Non-Tax Revenue
| Item | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Fees** | Hospital registration (₹10 ticket at AIIMS — ₹10 × 1 crore patients/month = significant revenue) |
| **Fines** | Traffic challans (no helmet/seat belt — through system, not "under the table") |
| **License** | Driving license, passport, **4G/5G spectrum license** (telecom operators pay huge sums) |
| **Dividend** | Part of profit given to shareholder — Government gets dividends from PSUs/PSEs (ONGC, SBI, etc.) |
| **Escheat** | Amount charged by govt for care-taking work on property due to absence of legal heir |
| **Special Assessment** | Amount charged for developmental activities done in a particular area (e.g., toll tax) |
| **Asset Monetisation** | Giving government property on rent/lease (NOT sale) to earn revenue |

### B.2 Capital Receipts

> **Definition — Three Key Characteristics (Reverse of Revenue Receipts):**
> 1. They **may have long-term character**
> 2. They **may be obtained through selling of government property**
> 3. They **may create liabilities** for the government

| Item | How it matches the definition |
|---|---|
| **Recovery of Loans** | Long-term character (e.g., India's loan to Sri Lanka — repaid after 10 years) |
| **Disinvestment** | Obtained through selling govt property (e.g., Air India sold to Tata) |
| **Borrowings** | Creates liability (₹1 lakh crore borrowed → must return ₹1.1 lakh crore with interest) |

> **Memory Aid:** Capital receipts = **huge sums** involved. "A capitalist has a good amount of money."

> **UPSC Logic Test:** *"Why is borrowing a Capital Receipt and NOT Revenue Receipt?"*
> Because borrowing **creates liability** → fits Capital Receipt definition. Revenue Receipt definition says "does NOT create liability" → borrowing cannot be kept under Revenue.

### B.3 Key Non-Tax Revenue Items — Detailed Notes

#### Dividend
- It is a **part of profit** given to the **share owner**
- **Example:** RBI transferred **~₹3 lakh crore** to Government of India in **2026** as a part of **surplus dividend**, while keeping around **5.6%** of its surplus dividend with itself
- This is based upon **Bimal Jalan Committee's recommendation** (Bimal Jalan = ex-RBI Governor)
- **How RBI earns profit:** Gold purchased at ₹30,000 (2004) now worth ₹1,30,000; foreign bond investments; interest on loans to commercial banks (bank rate, repo rate)
- The Bimal Jalan Committee recommended RBI can keep **5.6% to 5.9%** of total profit with itself, remaining to be transferred to GoI

#### Escheat
- It is the amount charged by the government for the **care-taking work** done on a property due to the **absence of legal heir**
- **Case 1:** Owner dies, no legal heir present → Govt takes control → When heir appears (after 6 months, 10 years etc.), property handed over after document verification → Govt charges escheat for care-taking period
- **Case 2:** Legal heir is a **minor** → Govt takes control until heir attains age of majority → Charges escheat for care-taking work

#### Special Assessment
- Amount charged by the government for **developmental activities done in a particular area**
- **Example:** Delhi expanding — forest area (Dwarka) developed into residential area → Government cut trees (with environmental ministry permission, 3× plantation elsewhere), constructed roads, water/permit system, public parks, street lights, police station, metro → Residents of that area pay special assessment
- Also applies as **toll tax** — specific road/bridge constructed → only those crossing it pay, not all residents

#### Asset Monetisation
- **Definition:** Giving government property **on rent or on lease** *(lease = temporary ownership)* — NOT selling
- **Key Distinction:** Disinvestment = **selling** govt property (Capital Receipt); Asset Monetisation = **renting/leasing** (Non-Tax Revenue Receipt)
- <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**UPSC Prelims Trap:** Under the National Monetisation Pipeline (NMP), core brownfield infrastructure assets (highways, pipelines, railway tracks) are leased out under structured contractual concession frameworks for a fixed period. Sovereign ownership is strictly retained by the government, and assets revert back. Hence, proceeds from NMP/Asset Monetisation are classified as **Non-Tax Revenue Receipts**, whereas Disinvestment (selling equity/assets permanently) is a **Non-Debt Capital Receipt**.</span>
- **Examples:**
  - National highway monetised → land under highway pillars given to hotels, petrol pumps → ₹15,000 crore earned
  - Railway land along tracks → given on rent to private parties (go-downs, commercial use)
  - Govt pipeline (after refinery sold) → given on rent to Ambani/Adani for oil transport
- **Target Achievement:** Last year target ₹88,000 crore → achieved ₹93,000 crore
- Compare: Disinvestment target ₹1.68 lakh crore → achieved only ₹15,000 crore (opposition to selling)

### B.4 Revenue Expenditure

> **Definition — Three Key Characteristics:**
> 1. They are to **maintain day-to-day services** of the government to **run the economy**
> 2. They **do NOT reduce liabilities** of the government
> 3. They **do NOT create assets**

Revenue Expenditure is further divided into:

#### (i) Developmental Revenue Expenditure
- Day-to-day services by the government **in order to run the economy**, like:
  - Health & Education services
  - Transport & Communication services
  - Irrigation & Flood control services
  - Research & Development services
  - Export services
  - A host of services which government provides on a day-to-day basis to **industry, agriculture and various other sectors**

> **Understanding "Developmental":** Teacher's salary in a government school = **Developmental** (directly contributes to skill development/education). Non-teaching staff salary = **Non-developmental** (essential but doesn't directly develop human capital). Similarly, doctor's salary = Developmental; hospital gatekeeper/clerk = Non-developmental.

#### (ii) Non-Developmental Revenue Expenditure
Comprises expenditure of:
- **Defence**
- **Law & Order**
- **Interest Payment** *(Key concept: interest is "free money" to banks — govt never received the interest amount, only the principal)*
- **Pension**
- **Civil Administration**
- **Subsidies**
- **Grants to States**

> **Critical Distinction — Interest vs Principal:**
> - Government borrows ₹1 lakh crore → constructs roads (development done with **principal**)
> - Interest of ₹10,000 crore paid to bank → **no development** from interest (never received that money)
> - Therefore: **Interest payment** = Non-developmental Revenue Expenditure
> - **Principal repayment** = Capital Expenditure
> - This is why loan repayment is always "**re**payment" (paid back in installments — EMI), not just "payment"

> **School Building vs School Staff:**
> - Government school **building construction** = **Capital Expenditure** (physical asset created — visible)
> - Government school **teacher salary** = **Developmental Revenue Expenditure** (runs day-to-day education)
> - Government school **non-teaching staff salary** = **Non-developmental Revenue Expenditure** (essential but no direct development contribution)

---

### B.5 Capital Expenditure

> **Definition (Reverse of Revenue Expenditure):**
> 1. They **may create assets**
> 2. They **may reduce liabilities**

| Item | How it matches |
|---|---|
| **Construction of Roads** | Creates physical asset (e.g., 10,000 km → 70,000 km) |
| **Construction of Airports** | Creates physical asset (78 → 170 airports) |
| **Repayment of Loans** | Reduces liability (principal repaid) |

## Section C: Taxation — Direct vs Indirect

### Classification of Tax — Key Principle

| | Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | **Directly paid** by the person on whom it is levied | **Indirectly paid** — transferred to another party |
| **Transferability** | **Cannot be transferred** | **Can be transferred** (consumer → seller → govt) |
| **Compulsion** | **Compulsory** — whether you consume or not, must pay | **Conditional** — only when you purchase/consume |
| **Example** | Income Tax (you earn ₹10 lakh, you pay ₹1 lakh tax — you cannot ask someone else to pay it) | GST (you pay fees + GST to Vajiram → Vajiram transfers GST to government) |

> **"Is it Double Taxation?"** — NO! Direct tax + Indirect tax ≠ Double Taxation. Double taxation occurs when the **same type of tax** is levied twice (e.g., income tax charged twice on the same income). Paying income tax on earnings AND GST on purchases is NOT double taxation — one is compulsory, other is conditional/by choice.

### C.2 Indirect Taxes — VAT vs GST

#### GST (Goods & Services Tax)
- Introduced: **1st July 2017**
- Slogan: **"One Nation, One Tax"**
- Covers: **All goods and services EXCEPT 7 items**
- GST Rates: **5%, 12%, 18%, 28%** (4 slabs)
- Constitutional Amendment: **101st Amendment Act**

##### GST Structure
- **CGST** (Central GST) — goes to Central Government
- **SGST** (State GST) — goes to State Government
- Always split **equally** (e.g., 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST)

##### Why "One Nation One Tax"?
> **Under VAT regime:**
> - Item produced in State A at ₹100 + 18% VAT = ₹118
> - Crosses state boundary → Entry tax ₹5 = ₹123
> - Enters municipal area → Octroi ₹2 = ₹125
> - **Tax in State A = ₹18; Tax in State B = ₹25** → Different prices in different states!
>
> **Under GST regime:**
> - Item at ₹100 + 18% GST = ₹118
> - Goes to State B → **still ₹118** (no additional entry tax, octroi etc.)
> - **One Nation, One Tax** (Standardized nationwide)

#### 7 Items Still Under VAT (NOT under GST)
| # | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Crude Oil** |
| 2 | **ATF** (Aviation Turbine Fuel) |
| 3 | **Petrol** |
| 4 | **Diesel** |
| 5 | **Natural Gas** |
| 6 | **Alcohol** (for human consumption) |
| 7 | **Electricity** |

> **5 Petroleum Products + Alcohol + Electricity = 7 items under VAT**

##### Why Petrol/Diesel Prices Differ Across States
- **Central Excise (CENVAT)** → Same across all states
- **State-Level VAT (SLVAT)** → **Different** in each state
  - Maharashtra: SLVAT = 21% → Petrol at ₹90+
  - Delhi: SLVAT = 21% → Different price
  - Uttarakhand: SLVAT = 14% → Cheaper petrol
- **The entire price difference is because of State VAT, NOT Central tax**
- If petroleum products come under GST (proposed at 30% rate in July 2022): CGST = 15%, SGST = 15% → **same price everywhere**

> **Political Awareness:** "When ANY political party (ruling or opposition) says petrol/diesel price is too high — they are ALSO responsible. SLVAT is in the hands of the State Government. If they want relaxation, they can reduce SLVAT. Both Centre and State levy tax on petroleum."

### FRBM Act — Fiscal Responsibility & Budget Management Act

> **Full Form:** Fiscal Responsibility **and** Budget Management Act
> **Core Idea:** It is the **duty of the government** ("fiscal" = government's money) to **responsibly manage** the budget (receipts and expenditures)

| Version | Year | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Old FRBM Act** | **2003** | Already asked in Mains |
| **New FRBM Act** | **2017** | Current applicable version |

#### Sri Lanka Case Study — Why FRBM Matters
- **Gotabaya Rajapaksa** became head → **reduced taxes** (revenue fell) + **increased expenditures** (free gas, unemployment allowance, old-age pension)
- **Huge gap** created between revenue and expenditure
- Borrowed from **IMF** → could NOT repay → **defaulted**
- After 1.5–2 years: **economy devastated** — "We do not have money to purchase petroleum products from tomorrow"
- Same people who praised him **forcefully entered his house and looted everything**
- India & China provided emergency loans to help Sri Lanka purchase petroleum

> **Lesson:** Government MUST balance expenditure with revenue. Irresponsible fiscal management → Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Argentina, Afghanistan, Bangladesh — all struggling examples.

#### India's Fiscal Responsibility Example
- **May 2022:** India reduced petroleum tax by 4% → expected revenue loss ₹1 lakh crore
- **July 2022 (one month later):** Introduced **Windfall Tax** on crude oil → expected recovery ₹96,000 crore
- Similarly: **PM-KISAN** (₹6,000/farmer) increased expenditure → introduced **Krishi Kalyan Cess** (tax on tax)
- **Ayushman Bharat** (₹5 lakh free treatment) increased expenditure → introduced **Health & Education Cess**
- India always **manages the gap** — unlike Sri Lanka

## Section E: Finance Commission

### Function
- Whatever **central government collects** as tax from the states goes into a **Divisible Pool**
- Finance Commission recommends **how to divide** this pool between Centre and States
- Rationale: Centre collects from the entire country (more tax revenue) but States have limited area → States need more than their direct collection
- Like a father having more money and giving additional to the child who has less

### Finance Commissions Studied

| FC | Period | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **14th FC** | — | Reference taken for comparison |
| **15th FC** | — | Current reference |
| **16th FC** | **1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031** (5 years) | Just started; will be studied in detail |

- **15th FC Devolution:** Centre transfers **41%** of divisible pool to states
- CGST collected ≠ all stays with Centre → part goes to States through devolution → On every item, States effectively get more than Centre

> **Note:** On a ₹100 item with 18% GST: CGST = ₹9 (but part shared with states), SGST = ₹9 (fully with state). Net effect: States get MORE, Centre gets LESS per item.

**Three Real-World Examples from Lecture:**

   - **Additional income:** Grows vegetables, sells at city market every 7 days = ₹2,600→3,300/month extra
   - Urban schools sending buses to villages because of good roads → children getting quality education

3. **Jacket Factory Example:**
   - 3 Mumbai workers (earning ₹18,000 each, saving ₹1,000-2,000 in expensive city) returned to village
   - Started jacket manufacturing factory using **quality electricity** infrastructure
   - Borrowed ₹50,000 from SBI → repaid → borrowed ₹5 lakh → repaid → now bank offers ₹15-20 lakh
   - Now earning ₹27,000 each in village (more than Mumbai!) + employing 2 workers at ₹10,000 each
   - From **job seekers** to **job creators** — all because of electricity infrastructure

## Section G: GDP, Tax-to-GDP Ratio & Government Income

### GDP ≠ Government Income
- **GDP** = Income of the **people** (farmers, companies, service providers)
- **Government's income** = **Tax** (direct + indirect)
- If GDP = ₹5 trillion and tax = 0, government has NO resources despite high GDP

### Tax-to-GDP Ratio — The Real Indicator

| Country | Tax-to-GDP Ratio | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **India** | ~11% | ₹100 crore GDP → ₹11 crore tax to govt |
| **Sweden** | ~42% | ₹100 crore GDP → ₹42 crore tax → better roads, salary, railways, metro, police, law & order |

- **Developing vs Developed:** Low tax-to-GDP = developing; High = developed
- Migration flows from India → Sweden (for better infrastructure, salary, quality of life) — NOT reverse
- **Agriculture is tax-free** in India → if GDP is mostly agricultural, government gets less revenue → less development capacity
- India's GDP has good mix of agriculture + secondary + tertiary → secondary & tertiary give good tax revenue

> **Comparative Context:** GDP of Maharashtra > GDP of Pakistan. Market cap of Tata > GDP of Pakistan.

## Section I: Sustainable Development

> GDP talks about **quantity only**. Quantity + Quality = **Inclusive Growth**. Inclusive Growth sustained over **long period** = **Sustainable Development**.

### Key Concepts (1 class)

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Zero Growth** | No economic growth — economy stagnates |
| **Uneconomic Growth** | Growth that causes more environmental/social damage than benefit |
| **Rebound Effect** | Efficiency gains leading to increased consumption (negating the benefit) |

### Additional Terms
- **Carbon Tax** — Tax on carbon emissions
- **Green Accounting** — Including environmental costs in national accounting
- **Sovereign Green Bond** — Government bonds specifically for environmental projects
- **GCF** (Green Climate Fund) — International fund for climate action
- **Carbon Credit Point** — Tradeable certificates for emission reduction

### Perspectives of Sustainable Development
- **Intergenerational** — Current generation's actions affecting future generations (e.g., Punjab groundwater depleted → future farming impossible)
- **Intragenerational** — Within the same generation (inequality in access to resources)

> **2020 Mains Question:** "Intergenerational & Intragenerational perspective of Inclusive Development" — Discuss

### Real-World Examples
- **Punjab:** Groundwater not available after 400-500 feet in several places — over-extraction for cultivation left nothing for future
- **Gangzhou, China:** "Ghost City" — state-of-the-art smart city, massive investment, but NO water available → people won't move in
- **Delhi:** Schools/offices closed for days due to pollution → if this continues, it will be permanent
- **Concrete transition:** Concrete surfaces prevent water from seeping into ground → worsens water table

> **Nature's Role:** Rich can buy water purifiers, air purifiers. But unskilled labourers work in open — they NEED pure air and water without any payment. Without preserving nature, no sustainable development.

## Section J: Employment & Unemployment

### Labour Force — Definition
- **Labour Force** = Those who are **willing AND capable** (employed + unemployed both included)
- NOT total population — excludes:
  - Children under 15 (willing but not capable)
  - Retired persons (willing but not capable)
  - Physically incapable persons

### Unemployment Rate Formula

> **Unemployment Rate** = (Number of Unemployed Persons ÷ **Labour Force**) × 100
>
> **Note:** Denominator is **Labour Force**, NOT Population

### Measurement — PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey)
- Conducted by: **NSO** (National Statistical Office) — formed in **2019**
- NSO was formed by merging **NSSO** (National Sample Survey Office) + **CSO** (Central Statistical Office)
- **Three Methods:**
  - **UPS** — Usual Principal Status
  - **CWS** — Current Weekly Status
  - **CDS** — Current Daily Status

### Types of Unemployment

| Type | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Voluntary** | Person chooses not to work |
| **Involuntary** | Person wants to work but cannot find employment |
| **Disguised** | More workers employed than needed (removing some won't affect output) — *2016 Prelims: definition asked* |
| **Frictional** | Temporary unemployment while switching jobs |
| **Structural** | Mismatch between skills and available jobs |
| **Seasonal** | Employment available only in certain seasons (e.g., agriculture) |
| **Cyclical** | Due to economic downturns/business cycles |
| **NRU** | Natural Rate of Unemployment — always exists in any economy |

### Jobless Growth
- GDP can increase without creating jobs — e.g., if only **prices increase**, GDP size grows from ₹1 trillion → ₹1.2 trillion, but no new employment generated
- Need to examine whether GDP growth is **job-full** or **jobless**

### New Labour Issues (PYQ Topics)
| Concept | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Gig Workers** | Short-term, contract-based work (e.g., Uber drivers, Zomato delivery) |
| **Platform Workers** | Workers dependent on digital platforms for employment |
| **Moonlighting** | Working for a second employer secretly (without primary employer's knowledge) — Tata removed 400+ workers for this |

#### Moonlighting — Detailed
- **"Moonlight Cheating":** Daylight working with Tata, moonlight (night) working with Mahindra
- **Why not allowed:** Fear of leaking confidential information to rival companies (e.g., Tata Motors secrets leaked to Mahindra Motors)
- **Freelancing ≠ Moonlighting** — Freelancing is independent work by choice, not hidden from primary employer
- **New Labour Laws (4 codes)** replaced the old 30+ labour laws

## Section K: Budget Summary — Revenue vs Capital (Quick Reference)

| | Revenue | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| **Receipts** | Do NOT create liability; NOT from selling govt property | MAY create liability; MAY be from selling govt property; MAY have long-term character |
| **Expenditure** | Do NOT reduce liability; Do NOT create assets | MAY reduce liability; MAY create assets |
| **Examples (Receipts)** | Tax (Direct + Indirect), Fees, Fines, License, Dividend, Escheat, Special Assessment, Asset Monetisation | Recovery of Loans, Disinvestment, Borrowings |
| **Examples (Expenditure)** | Developmental (health, education, transport services salary) + Non-developmental (defence, interest, pension, subsidies) | Construction of roads/airports, Loan repayment |
