## 1. Who, and for how long (ECO-15-01)

The **16th Finance Commission** was constituted under **Arvind Panagariya**. It was **tabled in Parliament along with the 2026–27 Budget**. The award runs **five years: 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031**.

He dictated a shift, and he put two pairs in brackets: from **entitlement-based** (he also said **suggestion-based**) to **compliance-driven** (he also said **need-based**). Lock both pairs. Do not collapse them into one cleaner phrase.

**Vertical devolution is unchanged from the 15th.** Of the share that leaves the Centre, **41%** goes to the States and **1%** goes entirely to the Union Territories of **Jammu & Kashmir** and **Ladakh**. Under the **14th** the whole **42%** went to the States, because Jammu and Kashmir was still a State. No other Union Territory gets this 1%. That split was already Lecture 10. The new sentence is that the **16th keeps it**.

The **divisible pool** is the direct and indirect taxes that are shared. It **excludes cess, surcharge, and the cost of collection**, all three taken out of gross central tax revenue. His illustration: tax **₹100 crore**, cess and surcharge **₹20 crore**, collection **₹120 crore** — only the **₹100 crore** enters the pool, and the States’ 41% is 41% of that. A heavier cess and surcharge, which the Centre does not share, shrinks the pool and therefore the States’ untied money. The **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** split on an 18% item (half Centre, half State) stays Lecture 8. Do not rebuild it.

## 2. The grants (ECO-15-02)

**Grants: ₹9.47 lakh crore over the five years.** Lecture 10 had previewed that figure against the 15th’s **₹4.36 lakh crore**. He called it more than double. These grants **comprise** grants for **urban and rural local bodies** and **disaster management**.

**Local bodies: ₹8 lakh crore.** Of that, **₹4.47 lakh crore** to **rural** local bodies and **₹3.6 lakh crore** to **urban** local bodies. Rural is the larger share because, he said, rural areas have less of their own money.

| Slice of the local-body grant | Share |
|:---|:---|
| **Basic** | **80%** |
| **Performance-based** | **20%** |
| Of the **basic** 80%: **untied** | **50%** |
| Of the **basic** 80%: **tied** | **50%** |

The tied half of the basic grant is tied to **sanitation, waste management, and water management**. His arithmetic on a **₹100 crore** grant: **₹80 crore** basic and **₹20 crore** performance; of the ₹80 crore, **₹40 crore** untied and **₹40 crore** tied. Untied means the body may spend it anywhere. Tied means it stays on the named programme. The **41%** tax share is untied. A road scheme such as the Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana is tied. That contrast was already Lecture 10.

He compared the new tied share with the 15th: earlier, he said, there was no such tied proposition, and **health grants** were **hardly 5 to 10%**. That 5-to-10 is his comparison, not a section he read out. The new complaint is that a State which has already done well on water, sanitation, and waste must still spend the tied half there, and cannot move it.

**Disaster management fund: ₹2.04 lakh crore**, for the **State Disaster Relief Fund** and the **State Disaster Management Fund**. He said “SDIF” for both. Do not invent a third short form. **Cost sharing:** **90:10** (Centre:State) for the **North-Eastern and Himalayan** States; **75:25** for the other States. On a **₹100 crore** disaster bill, the hill and North-East case is **₹90 crore** from the Centre and **₹10 crore** from the State; the plains are **₹75 crore** and **₹25 crore**.

**These figures do not add up on the page, and he did not reconcile them.** ₹4.47 lakh crore plus ₹3.6 lakh crore is **₹8.07 lakh crore**, which he called **₹8 lakh crore**. ₹8 lakh crore plus ₹2.04 lakh crore is **₹10.04 lakh crore**, not the **₹9.47 lakh crore** total. Lock each number as spoken. Do not invent a missing grant to force the sum.

## 3. What the 15th’s grants lose, and the deficit path (ECO-15-03)

**Discontinued**, as recommendations of the **15th**: **revenue-deficit grants**, **sector-specific grants**, and **state-specific grants**.

Lecture 10 had said the 16th also has a revenue-deficit grant, and parked the detail. **This class scraps it.** The Centre’s allegation, as he gave it: the deficit is not developmental spending. It is **cash** distributed to attract voters, and tax money will not finance that. Hill and North-Eastern States, which have less of their own revenue, had been receiving the grant so that a revenue deficit could be brought towards zero. He also put their complaint on the concerns page: some of that spending was necessary, and scrapping the grant forces them to cut it.

**Off-budget borrowing stops.** A public-sector undertaking that is given less in the Budget than it asked for, and then borrows the rest from banks or from outside, is borrowing off the Budget. The Indian Oil numbers stay **ECO-06**. The new rule: such borrowings **end**, and **all such liabilities are included in the fiscal deficit and in debt**. The undertaking borrows through the government, which he said knows how the repayment will be made. The reason he gave for the ban: delayed outside repayment gives the country a bad name.

**Fiscal deficit.** The Centre should come down to **3.5% of gross domestic product (GDP) by 2030–31**. States should be at **3% of Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP)**. The 14th had allowed States to borrow past the cap. The 15th stopped that, and the **16th continues the stop**. Same reason as the cash point: extra borrowing was going into non-developmental cash, which he put at about **44%** in one sentence and at **47.4%** on the dictated line. Use **47.4%** for the dictated statistic. The path the **15th** set (Centre **4% by 2025–26**; States **4 / 3.5 / 3**) stays Lecture 10. Do not merge the two end-years.

**Combined Centre-plus-State debt** is projected to fall from **77.3% of GDP in 2026–27** to **73.1% in 2030–31**. He said **77.3** more than once. The sheet can be read as **77.7**. Lock the spoken **77.3**.

## 4. Discoms, cash, and a public divisible pool (ECO-15-04)

**Distribution companies (discoms)** are in heavy debt. States are encouraged to **privatise** them. **Legacy debt** — he glossed it as all the debt — is to be parked in a **special purpose vehicle (SPV)**, with repayment allowed through the **special assistance scheme for capital investment**.

An SPV, as he defined it, is a **temporary company**, under the **Companies Act, 2013**, formed to finish one programme, often in a **public-private partnership**, and then wound up. His picture: a Delhi–Mumbai expressway announced for five years gets an SPV; when the road is done, the company goes. The past scheme he named for discom debt is the **Ujwal DISCOM Assurance Yojana (UDAY)**, which he placed in **2016–17**, clearing loans by issuing bonds. The proposal now is a scheme of that kind, “even more than that.” The sheet writes **UDAY** in the margin of this page.

**Subsidies, especially unconditional cash transfers**, should be rationalised. He wants a **clear exclusion** — his Delhi picture was **200 units** of free electricity for everyone, including a household whose daily spend is ₹10,000 or ₹20,000 — plus **uniform accounting** and **disclosure** of subsidy transfers.

Two cash statistics. Do not merge them.

- **Across 21 States**, these schemes now account for **20.2%** of total subsidy spending in **2025–26**, **up from just 3% in 2018–19**.
- **By 2025–26, larger cash-transfer schemes alone account for 47.4%**, because of the **JAM trinity** — **Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, and Mobile** — and this **overtakes traditional social-sector spending**. Lecture 10 had the same **3%** and **47.4%** pair, dated there as **2019** and “recently.” This class dates them **2018–19** and **2025–26**, and adds the 21-State **20.2%** line. Keep both datings.

**308 inactive State public-sector enterprises** should be closed. Where one is **strategically important**, continue it, and the route is **privatisation or continuation on that importance**. **Air India** was the loss he used again: about **₹20 crore a day**, **₹600 crore a month**, **₹7,200 crore a year**. The yearly figure is already on Lecture 10. After the sale to Tata, that yearly loss stopped, and the money can go to metros, hospitals, and schools. He added an *Economic Times* picture he did not document: two or three airports in five years, so fifteen could be added. Do not treat the fifteen as a scheme.

**Article 279.** States keep saying the 41% has not arrived. The Commission recommends that the Union **annually disclose Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)-certified data on net tax proceeds**, so the **size of the divisible pool** and the **actual devolution** to each State are public. His illustration: a State due ₹10,000 crore that has already received ₹8,000 crore is due ₹2,000 crore — a claim of ₹5,000 crore then fails in public. He placed this transparency under **Article 279**. **Article 280**, the Finance Commission article, stays the polity note. He also asked why the Economic Survey already publishes expenditure and receipts, and this share does not.

## 5. Concerns, and the three suggestions (ECO-15-05)

He would not write “demerits.” The concerns:

1. **Vertical devolution is stuck at 41%**, though States have demanded **50%** since the 15th. They also note the step down from the 14th’s **42%**.
2. **Cess and surcharge** sit **outside** the pool (tax ₹100 crore, cess and surcharge ₹50 crore, only ₹100 crore shared). That **shrinks untied fiscal space** and **worsens the vertical imbalance**. He said there is still **no cap**, and no guideline that cess and surcharge stay within a percentage of revenue. The count of cesses still running stays Lecture 6. Do not start a second count from his “seven or eight.”
3. A **lower weight on income distance weakens equity**. A **higher weight on population**, and the **new 10% on contribution to GDP**, favour **populous** States and **industrialised** States. **Southern States** will say the formula now punishes successful population control. **Poorer States** fear the tighter transfers will hold development back. The weights themselves are the Lecture 10 table. **Tax and fiscal effort (2.5% under the 15th) is out**, so a small State that collects well is no longer credited on that line, while a large GDP is. His line: the rich become richer and the poor poorer, which is the opposite of a commission whose job is balance. A small State’s GDP cannot grow the way **Maharashtra’s** can.
4. **Scrapping revenue-deficit grants is controversial.** Hill and special-category States, including the North-East, say **structural and geographical constraints** make a revenue deficit **unavoidable**. Removing the support **widens regional imbalance**.
5. A **3% of GSDP** cap, and a **complete end** to off-budget borrowing, look **fiscally prudent** and are **potentially contractionary**. Not every State was spending the extra on cash. Essential developmental spending gets cut with the rest. His family picture: income ₹20,000, spend only ₹20,000, and the child’s education stops.
6. A **larger tied share limits flexibility** for local priorities. See the 50% tie in section 2.

**Suggestions to strengthen fiscal federalism** — Centre and States working together on the shares:

1. **Link a portion of the devolution to a State’s revenue buoyancy.** A State that improves its tax system is rewarded **even if its absolute GDP is still low**. That is his answer to the GDP-contribution weight.
2. The Union could give a **matching grant** to a State that **successfully implements its own State Finance Commission**. That turns a penalty-based system into a **reward-based** one. Performance, in this suggestion, is checked by how the State spends the State Finance Commission’s own grants. Tied grants, he added, can be State-specific through that commission. The Centre’s schemes stay uniform, which is why untied money still has to exist for local need.
3. **Legislation to cap cess and surcharge at a specific percentage of gross tax revenue**, so they stay **temporary**, as he said the Constitution originally intended. He did not name the percentage.

**Tax expenditure, tax preference, and revenue foregone** are three names for one thing: revenue the government does not collect because it prefers not to tax. His two examples. Agriculture is outside the direct-tax net; if the tax would have been **₹5 lakh crore**, that is revenue foregone, and it is only a possible loss until the sector is actually taxed. Second, income **up to ₹12 lakh**: he put the tax at **₹60,000**, and said that from **1 April 2026** that tax is not paid. The rebate mechanism, and why “up to ₹12 lakh” is not a slab that vanishes the moment income crosses it, stays Lecture 6. The **₹60,000** is his illustration of the hole, not a new rate table.

**Retrospective tax** is tax on a transaction dated before the levy. The dividend case of **October 2023** (France, the Netherlands, Switzerland, about **₹11,000 crore**) stays Lecture 5. The case he added here is **Vodafone**. **Hutchison** (he also said a Singapore and Hong Kong root) sold the Indian business to **Vodafone** of England. The deal was in the **Cayman Islands**, which he called a tax-haven with **zero** direct tax. The tax was not in force at the **2008** deal; it was levied in **2012**. The **Bombay High Court** went with the government (undervalued shares, tax avoided). The **Supreme Court** went with Vodafone: two foreign companies, a foreign deal, outside jurisdiction, and Vodafone would pay Indian tax once it operated in India. **Manmohan Singh** and Finance Minister **Pranab Mukherjee** then amended the law so deals of the **last 50 years** were covered. A demand he put at **₹7,000 crore** became **up to ₹12,000 crore**. India **lost** in international arbitration, the tribunal using the Supreme Court’s own reasoning. **From 2017** the government decided to scrap retrospective tax.

## 6. Inclusive growth — the features (ECO-15-06)

He set **growth** against **inclusive growth**, which he also called **development**.

**Growth** is **quantitative**. It is an increase in **income**, which is a **flow** — what is added over a **period** — so that **stock** can accumulate. Stock is what is held at a **point** of time: wealth on 31 December, a pile of bags at 8 o’clock, knowledge already held. You cannot see how the stock was built unless you watch the period. Income added each month is the flow. A stock can be ancestral, or borrowed, and need not be that person’s income. Flow, if it is positive, raises the stock. **Dhirubhai Ambani** to **Mukesh Ambani** was his illustration of a stock that later grew and could also be drawn down. He did not lock a clean rupee or dollar figure for that transfer. Do not invent one.

Growth, in this class, also means more **commercialisation, industrialisation, and urbanisation**, which raise productivity and the standard of living, **including the lowest income group**. A village that makes curd but sells it only for the day’s vegetables, while **Amul**, **Mother Dairy**, and **Britannia** know how to sell it, was the commercialisation picture. Agro-based factories — tomato, potato, sugarcane, fruit — give the grower a buyer and cut the transport. He did not give a scheme name for that.

**Inclusive growth** is **qualitative**. It is not only the amount of GDP. It includes **all sections**, **minimises deprivation**, and gives **equal opportunity** to every **geographical and economic** area.

Four features, and the instruments he hung on them.

| Feature | What he used |
|:---|:---|
| **Equitable distribution of resources** | Subsidies, paid for by the extra tax on higher incomes (the slabs stay Lecture 6). **Ayushman Bharat** for health, **Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan** for education, an **agricultural input subsidy** so a poor farmer can produce as a rich one can, **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana** for a respectable house. |
| **Poverty alleviation** | Employment programmes: **PM Vishwakarma** (a village carpenter or artisan, money to start, and to employ others), **PM SVANidhi** — **Pradhan Mantri Street Vendor’s AtmaNirbhar Nidhi**, which he corrected away from “ATM” — and **VB-G RAM G**. |
| **Sustainable development** | Growth **with** environmental protection, so the next generation still has resources **in adequate quantity**. A producer cannot last if nature’s free clean water and air are gone. The unskilled worker cannot buy a purifier. Punjab, he said, now finds water only at **400 to 500 feet**, after free water and free electricity drove extraction. Delhi cannot drink the Yamuna as it arrives; the water that can be used comes from **Haryana**, and he said there is no aquatic life in the city stretch. |
| **Removing capability deprivation** | **Amartya Sen’s capability approach**: the person is capable, and is deprived for want of training and support. **Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY)** and **Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY)**. A village mason, carpenter, or school dropout gets a certificate course — he said **six, nine, or twelve months** — and can then be considered for a government or a private job. |

**VB-G RAM G** is **Viksit Bharat — Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin)**. The older Act: he said it was presented in Parliament in **2005** under the name **NREGA**, **started in 2006**, and was renamed **MGNREGA on 2 October 2009**. The idea, he said, came from a foreign economist whose name on the audio is **Jean Drèze** (it arrived as “Gene Dredge”). Working days have **increased**. He did not give the new number.

On **PM Vishwakarma** and **PM SVANidhi** he stated one loan picture while moving from the first scheme to the second: a loan at **7%**, a **2%** interest subvention if it is paid on time, so only **5%** is paid. Do not lock the 7 / 2 / 5 onto one of the two schemes. He did not separate them.

The jacket factory (₹18,000 in Mumbai, ₹27,000 each in the village, ₹50,000 then ₹5 lakh then ₹15–20 lakh, two workers at ₹10,000, job-seekers becoming job-creators) is already in Section F. He retold it and added: the village had **electricity for 20 to 22 hours**, enough to carry the load of the automatic machines, and that is why they came back. Roads carry the goods. The bank is the **financial inclusion** piece, which he then parked. Irrigation: a diesel pump-set at **one hour, one litre, ₹100**, against an electric motor at **75 paise** an hour. **18,000 villages** were still without electricity **till 2013**. He also said more than **6,30,000** villages had been without lights. That second count was a loose sentence. Do not turn it into an official village total.

## 7. The factors he did finish (ECO-15-07)

**Physical development** has the three heads already drawn in Section F: infrastructure, civic amenities, financial inclusion. **Human development** is health and education. **Economic infrastructure:** electricity, transport, communication. **Social infrastructure:** education, health, housing. The mains line he repeated — investment in infrastructure ensures inclusive growth — is the same question Section F already flags. Economic infrastructure feeds physical development. Social infrastructure feeds human development.

What this sitting added under the heads:

**Transport** (roads and railways) gives a supply chain and ends the **segregation of markets**. A road lets the urban buyer in, so the village price is not stuck with the local buyer. He also put roadside work on the same road: dhabas, restaurants, petrol pumps. **Expressways** were the emphasis, against a national highway that still carries local rickshaws, tongas, bicycles, and animals. An expressway is away from the city, elevated, with a **toll at entry and at exit**, and he put the speed at **120 km per hour**. A peripheral road lets the truck cross the city without the night ban he described (arrive at 3, enter only at 9). The **Ministry of Food Processing**: Indian trucks manage **250 to 300 km a day**; in America, because of expressways, **600 to 800 km**. Fruits and vegetables arrive with **hardly 40%** of their quality left, the rest lost to delay.

**Electricity** raises production by **mechanisation**, by **longer hours of work**, and by **modern machines** used in training. Late-night work and study, e-rickshaws, and a **UPI** transfer (which needs electricity and the internet together) were the pictures. The rural gain is the cheaper irrigation above.

**Communication** cuts the **search cost** — the real expense of finding the better opportunity — and moves information, including technical information, into distant regions. Long-distance education and a quick money transfer sit on the same line. A network that fails sometimes, including in Delhi, does not cancel the point: the failure is not the usual case.

**Civic amenities** — **treated water**, a **better drainage system**, **public transport** — cut the cost of living and raise **disposable income**, which he defined as income left in hand with no obligation on it, available to raise the standard of living. Untreated water and a failed drain were water-borne and mosquito-borne disease. He stopped on the heading **financial inclusion**.
