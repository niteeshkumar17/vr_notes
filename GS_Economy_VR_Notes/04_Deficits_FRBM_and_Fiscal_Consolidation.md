## 1. Budget Deficit vs Fiscal Deficit (ECO-06-01)

**Revenue Expenditure (RE) + Capital Expenditure (CE)** = total expenditure.  
**Revenue Receipts (RR) + Capital Receipts (CR)** = total receipts.

| | Formula (class) | What it captures |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Budget deficit** | Total exp − Total receipt = **(RE + CE) − (RR + CR)** | Gap after **all** receipts, **including borrowing**. Pre-1997 India used this and then *made the gap look zero* after the year ended. |
| **Fiscal deficit** | Total exp − Total receipt **except borrowing** = **(RE + CE) − (RR + CR except borrowing)** | How much the government **must borrow**. Announced **in advance** so others can help close the gap (tax, disinvestment, better spending). |

**Capital receipts** in class: (a) **recovery of loans**, (b) **disinvestment**, (c) **borrowing**. For fiscal deficit, **(c) is dropped**. Recovery + disinvestment = **CR except borrowing** = **non-debt CR**.

Class logic: telling the country the gap **after** the year is over is useless — economists cannot suggest better ways to close it. Fiscal deficit is the **roadmap**.

**Ways and Means Advances (WMA):** sheet “Ways & Means bill” — **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** can lend to the **Government of India (GoI)** for **90 days**.

## 2. Before 1997: Monetised Deficit and Deficit Financing (ECO-06-02)

Before **1997**, GoI tried to make the **budget deficit look zero** by:

1. **Printing currency** = **monetised deficit**. Class: **most inflationary** way to finance government spending. Extra cash in pockets (freebies, cash transfers) raises **demand** with **no extra supply** → inflation. Class illustrations of how bad printing can get: **England ~11.7%** (highest in 40 years, about three years back); **Pakistan 27%**; **Sri Lanka 68%**; **Argentina 292%** (and a Venezuela figure on the sheet trail — do not mix them). Inflation **kills purchasing power** even if the nominal salary is unchanged.
2. **Borrowing from RBI** — unsold government bonds parked with RBI as security; RBI was administratively bound to fund the gap (plus the **90-day** WMA window).

**Deficit financing** (sheet) = those two **plus** borrowing from financial institutions (**World Bank**, **Asian Development Bank (ADB)**, **New Development Bank (NDB)**, etc.) **plus** issuing **government bonds**.

**From 1997** (Sukhamoy Chakravarty Committee): GoI **almost stopped** printing and RBI-window financing. It now **relies on market-based financial institutions** (banks, markets) to fund the deficit. World Bank / ADB-type loans are for **capital / developmental** use, not for cash freebies — so **printing-based promises run hotter** than **borrowing-based** ones.

## 3. Market Borrowing: Crowding Out, Prices, Inflation Band (ECO-06-03)

Higher **fiscal deficit** = higher **borrowings**. Borrowing is **not bad** if it funds **productive** use and **asset creation**. Class: **in India it is mostly non-developmental revenue expenditure.**

**Implications of financing the deficit through market-based institutions**

1. **Crowding out.** Government borrows large against **government securities**. Banks are left with **less** to lend the **private** sector. Opposite picture in class: if banks are flush, they cut the rate and **crowd in** small investors.
2. The **borrowing rate of interest** rises (interest = **price of money**). Higher cost can be **passed to consumers** as higher prices.
3. Because the borrowing is largely for **non-developmental RE**, **income and demand** are transferred **without corresponding output** → **inflation risk**.

**India’s inflation target (margin):** **4% ± 2** → band **2–6%**. Class: should not go below **2** or above **6**.

## 4. Revenue Deficit, Credit Rating, Primary Deficit (ECO-06-04)

**Revenue Deficit (RD) = RE − RR.**

High borrowing → high **interest burden**. Interest is paid from **revenue receipts**, so **RE − RR widens**. Financing **non-developmental RE** by borrowing **hurts the sovereign credit rating**; the **next** foreign loan comes **dearer**.

Agencies class named: **Standard & Poor’s, Moody’s, Fitch** — together **>96%** of world rating business; S&P + Moody’s **>80%**. Better rating = country’s **trust**; class recalled an upgrade **BBB− → BBB** with a **positive** outlook (do not invent the year).

**Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payment** (sheet wrote “interest rate payment” = **interest payment**).

It shows the **direction** of the fiscal deficit. **Low primary deficit is not a healthy sign** if it means **almost the entire fiscal deficit is interest on old loans** — little left for roads, rails, airports, welfare. Class contrast: a large fiscal deficit with **small** interest share can still be going into **capital / developmental** spending.

## 5. Effective Revenue Deficit — Pranab Mukherjee, 2012 (ECO-06-05)

**Effective Revenue Deficit** = **RD − grants to states for production / asset creation** (developmental RE). Introduced by **Pranab Mukherjee** as Finance Minister in **2012**.

It tries to show the **net / real adverse impact** of the revenue deficit: take total RD, **subtract** the grants that are **meant** for productive activity (even if they sit in the revenue account). Class: still **not** the headline number the Budget treats as the main RD — **knowledge surface** only. One concept was **introduced**; another was **recommended but not accepted** (PSBR sits in that second bucket).

## 6. PSBR and Off-Budget Borrowing (ECO-06-06)

**Public Sector Borrowing Requirement (PSBR)** = **gross fiscal deficit** of **Centre + States** (and the borrowing that finances it) **+ independent borrowings by Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)** = **off-budget borrowings**.

Class: when we say “India’s fiscal deficit is x%” we usually mean the **Centre only**. Combine Centre + States and the number is **much larger** (class said **more than 10%** in that illustration — do not treat as a current official print). **Indian Oil** example: asked the Centre for **₹70,000 crore**; Budget support **₹10,000 crore**; remaining **₹60,000 crore** borrowed outside = off-budget.

Based on **C. Rangarajan** and **Bimal Jalan** committee recommendations (sheet: “Vimal”; same Jalan as the RBI surplus committee in L01). **Not accepted** as the headline deficit. **16th Finance Commission** (from **1 April 2026**): class — **off-budget borrowings to stop**.

## 7. Old FRBM Act 2003 (Vijay Kelkar) (ECO-06-07)

**Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act** — passed **2003**, path from **2004–05 to 2008–09** (five years). **Vijay Kelkar Committee.**

**Fiscal consolidation** = reduce the deficit by **rationalising expenditure** on one side and **raising revenue** on the other. Aim: government **responsible, accountable, transparent** in fiscal exercise.

| Target (old FRBM) | Path | By 2008–09 |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Revenue deficit** | Down **0.5%** of GDP **each year** | **Zero** (does **not** mean expenditure zero — revenue must rise to match) |
| **Fiscal deficit** | Down **0.3%** of GDP each year | **Not more than 3% of GDP** |

**Not achieved.** Targets were reset / extended (class: 2012, then 2014); **effective revenue deficit** and a **macroeconomic framework** entered the story; **N.K. Singh Committee** formed **May 2016**, report **early 2017**, to ask why the path failed.

---

## 8. N.K. Singh Committee — New FRBM Path (ECO-06-08)

Sheet: **old FRBM 2004** vs **new FRBM 2017 / 2022–23 / 2023–24**.

1. **Revenue deficit:** bring down from **2.1% of GDP (2017)** by **0.25% each year** so that by **2022–23** RD is **not more than 0.8% of GDP**.
2. **Fiscal deficit:** reduce to **3% by 2017–18**, **stay at 3% for the next three years**, then cut **0.2% each year** so that by **2023–24** FD is **not more than 2.5% of GDP**.
3. **Escape route:** **±0.5%** around the **annual fiscal-deficit target**. Economy **does well** → policymakers may **cut** the target by **0.5%** (right time to trim subsidies / raise tax). Economy **does not** → may **raise** it by **0.5%**. A **range**, not a single point — so the government need not freeze salaries and halt roads in Q4 just to hit a point target (class: **Himachal Pradesh** salary-stop illustration).
4. **Fiscal Council** — autonomous body for coordination of **monetary and fiscal policy**, **Centre and States**, and **direct + indirect tax** fixation (GST has a split logic; **VAT** on petrol etc. does not).

**Debt:** bring **total outstanding debt** from **70% of GDP** (**49% Centre + 21% States**) to **60%** (**40% Centre + 20% States**). Sheet margin: **2019 → 67%**. Class: 2019 was a **better** fiscal year; pandemic blew the path (do not treat later % as a drill unless re-taught).

**Rename:** FRBM → **Debt Management and FRBM Act and Rules**.

**Way forward (class)**

- National consensus on **non-developmental RE**. **Economic Times 2022:** state subsidies should **not exceed 1% of Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP)**.
- More **capital expenditure / infrastructure**. **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD):** **80%** of rural government expenditure on **subsidies**, only **20%** on infrastructure.
- Better **data** to assess subsidies and tax.
- **Fiscal prudence** with development: **debt-to-GDP can be higher** if it finances **developmental RE and CE**, **not** non-developmental RE. Class: US internal debt-to-GDP cited as **~130%** — the issue is **use and repayment**, not the ratio alone.
- Sheet closed on **Windfall tax** — numbers already in L01 (July 2022 crude).

## UPSC Cues

- **Budget deficit includes borrowing; fiscal deficit excludes it.** Non-debt CR = recovery + disinvestment.
- **Monetised deficit = printing = most inflationary.** Stopped as the main plug from **1997 (Chakravarty)** → **market** finance.
- Market finance → **crowding out** + higher interest + inflation if the money is **non-developmental RE**. India inflation **4% ± 2**.
- **Low primary deficit ≠ healthy** if it is all **old interest**.
- **Effective RD (Mukherjee, 2012)** = RD minus **state grants for assets**. **PSBR** = Centre + State FD + **PSU off-budget**; **16th FC: stop off-budget**.
- Old FRBM: RD **0.5%/yr → 0**; FD **0.3%/yr → 3%** by **2008–09**. NK Singh: RD **2.1 → 0.8** by **2022–23**; FD **3% then 2.5% by 2023–24**; escape **±0.5%**; debt **70 → 60**.
