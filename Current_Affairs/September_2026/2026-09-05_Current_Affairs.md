## Topic 1: A BIT of a reset, with a wider debate

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260905-01`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-II** (bilateral agreements, Parliament) + **GS-III** (investment)

### 1. What is in the news
India is revising its **model Bilateral Investment Treaty (BIT)**. A revised text is expected to go to the **Union Cabinet** soon. **Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman** flagged the need in **Union Budget 2025**, after several foreign investors sued India under older BITs and after the **2015 Model BIT** led to **unilateral termination** of many treaties.

**Prabhash Ranjan** (Professor and Vice Dean, Jindal Global Law School; was on the **Law Commission of India** expert team on the 2015 draft — **260th Report**) argues the reset must be **substance + process**.

### 2. Why the 2015 model is “out of balance”
Only a **handful** of BITs were signed on the 2015 text. It **tilts heavily toward the State’s right to regulate** and **under-protects investors** — uncertainty for countries that **export capital** to India. Aim now: **pull the pendulum back toward the centre**.

Two review tracks (clip):

1. **Substantive** — legal content / protections  
2. **Procedural** — how the model is drafted, consulted, and adopted  

### 3. Democratic deficit
International economic treaties hit citizens, but are often cut by **technocrats / the political executive** behind closed doors. **United Kingdom, Australia, Norway, Colombia** place treaty texts before **Parliament** or run **public consultations** before ratification.

### 4. What a real consultative process would look like (clip)
1. **Core team** of external experts (international lawyers, economists from think tanks / universities) as a sounding board  
2. Views from **industry, law firms, civil society**  
3. Publish a **draft for public comment** (done briefly in **2015**)  
4. Place the draft on the **floor of Parliament**; use **department-related parliamentary committees**  

Not a box-tick — include **dissent**.

### 5. One-liner
**2015 Model BIT** was **State-heavy**; the new text must rebalance **investor protection** and go through a **public / parliamentary** process, not only the Cabinet.

## Topic 2: SC — raise district-judiciary retirement age; backlog over 5 crore

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260905-02`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-II** (judiciary, Centre–State, access to justice)

### 1. What is in the news
**Krishnadas Rajagopal.** District-court pendency **> 5.18 crore**. Supreme Court is pushing States to lift judicial officers’ retirement from **60 to 62**. Three-judge Bench headed by **Chief Justice of India Surya Kant**; order **1 September**; next listing **1 October**. “Crying need of the hour” — else access to justice stays a **chimera**.

### 2. Who has said yes
**Seven** States: **Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal**.

The rest (clip names **Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Kerala, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Odisha, Punjab, Rajasthan, Telangana, Tripura, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh**, plus **NCT of Delhi, Jammu and Kashmir, Puducherry**) are hesitant or silent. Bench: amend **service rules within two months**, or decide **within two weeks**.

### 3. States’ fear vs Bench
States: **fiscal burden**; other services will demand the same age. Court: **misconceived**. Judicial officers are a **specialised service**; cost is **negligible** next to the gain. Keeping experienced judges also avoids paying **pension + a new salary** at once (“double-spend”).

**Not automatic:** only officers of **proven integrity** and **work** after assessment — keep talent, drop “deadwood.” **Sanctioned vs working strength** still has a “yawning gap”; recruitment drives have not filled cadres.

### 4. One-liner
SC: **60 → 62** (merit-based) for district judges to cut a **5.18 crore** backlog — **seven** States on board; others given a short clock.
