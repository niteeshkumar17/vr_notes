## Topic 1: Make in India at twelve years

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260925-01`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-III** (manufacturing, investment)

### 1. What is in the news

**TCA Sharad Raghavan.** Twelve years after **Make in India** was launched on **25 September 2014**. Across 12 metrics the manufacturing sector has not materially raised its share of growth, employment, or global exports. Incentive gains are confined to some sectors. Chart source: Ministry of Commerce and Industry, and **UNCTAD**.

### 2. Share of the economy, and of exports

| Lock | Figure |
|:---|:---|
| Manufacturing share of gross value added | **14.6%** in **2022–23** to **15.6%** in **2025–26** |
| Old series | A **lower** share of GDP in **2023–24** (latest old-series year) than in **2014–15** |
| Old series, versus the whole economy | Manufacturing grew faster in only **half** of the 12 years |
| New series, **2023–24 to 2025–26** | Manufacturing outpaced overall growth in **all three** years, but the gap is shrinking |
| Index of Industrial Production, old series | Manufacturing beat the overall index in only **three** of 12 years |
| New series | Manufacturing growth **matched** the overall index in **2023–24**, then was **slower** for the next two years |

**Non-petroleum goods exports** grew **53%**, from **$253.5 billion** in the launch year to **$388.3 billion** in **2025–26**. The chart on the same page prints **386.3**. Lock the prose figure, and do not treat 386.3 as a second official number. A longer window in the clip is “more than **400%**” off a much smaller base. That is not the 53%.

**UNCTAD:** India’s share of global merchandise exports went from about **0.8%** in **2002** to **1.7%** in **2013**, and was still **1.7%** in **2025–26**. The chart’s right-hand end is that same 1.7.

### 3. Investment

Gross fixed capital formation as a share of GDP has been **falling since 2022–23**. Manufacturing’s share of foreign direct investment rose from nearly **48%** in **2014–15** to **55%** in **2025–26**, but that sector’s FDI grew more slowly than overall FDI in **7 of the 12** years. Capacity utilisation has crept up and is still **below 80%**. Bank credit to industry, especially to micro, small and medium enterprises, has grown. The clip’s experts read the loans as capital already in place, not as fresh investment, because output has not grown fast enough.

**Production-Linked Incentive** schemes, launched across **2020 and 2021**: **14** schemes, cumulative investment **₹2.4 lakh crore** as of **March 2026**. The top five sectors account for nearly **83%**. The clip names solar modules, pharmaceuticals, drugs, automobiles and their components, specialty steel, and large-scale electronics. Do not renumber that list.
