## Topic 1: Battle of Haifa — 23 September 1918

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260923-01`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-I** (modern history; the politics of memory)

### 1. What is in the news

**Shashi Tharoor.** Fourth-term Member of Parliament (Lok Sabha) for **Thiruvananthapuram** (Congress). Chairman of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on External Affairs. Sahitya Akademi award. The clip says **25** books, including *An Era of Darkness: The British Empire in India*.

The **23 September 1918** Battle of **Haifa** is a small action in the last phase of the **First World War**, and a large argument about what India, Israel and Palestine each remember.

### 2. The charge

| Clip | Lock |
|:---|:---|
| Indian service overseas | **More than a million** served, from **Flanders** to **Mesopotamia**. **Over 74,000** died |
| Who charged | **Jodhpur, Mysore and Hyderabad Lancers**, in the **15th Imperial Service Cavalry Brigade** |
| Against whom | Principally **Ottoman** forces. The Indian cavalry was under **British** command. Many Palestinian Arabs were on the Ottoman side |
| What it did | Secured the **port**. Helped the Allied advance north toward **Damascus**. One of the last great cavalry charges. The slopes named are **Mount Carmel** |
| How India marks it | A First World War memorial renamed **Teen Murti Haifa Chowk** |

He calls the commemoration a recovery of Indian agency inside an imperial command. The courage belongs to India’s national story. The soldiers did not choose the political result, and they were not fighting under their own flag.

### 3. Why the memory splits

The same victory helped Britain hold **Palestine**, and so helped the **Balfour Declaration** and the later creation of Israel. For many Palestinians it is dispossession, not liberation. The line he records from Palestinian representatives: “We did not ask you to liberate us.” That is not a denial of the soldiers’ bravery.

**Abdullah Mohammed Abu Shawesh**, Palestinian Ambassador to India, in a letter to the writer: history is being used to tie today’s India to the establishment of Israel, and to recast the soldiers as victims of British exploitation rather than as heroes. Tharoor’s own close: pride and discomfort sit together, because Indian blood served a project that displaced another people. Haifa is a diplomatic bridge with Israel and a morally ambiguous page with Palestine. He does not ask the reader to pick one story.

Historians in the clip disagree on credit. Some Israeli scholars say the Indian role is overstated and that British and Australian soldiers did the decisive work. Indian military historians point to British war records of the breakthrough. Do not turn that disagreement into a verdict.
