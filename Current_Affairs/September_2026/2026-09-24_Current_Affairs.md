## Topic 1: Delhi Cabinet approves DURGA e-autos

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260924-01`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-II** (welfare) + **GS-III** (urban transport)

### 1. What is in the news

**The Hindu Bureau.** The Delhi Cabinet, on Wednesday, approved **DURGA**. The clip does not expand the letters. Do not invent a full form.

Electric autos go **only** to **women** and **transgender persons**. The Chief Minister’s Office says the aims are dignified self-employment, women’s safety, a larger place for transgender persons in transport, last-mile connectivity, and clean urban transport.

### 2. The first phase

| Women | **1,170** |

Applicants use an online portal. They must be **Delhi residents**, aged **20–40**, with a **voter ID**, a driving licence the clip prints as **LMW** (it does not expand the letters; the usual class written on a licence is LMV), and a **Public Service Vehicle (PSV)** badge. **Women with more than three children are ineligible.**

## Topic 2: Quality control orders and manufacturing

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260924-02`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-III** (industry, trade)

### 1. What changed

**Nancy Gupta** (Visiting Fellow, Centre for Policy Research). On **25 June 2026** the **Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT)** notified a **Quality Control Order** transition order for 2026. It is aimed at firms that are stuck getting **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** Scheme-I certification, and it allows a regulatory forbearance so that burden can be eased. The photo’s sentence on how the forbearance works is cramped. Do not add a procedure the clip does not spell out.

**Quality Control Orders (QCOs)** grew from **89** products in **2019** to **785** by the end of **December 2024**. The pace of that expansion slowed considerably toward the end of **2025**.

At the **World Trade Organization’s** eighth **Trade Policy Review** of India, in **July 2026**, non-tariff barriers were raised not only by major trading partners (the European Union and the United States) but also by fellow **BRICS** members, including Brazil, China and Indonesia.

### 2. What the studies found

**Prerna Prabhakar** (Fellow, Centre for Social and Economic Progress) is cited for a recent study. QCOs can put a disproportionate burden on smaller industrial players, by curtailing their access to critical inputs and by the cost of compliance. The adverse effect on inputs is **not** confined to small downstream firms. Larger firms, better placed to get the certification, also see declines in value addition, output, competitiveness and domestic value addition.

The chemical-using share of firms covered rose from **52%** in **2018** to **57%** in **2024**. On the input side, exposure to regulation rose from **11.8%** in **2019** to **56.6%** in **2024**. Among larger firms the clip pairs a **35%** rise in production with a **57%** drop in productivity, and input-cost rises of **6%** and **2%**. Do not collapse those into one number.

India needs to compete in **more than 600** global value chains. For smaller firms the same study links input QCOs to about a **2.6%** decline in profitability and a **7.6%** decline in output prices. The **Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006** is the definition the clip uses for a small firm. Supply-chain rules should not make compliance an entry ticket that smaller firms cannot afford. The long aim named is **Bharat 2047**.
