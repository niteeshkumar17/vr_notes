## Topic 1: Karnataka — official-language status for Tulu in two districts

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260921-01`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-II** (federalism, official language, local government)

### 1. What is in the news

The **Karnataka** government, in a **Cabinet meeting at Mangaluru on 18 September**, decided to accord **language status to Tulu**.

**Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar:** the status will apply to **administrative matters in the two districts**. The government will release **₹82 lakh annually** for **translation, training and language-usage**.

**Where Tulu is commonly spoken (clip):** **Dakshina Kannada** district; **southern parts of Udupi** district (both Karnataka); **northern parts of Kasaragod** district (**Kerala**). Kasaragod is outside a Karnataka Cabinet decision. The “two districts” are the Karnataka ones.

### 2. What the status changes in those districts

**B. Shivarama Shetty**, former Project Director, Centre of Excellence for Studies in **Classical Kannada**, **Central Institute of Indian Languages (CIIL)**, Mysuru:

| Forum | Change |
|:---|:---|
| **Gram sabha** of a **gram panchayat**; **taluk panchayat**; **zilla panchayat** | Elected representatives **can speak in Tulu** |
| Urban local bodies | Same for **city corporation** council, **city municipal** council, **town municipal** council |
| Government paper | **Circulars and orders** issued in the two districts should **also contain Tulu** |
| Public | People can **submit applications** to government departments **in Tulu** |
| Officials who do not know Tulu | Will depend on **translators**, or learn the **basics** of Tulu |

### 3. How the move started (clip only)

| When | What |
|:---|:---|
| **30 January 2023** | **BJP** in power. Then Minister for **Kannada and Culture**, **V. Sunil Kumar**, formed a committee headed by **M. Mohan Alva** (Chairman, **Alva’s Education Foundation**, **Moodbidri**, Dakshina Kannada) to study declaring Tulu the **second official language of Karnataka**. Report asked **within a week**. |
| **29 March 2023** | **Model code of conduct** for the **2023** Karnataka Assembly elections. The BJP government **could not** grant the status. It then **lost** the election. |
| Congress government | Appointed a committee led by **K.M. Gayathri**, Director of **Kannada and Culture**, to study how **Andhra Pradesh** granted **additional language status to Urdu**. |
| **19–20 January 2026** | That committee **visited Andhra Pradesh**. The clip stops here. |

### 4. One-liner

Mangaluru Cabinet, **18 September**: Tulu gets **official-language status for administration in two Karnataka districts** (Dakshina Kannada and Udupi). **₹82 lakh a year** for translation, training and use. Not the Eighth Schedule. Kasaragod (Kerala) is a speech area, not this decision.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** This is **not** an Eighth Schedule entry and **not** a “classical language” tag. “Classical Kannada” is the **name of Shetty’s CIIL centre**. **₹82 lakh** is the annual outlay, not a one-time grant. **30 Jan 2023** = Alva committee; **29 March 2023** = model code, which is why the BJP government stopped. **19–20 Jan 2026** = the Gayathri committee’s Andhra visit, not the Cabinet date. Cabinet date = **18 September** (this year, at Mangaluru).</span>

## Topic 2: Supreme Court — a law for litigants’ court deposits

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260921-02`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-II** (judiciary, Law Commission) + **GS-III** (money, financial instruments)

### 1. What is in the news

A **law** is necessary to protect **crores** deposited by **litigants** across **courts and tribunals** during **pending appeals**, the Supreme Court said in a **recent judgment**. The clip does **not** name the case. Do not invent a title.

**Bench:** Justices **P.S. Narasimha** and **Alok Aradhe**.

Litigants’ money should be saved from **ad-hoc management**. The Court called for **nationwide legislative reform** so that courts and tribunals handle this money in one standard way.

### 2. The problem

There are **no uniform rules** for **court-ordered deposits**. Example: a court directs a litigant to **deposit money in order to get a stay** while the appeal is heard. So far, each court and tribunal has ordered **deposits and their investment** **case to case**.

That has left **huge sums** in **financial instruments across different banks**, earning **inconsistent interest**, and then **post-judgment litigation** over the **interest-accounting**. Inflation and economic uncertainty add to the anxiety about the rate.

The **asymmetry** is itself causing **further pendency**. Lack of common standards means courts keep re-deciding routine questions: **investment, renewal, quantum, accounting and interest**.

**Quote (Bench):** to preserve the **economic integrity** of any deposit and to **provide interest**, there must be **clarity and uniformity** in how the deposit is handled. Without that, the **time value of money** and a **certain, clear accrual of interest** both fail.

### 3. What the Court asked for

| Ask | Lock |
|:---|:---|
| Model | **United States Court Registry Investment System** — a **common platform**. Deposits in courts and tribunals are **pooled into one scheme** and put into the **most beneficial** instrument for the parties. |
| Why | **Certain** interest rates; easier access for litigants; courts stop deciding, case by case, how the money is invested. |
| Legislation | “Necessary to evolve and formulate **suitable legislation**.” |
| Who examines it | **Law Commission of India**, including laws **other countries** have enacted. |
| Who to consult | **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**, **Ministry of Finance**, and the nodal **Ministry of Law and Justice**. |
| Copy of the judgment | To the **Law Commission chairperson**, the **RBI Governor**, and the **Secretaries** of Finance and of Law and Justice. |

### 4. One-liner

Narasimha and Aradhe: litigants’ deposits need a **statute** and a **common pool**, on the lines of the **US Court Registry Investment System**. Ask the **Law Commission** (consult **RBI**, Finance, Law and Justice). No case name and no rupee total in the clip.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** The Court **requested a law**. It did **not** itself create the investment scheme. **“Crores”** is the clip’s word — there is **no** figure to memorise. The US name is **Court Registry Investment System**, not a Federal Reserve facility. This Law Commission reference is **not** the 1833 Macaulay commission and **not** the 21st Commission’s Uniform Civil Code paper.</span>
