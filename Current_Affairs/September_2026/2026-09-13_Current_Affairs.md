## Topic 1: Rapid Himalayan erosion can be a hidden CO₂ *source*

> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260913-01`  
> **GS Paper:** **GS-I** (Himalaya, Indus, weathering) + **GS-III** (carbon cycle, climate)

### 1. What is in the news
**Vasudevan Mukunth.** A team from the **Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Pune**, the **Wadia Institute of Himalayan Geology**, and the **Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Roorkee** — paper in ***Chemical Geology***. Textbook story: Himalayan **silicate weathering** *eats* carbon dioxide (CO₂) and has helped **cool** the Earth for about **50 million years**. This paper: another mineral path can **more than offset** that cooling in the **upper Indus**.

### 2. The two paths (clip)

**Sink (the usual story).** The **Indian plate** keeps pushing into the **Eurasian plate**. Mountains erode → fresh **silicate** minerals meet atmospheric CO₂ → rivers carry the carbon to the **oceans** → it locks into **seafloor sediments**.

**Source (the paper).** Himalayan rock also holds **pyrite** (“fool’s gold”). Rapid slope erosion (glaciers are good at **crushing** rock) exposes **sulphides** to air and water for the first time in **millions of years**. Sulphides **oxidise to sulphuric acid**. That acid attacks **carbonate rocks** (common in the **Indus** basin) → **CO₂ is released to the air**.

How they told gypsum apart from pyrite: **sulphur and oxygen isotope** ratios of **sulphates** in the Indus water.

### 3. Numbers and map (clip only)

| Lock | Clip |
|:---|:---|
| Where | **Headwaters / upper Indus** |
| Rate | **CO₂ release** from this oxidation path about **three times** the **CO₂ uptake** by silicate weathering |
| Steep mountains | **Net CO₂ sources** |
| Flatter **floodplains** downstream | **CO₂ sinks** — water moves **more slowly**, so silicate weathering can dominate |
| Timescale the authors claim | Glacier-fed catchments as geological CO₂ sources over **million-year** climate, not a next-monsoon inventory |

**Double-edged erosion:** the same grinding that supplies silicates to *remove* CO₂ also feeds the **more aggressive sulphide** reactions.

### 4. One-liner
Upper **Indus**: pyrite → sulphuric acid → carbonate weathering **outgasses** CO₂ at about **3×** the silicate sink; **mountains source / floodplains sink**. IISER Pune + **Wadia** + IIT Roorkee, *Chemical Geology*.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** Himalayan weathering is **not only** a CO₂ sink. **Pyrite oxidation ≠ gypsum** (isotopes). **3×** is **upper Indus**, not a global Himalaya average. Flood hook ≠ a new GLOF topic.</span>
