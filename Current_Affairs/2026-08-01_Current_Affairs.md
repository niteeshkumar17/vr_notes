# Current Affairs - August 1, 2026

## 1. Polity & Governance

### Lok Sabha passes Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026
*   **Context:** The Lok Sabha passed the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 amid protests by the Opposition.
*   **Key Provisions of the Bill:**
    *   It seeks to make the delayed registration of births and deaths more stringent.
    *   It requires an order from a judicial magistrate (first class) for registrations done after two years.
*   **Related Constitutional Articles:** Article 246 (Seventh Schedule - Concurrent List Entry 30: Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths).
*   **Other Developments in Parliament:**
    *   **Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill, 2025:** A deadline extension was granted to the Joint Parliamentary Committee examining this bill for the submission of its report. The bill proposes the automatic removal of Ministers, Chief Minister, or Prime Minister if they are arrested and held in custody for 30 days on serious criminal charges carrying a jail term of five years or more.
    *   **Related Constitutional Articles:** Article 74, 75 (Council of Ministers for Union); Article 163, 164 (Council of Ministers for States); Article 102, 191 (Disqualifications for membership).

### Need for Humane Education System Reform (Editorial)
*   **Context:** Recent student protests have highlighted the need for a complete overhaul of the Indian education system, particularly the competitive examination and admission culture.
*   **Related Constitutional Articles:** Article 21A (Right to Education); Article 45 (Early childhood care and education); Article 46 (Educational interests of weaker sections); Article 246 (Seventh Schedule - Concurrent List Entry 25: Education).
*   **Critique of Current System:**
    *   The single-shot, marks-based competitive exams create a dangerous emotional environment and high stress.
    *   A centralized agency like the National Testing Agency (NTA) assumes a "one-size-fits-all" model which is flawed for a diverse country like India.
    *   A single national test creates logistical overload and demographic unfairness (language, geography, school background, digital access).
    *   The coaching ecosystem displaces formal schooling and exploits student vulnerabilities.
*   **Proposed Reforms:**
    *   **Multi-dimensional Assessment:** Shift from purely memory/speed-based tests to assessing reasoning, persistence, communication, judgment, and ethical orientation. Assessments should include school performance, aptitude, analytical writing, structured interviews, verifiable portfolios, and internships.
    *   **Decentralized System:** Allow universities or clusters of institutions to design and conduct their own assessments within broad University Grants Commission (UGC) norms, moving away from a single centralized exam.
    *   **Safeguards for Exams:** Implement encrypted digital paper transmission, multi-layered question banks with algorithmic randomization, biometric candidate verification, time-staggered papers, and post-examination forensic review to prevent leaks and ensure the sanctity of exams.
    *   **Shift in Focus:** Move from rank-based filtration to capacity-based recognition, emphasizing holistic development over rote learning.

## 2. Environment & Ecology

### Conservation of Western Ghats (Editorial)
*   **Context:** The debate over the declaration of the Western Ghats as an Ecologically Sensitive Area (ESA) is ongoing, with the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC) extending the tenure of an expert panel by another year after the expiry of the last draft notification in July 2026.
*   **Related Constitutional Articles:** Article 48A (Protection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wild life); Article 51A(g) (Fundamental duty to protect and improve the natural environment).
*   **Background:**
    *   **Western Ghats:** Spans six coastal states in India, internationally recognized for its rich biodiversity, and declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012.
    *   **WGEEP (Madhav Gadgil Committee, 2010):** The Western Ghats Ecology Expert Panel recommended declaring the entire Western Ghats as an ESA and subdividing it into three Ecologically Sensitive Zones (ESZ) to regulate human activities under the Environment Protection Act, 1986.
    *   **Kasturirangan Committee (2012):** Constituted due to strong local and political opposition to the WGEEP report. It limited restrictions to ecologically sensitive natural landscapes, reducing the ESA coverage to only 37% of the Western Ghats.
*   **Current Status & Challenges:**
    *   Between 2015 and 2026, multiple draft notifications have been issued, but consensus between the Centre and States (especially Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa) remains elusive.
    *   **Case of Karnataka:** The state has consistently opposed the ESA declaration, which covers ~20,668 sq km across 10 of its districts. The state cites potential negative impacts on agriculture, plantations (shade-grown coffee, rubber, coconut), mining, and infrastructure development.
    *   **Local Concerns:** Villagers and stakeholders express fear of possible eviction, restrictions on agricultural practices, and challenges to forest rights (e.g., minor forest produce collection).
*   **Way Forward:**
    *   Conservation efforts cannot be indefinitely delayed as it can result in states losing control over ecological systems to administrative boundaries.
    *   The ESA declaration should proactively include and protect indigenous communities living within forests. They naturally follow conservation principles and their presence serves as a vital bridge in preserving biocultural diversity.
