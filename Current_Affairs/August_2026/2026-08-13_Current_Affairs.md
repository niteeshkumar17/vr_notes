## 1. Ladakh's Giant 'Rivers of Ice' are Slowing Down

> **Tags:** `[GS-1: Physical Geography - Glaciology & Climate Change]` · `[GS-3: Environment - Climate Change Impacts]`

### Context & Key Findings
- A recent study published in the journal *The Cryosphere* highlights that glaciers in the **Zanskar region of Ladakh** (home to some of the largest glaciers in the Himalayas) are moving more slowly than they did 30 years ago.
- **Data:** Glaciers have slowed by **2.4 m/year per decade** on average. The pace of surface thinning increased from ~0.22 m/year (2000-2005) to ~0.57 m/year (2015-2020).
- **Zanskar Glaciers' Source:** These glaciers receive most of their snowfall from the **mid-latitude westerly disturbances** during winter.

### Mechanism Behind the Slowdown
- **Ice Transport Slows:** As glaciers lose more ice than they gain, they become thinner. Thinner ice exerts less driving force (basal sliding/glacier flow), causing the glacier to flow more slowly. 
- While most Himalayan studies focus on glacier retreat or mass loss, this study links three processes: **mass loss, glacier thinning, and glacier flow**, showing that thinning changes the glacier's ability to move.
- The lower parts of the glacier receive less replenishment from higher elevations, making continued shrinkage more likely.

### Implications for the Indus Basin
- **Peak Water Phenomenon:** More melting may temporarily increase runoff ("peak water"), but once glaciers lose a substantial fraction of stored ice, meltwater contributions are expected to drop.
- **Indus Basin Impact:** Glacier thinning and slowdown have long-term implications for the **Indus basin**, where glacier melt contributes significantly to river flows during the dry summer. 
- **Long-term Risk:** Continued decline in glacier water storage impacts water security, agriculture, hydropower, and downstream ecosystems.
- <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**UPSC Prelims Drainage Divide Trap:** Glacial meltwater from the Zanskar region drains exclusively via the north-flowing Zanskar River into the **Indus River Basin** (confluence at Nimmu). It has **ZERO connection to the Ganga or Yamuna drainage basins**, separated completely by the Great Himalayan Range.</span>

## 2. A Timely Reset for the Food Security Act

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Social Justice - Public Distribution System (PDS), Hunger & Poverty]` · `[GS-3: Indian Economy - Food Security]`

### Context: Draft National Food Security (Amendment) Bill, 2026
- The draft bill proposes changes to the entitlements under the **Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY)**.
- **Current Provision:** Priority Households receive **5 kg of foodgrains per person per month**, while AAY households receive a flat **35 kg per household**.
- **Proposed Amendment:** The draft proposes **7 kg per person, capped at 35 kg** for AAY households.

### Concerns with the Proposed Amendment
- **Impact on Smaller Vulnerable Households:** The proposed formula would reduce support for households with 1 to 4 members (by 20% to 80%). For instance, a smaller AAY household consisting of an older person living alone, a widow, or a person with a disability would receive significantly less than the current 35 kg.
- **Recommendation:** Experts suggest the existing 35 kg entitlement should be preserved through an explicit **no-loss safeguard**.

### Broader Challenge: Nutritional Security over Food Security
- **Dietary Diversification:** India faces a double burden of malnutrition (undernutrition alongside rising non-communicable diseases like diabetes). The ICMR-NIN's 2024 guidelines recommend that cereals and millets provide at most **45% of energy**.
- The PDS is currently heavily cereal-based, which may reinforce dietary imbalance. 
- **Way Forward:** 
  1. Foodgrain entitlement should not itself be equated with diabetes risk, but dietary diversification (pulses, beans, milk, nuts, vegetables, edible oils) must be separately financed and progressively implemented without reducing existing cereal entitlements.
  2. Integration of fair price shops with health interventions (e.g., Ayushman Arogya Mandirs for diabetes/hypertension screenings).

## 4. Lok Sabha Clears Mines and Minerals Amendment Bill

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Federalism - Centre-State Relations]` · `[GS-3: Economy - Mining Sector & Resource Mobilization]`

### Context
- Amid Opposition protests, the Lok Sabha passed the **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026** without a debate.

### Key Provisions & Controversies
- **Centralization of Control:** The Bill prevents State governments from imposing additional taxes, cesses, or levies on mineral rights, granting the **Centre greater control** over regulating mineral-laden lands.
- **Government's Rationale:** Coal and Mines Minister G. Kishan Reddy stated that divergent fiscal levies by States created uncertainty in the mineral sector, which could raise costs, encourage imports, and undermine domestic supply chains.
- **Opposition's Stance:** Argued strongly that the Bill violates the principles of **federalism** (states' rights to taxation on minerals).

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">
### Constitutional Framework & Supreme Court Jurisprudence (9-Judge Bench 2024)
- **Royalty is NOT a Tax:** The 9-Judge Constitution Bench (led by CJI D.Y. Chandrachud in *Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL*, July 2024) ruled 8:1 that **Royalty payable under Section 9 of the MMDR Act, 1957 is NOT a tax**, but a contractual consideration paid to the mineral owner.
- **State Taxing Power (Entry 49, List II):** State legislatures possess full constitutional power to levy taxes on **mineral-bearing lands** under Entry 49 of the State List (*"Taxes on lands and buildings"*).
- **Parliamentary Limitation Power (Entry 54, List I):** However, Parliament can impose statutory limitations or ceilings on States' taxing power over mineral rights via express legislative declarations under Entry 54 of the Union List (*"Regulation of mines and mineral development to the extent declared by Parliament"*). The MMDR Amendment Bill 2026 exercises this Entry 54 power to establish a uniform national fiscal framework.
</span>
