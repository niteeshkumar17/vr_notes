## Topic 1: Union Govt Opposes Income-Based "Creamy Layer" in SC/ST Quotas

> **Headlines:** *"Govt. opposes income-based creamy layer in SC/ST quotas"* (by Abhinay Lakshman)  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II (Polity & Governance — Constitutional Rights, Reservations & Social Justice)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260807-01`

### 1. Context & Union Government's Stand in Supreme Court
* The Union Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment filed an affidavit in the Supreme Court opposing a batch of petitions seeking the exclusion of the **"creamy layer"** from Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) on the lines of Other Backward Classes (OBCs).
* The Centre asserted that any modification of reservation policy—particularly introducing income-based preferences within SC/ST categories—must be preceded by a **holistic review and empirical study**, including socio-economic data of reserved category beneficiaries, rather than ad-hoc judicial mandates.

### 2. Constitutional Architecture & Judicial Precedents

| Landmark Case / Provision | Core Ruling / Context | Application to SC/ST Creamy Layer |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **[Article 15(4)](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_15.md) & [Article 16(4)](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_16.md)** | Enable Special Provisions & Reservations for Socially & Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs), SCs, and STs. | Forms the constitutional bedrock for affirmative action in education and public employment. |
| ***Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)*** | 9-judge bench established that the "creamy layer" test applies to **OBCs** to filter out economically/socially advanced individuals. | SC explicitly held that the creamy layer concept is **NOT applicable to SCs and STs**. |
| ***M. Nagaraj v. Union of India (2006)*** | Applied criteria of backwardness, inadequacy of representation, and overall efficiency (Art. 335) for SC/ST promotions. | Observations on creamy layer in SC/ST promotion were treated by Centre as *obiter dicta* (general observations), not binding ratio. |
| ***State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh (2024)*** | 7-judge bench upheld state governments' power to **sub-categorize** SC/STs to give preference to more backward sub-groups. | Justice B.R. Gavai's concurring opinion suggested identifying creamy layer in SC/STs, but Centre clarified that sub-categorization sanction does not equate to mandating an income-based creamy layer at the national level. |

### 3. Key Substantive Arguments of the Centre
* **Social Stigma vs. Income Poverty:** SC/ST reservations were instituted to address centuries of structural social discrimination, untouchability, and historical exclusion—**not merely economic backwardness**. Income elevation does not automatically erase social stigma or untouchability.
* **Pre-existing "Means Test" in Welfare Schemes:** The Centre highlighted that a "means test" (income threshold) is already actively enforced across most government welfare, scholarship, and development schemes for SC/STs to ensure targeted benefit delivery to the poorest. However, constitutional reservation under Part III aims at **representation and social empowerment**.
* **Lack of Empirical Basis:** Introducing income criteria without a nationwide socio-economic survey risks denying constitutional guarantees of equal opportunity ([Article 16](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_16.md)).

## Topic 2: SC Clarification on Ramsar Wetland Sites & Mining Buffer Zones

> **Headlines:** *"How will the Supreme Court order impact mining around Ramsar wetland sites?"* (by Jacob P Koshy)  
> **GS Paper:** GS-III (Environment & Ecology — Wetlands, Environmental Clearance & SC Judgments)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260807-02`

### 1. Supreme Court Clarification (August 2026)
* **Background:** In February 2024, the SC directed that mining proposals within a **10-km radius** of the **Asan Wetland Conservation Reserve** (Uttarakhand) require prior approval from the Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL).
* **Clarification:** Hearing an application from Himachal Pradesh, the SC clarified that the 10-km mining restriction in the Feb 2024 order was **specific to the Asan Reserve** (due to its distinct conservation reserve status & NBWL mandate) and **does NOT automatically apply as a universal 10-km statutory buffer zone to all Ramsar sites across India**.

### 2. Legal & Environmental Framework Comparison

| Regime | Governing Law / Treaty | Buffer Zone & Mining Rules |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Ramsar Convention Sites** | International Treaty (Ramsar, 1971; India joined 1982). India has **101 Ramsar sites** *(101st: Glaw Lake, Arunachal Pradesh — Aug 3, 2026)*. | Ramsar status is an international commitment for "wise use". It **does NOT automatically create a statutory buffer zone** under domestic law unless designated under WPA 1972 or ESZ rules. |
| **Wetlands Conservation Rules, 2017** | Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. | Prohibits solid waste dumping, untreated effluent discharge, and permanent construction inside wetlands. Does **not** mandate a default 10-km buffer zone outside wetland boundaries. |
| **National Parks & Wildlife Sanctuaries** | Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. | Mining is strictly **prohibited within 1 km** of notified boundaries or designated Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZs). |
| **Forest Clearances** | Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980 & EIA 2006. | Mining in forest land requires prior Forest Clearance (Stage I & II) and Environmental Clearance (EC) subject to site-specific appraisal. |

### 3. Key Takeaway for UPSC
* International treaty designations (like Ramsar) provide high conservation priority but require specific domestic statutory notifications (ESZs or PA declarations) to enforce mandatory territorial buffer zones like mining bans.

## Topic 3: 12th National Handloom Day — Handloom 4.0 & Textile Heritage

> **Headlines:** *"Handloom — weaving India's heritage into global growth"* (by Union Minister Giriraj Singh)  
> **GS Paper:** GS-III (Economy — Cottage Industry & MSMEs) & GS-I (Indian Heritage — Swadeshi Movement)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260807-03`

### 1. Historical Context of National Handloom Day
* **Date:** Celebrated annually on **August 7** (instituted in 2015).
* **Historical Linkage:** Commemorates the **Swadeshi Movement**, which was formally launched on **August 7, 1905** at the Calcutta Town Hall to protest the Partition of Bengal by Lord Curzon, championing indigenous handloom and boycott of foreign goods.

### 2. Socio-Economic Profile of Handloom Sector
* **Largest Cottage Industry:** Employs **over 35 lakh weavers and allied workers**.
* **Women Empowerment:** **Nearly 72%** of handloom weavers and workers in India are **women**.
* **Iconic Traditions:** Bengal Muslin, Banarasi Silk, Kanchipuram Weaves, Pochampally Ikat, Assam's Maina Loom & Sivasagar Chitranjan Handloom, Ambar Charkha.

## Topic 4: Police Reforms — Kerala's "My Police Station" Democratic Policing Initiative

> **Headlines:** *"Kerala set to launch project to improve police-citizen ties"* (The Hindu Bureau)  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II (Polity & Governance — Police Reforms, Custodial Violence & Accountability)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260807-04`

### 1. Core Features of "My Police Station" Project
* **Launch:** August 15, 2026 by Kerala Police.
* **Structural Reform:** Entrusts the position of **Station House Officer (SHO)** to **Sub-Inspectors (SIs)** in 419 out of 484 law-and-order stations (including female SIs as SHOs in 63 police stations). Reverses the earlier practice of assigning Circle Inspectors (CIs) as SHOs.
* **Strategic Exception:** 64 high-importance stations (e.g. Mullaperiyar dam station) will retain higher-ranking officers (DySP / CIs).

### 2. Objectives & Governance Relevance
* **Elimination of Custodial Crimes:** Primary mission to eliminate custodial deaths, custodial torture, and arbitrary violence.
* **Empowering Young Leadership:** Direct-recruit SIs handle ground-level station leadership, fostering agility and fresh administrative accountability.
* **Alignment with Police Reforms Commissions:** Aligns with recommendations of the **National Police Commission (1977-81)**, **Padmanabhaiah Committee (2000)**, and SC's ***Prakash Singh Judgment (2006)*** regarding SHO operational clarity, human-rights-centric policing, and community accountability.

## Topic 5: Future of Multi-Sport Events — Commonwealth Games (CWG) Governance & India's 2036 Bid

> **Headlines:** *"Should the Commonwealth Games be scrapped?"* (Parley — Debate between Anju Bobby George & Heena Sidhu)  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II / GS-III (Sports Governance, International Multi-Sport Economics & Olympic 2036 Strategy)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260807-05`

### 1. Key Challenges Facing CWG
* **Hosting Cost Escalations:** Victoria (Australia) withdrew from hosting CWG 2026 due to ballooning budgets. Glasgow stepped in with a drastically scaled-down format.
* **Exclusion of Core Sports:** Scaled-down formats omit key disciplines (Shooting, Badminton, Wrestling, Hockey, Cricket), eroding audience engagement and damaging elite athlete preparation pipelines.

### 2. Strategic Perspectives & Recommendations
* **Decentralized Hosting Model:** Experts advocate shifting from single-city host models to **multi-city or regional co-hosting** (similar to the FIFA World Cup format) to utilize existing infrastructure and prevent "white elephant" stadiums.
* **Stepping Stone for India's 2036 Olympic Bid:** Participation and targeted hosting of multi-sport events serve as critical benchmarks for building sports infrastructure, event-management capability, and high-performance training systems for India's **2036 Olympic Hosting Bid**.
