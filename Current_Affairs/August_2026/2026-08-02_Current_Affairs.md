## 1. CAPF vs IPS: Dispute Over Leadership Positions

**Context:** Over 3,000 serving Group A officers from Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs) have approached the Supreme Court against the recently notified **Central Armed Police Force (General Administration) Act, 2026**.

**The Core Issue:**
*   **The CAPF Act 2026:** Reserves the majority of top leadership positions for Indian Police Service (IPS) officers on deputation. Specifically:
    *   **50%** of Inspector General (IG) posts.
    *   At least **67%** of Additional Director General (ADG) posts.
    *   **100%** of Special Director General (SDG) and Director General (DG) posts.
*   **Negation of SC Ruling:** The new Act effectively negates a landmark May 2025 Supreme Court judgment (*Sanjay Prakash v. Union of India*), which had directed:
    *   Recognizing Group A Executive Cadre officers of CAPFs as **Organised Group A Services (OGAS)**.
    *   Progressively *reducing* IPS deputation in CAPFs up to the IG level over two years.
    *   The SC had previously dismissed the Ministry of Home Affairs' (MHA) review petition against this ruling, prompting the MHA to introduce the Bill in Parliament to bypass the judgment.

**Legal & Constitutional Grounds of Challenge:**
*   The petitioners argue that the law hinders the career progression of CAPF cadre officers, depriving them of leadership roles despite years of service.
*   The petition seeks to declare **Sections 3 and 4** of the CAPF Act as *ultra vires* (beyond the powers) of the Constitution.
    *   *Section 3:* Pertains to regulation of recruitment and conditions of service.
    *   *Section 4:* Gives the Union government power to amend the First and Second Schedules of the Act through mere notification.

**About CAPFs:**
*   They function under the administrative control of the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)**.
*   Includes: Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), Border Security Force (BSF), Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP), Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB), and Central Industrial Security Force (CISF).

**Related Constitutional Articles:**
*   **[Article 13](../../Articles/Article_13.md):** Basis for judicial review; laws inconsistent with fundamental rights can be declared *ultra vires*.
*   **[Article 33](../../Articles/Article_33.md):** Power of Parliament to restrict/modify fundamental rights in their application to armed forces and police forces to ensure proper discharge of duties and discipline.
*   **[Article 312](../../Articles/Article_312.md):** Deals with All-India Services, under which the IPS is constituted, whose officers go on deputation to CAPFs.

## 2. Drug Testing in Educational Institutions

**Context:** Under directions from the Union government, higher educational institutions across India, starting with IITs and IIMs this year, will begin suggesting voluntary drug testing as part of the admission process. Results will remain confidential.

**Key Initiative:**
*   This is part of a broader government push against substance abuse among the youth.
*   The Prime Minister is scheduled to launch the **'Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat'** initiative.
*   It follows an expansive survey of substance users across India, with results expected in the coming years.

**Related Constitutional Articles:**
*   **[Article 47](../../Articles/Article_47.md) (DPSP):** It is the primary duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and standard of living and improve public health. Specifically, the State shall endeavour to bring about the **prohibition of the consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs** which are injurious to health (except for medicinal purposes).

## 3. Bhitarkanika National Park Reopens with Plastic Ban

**Context:** The Bhitarkanika National Park in Kendrapara district, Odisha, has reopened to tourists after a three-month closure.

**Key Details:**
*   **Reason for Closure:** Closed from May 1 to July 31 annually for the **nesting and breeding season of estuarine (saltwater) crocodiles**.
*   **New Measure:** Authorities have enforced a **complete ban on plastics** within the park.
*   **Features:** It is a vital mangrove ecosystem and wildlife habitat.

**Related Constitutional Articles:**
*   **[Article 48A](../../Articles/Article_48A.md) (DPSP):** The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country.
*   **[Article 51A(g)](../../Articles/Article_51A_g.md) (Fundamental Duties):** It shall be the duty of every citizen of India to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures.
