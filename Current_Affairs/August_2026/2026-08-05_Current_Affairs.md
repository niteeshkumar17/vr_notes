## 1. Polity & Governance

### EC Revises SIR Deadline for Delhi Electoral Rolls — Second Extension in a Month
*   **Context:** The Election Commission of India (EC) on Tuesday **revised the schedule for the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of Delhi's electoral rolls for the **second time in a month**, extending the ongoing door-to-door verification by Booth Level Officers (BLOs) **till August 17** and postponing the publication of the final electoral roll to **October 27**.
*   **Background:**
    *   The distribution and collection of enumeration forms was initially scheduled to conclude within a month of the exercise beginning on **June 30**.
    *   It was first rescheduled to conclude on **August 8** after the first extension announced by the EC on **July 15**.
    *   This is the **second extension** in a month after Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) **Ashok Kumar** sought additional time, citing **"slow progress in the enumeration process"**.
*   **Key Data (as of Tuesday):**
    *   **63.23%** of the **1.45 crore** enumeration forms had been digitised.
    *   **99.199%** forms were distributed, with just **four days remaining** for the first extension deadline.
*   **'Slow Progress' — CEO's Office Report:**
    *   Reports of slow progress in distribution and collection of enumeration forms.
    *   The proposal for an extension was prepared and sent to the EC.
    *   The remaining time was **not sufficient** for BLOs to complete the exercise without errors and verify all details of the electors.
*   **BLOs' Role During Ongoing Enumeration:**
    *   Visiting households to distribute and collect filled enumeration forms containing details of electors.
    *   Where available, mapping them with the last SIR roll.
    *   Responsible for identifying absent, dead, shifted, and duplicate voters.
    *   Reported challenges in verifying voters who shifted out, and helping electors map their details or those of their relatives with entries in previous rolls.
*   **'Unbearable Burden' on Government School Teachers:**
    *   The extension was in line with a recent **Delhi High Court** direction to ensure that election-related work **does not place an unbearable burden on government school teachers** deployed for the exercise.
    *   *"Some teachers are doing the SIR work after their regular school duties. By the time they come to the field, they are already exhausted."*
*   **Revised Timeline:**
    *   Door-to-door verification by BLOs: Extended till **August 17**.
    *   Draft roll publication: **August 24**.
    *   Claims and objections filing: **24 September — 23 October**.
    *   Followed by a **month-long** notice period and disposal of claims.
    *   **Final electoral roll publication: October 27**.

## 2. Internal Security & Judiciary

### Supreme Court Directs RBI to Frame SOPs to Curb 'Digital Arrest' Scams
*   **Context:** The Supreme Court directed the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** to prepare and circulate within **four weeks** a **Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)** for banks for placing **temporary debit holds on mule accounts** linked to money laundering activity and cyber-enabled fraud.
*   **Key Directives by the SC:**
    *   A copy of the SOP shall be furnished to the **Registrars-General of all High Courts**.
    *   States and Union Territories directed to **frame modules for grievance redressal and money restoration** as per the Ministry of Home Affairs' SOP concerning the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal** and **Cyber RMS**.
    *   States asked to **notify Cyber Crime Coordination Centres and e-Zero FIRs within four weeks**.
*   **Fourth Status Report — Key Data:**
    *   A **data-sharing MoU** between the **Reserve Bank Innovation Hub** and **I4C (Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre)** was executed on **May 11, 2026**.
    *   The **grievance redressal mechanism portal** now covers **1,23,590 branches of 69 banks**.
    *   **Money Restoration Mechanism Portal:** 57 participating banks, covers all **36 States and UTs**; restoration completed in **36,290 cases** involving aggregate amount of **₹18.05 crore**.
    *   **e-Zero FIR mechanism** functional in **19 States**; only **14 States** had notified their State Cyber Crime Coordination Centres.
*   **Telecommunications Regulations Status:**
    *   **Telecommunications (Radio Equipment Possession Authorisation) Rules, 2025** — notified.
    *   **Telecommunications (User Identification) Rules, 2025** — at final stage of notification.
*   **CBI Investigation Findings:**
    *   Presently registered **10 digital arrest cases** and several connected cases.
    *   Identified **238 victims**, **67 first-layer bank accounts**.
    *   Transactions of approximately **₹80 crore**.
    *   Conducted searches at **93 locations across 16 States**.

**Related Constitutional Provisions:**
*   **[Article 21](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_21.md)** — Right to Life and Personal Liberty. Digital arrest scams violate citizens' right to live with dignity, free from fraud and coercion. SC's intervention protects this fundamental right.
*   **[Article 142](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_142.md)** — SC's power to pass any order necessary for doing "complete justice"; basis for directing RBI and States to frame SOPs.

*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-II (Judiciary):** SC's suo motu intervention; judicial activism in directing executive agencies (RBI, MHA) to act.
    *   **GS-III (Internal Security):** Cyber security infrastructure — I4C, National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, e-Zero FIRs; digital fraud typology (mule accounts, digital arrest scams).

## 3. International Relations

### NATO Enters a New Strategic Era of Transformation
*   **Context:** An analysis of NATO's evolving strategic posture, its adaptation to the changing global security landscape, and implications for India.
*   **NATO's Evolution Over Time:**
    *   **Founded:** April 4, 1949 (North Atlantic Treaty / Washington Treaty).
    *   **Original Purpose:** Collective defence against the Soviet Union during the Cold War (Article 5 — an attack on one is an attack on all).
    *   **Post-Cold War:** Expanded role — peacekeeping (Balkans), counter-terrorism (Afghanistan post-9/11), humanitarian interventions.
    *   **2010 Strategic Concept:** Adopted at Lisbon Summit — identified three core tasks: collective defence, crisis management, and cooperative security.
    *   **2022 Madrid Summit:** New Strategic Concept — identified **Russia as the most significant and direct threat**; first time acknowledged challenges posed by **China**.
*   **Current Transformation:**
    *   **Defence spending:** NATO members committed to spending **2% of GDP** on defence; many now pushing towards **2.5–3%**.
    *   **Military readiness:** 75,000-strong rapid-deployment force; NATO Response Force (NRF) enhanced.
    *   **Technology focus:** AI, cyber defence, space domain awareness.
    *   NATO's defence spending by 2029 target: **$400 billion** (up from $130 billion in 2006).
    *   **Sustaining support for Ukraine** in its conflict with Russia — NATO certified military assistance.
*   **America's Skin — US Role:**
    *   US remains NATO's **principal financier** and military guarantor.
    *   Questions about long-term US commitment amid domestic political shifts.
    *   US accounts for roughly **70%** of total NATO defence spending.
*   **New Delhi and the Rebalance — India-NATO Dynamics:**
    *   India is not a NATO member and maintains **strategic autonomy**.
    *   However, India has engaged with NATO through **partnership mechanisms** and dialogue on shared concerns (counter-terrorism, maritime security).
    *   India's concerns about NATO's **Indo-Pacific tilt** — potential to complicate India-China and India-Russia equations.
    *   Model for India: Can cooperate with NATO's **newer strategic agenda** (cyber, tech, counter-terrorism) without formal membership, using bilateral partnerships with individual NATO members (US, France, UK).
*   **NATO's Shift:**
    *   From a purely **Euro-Atlantic** defence alliance to one with **Indo-Pacific consciousness**.
    *   Invited **Australia, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand** (AP4 partners) to recent summits.

*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-II (International Relations):** NATO's evolution; India's strategic autonomy doctrine; Indo-Pacific geopolitics; India-US-Europe trilateral dynamics.
    *   **GS-II (Bilateral Relations):** India's engagement with NATO members bilaterally vs. alliance-level engagement.
    *   **GS-III (Security):** Collective security mechanisms; defence spending benchmarks; cyber and space as new domains of conflict.
