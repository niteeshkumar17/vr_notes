## Topic 1: PMLA Prosecution Post-Predicate Conviction & Double Jeopardy — Karnataka HC Ruling

> **Headlines:** *"PMLA case after conviction for predicate offence doesn't amount to second trial: HC"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II (Polity & Governance — Fundamental Rights: Article 20(2), Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002, Criminal Procedure Code Sec 300)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260811-01`

### 1. Context & Judicial Ruling
* The **Karnataka High Court** (single bench of **Justice M. Nagaprasanna**) held that prosecuting an individual under the **Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002** after conviction for a predicate/scheduled offence under the Indian Penal Code (IPC) or other penal laws does **NOT** amount to a second trial for the same offence.
* The ruling dismissed petitions filed by 11 individuals convicted in 2024 for a 2016 demonetisation note-exchange cheating scam (looting people under the guise of exchanging demonetised notes).

### 2. Core Legal Principles & Rationale

| Legal Parameter | Constitutional / Statutory Basis | High Court Analysis & Distinction |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Double Jeopardy** | **[Article 20(2)](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_20.md)** & **Section 300 CrPC** (Section 337 BNSS) | Bars trial and punishment for the **same offence** more than once (*Nemo debet bis vexari*). |
| **PMLA Offence Nature** | **Section 3, PMLA 2002** | Independent statutory offence deriving existence from laundering of **proceeds of crime**, though emanating from the commission of a predicate offence. |
| **Section 300 CrPC Scope** | **Criminal Procedure Code, 1973** | Extends **only** to a second trial for the exact same offence, **not** to prosecution for a distinct statutory offence under a separate enactment. |

### 3. Key Takeaways for UPSC
* **Distinct Statutory Offence:** The crime of money laundering (concealment, possession, acquisition, or use of proceeds of crime) is legally distinct from the underlying predicate crime (e.g., forgery, theft, cheating).
* **Constitutional Validity:** PMLA proceedings running concurrently or post-conviction of predicate offences do not violate Article 20(2) or fundamental rights.

### 3. Demographic & Educational Profile (Census Data)
* **Census 2011 Statistics:**
  * **~129 Million** total English speakers in India (1st, 2nd, or 3rd language).
  * **~260,000** individuals reported English as their mother tongue (83rd rank among Indian languages).
  * **46 Million** speak English as 2nd language; **83 Million** speak it as 3rd language.
* **Present Scale:** Estimated between **180 Million to 250 Million** speakers, driven by modern schooling.
* **School Boards:** ~31,000 schools affiliated with CBSE and ~3,000 with CISCE offering English-medium education.

### 4. Literary & Cultural Naturalization
* **Sahitya Akademi:** Formally recognized English as one of the official languages of Indian literature.
* **Literary Masters:** R.K. Narayan, Mulk Raj Anand, Raja Rao (*Kanthapura* 1938 foreword), A.K. Ramanujan, V.K. Gokak.

## Topic 3: Tribunals Reforms Bill 2026 — National Tribunals Commission (NTC) Architecture

> **Headlines:** *"Tribunals Reforms Bill passed amid din in LS"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II (Polity & Governance — Tribunals, Article 323A/323B, Separation of Powers, Judicial Independence)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260811-03`

### 1. Context & Legislative Background
* The Lok Sabha passed the **Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026** (introduced by Union Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal).
* The new legislation **repeals the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021**, which had faced critical Supreme Court invalidations regarding judicial independence and short tenures.

### 2. National Tribunals Commission (NTC) Structure

| Parameter | Provision under Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026 |
|:---|:---|
| **Headquarters** | New Delhi |
| **Primary Mandate** | Centralized body to oversee selection, appointment, qualifications, salaries, allowances, tenure, resignation, and removal of tribunal members. |
| **Composition** | **5 Members Total:** 1 Chairperson + 4 Members (2 Judicial Members + 2 Technical Members). |
| **Chairperson Eligibility** | Retired **Supreme Court Judge** OR Retired **Chief Justice of a High Court**. |
| **SC Directive Link** | Implements long-standing Supreme Court directives mandating an independent, professional umbrella commission for tribunal administration. |

### 3. Significance for Governance & Judicial Independence
* **Uniformity:** Replaces fragmented appointment mechanisms across different ministries with a single, transparent selection panel.
* **Separation of Powers:** Reduces executive dominance over tribunal appointments, aligning with constitutional principles.
