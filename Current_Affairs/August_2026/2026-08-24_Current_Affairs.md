## Topic 1: MMDR Amendment Act, 2026 — Mineral Regulation and Fiscal Federalism

> **Headline:** *"Digging for gold"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II (Federalism) & GS-III (Mineral Resources, Investment and Tribal Issues)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260824-01`

### What changed?
* Parliament passed the **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026** on August 13; it became an Act after presidential assent.
* The amendment extends Union regulation from the development of mines and minerals to **mineral-bearing land** and restricts States from imposing additional taxes, cesses or similar levies on mineral rights, mineral-bearing land and mining operations except under centrally prescribed conditions.
* It mainly covers **major minerals** such as coal, lignite, iron ore, copper, manganese, graphite, cobalt, lithium and nickel. States retain control over **49 minor minerals**.
* A lessee extracting one mineral may apply to include another mineral found within the same lease area, reducing the need for a separate licence.

### Competing arguments

| Union government's case | Concerns of mineral-rich States |
|:---|:---|
| Uniform and predictable fiscal regime | Erosion of States' taxation and land-related powers |
| Lower input costs and improved investor confidence | Revenue loss for Odisha, Jharkhand and other producing States |
| Higher domestic production and reduced imports | Benefits may accrue to consuming States and large firms while ecological costs remain local |
| Secure supplies of coal and critical minerals | Weakening of fiscal federalism and local accountability |

### Constitutional and social dimensions
* **Seventh Schedule:** Regulation of mines and mineral development falls under **Union List Entry 54** to the extent Parliament declares Union control expedient in the public interest. **State List Entry 50** covers taxes on mineral rights, subject to limitations imposed by Parliament by law relating to mineral development; **Entry 49** covers taxes on lands and buildings.
* **Judicial backdrop:** In ***Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL* (2024)**, a nine-judge Supreme Court Bench held that **royalty is not a tax** and recognised States' competence to tax mineral rights, subject to constitutional and parliamentary limitations. The 2026 amendment is therefore likely to invite scrutiny over the permissible extent of those limitations.
* **Distributional justice:** Mineral-bearing areas often overlap with Scheduled Areas and Adivasi habitats. Policy must account for **Gram Sabha consultation under PESA**, forest rights, rehabilitation, pollution, livelihood loss and inter-generational equity.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** Minerals regulation is not exclusively a State subject. Entry 54 of the Union List enables parliamentary control, while Entry 50 of the State List expressly makes State mineral-right taxation subject to limitations imposed by Parliament.</span>

## Topic 2: AI Tutor as Digital Public Infrastructure for Education

> **Headline:** *"Not 'delulu' — an AI tutor for every child in India"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-II (Education and Governance) & GS-III (AI, Digital Public Infrastructure)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260824-02`

### Why is it relevant?
* India's test-preparation market was estimated at **$14.8 billion in FY26** and is projected at **$23–26 billion by FY30**. About **27%** of students had taken private coaching; an estimated **17–20 million** students in Classes 9–12 participate in test preparation.
* A single vendor cannot serve India's scale. The proposal is a **"public rails, private engines"** model: government provides interoperable infrastructure and trust, while competing teachers and firms provide content, mentoring and applications.

### Proposed open education network

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Public rails<br/>Aadhaar/DigiLocker · APAAR · DIKSHA"] --> B["Open content registry<br/>Common syllabus taxonomy & metadata"]
    B --> C["Private engines<br/>Teachers · Ed-tech firms · Local tutors"]
    C --> D["AI layer<br/>Diagnosis · Adaptive practice · Doubt solving"]
    D --> E["Student-owned learning wallet<br/>Portable progress ledger"]
    E --> F["Open ratings & verified outcomes<br/>No provider lock-in"]
```

* Begin with **JEE and NEET**, which are high-volume, objective and machine-gradable.
* Use open APIs so DIKSHA, State apps and private interfaces can access the same catalogue.
* Make progress portable across providers; consent-gate student data through a **DEPA/Account Aggregator-like** architecture.
* Permit optional UPI-linked premium mentoring or voucher-funded services while keeping core learning largely free.
* Ensure inclusion through data-light streaming, downloads, school computer labs and over five lakh **Common Service Centres**.

### Governance safeguards
* Multilingual, curriculum-aligned and accessible content; independent accreditation and transparent outcome audits.
* Data minimisation, purpose limitation, child-appropriate consent, algorithmic audits and grievance redress under the **Digital Personal Data Protection framework**.
* AI should assist, not replace, teachers; risks include hallucination, bias, surveillance, screen dependence and unequal device/connectivity access.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Core takeaway:** The State should be the protocol-setter, market-maker and guarantor of equity—not the monopoly tutor or content producer.</span>

## Topic 3: A Child-Safety and Civilisational Approach to Social Media

> **Headline:** *"A civilisational approach to social media"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-I (Society) & GS-II (Child Rights, Governance and Regulation)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260824-03`

### Central argument
* Social media exposes minors to addiction, manipulation, cyberbullying and harmful content, but a blanket ban may be circumvented through VPNs, encrypted applications and alternative platforms.
* As AI systems increasingly educate, mentor, entertain and potentially manipulate children, the deeper challenge is to develop **judgment, resilience and responsible digital citizenship**, not merely erect a larger firewall.

### A balanced framework
1. **Government:** age-appropriate design standards, platform transparency, privacy protection, swift action against criminal harm and effective grievance redress.
2. **Platforms:** safety by design, risk assessments, limits on manipulative recommendation systems and meaningful parental controls.
3. **Families:** digital literacy, active engagement and age-sensitive boundaries rather than passive surveillance.
4. **Schools:** critical thinking, media literacy, ethical reasoning, cyber-hygiene and emotional resilience.
5. **Community institutions:** adapt the character-building and mentorship ethic of the **guru-shishya** tradition to modern digital life.

### Critical evaluation
* Civilisational values can support responsibility and social trust, but they cannot substitute for enforceable rights, evidence-based regulation or children's agency.
* The sound approach combines **proportionate safeguards + platform accountability + education + family/community support**.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Mains value-add:** Frame online child safety through the best-interests principle of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child—balancing protection, privacy, participation and access to information.</span>

## Topic 4: India as a Global Shipbuilding Hub

> **Headline:** *"India can become global shipbuilding hub: Rajnath"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-III (Infrastructure, Defence Indigenisation, Manufacturing and Blue Economy)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260824-04`

### Current development
* The Defence Minister virtually presided over the foundation ceremony for **three GRSE** expansion projects and **two Yantra India Ltd.** projects, involving about **₹3,500 crore**.
* GRSE projects at Kidderpore, Shalimar and Raichak will expand shipbuilding, repair and refit capacity. A **32-acre Raichak hub** entails investment exceeding **₹2,500 crore**.
* YIL's Ishapore projects, costing over **₹750 crore**, include a **3,000-tonne hydraulic open-die forging press** and a radial forging plant for gun barrels, ammunition and advanced weapon, railway and nuclear components.
* GRSE has delivered more than 100 vessels and is constructing a cargo ship for Germany, signalling export potential.

### Strategic significance
* **Strategic autonomy:** indigenous warships, repair capacity and metallurgy reduce external dependence during conflict.
* **Economic multiplier:** shipbuilding generates long-cycle demand for steel, electronics, engines, design, ports and MSMEs.
* **Global opening:** ageing shipyards and capacity constraints in traditional shipbuilding countries offer India an opportunity to diversify global supply chains.
* **Policy support:** a **₹69,725-crore maritime package** approved in 2025 aims to improve financing, shipyard capacity, clusters, skills and legal/tax frameworks.

### Challenges and way forward
* High capital cost and expensive long-term finance; delayed delivery and limited commercial scale.
* Dependence on imported marine engines and specialised components; need for design capability, skilled workers and R&D.
* Improve quality assurance, standardised procurement, green-vessel capability, assured orders and lifecycle maintenance.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims link:** GRSE is a Defence Public Sector Undertaking under the Ministry of Defence; shipbuilding also advances Sagarmala, Maritime India Vision 2030 and Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047.</span>

## Topic 5: Kaziranga — Mining, Watersheds and Wildlife Corridors

> **Headline:** *"SC committee pulls up Assam government for inaction over mining activity near Kaziranga"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-III (Environment, Protected Areas, Wildlife Corridors and Environmental Governance)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260824-05`

### What did the Central Empowered Committee find?
* The Supreme Court-constituted **Central Empowered Committee (CEC)** criticised inaction against mining around **Parkup Pahar Range**, a wildlife sanctuary and ecological corridor on Kaziranga's southern boundary.
* A **Supreme Court order of April 12, 2019** restrained mining and related activities along Kaziranga's southern boundary and in catchments of rivers and streams flowing from the Karbi Anglong hills into the park. It also prohibited new construction on private land within **nine identified animal corridors**.
* The State had not completed the comprehensive **Watershed Drainage Analysis Report**, fully implemented the CEC's May 2025 recommendations or notified the nine corridors.
* The CEC sought immediate implementation, corridor notification and communication of the Supreme Court order to all Assam courts.

### Why landscape-level conservation matters
* During annual floods, animals move from the Brahmaputra floodplain to the higher **Karbi Anglong hills**; roads, construction and mining can sever this refuge route.
* Quarrying and riverbed mining alter drainage, increase sediment and pollution, fragment habitats and aggravate human-elephant conflict.
* Protecting only the notified park core is insufficient: **catchments, corridors, elephant reserves and eco-sensitive zones form one ecological unit**.

### Static facts
* Kaziranga is a **National Park, Tiger Reserve and UNESCO World Heritage Site**, globally known for the greater one-horned rhinoceros.
* The **CEC** is an expert body constituted by the Supreme Court in the *T.N. Godavarman* forest litigation to monitor compliance with forest and wildlife orders.
* Eco-Sensitive Zones act as transition or "shock-absorber" areas around protected areas; their width can be site-specific. Commercial mining remains a prohibited high-impact activity around protected areas.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** An animal corridor need not itself be a National Park or Sanctuary. Its legal protection may flow from court orders, ESZ notifications, forest/wildlife laws, land-use regulation and site-specific corridor notification.</span>

## Topic 6: *MV Ocean Winner* Sinking — Maritime Search and Rescue

> **Headline:** *"Search continues for 22 from vessel that sunk off Odisha"*  
> **GS Paper:** GS-III (Disaster Management, Maritime Security and Transport Safety)  
> **Micro-Topic ID:** `CA-260824-06`

### Incident
* The Panama-flagged bulk carrier ***MV Ocean Winner***, carrying iron ore from Paradip, sank in the Bay of Bengal after sailing on August 20.
* It carried 24 crew members—**20 Chinese, three Myanmar nationals and one Bangladeshi**. The merchant vessel ***MT Aisopos*** rescued two Chinese crew members; the search continued for 22.
* The Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre at **Sri Vijaya Puram** coordinated the response. The Indian Navy redeployed a **P-8I maritime reconnaissance aircraft**, which detected dispersed liferafts and an oil slick, while Navy and Coast Guard assets continued the search.
* The 28-year-old, Panama-flagged ship was built in 1998, had changed ownership repeatedly and had reported inspection deficiencies, including one involving its magnetic compass.

### UPSC relevance
* Under the **International Convention on Maritime Search and Rescue, 1979**, coastal States organise coordinated search-and-rescue services through rescue coordination centres.
* The **Indian Coast Guard** is the national maritime search-and-rescue coordinating authority in the Indian Search and Rescue Region; the Navy, merchant vessels, ports and coastal authorities provide operational support.
* The case highlights risks from ageing vessels, weak flag-State enforcement and the **"flags of convenience"** model, where ship ownership, registration, crewing and operation span several jurisdictions.
* Prevention requires robust port-State control, seaworthiness checks, functioning distress and navigation systems, crew training, insurance and rapid merchant-vessel cooperation.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims link:** Search and rescue is distinct from marine-pollution response, though a sinking may trigger both. The Coast Guard coordinates maritime SAR; oil-spill response is governed through a separate national contingency architecture.</span>

## High-Yield UPSC Practice Questions

### Prelims MCQ 1
With reference to the constitutional distribution of powers over minerals, consider the following statements:
1. Regulation of mines and mineral development can come under Union control through a parliamentary declaration in the public interest.
2. State taxation of mineral rights is subject to limitations imposed by Parliament by law relating to mineral development.
3. Royalty paid by a mining lessee is constitutionally identical to a tax.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

> **Answer:** **(a)**  
> **Explanation:** Statements 1 and 2 reflect Union List Entry 54 and State List Entry 50. In *Mineral Area Development Authority* (2024), the Supreme Court held that royalty is not a tax.

### Prelims MCQ 2
Consider the following pairs:
1. Central Empowered Committee — monitoring compliance with Supreme Court forest and wildlife orders
2. Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre — coordination of maritime search-and-rescue operations
3. Eco-Sensitive Zone — compulsory uniform ten-kilometre exclusion zone around every protected area

How many pairs given above are correctly matched?
- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

> **Answer:** **(b)**  
> **Explanation:** Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. ESZs are site-specific and are not a compulsory uniform ten-kilometre exclusion zone.

### Mains Question (GS-II/GS-III)
> **Q. "Digital and natural-resource infrastructure both raise the same governance question: who sets the rules, who captures the gains, and who bears the risks?" Discuss with reference to an open AI-education network and the MMDR Amendment Act, 2026. (15 marks, 250 words)**

**Answer framework:** introduce the public-interest role of rule-setting; compare interoperability and educational equity with mineral regulation and supply security; examine data federalism, fiscal federalism, private concentration, tribal rights and environmental externalities; conclude with transparent standards, meaningful consultation, independent regulation, portability/competition and fair benefit-sharing.
