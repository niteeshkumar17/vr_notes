## 2. Strong Health Systems for All with Better Public Spending (Editorial)

**Context:** An editorial by **Nahil Golafi** and **Vasa Thiru** (former WHO officials) argues for building robust public health systems through increased and better-directed public financing, especially in Low- and Middle-Income Countries (LMICs).

**Key Arguments & Data Points:**

### Health Aid Under Pressure
*   Global development assistance for health reached a peak during the **COVID-19 pandemic** but has since **plateaued and declined**.
*   The trend of donor aid is **heterogeneous** — some countries contributed more, while others reduced aid.
*   Between **LMICs and high-income countries (HICs)**, the health spending gap remains enormous — per capita spending on health can be **100× lower** in LMICs.

### Developing Countries' Debt Crisis
*   In 2024, the **public debt of developing countries** has risen to approximately **$29 trillion** — **twice** as fast as that of advanced countries, according to **UNCTAD (United Nations Conference on Trade and Development)**.
*   Developing countries paid **$823 billion** in net interest payments in 2024.
*   This debt burden is **crowding out** essential public health and social sector spending.

### The Right Health Investments
*   The editorial argues that there is **chronic underspending** in public health care globally.
*   Key recommendation: Increase **public financing of health** to enhance service delivery, governance, and accountability.
*   Countries that invest in **primary healthcare systems** show better health outcomes and greater resilience to pandemics.

**UPSC Significance:**
*   **GS-II (Health / Governance):** India's **National Health Policy 2017** targets raising public health expenditure to **2.5% of GDP** (currently ~2.1% as of 2024-25).
*   **GS-III (Economy):** Debt distress in developing countries and its impact on social sector allocation — links to India's fiscal policy and **FRBM Act** considerations.
*   Key organizations: **WHO**, **UNCTAD**, **World Bank**.

## 3. Drugs-Free Youth Crucial for Developed India: PM Modi

**Context:** Prime Minister Narendra Modi launched a nationwide campaign — **"Nasha Mukt Yun for Viksit Bharat Sankalp Abhiyan"** (Drug-Free Youth for Developed India Pledge Campaign) — calling for physically and mentally fit young Indians as the foundation of 'Viksit Bharat' (Developed India by 2047).

**Key Details:**
*   **Over 1 crore participants** joined the pledge against substance abuse.
*   Supported by **125+ spiritual and public organisations**, educational institutions, and industrial associations.
*   Conducted at **28,000+ locations** across the country, with participation from several Chief Ministers and Governors.
*   The pledge will continue over the **next 100 weeks**, with weekly activities related to art, culture, sports, meditation, spirituality, and service.

**PM's Key Statements:**
*   *"Over the next 20 to 25 years, you will shape your own lives, and by 2047, it is you who will lead India to the goal of becoming a developed country."*
*   *"When a young person falls into drug addiction, it is not only that individual who loses. A dream dies before it can be fulfilled. An entire family suffers and often falls apart."*
*   *"Hostile countries also conspire to lure our youth into drug addiction. Profit from drug trafficking while weakening our country is part of their malicious agenda."*
*   Urged people not to hide cases of addiction and to seek professional help. Rehabilitation centres have been established to aid recovery.
*   Government taking **stringent action against drug traffickers** — arrests have increased significantly compared to previous years.

**UPSC Significance:**
*   **GS-II (Social Issues / Governance):** Drug abuse as a social challenge; government's multi-pronged approach (awareness, rehabilitation, enforcement).
*   **GS-IV (Ethics):** Societal responsibility toward addiction — *"Someone who defeats addiction is a true warrior. Society should respect them and give them a second chance."*
*   Relevant Schemes: **NMBA (Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyan)** launched in 2020 across 272 most-affected districts.
*   **NDPS Act, 1985 (Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act)** — India's primary anti-drug legislation.

## 4. Lepidoptera Hotspot: India Is Home to 8.25% of the World's Butterflies & Moths

**Context:** A landmark scientific publication — **"Catalogue of Lepidoptera of India"** — published in the peer-reviewed journal **Zootaxa**, documents India's extraordinary lepidopteran (butterfly and moth) diversity.

**Key Data Points:**
*   India hosts **13,703 species** of Lepidoptera:
    *   **12,286 are moths**
    *   **1,417 are butterflies**
*   This represents **8.25% of the world's total** lepidopteran species.
*   Classified into **240 subfamilies, 102 families, 2,048 genera**.
*   The catalogue was compiled by scientists **Navneet Singh** and **Rahul Joshi** over **12 years** of research.
*   India has **31 superfamilies** of butterflies and moths.

**Ecological Significance:**
*   Butterflies and moths serve as **ecological indicators** — their populations reflect ecosystem health.
*   They play a **crucial role as pollinators** and serve as a vital part of the food chain.
*   They are also important as **natural assets for biodiversity science** — helping researchers track environmental degradation.
*   These insects are among the most **ancient fauna** — predating even trees, serving as essential herbivores.

**UPSC Significance:**
*   **GS-III (Environment & Biodiversity):** India as a **mega-biodiversity hotspot** — links to Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD), Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF, 2022).
*   India has **4 of the world's 36 biodiversity hotspots**: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, and Sundaland.
*   **National Mission on Biodiversity and Human Well-Being** — proposed under the Prime Minister's Science, Technology and Innovation Advisory Council (PM-STIAC).
*   Butterflies as **bio-indicators** — frequently asked in Prelims context (e.g., indicator species, keystone species, flagship species).
