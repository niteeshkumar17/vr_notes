### Lok Sabha Passes Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026
*   **Context:** The Lok Sabha passed a bill to **increase the strength of judges in the Supreme Court from 34 to 38** (including the Chief Justice of India). The bill was passed **without any discussion** as the House was adjourned repeatedly due to Opposition protests.
*   **Background:**
    *   The Supreme Court's sanctioned strength was last increased in 2019 from 31 to 34.
    *   Rising pendency of cases (over 80,000 cases pending in SC) necessitates additional judges.
    *   The bill amends the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956**.
*   **Other Bills Passed:**
    *   **Indian Statistical Institute Bill** — restructuring and modernizing the Indian Statistical Institute.
    *   **Bankers' Books Evidence (Amendment) Bill** — updating evidence rules for digital banking records.
*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-II (Polity — Judiciary):** Appointment of SC judges under [Article 124](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_124.md); Collegium system; sanctioned vs. working strength.
    *   Repeated adjournments without debate raise concerns about **Parliamentary productivity** and the role of the Opposition.

**Related Constitutional Provisions:**
*   **[Article 124](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_124.md)** — Establishment and constitution of the Supreme Court; appointment of judges by the President after consultation with CJI and other judges.
*   **[Article 142](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_142.md)** — SC's power to do complete justice.

### Rajya Sabha Passes MSME Bill Amid Opposition Protests
*   **Context:** The Rajya Sabha passed a bill aimed at addressing **liquidity concerns and delayed payments** to Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs).
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   Seeks to address the longstanding issue of delayed payments from large companies and government bodies to MSMEs.
    *   Aims to strengthen the credit ecosystem for MSMEs through institutional support.
    *   Mandates timely payments and creates mechanisms for dispute resolution.
*   **Background:**
    *   MSMEs contribute ~30% to India's GDP and employ over 11 crore people.
    *   Delayed payments remain the single biggest challenge — estimated ₹10.7 lakh crore outstanding.
    *   MSME sector classified under **3 tiers** based on investment and turnover criteria (revised in 2020).
*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-III (Economy):** MSME sector's role in employment, GDP contribution, and export competitiveness.
    *   **GS-II (Governance):** Legislative process — passage amid disruptions raises democratic accountability questions.

### Tamil Nadu Urges SC to Enforce Cauvery Water Order
*   **Context:** Tamil Nadu approached the Supreme Court urging enforcement of the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal's order regarding water-sharing with Karnataka.
*   **Background:**
    *   The Cauvery dispute involves 4 states: Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Puducherry (UT).
    *   The **Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal (CWDT)** gave its final award in 2007.
    *   The SC modified the tribunal's award in 2018, allocating 177.75 TMC to Tamil Nadu.
    *   The **Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA)** was constituted in 2018 to implement the SC order.
*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-II (Polity — Centre-State / Inter-State Relations):** [Article 262](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_262.md) — Adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-State rivers; Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956.
    *   **GS-I (Geography):** Cauvery river basin — originates at Talakaveri (Coorg, Karnataka), flows through Karnataka and Tamil Nadu into Bay of Bengal.

**Related Constitutional Provisions:**
*   **[Article 262](file:///c:/Users/nitee/OneDrive/Desktop/UPSE_Syllabus/Articles/Article_262.md)** — Parliament may provide for adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-State rivers or river valleys.

## 2. Education & Governance

### The Dilemma Over PM SHRI Schools in Kerala (Editorial — The Hindu)
*   **Context:** The newly elected Congress-led **United Democratic Front (UDF)** government in Kerala is caught in a dilemma over the **PM SHRI (PM Schools for Rising India)** scheme — balancing the need for central funds against maintaining educational autonomy and opposing the **National Education Policy (NEP) 2020**.
*   **What is PM SHRI Scheme?**
    *   Launched in 2022 to upgrade and develop existing government schools as **model schools** showcasing the implementation of NEP 2020.
    *   Requires states to sign an **MoU with the Union government** committing to implement the NEP framework.
    *   Over 14,500 schools selected across India; central funding of ₹1,58,813 crore earmarked.
*   **Kerala's Dilemma:**
    *   The previous **LDF (Left) government** had opted out of PM SHRI in 2022, arguing it would impose NEP's curriculum framework on the state.
    *   The new UDF government initially signalled willingness to implement PM SHRI, but faces internal resistance from coalition partners and Muslim organizations (IUML) who oppose NEP.
    *   **Wavering Stance:** Chief Minister V.D. Satheesan argued Kerala was forced to comply since the previous LDF government had already entered into an agreement; the Opposition vehemently denies this.
    *   Union Minister of State for Education Jayant Chaudhary said: *"States that do not sign the MoU or withdraw from the scheme stand to miss out on the benefits of PM SHRI scheme."*
*   **Pros & Cons:**
    *   **Pro:** Access to substantial central education funding; school infrastructure upgradation.
    *   **Con:** Loss of curricular autonomy; NEP's National Curriculum Framework may be imposed.
    *   **Punjab precedent:** Opted out in 2022, later reversed after Union government froze education funds.
    *   **Tamil Nadu route:** Wrote to Union government seeking freedom to decide its own curriculum within the scheme.
*   **Key Issue — Fiscal Federalism:**
    *   States that don't implement PM SHRI risk losing crucial **central education funding** — creating a *"damned if they do, damned if they don't"* situation.
    *   Raises questions about **conditional central funding** as a tool for policy imposition on states.
*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-II (Education + Centre-State Relations):** NEP 2020 implementation challenges; Education is on the **Concurrent List** (Entry 25, Seventh Schedule) — both Centre and States can legislate.
    *   **GS-II (Fiscal Federalism):** Conditional vs. unconditional central transfers; states' autonomy in education policy.

## 3. Economy & Strategic Affairs

### Critical Minerals — The Foundation of Strategic Power (Op-Ed — The Hindu)
*   **Context:** An op-ed by Vinayak Vipul (EY-Parthenon India) highlighting why **critical minerals** are the new geopolitical battleground and India's urgent need to secure supply chains.
*   **What are Critical Minerals?**
    *   Minerals essential for **clean energy transition, EVs, semiconductors, defence, and advanced manufacturing**.
    *   Key minerals: **Lithium, Cobalt, Nickel, Graphite, Copper, Rare Earth Elements (REEs), Gallium, Germanium**.
*   **Why Critical Now?**
    *   Global shift to clean energy (solar, wind, EVs, battery storage) is **mineral-intensive** — an EV requires 6x more minerals than a conventional car.
    *   **Demand projection:** IEA estimates demand for critical minerals could increase **4–6x by 2040**.
*   **Geopolitical Risk — China's Dominance:**
    *   China controls ~**60% of global mining** and ~**90% of processing** for rare earth elements.
    *   China is the leading refiner in 19 out of 20 strategic minerals.
    *   Average market share: ~86% for rare earths, ~70% for cobalt processing, ~65% for lithium processing.
    *   The EU, US, and Australia are building **parallel supply chains** through partnerships (e.g., Minerals Security Partnership).
*   **India's Position:**
    *   India depends on **imports for over 90%** of its critical mineral needs.
    *   **KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Limited)** — Joint venture (NALCO + HCL + MECL) for overseas mineral acquisition.
    *   India's **Critical Mineral Strategy** identifies 30 critical minerals (updated 2023 list).
    *   Domestic potential: India has significant reserves of **rare earths** (beach sands of Kerala, Odisha) and **lithium** (Jammu & Kashmir — Reasi discovery, 2023).
*   **Structural Constraints:**
    *   India's mineral processing capacity is limited — mostly exports raw ore.
    *   No domestic graphite processing; nearly **75% of critical mineral imports** come from China.
    *   Recycling and urban mining are underdeveloped.
*   **Mission to Execution:**
    *   Phased approach: Explore → Mine → Process → Manufacture.
    *   Need: Scale up domestic exploration, build processing infrastructure, diversify sources through bilateral deals (Australia, Chile, Argentina, Africa), promote recycling.
*   **UPSC Relevance:**
    *   **GS-III (Economy — Minerals & Mining):** Critical Mineral Strategy, KABIL, Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act amendments.
    *   **GS-II (IR):** Supply chain diversification, Minerals Security Partnership, India's engagement with resource-rich nations.
    *   **GS-III (Security):** Defence applications — rare earths in fighter jets, submarines, missile systems.
