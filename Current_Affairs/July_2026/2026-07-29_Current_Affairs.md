## 1. Release All Minors Held Over Student Protests: SC

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Social Justice & Child Rights]` · `[GS-2: Criminal Justice System & Judiciary]`

### Juvenile Justice (Care & Protection of Children) Act, 2015 (JJ Act)
- The law prefers the term **"apprehended"** rather than **"arrested"** to remove the stigma of criminality from children.

### Age of Criminal Responsibility in India
The Indian Penal Code (IPC) sets strict age limits on who can be held legally responsible for their actions:

| Age Bracket | Legal Standing / Responsibility |
|-------------|---------------------------------|
| **Under 7 years old** | **Absolute immunity.** A child under 7 cannot be charged with any criminal offence, regardless of the act. |
| **7 to 12 years old** | **Conditional responsibility.** A child in this age group can only be held responsible if the court determines they had attained "sufficient maturity of understanding" to judge the nature & consequences of their conduct. |
| **12 to 18 years old** | **Juvenile processing.** Minors in this group are considered to lack full criminal capacity and are processed through the juvenile justice system rather than regular courts. |

### How Apprehensions Are Handled
When a minor is suspected of a crime, standard police procedures are heavily modified to protect the child:
- **Specialized Officers:** Minors should ideally be apprehended by a **Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU)** or a designated child welfare police officer.
- **No Adult Jails/Lock-ups:** A minor can **never** be kept in a regular police lock-up or adult prison.
- **Bail is the Rule:** For minors, bail is almost always granted (even for typically non-bailable offences). The **Juvenile Justice Board (JJB)** will only deny bail if releasing the minor would expose them to moral, physical, or psychological danger, or bring them into contact with known criminals.

### "Tried as an Adult" Exception
The JJ Act divides offences into three categories:
- **Petty:** Up to 3 years imprisonment.
- **Serious:** 3 to 7 years imprisonment.
- **Heinous:** 7 or more years (e.g., murder or rape).

For older minors accused of severe crimes:
- **Age 16 to 18:** If a minor is between 16 and 18 years old and is accused of a **Heinous** offence, the JJB will conduct a preliminary assessment of their physical & mental capacity to commit the crime.
- **Children's Court:** Based on this assessment, the Children's Court may decide that the minor should be tried as an adult.
- **No Capital Punishment:** Even if tried as an adult, a minor can **never** be sentenced to the death penalty or life imprisonment without the possibility of release (Section 21, JJ Act).

### Mains Utility / Way Forward (Juvenile Justice Governance)
1. **Sensitization of Police Forces:** Standardize training for Special Juvenile Police Units (SJPU) to prevent unlawful detention of student protesters.
2. **Community-Based Rehabilitation:** Prioritize non-custodial measures, counseling, and community service for juvenile agitators instead of institutionalization.

## 2. Centre Declares 56,825 km of Western Ghats as Eco-Sensitive Area (ESA)

> **Tags:** `[GS-3: Environment & Ecology — Conservation & Biodiversity]` · `[GS-2: Federalism & Governance]`

- **Western Ghats Coverage:** The Western Ghats span across six states: **Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu**.
- **7th Draft Notification:** The Centre has issued its 7th draft notification to designate this area as an Ecologically Sensitive Area (ESA).
- **Deadlock:** The 6th draft notification lapsed due to a continuing deadlock between the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) and state governments.
- **Extension:** The Centre has given a one-year extension to the expert committee (formed in April 2022) to continue examining state governments' feedback regarding their developmental aspirations.

### Historical Background: Key Committees on Western Ghats

| Committee | Year | Key Recommendation / Model | Result / Status |
|-----------|------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| **Madhav Gadgil Committee** *(WGEEP)* | **2011** | Recommended **75% of Western Ghats** as ESA. Proposed strict 3-tier eco-zoning (ESZ-1, ESZ-2, ESZ-3) & complete ban on mining/large dams. | **Rejected** by all 6 state governments as overly rigid and anti-development. |
| **K. Kasturirangan Committee** *(HLWG)* | **2013** | Scaled down ESA to **~37% of Western Ghats** (**56,825 sq km**). Proposed ban on mining, quarrying, thermal plants, and red-category industries. | **Basis of current draft notifications**; states still negotiating boundaries. |

### Map & Ecological Context: Western Ghats

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Extent** | **~1,600 km** length from **Tapti River valley (Gujarat)** to **Kanyakumari (Tamil Nadu)**. |
| **Status** | UNESCO World Heritage Site; one of the 8 *"Hottest Biodiversity Hotspots"* in the world. |
| **Highest Peak** | **Anamudi** (2,695 m, Idukki, Kerala) — highest peak in Peninsular India. Second highest: **Doddabetta** (2,637 m, Nilgiris). |
| **Passes (Ghats)** | **Thal Ghat** (Mumbai–Nashik), **Bhor Ghat** (Mumbai–Pune), **Pal Ghat** (Palakkad Gap between Nilgiris and Anaimalai), **Shencottah Gap**. |

### Constitutional Provisions for Environmental Protection

| Provision | Type | Description |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| **Article 48A** | Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) | Directs the State to endeavor to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country. |
| **Article 51A(g)** | Fundamental Duties | Mandates that it is the duty of every citizen of India to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures. |
| **Article 21** | Fundamental Rights (Right to Life) | The Supreme Court of India has interpreted the Right to Life to encompass the right to a clean, pollution-free, and healthy environment. |

### Mains Utility / Way Forward (Sustainable Ecology vs. Development)
1. **Participatory Conservation:** Involve Gram Sabhas under FRA 2006 and PESA 1996 in demarcating ESA boundaries to address local livelihoods.
2. **Payment for Ecosystem Services (PES):** Provide financial compensation to local communities and states for preserving forest cover rather than enforcing blanket economic bans.

## 3. India Among the Powers: Forever Rising, Never Risen?

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: International Relations — Global Geopolitics & Strategic Autonomy]`

- **Middle Power Collaboration:** Canadian PM called on the world's "middle powers" to collaborate amid growing turbulence in the international order.
- **Almost Great Power:** In his book *The Return of the Great Powers*, Cambridge historian Brendan Simms categorizes India as an **"almost great power"** alongside France, Germany, and Japan.
- **Great Power Dynamics:** Simms emphasizes that international politics has historically been driven by interactions among major great powers.

### What Defines a Great Power?
Simms offers an unapologetically **material** definition. A great power must possess:
1. **Industrial strength** (manufacturing output & economic capacity)
2. **Technological leadership** (cutting-edge innovation & R&D)
3. **Military capability** (including nuclear deterrence)
4. **Power projection** (ability to project force beyond home borders)
5. **Geopolitical influence** (ability to influence war and peace across multiple theatres)

### Mains Utility / Way Forward (India's Great Power Ambitions)
1. **Economic Engine:** Accelerate domestic manufacturing (*Make in India*, PLI schemes) to convert demographic dividend into material economic power.
2. **Defense Indigenization:** Reduce import dependence by boosting domestic defense R&D (DRDO, private sector defense manufacturing).
3. **Multi-Alignment Strategy:** Maintain strategic autonomy while leveraging partnerships in QUAD, BRICS, SCO, and G20 to shape global rules.
