## 1. Govt. Launches Sovereign-Backed P&I Cover for Mariners (Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool)

> **Tags:** `[GS-3: Infrastructure (Ports, Shipping & Waterways)]` · `[GS-3: Financial Sector & Insurance Reforms]`

### Core News & Key Details
- The **Department of Financial Services (DFS)**, Ministry of Finance, launched India's first sovereign-backed **Protection and Indemnity (P&I)** insurance product under the **Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool (BMIP)**.
- **Designed By:** **The New India Assurance Company Ltd.**
- **First Recipient:** First policy document handed over to the **Shipping Corporation of India Ltd. (SCI)**.

### Financial & Operational Capacity

| Feature | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Combined Capacity** | Up to **$1.5 Billion** |
| **Sovereign Guarantee** | Backed by Government of India sovereign guarantee to ensure uninterrupted war risk and indemnity capacity |
| **Coverage Scope** | Third-party liabilities: Crew liability, Cargo liability, Pollution liability, Wreck removal, and 24x7 Port Correspondent Network |
| **Operational Trigger** | Operationalized on May 12, 2026 to cushion Indian shipping lines from international geopolitical disruptions and war-risk premiums |

### Strategic & Economic Significance
1. **Overcoming Global P&I Monopoly:** Globally, over 90% of ocean-going tonnage is insured by the International Group of P&I Clubs (IG P&I), predominantly based in the UK/EU. Sovereign-backed BMIP reduces India's critical dependence on foreign P&I clubs.
2. **Sanction & War-Risk Insulation:** Protects national energy and trade supply chains (e.g., Red Sea conflict, Black Sea disruptions) against sudden unilateral withdrawal of cover by foreign underwriters.
3. **Foreign Exchange Retention:** Retains premium capital within the domestic financial ecosystem (GIC Re and domestic general insurers).

## 2. Artificial Intelligence as the 4th Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) Pillar

> **Tags:** `[GS-3: Science & Technology - Artificial Intelligence & Digital Infrastructure]` · `[GS-2: Governance & E-Governance]`

### Core Concept: The Next DPI Layer
- India successfully commoditised Identity (**Aadhaar**), Payments (**UPI**), and Data Governance (**DEPA / Account Aggregator**).
- **Proposal:** Treat **Artificial Intelligence (Intelligence Layer)** as India's **4th foundational Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)** to prevent global AI trade asymmetry (where India exports raw data/talent and imports expensive proprietary models).

### The 3 Pillars of India's AI Token Economy

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   INDIA'S AI DIGITAL PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE               │
├─────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│    1. RAW COMPUTE       │  2. FOUNDATION MODELS │ 3. STANDARDISED API   │
│   (Hardware/Grid)       │   & OPEN DATASETS     │    INTERFACE (UI)     │
└─────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

#### Detailed Breakdown of the 3 Pillars

| Pillar | Focus Area | Key Implementation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Raw Compute** | Hardware & Energy Grid | • **IndiaAI Mission** (Outlay: ₹10,372 Cr): Public-Private Partnership model allocating 38,000 GPUs (scaling to 1,000,000).<br>• Subsidised compute access at **₹65/GPU hour** for researchers/startups.<br>• Treating AI datacenter load as a dedicated category in National Electricity Grid planning. |
| **2. Foundation Models** | Anonymized Datasets & Open-Weights | • State aggregates anonymized public datasets across 22 official languages.<br>• State-subsidized compute models must adopt an **open-weights license** (mirroring UPI open rail philosophy). |
| **3. Standardised API Layer** | Interoperable Intelligence Layer | • Standardized interface allowing any app (healthcare, agriculture, education) to call LLMs/translation/vision APIs for fractions of a cent. |

### Governance Model: The "Freemium" Token Rail
- **Citizen Tier:** Aadhaar-linked digital identity receives monthly free API tokens (e.g., powering 300 million personalized local-dialect student AI tutors or rural crop claim resolution via voice).
- **Enterprise Tier:** Commercial enterprises pay standard market rates, cross-subsidizing public-good access.

## 3. The Bay of Bengal as India's SHANTI Anchor

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: International Relations - India's Neighbourhood & Regional Groupings (BIMSTEC, SAGAR)]` · `[GS-3: Disaster Management & Maritime Security]`

### Evolution of India's Maritime Security Vision

```
  SAGAR (2015) ────────► MAHASAGAR (2025) ────────► SHANTI (2026)
  (Indian Ocean)        (Wider Indo-Pacific)       (Normative Governance)
```

- **SAGAR (2015):** *Security and Growth for All in the Region* — Initial Indian Ocean littoral focus.
- **MAHASAGAR (2025):** *Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions* — Extended scope across Indo-Pacific & Global South.
- **SHANTI (2026):** **Securing Holistic Advancement through Norms, Trust and Integrity** — Introduced by EAM S. Jaishankar during India's UNSC candidacy launch (2028–29). Shift from power rivalry to rules-based governance, shared responsibility, and disaster resilience.

### Strategic Importance of the Bay of Bengal

| Dimension | Analysis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Geopolitical Pivot** | Bridges South Asia and Southeast Asia (ASEAN); key to **Act East Policy** and mitigating the *Malacca Dilemma*. |
| **Ecosystem Interconnectedness** | Single ecological unit: cyclones, fish stocks, shipping lanes, and marine pollution cross national maritime boundaries. |
| **Non-Traditional Threats** | World's most disaster-prone region; vulnerable to illegal fishing, cyber threats, and climate crisis. |

### Operationalization via BIMSTEC

1. **BIMSTEC NSA Meeting (New Delhi, July 2026):** Adopted common principles for maritime law enforcement and Humanitarian Assistance & Disaster Relief (HADR).
2. **Joint Exercises & Data Sharing:** BIMSTEC Joint Maritime Security Exercise and White Shipping Information-Sharing Agreement scheduled for November 2026.

## 4. Fast-Track Special Courts (FTSCs) & Structural Justice Reforms

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Judiciary - Pendency, Fast-Track Courts & Right to Fair Trial]` · `[GS-2: Governance & Judicial Reforms]`

### Context & Background
- Prime Minister announced fast-track courts for paper leak cases under the **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024**.
- Delhi High Court constituted a specialized fast-track court at the **Rouse Avenue Courts Complex** to hear exam leak cases.

### Critical Assessment of Fast-Track Special Courts (FTSCs)

```
                       FAST-TRACK COURTS (FTSCs)
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                   ▼
  STRUCTURAL BOTTLENECK                              CONSTITUTIONAL RISKS
  • 2.45 Lakh FTSC Backlog                           • Violation of Art 21 (Fair Trial)
  • Reallocates existing judges                       • Arbitrary classification (Art 14)
  • Lack of Forensic/Prosecution support             • Rushed trials vs Defense preparation
```

#### Major Challenges Identified

| Challenge Area | Detailed Analysis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Resource Reallocation Trap** | FTSCs reassign existing judges and courtrooms rather than expanding total judicial capacity. This shifts judicial backlog onto regular court dockets (total trial court pendency: **~4.8 Crore cases**, 74% criminal). |
| **Operational Bottlenecks** | Severe shortage of trained Public Prosecutors and cyber/digital forensic laboratory support. |
| **Article 21 (Due Process)** | Supreme Court cautioned against imposing rigid, statutory trial deadlines. Rushed trials in complex paper leak cases (voluminous chargesheets, hundreds of witnesses) compromise the right to a fair trial and defense preparation. |
| **Article 14 (Equal Protection)** | SC warned that ad-hoc designation of specific case categories without rational classification or institutional expansion risks violating equal protection. |

### Mains Utility / Way Forward for Judicial Reform
1. **Capacity Expansion over Reallocation:** Increase the baseline judge-to-population ratio (India has ~21 judges per million vs. 50 recommended by Law Commission 120th Report).
2. **Investigative & Forensic Strengthening:** Modernize forensic science laboratories (FSLs) and establish a dedicated cadre of trained prosecutors before prescribing tight trial timelines.
3. **Procedural Discipline:** Enforce strict limits on frivolous adjournments under Section 309 of CrPC / Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS).
