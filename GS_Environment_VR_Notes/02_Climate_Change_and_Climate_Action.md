## 1. What Climate Change Is (ENV-02-01)

**Weather** = the meteorological mix at a place *now* (insolation, temperature, cloud, humidity, wind, rainfall).

**Climate** = the **average of weather over at least ~30 years**.

> **Climate change** is a **significant variation in the mean state of climate** (temperature, precipitation, wind, etc.) lasting **at least a decade or more**.

A one-year spike is **not** climate change. The deviation must be **consistent** (~10 years+).

### 1.1 Natural vs anthropogenic

Earth’s 4.5-billion-year history has many **natural** climate shifts (solar input, axial tilt, land–water layout — Geography will do these). Example: **Pleistocene** glaciation ended ~10,000–11,000 years ago; **Holocene** interglacial (relatively warmer) began.

The 21st-century environmental concern is **not** that natural cycle. It is **human-caused** — **anthropogenic climate change**. Qualify that claim with a scientific body, not with opinion.

### 1.2 IPCC — the citing machine for Mains

| Item | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **What** | **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)** — scientists from governments worldwide (including India) |
| **Set up** | **1988**, jointly by **UNEP** (United Nations Environment Programme) and **WMO** |
| **Product** | Periodic **Assessment Reports (AR)**, generally every **~6 years** |
| **AR1** | **1990** — already said anthropogenic climate change is happening (this is why Rio 1992 could act) |
| **AR6** | Volumes through **2022–23** (typically four volumes) |

> **As per IPCC (AR6):** human activities have caused a **global average temperature rise of 1.1 °C** compared with **pre-industrial** levels.

Class illustration (not a figure to over-memorise): if pre-industrial mean was ~13.5 °C, present mean is ~14.6 °C.

Simplest indicator of climate change = **global warming** (temperature rise). IPCC also talks of extremes as **“climate on steroids”** — not new disasters, but **more frequent and more intense** ones. Tropical cyclones still need ~**27 °C** sea-surface temperature to form; climate change does not *invent* cyclones, it **loads** them.

## 2. Two Causes: More GHGs and Smaller Sinks (ENV-02-02)

**Causes of present climate change**

1. **Increased emissions of greenhouse gases (GHGs).**
2. **Shrinking carbon sinks** and **land-use change**.

Both trap a **greater amount of heat** in the **Earth-atmosphere system**.

### 2.1 Greenhouse effect (needed, not the villain)

Sun → **short-wave** radiation heats the surface. The surface emits **long-wave terrestrial radiation**. **Water vapour** and **CO₂** (carbon dioxide) trap that long-wave radiation. That **greenhouse effect** is one reason Earth is warm enough for life (~13–14 °C). The problem is **too much** of it after the industrial revolution.

Natural GHGs: water vapour (most important natural), CO₂, ozone, methane. After industrialisation the energy base is **fossil fuels — coal, oil and gas**.

### 2.2 Carbon dioxide — the headline number

| | Pre-industrial | Now |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CO₂** | **280 ppm** (parts per **million** — the audio said “billion”; the sheet is ppm) | **~420 ppm** |

- Present CO₂ is **150% of pre-industrial** (420 / 280).
- **420 ppm is the highest in ~8 lakh (800,000) years.** For most of that span the planet held CO₂ near 280 ppm; the jump is in ~200 industrial years.

**Anthropogenic CO₂ sources:** combustion of fossil fuels in **power, heavy industry** (cement, fertiliser, steel), **transport**.

### 2.3 Scale of human emissions (handout / IPCC–WMO table)

- Humanity’s total annual GHG emissions ~ **59 Gt** (gigatonnes).
- **Gas-wise (lecture):** ~**75% CO₂**, ~**18% methane (CH₄)**, ~**5% nitrous oxide (N₂O)**; rest **F-gases** (fluorinated gases, e.g. chlorofluorocarbons — later lecture).
- **Sector-wise (lecture):** **energy ~ three-fourths**; **agriculture ~12%** (second); then industry; landfill; land-use / shrinking sinks.

## 3. Methane, Landfills and Swachh Bharat (ENV-02-03)

**Methane (CH₄)** is measured in **ppb** (parts per **billion**), not ppm. Present concentration = **260% of pre-industrial**.

**Anthropogenic sources**

| Source | Why methane |
|:---|:---|
| **Agriculture** | **Paddy** (flooded, anaerobic) + **livestock** (enteric fermentation) |
| **Coal, oil and gas extraction** | Methane trapped in pore spaces is released when the field is opened |
| **Landfill sites** | Unsegregated **legacy waste**; **anaerobic** decay → CH₄ |

**Aerobic** decay (with oxygen) → **CO₂**. **Anaerobic** (no oxygen) → **CH₄**. Methane is highly flammable; landfill fires in the **pre-monsoon dry** months are hard to kill because CH₄ keeps forming. **Kochi** landfill fire is the news example; **Deonar** (Mumbai) and **Ghazipur** (Delhi) are the named dumps.

### 3.1 Why Indian cities leak methane

Urbanisation → more consumption → more solid waste.

| | What class said |
|:---|:---|
| **Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM) 1.0** | **Open Defecation Free (ODF)** — largely achieved |
| **SBM 2.0** | **Solid waste management:** door-to-door collection → **segregation at source** → **treatment** |
| **Economic Survey (class)** | Collection **>90%**; scientific treatment only **~20%**. The rest (~80%) → landfills |

**Indore model** (Mains has asked Indore vs Delhi): source segregation by citizens; near-100% collection; **GPS-enabled** vans; **bio-CNG** (compressed natural gas) plants for wet/biodegradable waste; **waste-to-energy** (high temperature, limited oxygen) for non-bio. Indore stays #1 on Swachhata rankings.

**Delhi (typical metro):** collection exists; **source segregation is weak**; **treatment capacity is short** → hills of **legacy waste** at the periphery.

## 4. Nitrous Oxide, NOx and GHG vs Air Pollutant (ENV-02-04)

**NOx** = nitrogen oxides, three species formed together:

| Gas | Formula | GHG? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Nitric oxide | NO | No (directly) |
| **Nitrous oxide** | **N₂O** | **Yes** — now **125% of pre-industrial** |
| Nitrogen dioxide | NO₂ | No (directly) |

**Sources of all three:** (1) **burning fossil fuels** — N₂ and O₂ do not react at room temperature; they do at **combustion** temperatures → power, industry, transport (same sectors as CO₂); (2) **agriculture** — **nitrogen fertilisers**: urea, **DAP** (diammonium phosphate), NPK.

**Trap (class true/false):** *“All types of NOx emissions are responsible for present global warming.”* **True** — N₂O is a GHG **directly**; **all three** help form **tropospheric ozone (O₃)**, which **is** a GHG. So NO and NO₂ are **indirect**.

**Tropospheric ozone** = GHG **and** air pollutant (toxic). Do **not** use GHG and air pollutant as synonyms.

> **Air pollutant:** an atmospheric **contaminant** (solid, liquid or gas) that poses a **health hazard** to humans and other life forms — a **toxin** in the air.

**PM2.5 / PM10** (particulate matter): diameter **≤ 2.5 / ≤ 10 microns**. Invisible; stay suspended; enter lungs and blood; can **carry** other toxins (SO₂, CO). **Not GHGs.** Dust you *see* in a sunbeam is **not** PM2.5.

## 5. Carbon Sinks, Ecological Degradation, Oceans as Heat Sponges (ENV-02-05)

> **Carbon sink:** a mass atmospheric-carbon reservoir that **absorbs carbon from the atmosphere and stores it for a long duration**. Forests and other ecosystems; oceans (dissolved CO₂ / seawater). **Sequestration** = absorption.

**Land-use change** (deforestation) and **ecological degradation** shrink those sinks.

> **Ecological degradation:** a **consistent decline in primary productivity** of an ecosystem, which then hurts human / economic well-being.

| Driver | What degrades |
|:---|:---|
| Forest fire | Forest (a sink can flip into a **source**) |
| Marine pollution | Ocean (photosynthetic capacity falls) |
| Soil erosion | Land / soil |
| Coral bleaching | Coral reef (technical mechanism = later class) |

**Before industry:** natural CO₂ sources (volcanoes, wildfire) **balanced** natural sinks → ~280 ppm held for ages.

**After industry:** sources **explode** *and* sinks **shrink** → more GHGs → more greenhouse effect → more heat in the Earth-atmosphere system.

Oceans have **high specific heat**. Atmosphere and ocean sit at an interface, so most extra heat does **not** stay only in air.

> **As per IPCC: more than 90% of the excess heat has been absorbed by the oceans.** Oceans are the **heat sinks / heat sponges** of the planet. That stored heat is why **thermal expansion** and marine impacts follow.

## 6. UNFCCC, COPs, Mitigation and CCS / CCUS (ENV-02-06)

**1990 AR1** gave the science. **1992 United Nations Conference on Environment and Development**, Rio de Janeiro (**Rio Summit**, Brazil) adopted the **United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC)**. **In force 1994**. Aim: **internationally coordinated climate action** through **mitigation and adaptation**. India was a party.

| Term | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Convention** | International **agreement** on a common agenda |
| **Adopted** | Final text acceptable to all is ready (1992 for UNFCCC) |
| **Ratification** | Countries sign on; **~two-thirds** ratification → **comes into force** (1994) |
| **Party** | A country that has joined |
| **COP** | **Conference of the Parties** — parties meet regularly |

**COPs of UNFCCC:** first **1995**. Held **every year** except **2020** (pandemic). **COP 30 — 2025**. Next class starts **COP 3, Kyoto, 1997**.

### 6.1 Mitigation

> Aim of **climate mitigation:** **reduce and stabilise atmospheric concentration of GHGs** (the class picture: try to walk CO₂ from **420 ppm back toward 280 ppm**).

Three methods in this lecture:

1. **Substitute fossil energy** with **non-fossil** — **renewables and nuclear**.
2. **Afforestation** (grow the sink).
3. **Geological sequestration** = **Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS)**.

**CCS:** capture CO₂ at a **point source** (e.g. thermal power plant) so it never enters the air; **pressurise** and **inject** it into empty pore space of **depleted oil and gas fields** / other sub-surface sedimentary rock; cap with an **impermeable** layer.

If some of the captured CO₂ is also **used** in industry, the name becomes **Carbon Capture, Utilisation and Storage (CCUS)**. Utilisation / recycling of carbon = **next lecture**. Prelims has already asked CCUS.

**Hard truth from class:** however hard we mitigate, we **cannot** return to pre-industrial GHG levels **in this century**. Climate change is a **lived reality** → adaptation is not optional.

> Aim of **climate adaptation:** **modify human ways of life** to **minimise negative impacts** of climate change on **human systems**.

## 7. Impacts: Disasters, Sea Level, SIDS (ENV-02-07)

**1. Increased intensity and frequency of climatic disasters** — tropical cyclones, **GLOFs** (glacial lake outburst floods), cloudbursts, floods, drought, heatwaves, forest fire. Climate change does **not create** cyclones; it makes them **more often and more severe**. Human cost: mortality, livelihood, food, poverty, trauma (class pointed to a unfolding Nepal disaster as a development-setback example).

**2. Sea-level rise (SLR)** — as per **IPCC**, average annual rate **3–4 mm**; **cumulative rise by end-century more than a metre**.

**Consequences of SLR**

1. More **coastal erosion** by waves and tides (loss of fertile delta farmland).
2. **Seawater intrusion** → **groundwater salinisation** of coastal habitats (water **quality** falls; Himalayan glacier melt after mid-century also hits **quantity** in Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra).
3. Worse **coastal flooding** in cyclones and tsunamis.
4. **Coastal land submersion.** Highest risk: **low-elevation coastal zones (LECZ)** and **Small Island Developing States (SIDS)**.

> **LECZ:** coastal land with elevation **up to 10 m above mean sea level**.

**SIDS** (sovereign, small, island, developing): **Maldives, Mauritius, Fiji, Tonga, Tuvalu, Solomon Islands, Papua New Guinea (PNG)** — some face **whole-country** submergence.

**Major causes of SLR (cryosphere + ocean)**

1. Faster melting of the **cryosphere**: Arctic sea ice, Greenland ice cap, Antarctic ice sheet, mountain snow and glaciers (class also listed high-latitude / Alaskan ice).
2. **Thermal expansion** of oceans (the 90% heat sponge).

## 8. Migration, Biodiversity, Health, Equity, Climate-Smart Agriculture (ENV-02-08)

**Environmental / climatic migration:** **distressed displacement** of people because of environment-related stress.

**Biodiversity / ecological hits:** climate change → intense heatwaves → forest fire (California, Australia); coral bleaching; habitat loss (polar bear); other stresses.

**Agriculture:** thermal stress + moisture stress in **Kharif and Rabi**. Class caution: India can still be **food-grain self-sufficient** even while climate hits yields — do not collapse every agri sentence into “food security collapse”.

**Human health**

- **Direct:** heatwave → heat stress / heat stroke.
- **Indirect:** climate → agriculture → food; climate → water → health.

Climate change **undercuts Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**. Impacts are **disproportionate**: **developing countries and Least Developed Countries (LDCs)**, and **marginalised** groups inside countries.

| | Developed | Developing / LDCs |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Emissions | High (growth ↔ emissions) | Lower / often negligible (Nepal, Fiji, PNG) |
| Livelihood mix | Secondary, tertiary, quaternary — less weather-tied | **Agriculture, forestry, fisheries** — directly climate-tied |
| Coping capacity | Money, tech, institutions, **Early Warning Systems (EWS)** | Weak. Himalayan cloudburst EWS needs hundreds of **Doppler weather radars**; India could afford **&lt;10** even with ministry funding. 1980s **African drought** = early documented climate-linked drought in the tropics |

Same split **inside India: rich vs poor**. Rich emit more, sit in non-farm jobs, can still buy food when prices spike. Poor / farmers / tribals / fishers emit less and absorb the hit.

**Two adaptation buckets in this lecture**

1. **Efficient disaster management** (EWS, Doppler radars, funding).
2. **Climate-smart agriculture (CSA)** — **FAO** (Food and Agriculture Organization) idea: an agri-system that **copes with climate impacts** (more **resilient**), **raises productivity and income**, and **cuts GHG emissions** from farming.

Examples: **Direct Seeded Rice (DSR)** (cuts paddy **CH₄** vs flooded transplanting); **millets** / drought-resilient varieties; **micro-irrigation — drip and sprinkler** (flood irrigation has low water-use efficiency; Himalayan-fed north faces tighter water).

## UPSC Cues

- Climate = ~30-year weather average; climate change = **≥ decade** shift. **Anthropogenic**, cite **IPCC**.
- **IPCC 1988 = UNEP + WMO.** AR1 1990; AR6 2022–23; **+1.1 °C**.
- Two engines: **GHG up + sinks down**. CO₂ **280 → 420 ppm**, **8 lakh-year** high, **150%** of pre-industrial. CH₄ **260%** (ppb). N₂O **125%**.
- **CO₂ and water vapour = GHG, not air pollutants.** **SO₂ and PM = pollutants, not GHGs.** Overlap: **CH₄, trop. O₃, CFCs**.
- Only **N₂O** among NOx is a GHG; all NOx still warm via **trop. ozone**.
- **SBM 1.0 = ODF; 2.0 = waste.** Collection ~90%, treatment ~20%. Indore vs Delhi / legacy waste / Deonar / Kochi fire.
- Oceans absorb **>90%** of excess heat.
- **UNFCCC** adopted **1992**, in force **1994**; **COP** yearly (no 2020); mitigation = non-fossil + forests + **CCS/CCUS**.
- SLR **3–4 mm/yr**, **>1 m** by 2100; **LECZ ≤ 10 m**; **SIDS** list; cryosphere + thermal expansion.
- **CSA:** DSR, millets, drip/sprinkler. Equity: those who emitted least are hit first.
