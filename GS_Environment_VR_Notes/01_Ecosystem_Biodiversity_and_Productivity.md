## 1. Environment vs Ecosystem (ENV-01-02)

**Environment** = our **surroundings**. It is made of:

| Class shortcut | Full form | Examples in a forest |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **l.f. / biotic** | **Living factors** (life forms) | Plants, animals |
| **n.l. / abiotic** | **Non-living** components | Air, water (surface + groundwater), landforms, nutrients in soil |

An **ecosystem** is not just a bag of those parts. It is an **entity** whose parts **interact**. Plants ↔ animals (food + pollination); plants ↔ air/water/nutrients (photosynthesis); animals ↔ oxygen, water, landforms (habitat); soil fertility ↔ flora and fauna; even abiotic–abiotic links exist.

**Orographic / relief rainfall** (school geography, used here only to show abiotic–abiotic linkage): moisture-laden winds hit a mountain → **windward** side wet → **leeward** side dry. Water resources of a forest therefore depend on rainfall, which often depends on **landforms**.

Because of these two-way associations the forest becomes **self-sustaining** — it can support life on its own — and that is what qualifies it as an ecosystem.

> **Dictated definition:** An ecosystem is a **functional unit** composed of a **biotic community** integrated with its **physical non-living environment** through the **flow of energy** and the **flow of nutrients**.

- **Functional unit** — every ecosystem *performs* functions (energy flow + nutrient flow).
- **Biotic community** — all living components taken together.
- **Integrated with** — associated / linked with the abiotic setting.

**A.G. Tansley** (Arthur George Tansley) coined the concept of ecosystem.

| Classification | Meaning | Examples |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Terrestrial** vs **aquatic** | Land-based vs water-based | Forest, grassland, desert vs river, lake, ocean |
| **Natural** vs **artificial** | Wild vs **human-managed** | Forest vs cropland / aquarium |
| Simple vs complex | Complexity generally rises with **size** | A pond is simpler to map than a large forest |

## 2. Trophic Structure and the 10% Rule (ENV-01-03)

**Trophic** from Greek *trophos* = food / nourishment.

> **Trophic structure** = hierarchical arrangement of **biotic** components of an ecosystem based on **feeding relationships**. Abiotic components are **not** in this diagram. The hierarchy is read off **food chains and food webs**.

Simple forest chain: plants → deer (herbivore) → tiger (carnivore) → vulture/hawk (scavenger on remains).

Energy flow follows the **two laws of thermodynamics**: (1) solar energy is converted into **chemical** energy (neither created nor destroyed); (2) that chemical energy is transferred **unidirectionally** from a lower trophic level (**T.L.**) to the next higher one — it does **not** cycle back as energy.

**10% rule:** generally only **up to ~10%** of energy at a given trophic level is transferred to the next. Ecological efficiency is low → food chains are **short** (generally **4**, at most **5** trophic levels — never 14–15).

## 3. Nutrients and Biogeochemical Cycles (ENV-01-04)

Nutrients = **elements** that are the building blocks of carbohydrates, fats, proteins.

| | Needed in | Examples (class) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Macronutrients** | Large quantities | **C** Carbon, **H** Hydrogen, **N** Nitrogen, **O** Oxygen (sheet also flagged **P** Phosphorus in the margin) |
| **Micronutrients** | Small quantities | **Cu** Copper, **Zn** Zinc, **Fe** Iron |

They sit in four **reservoirs** (storage pools) of the planet — **atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere** — in different chemical forms, and they **move cyclically**. Energy flow is **unidirectional**; nutrient flow is **cyclical**. That cycling, driven by the flow of energy, is a **biogeochemical (B.G.C.) cycle**. Syllabus cycles: **carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus** (NCERT last unit — read all three).

Energy flow is a **one-way street**. Nutrient flow is a **closed loop** — the element leaves a reservoir and **must return** to it. That is why the class sketches are drawn as **cycles**, not chains.

### Carbon cycle

Carbon starts as **CO₂ (carbon dioxide) in the atmosphere** and **returns as CO₂** — same form in, same form out.

### Gaseous vs sedimentary cycles (ENV-01-05)

| Type | Major reservoir | Examples | How the nutrient enters the cycle |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Gaseous** | **Atmosphere** | Carbon (as **CO₂**), Nitrogen (as **N₂**), hydrological cycle (water vapour) | Already in the air |
| **Sedimentary** | **Earth's crust only** (rocks) | **Phosphorus**, **Sulphur / Sulfur** | Released from rock |

- **Phosphorus:** **weathering** is the **only** release path.  
- **Sulphur:** **weathering + volcanism** (high-T lava → **SO₂ / sulphur dioxide** out of the volcano).

**Prelims trap (class):** “Weathering of rock is the **main** source of release” → **phosphorus**. Drop the word *main* and both **P and S** become correct. UPSC repeats by changing **one word**.

## 4. Ecological Productivity (ENV-01-06)

**Biomass** ≈ body weight of an organism. **Production** = gain in body weight over time. **Productivity** = **rate** of biomass production.

> Ecological productivity = rate of biomass production by the biotic components of an ecosystem. Two types: **primary** (producers) and **secondary** (consumers).

**Unit of primary productivity:** **grams per metre square per year** (g m⁻² yr⁻¹) — production ÷ **area of the ecosystem (E.S.)**. Whenever a question says only “productivity”, it means **primary** productivity.

### Gross vs net primary productivity

- **GPP (Gross Primary Productivity)** = rate at which **solar energy is captured** by producers during **photosynthesis**.
- While capture is happening, part of that energy is used in **respiration** or lost as heat in **transpiration**. Together these are **respiration losses (~20% of GPP)**.
- **NPP (Net Primary Productivity)** = rate at which the **leftover** energy is used by producers for their own biomass. **NPP = GPP − respiration losses ≈ 80% of GPP**.

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)** = the slice of **insolation** (incoming solar radiation) that can support photosynthesis.

| Quantity | Share (class figures — learn the **range**) |
|:---|:---|
| **GPP** | **2–10% of PAR** |
| Respiration losses | **~20% of GPP** |
| **NPP** | **~80% of GPP** = **1.6–8% of PAR** (audio said “1.628%”; that is 80% of the *lower* bound 2% — the sheet’s **1.6 to 8%** is the full range) |

### Secondary productivity

Rate of biomass production by **consumers** (T.L. II, III, IV). Practically: change in consumer body weight over a year ÷ area. It **depends on** primary productivity; numerical tables of ecosystem productivity need **not** be memorised.

### Controls on terrestrial primary productivity (ENV-01-07)

Three major environmental controls, acting **simultaneously**:

1. **Temperature**
2. **Availability of water**
3. **Availability of nutrients**

Primary productivity is **positively correlated** with all three — but **no single factor is “the” control**. Warmer / wetter / more nutrients → generally higher NPP, so **lower latitude > higher latitude** and **lower elevation > higher elevation** *other things equal*. **Sahara** is hot yet poor in NPP because **soil moisture** is the **limiting factor**. If any one control goes too low, it caps productivity.

**Amazon rainforest (Brazil) vs Sahara:** visible biomass is the everyday indicator of NPP.

## 5. Biodiversity — Three Levels (ENV-01-08)

> Biodiversity = **totality of genes, species and ecosystems** over the planet (*bio* = life, *diversity* = variety).

| Level | Definition |
|:---|:---|
| **Genetic diversity** | Variety of genes within the individuals of a **population of a given species** (the variable part of that species’ **gene pool**) |
| **Species diversity** | **Species richness** (how many kinds) **+ species evenness** (relative abundance of their populations) |
| **Ecosystem diversity** | Variety of ecosystems in a **region** (India as a region: Thar desert, alpine grassland, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands…) |

**Why evenness matters:** because of the 10% rule, higher trophic levels support **smaller populations**. Long-term balance needs a **proportion**, not just a species count. Do **not** memorise ratios — they vary and even ecologists rarely compute them.

### Genetic diversity → survival (the Mains articulation)

Higher genetic diversity **expands the range of tolerance** to environmental stresses (moisture, heat, pests, weeds, disease) → improves **resilience and adaptability** → **better chance of survival** of the species. Prelims can be a bubble; **Mains needs this sentence written out**.

## 6. Project Tiger and Genetic Diversity in the Wild (ENV-01-09)

This is the **applied** side of genetic diversity.

| Item | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Started | **1973**, **centrally sponsored scheme** |
| Implementing agency | **NTCA (National Tiger Conservation Authority)** — a **statutory** authority under **MoEF&CC (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change)** |
| Phases | First **50 years** done; **second phase** now running |
| Last census | **3,682** tigers |
| 2006 baseline | **1,411** — population has more than doubled; rising **since 2006** |
| Global rank | **Tiger capital of the world** — **>70%** of world tigers; **only** one of 13 tiger-range countries where numbers are **consistently rising** |

Conservationists now watch **two** things: **headcount** (anti-poaching / anti-trafficking has been strong) **and genetic diversity**. Some landscapes show diversity **declining or plateauing**.

### Similipal Tiger Reserve (T.R.), Odisha (ENV-01-10)

Largest tiger reserve of Odisha. Famous for **pseudo-melanistic** tigers: **black stripes become wider and start to merge**, so black dominates over gold — but it is **not fully black**. Full black would be **melanistic**. This is an **aberration of appearance**, **not a disease**.

**Cause chain taught in class:**

1. Human demand for land (agriculture, mining, industry, settlements) → **deforestation**.
2. One large forest becomes **islands** of forest.
3. **Habitat loss** = area shrinks. **Habitat fragmentation** = remaining patches are **geographically disconnected**.
4. Wildlife **mobility falls** (human footprint in between; a leopard in a city is called “conflict”, not ecology).
5. Isolated groups **breed among themselves** → **inbreeding** → rare genes (e.g. pseudo-melanism) become locally common, **overall genetic diversity falls**.

**Policy response mentioned:** **wildlife corridors** reconnect fragmented habitats and reduce inbreeding.

## 7. High Primary Productivity ↔ High Biodiversity (ENV-01-11)

**Positive correlation:** high NPP at T.L. I → more energy to transfer → more species **and** larger populations at every level → higher biodiversity. These ecosystems dominate newspaper Environment copy; **detail in later lectures**. Sheet wrote “10% / 30% North & South” for latitudes — the lecture said **degrees**, not percent.

| Ecosystem | What it is | Where |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Equatorial rainforest** | Tropical **evergreen** forest | **~10° N–S**. Example: **Amazon**, South America |
| **Mangrove forest** | Tropical evergreen forest of the **intertidal zone** (between high-tide and low-tide lines on the coast) | **~30° N–S**. Largest: **Sundarbans** (India + Bangladesh) |
| **Coral reef** | **Marine** ecosystem | **~30° N–S**. Largest: **Great Barrier Reef**, Australia |
| **Wetlands** | Most technical — full definition later | **Wular Lake** (Kashmir), **Sambhar Lake** (Rajasthan), **Chilika** (Odisha), **Loktak** (Manipur), **Kanwar Lake** (Bihar, from the sheet), **Vembanad** (Kerala, from the lecture) |

**Prelims habit:** “sequence of ecosystems in **decreasing productivity**” → default = **primary** productivity = production ÷ **area**. A huge ocean can have large *total* production but **low productivity per unit area**.

## UPSC Cues & PYQ Connections

- Ecosystem = **functional unit** (energy + nutrients), coined by **A.G. Tansley**.
- Energy **unidirectional + 10% rule** vs nutrients **cyclical (B.G.C.)**.
- **NPP = GPP − R**; GPP = 2–10% of **PAR**; NPP ≈ 80% of GPP.
- Terrestrial NPP controls: **T + water + nutrients**, simultaneous; Sahara = water-limited.
- Biodiversity = genes + species (richness **and** evenness) + ecosystems.
- High genetic diversity → **range of tolerance → resilience → survival**.
- **Project Tiger 1973** / **NTCA** statutory under **MoEF&CC**; **3,682** vs **1,411 (2006)**; **>70%** world share.
- **Similipal:** pseudo-melanism ≠ disease; **habitat fragmentation → inbreeding**.
- **P vs S:** weathering is the **main/only** source for **phosphorus**; sulphur also **volcanism**.
- High-NPP, high-biodiversity set: equatorial rainforest, mangrove, coral, wetland.
- Productivity questions = **primary**, **per unit area**.
