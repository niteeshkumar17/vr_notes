## 1. The handout, before the argument (orientation)

Social justice, in the line he had already used in Social Issues, is social empowerment. It follows **equity**: unequals must be treated unequally. The unequals are the **vulnerable sections**. The instrument he named first is **reservation**.

Vulnerable sections he listed, and then parked: Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes, Denotified Tribes, the elderly, persons with disabilities, and transgenders. Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes, and Denotified Tribes he has already done in Social Issues. Women, children, persons with disabilities, and transgenders he said he would finish in this course. Not today.

The bodies are for **protection**, not for welfare. Welfare is the schemes. Protection is watching that rights are not violated. Constitutional bodies he named: the National Commission for Scheduled Castes, the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes, and the National Commission for Backward Classes. Statutory bodies, meaning bodies made by **law**: the National Human Rights Commission, the National Commission for Women, and what he called a national commission for children. He did not say National Commission for Protection of Child Rights. Do not swap the name in.

Human resource development, when he reaches it, is not only education and skill. A skilled person who is not healthy is not a resource. He flagged India’s rising burden of **non-communicable** disease — diabetes, blood pressure, thyroid, polycystic ovary syndrome — and left the sector there.

## 2. Development changed its meaning in 1990 (GOV-02-01)

Before **1990**, development was only an economic word, and the only measure was **gross domestic product**. A higher product meant a more developed country. On that rule, a country with the fifth or sixth largest product would be the fifth or sixth most developed. He would not choose between fifth and sixth. Keep both.

After 1990, **Amartya Sen** and **Mahbub ul Haq** (sheet “M. ul. Haq”; audio “Mahboob Ullhaq”) called that the wrong measure. Development had to be **human-centric**: are people healthy, are they educated, and what is their standard of living. The index they are associated with is the **Human Development Index**.

| What is measured | How he measured it | The comparison he locked |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Health | **Life expectancy at birth** | India about **71** years. Western countries about **80 to 85**. The sheet writes **~80** |
| Education | **Expected years of schooling** — years a child born today is expected to spend in school | India about **8**. Western countries about **12**. The sheet also writes **mean** years of schooling beside the expected years. He taught the expected-years line |
| Standard of living | **Gross national income divided by total population**, which is per capita income. House, water, a vehicle, and cooking fuel all depend on that income | India’s is very poor, because so much of the workforce earns little |

When the measure changed, some countries that looked strong on the product looked weak on the index. **India** is his case: **fifth or sixth** on the size of the product, and **130th out of 180** on the Human Development Index.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** The index is not a second name for gross domestic product. Life expectancy is **at birth**. The income line is **gross national income per person**, not the size of the product. **130 / 180** is the index rank. Fifth or sixth is the product rank. Do not swap them.</span>

## 3. Why the two ranks do not match (GOV-02-02)

**Vulnerable sections**, in the definition he wanted written: people who are **completely dependent on the State**.

1. **Inequality.** **10 per cent** of the population holds **70 per cent** of the resources. The majority does not have enough to invest in health, education, or the standard of living, so it depends entirely on the State.
2. **The State cannot raise enough revenue** to look after that population. The **tax base** is small. The rich **evade** tax and **avoid** tax. Evasion is hiding the liability. Avoidance is using a **loophole** in the law. He said online administration has made evasion harder, and avoidance remains. A State that is short of money, and responsible for a huge dependent population, cannot do **inclusive development**. Inclusive development, here, means development of the vulnerable sections.
3. **Short-term gains beat long-term ones.** Limited money has to be prioritised. He is not against a subsidy. He is against starving the spend that builds human capital. The State invests about **2 per cent of gross domestic product in health** and about **2 per cent in education**. Subsidies receive about **10 per cent**. Health and education do not buy the next election. A subsidy does. The two 2 per cent figures stay separate.
4. **What is left is wasted in corruption.**
5. **The work does not pay.** A majority depends on agriculture, or does semi-skilled and unskilled work elsewhere. The sheet puts agriculture at **14–16 per cent** of the product and about **48 per cent** of the population, and puts unskilled and semi-skilled work at about **80**. Low income is a low standard of living.
6. **Women’s participation in the workforce is very low**, but women are still counted in the population. Per capita income falls because the denominator counts people the numerator is not paying.

## 4. Three actors, and the third sector (GOV-02-03)

Before **1991** the State did the development. Rich and poor both depended on the State hospital. **1991** is delicensing: a private company can open without that licence. The private sector then grew, and it works for **profit**, so it caters to the **rich** — people whose income is enough that they do not need the State. After 1991 the State’s development role narrows to the **vulnerable sections**. Its money is still too small to reach all of them, so the process leaves **gaps**.

The body that fills the gaps is the **third sector**, a **civil society organisation**. Any organisation that has all three features qualifies:

1. **Non-state.** It does not work under a ministry or a department. No bureaucrat sits over it.
2. **Non-profit.** Serving people who cannot pay does not generate a profit. Profit is the private sector’s motive.
3. **A voluntary association of people**, formed to solve a problem the State has failed to solve.

He included non-governmental organisations, self-help groups, and cooperatives. Pressure groups, donor organisations, and charitable trusts sit in the same family. A donor organisation or a charitable trust, he said, can itself be called a non-governmental organisation. They **mobilise their own resources**. The cooperative and self-help-group movements were named and not taught.

**Who, inside the State, does the work.** The **temporary executive** is the Council of Ministers, elected for five years. Because the vote is theirs, they formulate policy and launch the schemes. The **permanent executive** is the bureaucracy. It implements. A cylinder reaches a house because an official carries the scheme, not because the minister does. This split is already the civil-service half of Lecture 1. The new point is what happens to a **small** fund on the way down.

He said people are corrupt by nature, and that officials divert the money. The “**99 per cent**” he used is a classroom picture of that diversion, not a measured share. Do not learn it as a statistic. The chain of accountability he drew: the bureaucracy answers to the Council of Ministers, and the Council answers to the people, and only every five years. In a paper leak, the person the crowd can reach is the education minister, not the secretary. Officials know this, and he said they buy postings by sharing the take. In a traditional society the vote follows caste and religion, so the minister is not questioned. The **rule of law does not prevail**: the person who would catch them is also corrupt, and a case is settled. Vulnerable sections keep paying for a scheme whose money never arrives.

**Public administration**, which he also called governance, studies that implementation. Its reform list, named and not taught: **e-governance**, a **citizen charter**, the **Right to Information**, and **direct benefit transfer**. Direct benefit transfer is the contrast with an official who used to assign the subsidy and keep a cut. The Acts themselves stay parked, as Lecture 1 already said.

## 5. A non-governmental organisation is not a pressure group (GOV-02-04)

A non-governmental organisation works for the welfare of **others**, and the others are the vulnerable sections. The work is charity.

A **pressure group** works for the welfare of **itself**. It presses the State for a policy in its own favour. Lecture 1 already has the definition: it does not want formal power, and it stays out of elections. Today’s addition is the method, and the contrast with the United States.

| | India | United States |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Form | **Informal.** Not formally recognised by the State | **Organised, formal, and recognised** |
| Method | **Protest** | **Lobbying** — engagement with lawmakers, not a march to Jantar Mantar |
| Money | Protest is the route he called the usual one | Lobbying is **legally allowed**, including payment to senators. Specialist law firms do it. He still called paying a lawmaker unethical. Legal, in his line, is not the same as clean |

Lobbying in a Western country can still produce a policy the rich paid for. He did not treat the American model as innocent. He treated it as recognised.

## 6. What the organisations actually do (GOV-02-05)

The purpose is inclusive development, because the work is for the vulnerable sections.

1. **Goods and services.** Food, education, healthcare, old-age homes. For persons with disabilities: prosthetics, hearing aids, wheelchairs. **Free legal aid** so a person can fight for a right. His examples were Muslim women against triple talaq, and transgender persons. He put the legal aid with the **National Legal Services Authority**. They also tell people about their rights, the laws made for them, the constitutional bodies, and the welfare schemes. Without funds none of this continues, and a non-profit does not earn its own funds.
2. **Research.** Some organisations exist for it. The **Association for Democratic Reforms** works to make the election free, fair, and transparent. He credited it with the **None of the Above** button, through a public-interest petition. None of the Above in a direct election is already on the polity note. This class only adds who pushed it. Other research he named: climate change, including migration caused by it, and **legislative research** — reading a Bill for the loophole, for whether it dilutes or strengthens democracy, and for how it hits the vulnerable sections. The body he named for that work is **PRS**. He expanded it as **Policy Research Studies**.
3. **Surveys** of the ground, and of the vulnerable sections, not of the rich. Is the scheme working? Where should the State concentrate? The State should do this and does not, for want of funds. The organisation collects the data, finds the gap, and gives it back as **feedback**, so the scheme becomes more effective. His school example: under the **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009**, almost every child is in school from class 1 to class 8, and the learning is still poor. He tied that to the **National Education Policy, 2020**, and to learning outcomes, and did not open the policy. **Environmental impact assessment** and **social impact assessment** are the same idea applied to a project: surveys the organisations can hand the government, so the project does less harm to the environment and to people.
4. **The Right to Information, then a report a citizen can read.** The file is voluminous. The organisation hires experts, finds the corruption in it, and writes the report in simple language. People then question the State. That is how he defined accountability here: awareness of the process of governance.

He added, as an illustration of civil-society research and not as a new election topic, that the **Special Intensive Revision** has been done poorly on **ERONet**. The software, he said, will not let the officer add an eligible voter, and it deletes different people who share a name and a father’s name. **Form 6** was mentioned and not explained. The revision itself stays the August note. Do not build a new cluster for the software.

## 7. Where the organisations fail, and where the money comes from (GOV-02-06)

1. **Political money and misinformation.** An organisation funded by a party criticises the government in office whether or not the criticism is earned.
2. **Diversion, which he called money laundering.** Black money is shown as white by **over-billing administrative expenses** — a high salary to a relative of an official, dressed up as a payment for a service. They do not disclose where the money came from or where it went. The State is then told that welfare was the cover for the laundry.
3. **The conflict with the State.** Each side has a stock line.

| The State’s charge | The organisation’s answer |
|:---|:---|
| Environment groups are anti-development, and often **foreign-funded** | If nobody speaks for the environment, development cannot be sustainable |
| Religion-based groups, especially Christian missionary and Islamic organisations, do **conversion** and are anti-national | The right to religion is a fundamental right. Forced conversion is the line he later told the organisations not to cross |
| Human-rights groups, and groups that attack a policy, are anti-national | Criticism is a healthy part of democracy and should be promoted, not suppressed |
| The label is then used to stop them **mobilising funds** | Funds are the lifeline. Without them the work for vulnerable sections stops |

**Foreign-funded activism**, in his nuclear example: a foreign government that dislikes an Indian plant funds an environment group to protest and delay it. He said the government found **Greenpeace** doing this, and that Greenpeace delayed a nuclear power plant. The plant’s name did not land. Do not supply one. Greenpeace also criticised palm-oil cultivation as **water-guzzling**, against India’s palm-oil mission. **Amnesty International** he placed on human rights. It criticised the abrogation of **Article 370**, and he said it wound up in the country once the foreign money stopped. A large organisation’s money, in his line, can only come from abroad. Indian private industry prefers profit.

**How he would narrow the fight.** The organisations should publish the audit — where the money came from and where it went — so trust has something to stand on. In a country still building infrastructure, protest should not be the only reflex. Religion-based groups should not force a conversion. The State should not treat every environment group as anti-development, or every religion-based group as anti-national, and it should let them raise funds.

**Four sources.** Individual donations. **Foreign funds**, which he called the biggest. The State, **directly or indirectly** — his indirect case was land for an organisation’s hospital at a subsidised rate. **Companies**, because **corporate social responsibility** obliges them to spend **2 per cent of net profit** on the social sector.

## 8. Foreign funds, and the 2010 Act as he taught it (GOV-02-07)

Foreign funds arrive in quantities large enough to be misused: to turn opinion against the State, or to run a mass conversion. The State therefore regulates how much may come, how it may come, and what it may be spent on. The statute is the **Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act**. A law of **1976** was replaced in form by the **2010** Act. The **2020** bar on re-granting, and the **2026** amendment, are below. The Bill text, the committee, and the hearing stay the July note.

**A foreign fund** is money received in India by an individual, a company, or an organisation, from a **foreign citizen**, a **foreign company**, a **foreign organisation**, or a **foreign government**. A church in France funding an Indian organisation is a foreign organisation. His own illustration was an Indian taking money from the United States government for a channel. That receipt is a foreign fund, and it has to be reported.

**Who may not receive it.** Judges, journalists, Members of the Legislative Assembly, and Members of Parliament. He also said **cartoonists**. That word is what the audio produced. Do not add “public servants” from the July note into his list, and do not drop cartoonists. **Organisations of a political nature**, including **political parties**, are barred. A party that wins on foreign money will decide for the country that paid it. He used a Chinese fund as the sovereignty case.

**Who may.** A non-governmental organisation, only with an **FCRA licence**. The licence is renewed every **five years**. If the licence goes, the foreign money goes, and he said many Indian organisations would collapse. Conditions he listed: no diversion and no laundering; an **annual audit**; the money must not be used for anything **anti-national** or **anti-development**. Those two phrases are **not defined** in the Act. The State decides. **Administrative expenses** — salary, rent, the office — are capped at **50 per cent** of the foreign funds, so the salary cannot be the laundry. The July note’s account of the **2020** amendment caps the same expenses at **20 per cent**. Keep both figures. Do not average them.

**Re-granting** means one organisation passing foreign funds to another. He dated the ban to **2020**. The sheet marks it with a cross and that year. The July note already has the 2020 bar on sub-granting.

**The 2026 amendment, as he taught it.** If the licence is **cancelled**, the money already in the account does not stay with the organisation to spend out. A **designated authority** under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** operates it. The authority is a bureaucrat. The organisation, which was non-state, is in his reading taken into the State, and the authority can close the work. He said the provision mainly targets **Christian missionaries**, because they receive the majority of foreign funds and run the schools and hospitals. Closing those hits the vulnerable population that uses them. The **Rajiv Gandhi Foundation** came up as a party-linked organisation that had received foreign money. The donor in that sentence did not land. The July note has the wider lock: on cancellation, surrender, or lapse, the contributions and the assets vest in the designated authority, and the opposition’s Article 300A argument is there. Do not rebuild it.

## 9. Poverty, only as far as the measure (GOV-02-08)

**Absolute poverty** is **deprivation** of goods and services: food, clothes, school. It is the poverty of the countries he called third-world, Global South, developing, and underdeveloped. It can be measured.

**Relative poverty** starts only after a **basic minimum income** has covered basic needs. What remains is **inequality**, a difference in per capita income. He placed it in **Western** countries, and he said there is **no method** to measure it.

Until **2010**, the global practice was a **poverty line**. His illustration, not an official Indian line: **₹1,000 a month**. A person below the line is below the poverty line. The **poverty estimate** is the number of such people divided by the population. He also called that the **head-count ratio**. A “30 per cent” that surfaced in the same stretch is an illustration of an estimate. It is not India’s rate.

After 2010 the global measure he named is the **Multidimensional Poverty Index**, associated again with Amartya Sen (audio “Amartya Singh”), and **designed at Oxford**. Poverty is not only income or consumption. His pictures: money is useless if the person is paralysed; a laptop is useless if the person cannot use it. Freedom from poverty, in the line he started and did not finish, is being able to take a decision of one’s own choice. The dimensions he wanted are **health, education, and a basic standard of living** — a bathroom, water, a motorcycle, a phone. “Garden” in the same list did not belong. Leave it out.

He said the global index has three dimensions and then both “**four, four, and six**” and “**ten in total**.” Those counts do not agree. Do not force them into one split.

**India’s own series.** Before **2014** the **Planning Commission** published a yearly estimate, using the poverty line, through 2014. For the **next six years** the government published none. In **2020** it started again, on the **National Multidimensional Poverty Index**, prepared by **NITI Aayog**. He said it is almost the global index, plus **two** parameters: **maternal health** and a **bank account**. The estimate is still a head-count: people who count as poor, divided by the population.

**Poverty reduction is the next class.** So is the rest of the pressure-group handout.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** Absolute poverty is deprivation, and it is the measure used for the Global South. Relative poverty is inequality **after** basic needs, and he put it in the West. The poverty line is the method **until 2010** globally and **until 2014** in India. The national index starts in **2020**, at NITI Aayog, not at the Planning Commission. **₹1,000** is his classroom line. The **50 per cent** cap is this class. The **20 per cent** cap is the 2020 amendment on the July note.</span>
