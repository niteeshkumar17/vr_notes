## 1. What the universe is, and how the picture changed (GEO-11-01)

- The universe = planets, stars, galaxies, space and time. Book: observable universe ≈ **93 billion light-years** across.
- **Geocentric:** ancient Greek picture — motionless Earth at the centre.
- **Aristarchus (312–230 BC):** Sun much farther and larger than the Moon; pushed against a simple Earth-centre model.
- **Eratosthenes (276–194 BC):** first successful measure of Earth’s size (class Fundamentals already uses him as the coiner of *geography*).
- **Ptolemy — *Almagest*:** **Ptolemaic system** — refined geocentric model.
- **Nicolaus Copernicus:** **heliocentric** — Sun at the centre of the solar system.
- **Galileo Galilei** and **Johannes Kepler** refined heliocentrism. Galileo: first telescope used for astronomy; evidence for Copernicus.

## 2. Theories of origin (GEO-11-01)

### 2.1 Traditional (mostly *solar system*, not the whole universe)

| Theory | Who (book) | Picture |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Nebular hypothesis** | **Immanuel Kant (1755)**; refined by **Pierre-Simon Laplace** | Cloud of gas and dust (**nebula**) contracts under gravity → spinning disc → planets and moons |
| **Planetesimal theory** | **Chamberlin and Moulton** (early 20th century) | Planets grow by accumulation of tiny orbiting **planetesimals** |
| **Tidal / wandering-star picture** (book continues this as a traditional line) | — | A wandering star approaches the Sun → **cigar-shaped** filament of material is pulled out → later condenses into planets |

### 2.2 Later: revised nebula, then the universe itself

- **Revised nebular hypothesis:** **Otto Schmidt** and **Carl Weizsäcker** (book spelling: Weizaskar). Sun already there, wrapped in a solar nebula of **hydrogen (H)** and **helium (He)**.
- **Accretion:** friction and collisions in a disc → material fuses into planets. Asteroids where gas rings converged.
- Scientists then asked not only “how planets?” but “how the **universe**?”

## 3. Big Bang / expanding universe (GEO-11-01)

- Also called the **expanding universe hypothesis**.
- Book body: universe began as a **singularity** (tiny ball, infinite density and temperature) ≈ **13.7 billion years** ago; chapter recap also writes **13.8 billion years**. Same order of magnitude — not two theories.
- That event starts **time, space, matter and energy**. Rapid expansion continues.
- **Edwin Hubble (1920):** galaxies recede; farther galaxies recede **faster**. Evidence = **redshift**. This recession rule is **Hubble’s Law** — main observational support for the Big Bang.
- Early universe: energy → matter; first atoms in the **first three minutes**; expansion cools the mix.
- **Four fundamental forces** (book order): **gravity** (weakest, infinite range) · **weak nuclear** · **electromagnetic** · **strong nuclear** (strongest, short range).
- Other evidence the book lists: **cosmic microwave background (CMB)** radiation, large-scale structure, abundance of light elements.

**Arguments the book records *against* the Big Bang (know the headings, not a Mains essay):**

| Objection | Book’s line |
|:---|:---|
| First law of thermodynamics | Scientific laws “break down” at the creation event |
| Entropy | If the early universe was fully homogeneous / isotropic, today’s clumpy universe is hard to square with entropy |
| Speed of light | Early **inflation** looks as if it outran the speed-of-light rule |

## 4. Galaxies (GEO-11-02)

- Gravity-bound systems of stars. Book: formation **300–500 million years** after the Big Bang; ~**100 billion** galaxies.
- **Hubble’s Law** again: as space expands, galaxies move apart; distance ↑ → speed ↑.
- Collisions: **stars almost never hit** (too far apart). Interaction is through **dark-matter halos**. Gravity redistributes stars; spiral arms can smear into **elliptical** shapes; starbursts and bigger bulges; some leftovers are **irregular**.

| Type | Shape / stars | Book traps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Irregular** | No distinct shape; often collision products | Gas + dust + **hot blue** (young) stars; smaller; relatively rare |
| **Elliptical** | Round / oval / cigar; **most abundant** | **Old red and yellow** stars; little gas/dust; **no new star formation** (book) |
| **Spiral** | Flattened disc + trailing arms | Arms trigger **young bright** stars. Examples: **Milky Way**, **Andromeda**. Some galaxies are hybrids / in transition |

### Milky Way

- **Barred spiral**; contains our solar system.
- Age ~**13.6–14 billion years**. Diameter ~**100,000 light-years**.
- ~**100–400 billion** stars + planets, gas, dust (**nebulae**).
- Central bulge + flat disc + spiral arms. Sun ~**26,000 light-years** from the centre.
- Centre: supermassive black hole **Sagittarius A\*** (~**4.1–4.5 million** solar masses).
- One solar-system lap around the centre ≈ **250 million years**.
- Name: Greek “river of milk” — the hazy band across the night sky. Dark matter shapes the rotation curve.

**Nebula (box):** giant cloud of gas and dust **inside** a galaxy; mostly H and He; gravity collapse → new stars. Prelims: **nebulae ≠ Cepheids** (see PYQs).

## 5. Stars (GEO-11-03)

- Hot, luminous spheres of gas (mostly H and He), held by gravity. Examples in the book: **Pollux, Sirius, Vega, Polaris**, the **Sun**.
- Source of heavier elements. Same *kind* of life cycle; mass decides lifespan and death.

**Star clusters**

| Kind | Where | Age / look |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Open** | Disc of a galaxy | Loose, relatively young; often still forming stars |
| **Globular** | Halo | Tight; very old; mostly red and yellow giants |
| **Young clusters** | Still in nebulae | Less than a few million years; still assembling |

### Life cycle (book diagram)

Common start: **nebula / giant gas cloud** → gravitational collapse → **protostar** → **T-Tauri** phase.

Then **nuclear fusion:** core ~**10 million K** → hydrogen nuclei fuse to helium. Gravity ↔ radiation pressure = **main-sequence** star (longest phase). Small stars (red dwarfs) burn slowly; massive stars burn fast.

- **Chandrasekhar limit:** maximum mass of a **white dwarf** ≈ **1.4 solar masses**. Above it the remnant cannot stay a white dwarf; collapse can give a **Type Ia supernova**.

**Black hole (later in the chapter):** region from which not even light escapes; massive-star collapse to a **singularity**. Invisible — inferred from nearby stars / specialised telescopes. **Event horizon** is the PYQ phrase (2017).

## 6. The Sun (GEO-11-04)

- Central star of the solar system. Book age ≈ **5 billion years** (Earth in class geomorphology is **4.6 billion years** — do not force them into one number).
- Diameter ≈ **1,392,000 km**.
- Core ≈ **15 million °C**; surface ≈ **5,500 °C**. Entirely **plasma** (not solid / liquid / gas).
- Composition pie (book): **73% hydrogen · 25% helium · 2% other**. Energy = core **nuclear fusion**.

**Inner layers:** core → **radiative zone** → **convective zone**.  
**Outer layers:** **photosphere** → **chromosphere** → transition region → **corona**.

| Layer / feature | Book details |
|:---|:---|
| **Core** | Fusion H → He; ~**15 million °C**; hottest |
| **Radiative zone** | Core to convection zone; energy as **photons**; ~**2–7 million °C** |
| **Convection zone** | Hot plasma rises, cool sinks; base ~**2 million °C** |
| **Photosphere** | Visible surface; book table **3,700–6,200 °C** |
| **Chromosphere** | Just above photosphere (~**2,000–3,000 km**); **3,700–10,000 °C**; reddish glow in eclipse |
| **Corona** | Outermost; **1–3 million °C**; seen in **total solar eclipse** |
| **Solar flares** | Magnetic-energy bursts; space weather; satellites / comms |
| **Sunspots** | Darker, cooler patches; strong magnetic fields; **11-year** cycle |
| **Solar wind** | Plasma streaming from the corona along radial field lines |
| **Solar cycle** | ~**11 years** for the magnetic poles to flip; another 11 to original polarity |
| **Solar maximum** | Many sunspots, flares, auroras; can hit power grids, satellites, GPS |
| **Solar minimum** | Quiet sunspots; wind still blows |
| **Sun halo** | **Not** a solar layer — optical: sunlight through **hexagonal ice crystals** in Earth’s air; **22° circular halo** (Sun or Moon) |
| **Alfvén critical surface** | Where solar material breaks free of gravity + magnetic binding. **Parker Solar Probe** crossed it in **2021** |

## 7. What is a planet? Solar system (GEO-11-05)

**International Astronomical Union (IAU), 2006 — three tests for a planet**

1. Orbits a star (in our system: the Sun).
2. Enough mass for a nearly **spherical** shape (hydrostatic equilibrium).
3. Has **cleared** its orbital neighbourhood.

Fail (3) but pass (1)–(2) and **not a satellite** → **dwarf planet**.

### How planets grow (book)

Nebula → local lumps collapse to a star + rotating disc → **planetesimals** by cohesion → collisions + **accretion** → planets that dominate their orbits.

### Our solar system

- Formed ≈ **4.5 billion years** ago from the **solar nebula**.
- Book overview list: Sun + **8 planets** + **63 moons** + asteroids, comets, dust and gas. **Use the planet table below for moon counts** — **63** is the old stock phrase; the same chapter later prints **Jupiter 95** and **Saturn 146**.
- **Inner / terrestrial** (inside the asteroid belt): **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars** — rock + metal, higher density.
- **Outer / Jovian (gas giants)** (beyond the belt): **Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune**.
- Diagram also marks **asteroid belt** (Mars–Jupiter), **Kuiper Belt** (beyond Neptune), **Oort Cloud** (much farther envelope).

| Planet | Book facts |
|:---|:---|
| **Mercury** | Smallest; closest; **57.9 million km**; year **88 days** |
| **Venus** | ~**108 million km** (Earth ~**150 million km**); **Earth’s twin** (size, mass, density) |
| **Earth** | Only known life; one moon |
| **Mars** | Fourth; dark patches; book flags plant-life / colonisation talk |
| **Jupiter** | Largest; H, He, methane; light/dark bands; **95 satellites**; very cold at its distance |
| **Saturn** | Second largest; thousands of ringlets in **7** major rings; **146** confirmed moons; orbit **29.5 years** |
| **Uranus** | ~**50×** Earth’s volume, **15×** mass (book); **27** satellites; telescope discovery, late 18th century; book: orbit one way, **clockwise (retrograde) rotation** |
| **Neptune** | Similar to Uranus; **14** known satellites; likely colder |

**Why terrestrial ≠ Jovian (book three lines)**

- Terrestrial: born **close** in; too warm for gases to condense; **strong solar wind** stripped gas; **small + low gravity** could not hold what escaped.
- Jovian: born **far**; winds too weak to strip gas; **larger**, so they kept atmospheres.

## 8. Dwarf planets, Moon, small bodies (GEO-11-06)

**IAU dwarf planet:** orbits the Sun · nearly round · **has not cleared** its path · **not a moon**.

| Body | Book placement |
|:---|:---|
| **Pluto** | Reclassified **2006**; Kuiper Belt; **5** moons, largest **Charon** |
| **Eris** | Scattered disc; slightly smaller than Pluto, **more massive**; found **2005**; moon **Dysnomia** |
| **Haumea** | Kuiper; elongated because it spins fast; moons **Namaka**, **Hiʻiaka** |
| **Makemake** | Kuiper; found **2005**; moon found **2016** (book: **MK2**) |
| **Ceres** | Largest in the **asteroid belt**; found **1801**; water ice / possible subsurface ocean talk |

### Moon (extras beyond class Theia)

- Earth’s only natural satellite. Book name for the impact story: **Big Splat** (~**4.44 billion years**; class **~4.5**).
- Mean distance **384,000 km** (book also **238,900 miles**). Sidereal month **27.32 days**.
- **Tidal locking:** rotation period = orbital period → about **59%** of the surface is ever seen from Earth.
- Atmosphere extremely thin.
- **Super Moon** = perigee **356,500 km**; **Micro Moon** = apogee **406,700 km**. Book mentions a “Super Pink Moon.”

### Comets, asteroids, meteors

- **Comets:** “cosmic snowballs” — frozen gases + rock + dust. Near the Sun they grow a glowing head and a tail (can stretch millions of miles). Many in the Kuiper Belt / farther reservoirs.
- **Asteroids:** rocky leftovers, mostly **Mars–Jupiter** main belt. **Trojans** share Jupiter’s orbit (ahead and behind). **Near-Earth Objects (NEOs)** come close to Earth.
- **Kuiper Belt:** disc from Neptune ~**30 astronomical units (AU)** to ~**50 AU**; ices, dwarf planets, comets, **trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs)**.

| Term | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Meteoroid** | Fragment of comet/asteroid **still in space** |
| **Meteor** | The **streak** (“shooting star”) as it burns in the atmosphere |
| **Meteorite** | The piece that **hits the ground** |

## UPSC Quick Recall

- Hubble **redshift** + **CMB** = expansion / Big Bang evidence. Asteroids are **not** that evidence (2012).
- **Elliptical** = old, little gas, **no new stars** (book). **Irregular** = young blue + collisions.
- **Chandrasekhar 1.4 M☉** = white-dwarf ceiling.
- IAU planet vs dwarf = **cleared the orbit** or not. Pluto **2006**.
- **Ceres** = asteroid belt; **Pluto / Haumea / Makemake** = Kuiper; **Eris** = scattered disc.
- Meteor**oid** / meteor / meteor**ite** = location (space / sky / ground).
- Asteroids **Mars–Jupiter**; comets are **not** “between Venus and Mercury” (2011).
- Cepheids ≠ nebulae (2023 swap).
- Sun halo = **ice crystals in air**, not a photosphere feature.
- Parker Probe **Alfvén surface 2021**.
- ISS ~**400 km / 90 min**; Tiangong **2022**; Indian station **2035**.
