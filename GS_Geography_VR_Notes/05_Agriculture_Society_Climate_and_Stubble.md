## 2. Village vs Town, and Why India’s Urbanisation Hurts (GEO-07-02)

**Census 2011** (last completed census): **~68–69% rural**, **~31% urban**. India is urbanising fast (metros, rural out-migration). The **United States (US)** and **Western Europe (EU / European Union)** are already **>80% urban** — that is *not* India’s problem. India’s problem is **distress / duress migration**: people leave because agriculture does not pay, not because a factory has already hired them.

**You cannot have smart cities without smart villages.** New urban infrastructure is never enough if rural distress keeps feeding slums and squatters (class: Delhi dividers near Patel Nagar / Connaught Place; slums of Delhi and Mumbai filled by migrants, not “old Delhi-born” poor).

### 2.1 There is no official definition of a “village”

Not population. Not administration. Not a primary-health-centre test. **Dhanbad** is a **town** even though coal mining is a *primary* activity — so “primary vs secondary vs tertiary” does not define village vs town.

Official papers rarely say *gaon* / *gram*. The working term is **mauza** = **revenue village**: the **smallest territorial unit for collecting land revenue**. For all practical purposes, that *is* a village in India.

### 2.2 Two kinds of town (Prelims lock)

| Type | Defined by | Rule |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Statutory town** | **Administration** | If a **municipal corporation / municipal council**, **cantonment board**, or **town area committee** governs the place, it is a town — *regardless* of population, density, occupation or infrastructure |
| **Census town** | **Population criteria** (Census of India) | Must satisfy **all three** conditions below |

**Census-town triple (all three):**

1. Population **≥ 5,000**
2. Density **≥ 400 persons / km²**
3. **≥ 75% of males** (not of total population) in **non-agricultural** work (any other primary / secondary / tertiary job is fine)

Eastern **Uttar Pradesh (UP)**, **Bihar**, **West Bengal (WB)** villages are often large *and* dense — they already pass (1) and (2). **Criterion 3** is what usually separates town from village.

## 3. Agriculture Is a Climate Bet (GEO-07-03)

Agri is high-risk **without** high-gain. A farmer is betting on climate even where irrigation exists.

### 3.1 Punjab wheat, March 2022 — grain shrink

**Punjab** is among India’s better-off agri regions (irrigation + machines). In **2022** the harvested **wheat grain was smaller** → lower yield. Scientists: **March heatwave** during **grain filling** (panicles filling).

Wheat is **rabi**: sown **October–November**, harvested **March–April**. Apparent sun moves from equator toward Cancer from March → summer arrives **earlier in the peninsula**, **mid-May peak in North India**.

| Region | Typical wheat harvest |
|:---|:---|
| **Madhya Pradesh (MP)**, **Maharashtra (MH)** | mid-**March** |
| **Punjab** | first week of **April** |
| **Himachal Pradesh (HP)** | last week of **April** |

Class analogy (no formula): on a hot afternoon, sprinting spends energy on **sweating / cooling**, so less energy is left for the sprint. A wheat plant does the same via **transpiration**. High March heat → more cooling cost → **less energy for grain filling** → **smaller grain**. Irrigation did not save the farmer.

### 3.2 Climate can hit you from another continent

- **El Niño** — Pacific warming that often **weakens the Indian monsoon**. A Pacific event, Indian farm incomes.
- **Desert locusts, 2020** (lockdown March–April pictures; Punjab, **Haryana (HR)**, **Rajasthan (RJ)**). Trigger: **exceptionally heavy 2019 rain in East Africa** → waterlogging → locust boom → swarms moved north via **Iran–Pakistan**. **UN FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization):** a **1 km** swarm (~**40 million** insects) can eat in a day what **~35,000 people** eat. Climate event in East Africa, crop loss in North-West India.

### 3.3 Meteorological drought becomes agricultural drought

Where irrigation is thin, a monsoon failure is not only “less rain” — it is **crop failure**. No rural industry → the only exit is the city. Unskilled distress migrants become **urban poor** (slums, congestion, squatters) instead of rural poor.

**Climate migration** (class article): people *do not want* to move. Two belts named:

| Belt | Where | Hook |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Marathwada (MH)** | around **Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar**, **Beed**, **Jalna**, **Aurangabad** belt | sugar / drought / later also farmer-suicide geography |
| **Bundelkhand** | **UP:** Hamirpur, Banda, Mahoba, Jhansi, Jalaun, Lalitpur. **MP:** Panna, Tikamgarh, Chhatarpur, Datia, Damoh | drought-prone; class: many Rajendra Nagar domestic workers are from here. **Panna (MP):** **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** — progressively less rain, rising heat. Estimate: Bundelkhand mean **+3.5 °C by 2100** |

## 4. Feminisation of Agriculture (GEO-07-04)

**Meaning (class, lock this):** *not* “more women than men in the field”. It means the **share of women in the agri workforce is rising** compared with the past — because **young males migrate** and women stay on the farm.

### 4.1 Sex ratio explains the map

**Sex ratio (India)** = **females per 1,000 males**.

**Census 2011:** India **943** (books / some government sites print **940** — class: **both acceptable**). **Rural 949**. **Urban 929**.

Migration is **age-selective** and **sex-selective**. Young men move more easily (jobs). Young women face safety / family resistance (class: Delhi’s image). The old rarely move for a career. Result:

- **Urban:** adverse sex ratio → weaker gender sensitivity; in extreme form, crime against women
- **Rural:** relatively more women left behind → they work the fields → **feminisation**

### 4.2 Three exam challenges

| Challenge | Class figure / fact |
|:---|:---|
| **Exploitation** | Women paid less than men for the same work. Extreme: **forced hysterectomies / tubal cutting** of women sugarcane workers in **Marathwada (MH)** so a contractor couple stays “productive” through the **October–April/May** crushing season (~**7–8 months**). In **UP** the *farmer* harvests and carts cane to the mill; in **MH** the **mill** must harvest and transport — so mills hire contractors → couples. Domestic *and* foreign press have covered this |
| **Workforce vs title** | Women ≈ **60%** of agri **workforce**; **< 15%** of **land titles** in women’s names → they enter as **landless workers**, not owner-cultivators |
| **Machines not designed for women’s work** | Productivity needs technology (the **Green Revolution** started with **Norman Borlaug**’s **high-yielding variety (HYV)** wheat in Mexico). **Tractors** exist for **ploughing** — culturally reserved for **men** in most of India. **Tea-leaf plucking** is largely women’s work — almost no machine. Machines are built for whoever has **purchasing power**, and that is concentrated with men |

### 4.3 Credit trap when the husband has left

The wife needs seed / fertiliser / pesticide **credit**. **Institutional** route: scheduled commercial bank, **Regional Rural Bank (RRB)**, cooperative — cheap, because **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** forces a farm quota (including small/marginal farmers). Bank still wants **collateral**. Land is in the *husband’s* name, and he is in the city. Formal “no-collateral” small loans are often blocked in practice.

**Farm-loan waivers** (class: **TN**, **MH** extensions) create a **moral hazard**: the honest repayer is punished; the strategic defaulter is rewarded before elections → more deliberate default → banks stop lending → women go to the **village moneylender**. North India class rate: **2–3% per month** = **24–36% per annum** vs a bank’s ~**0.5%** (class contrast). Two failed monsoons + that interest → public humiliation / violence → **suicide**.

## 5. Farmer Suicides — Not a “Backward-State Only” Story (GEO-07-05)

**National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB):**

| Window | Figure (class) |
|:---|:---|
| **1995–2015** | **> 3 lakh** farmer suicides. After the 2015 report hit Parliament, the government **stopped publishing** the series for **three years** |
| **2023** | **10,786** — **cultivators + landless agricultural workers** |

**Maharashtra** has high per-capita income and is still the worst belt: class **~35–40%** of India’s farmer suicides, concentrated in **Marathwada** and **Vidarbha** (link yesterday’s **cotton / pink bollworm** map).

**Tamil Nadu** has industry + social security + a well-irrigated **Cauvery** delta — and a **drought-prone south-east**.

### Why south TN can starve in a “normal monsoon” year

Most of India: **June–September South-West (SW) monsoon**. The peninsula splits the SW into an **Arabian Sea** branch and a **Bay of Bengal** branch. The **Bay branch bypasses far-south / south-east TN**. **Maximum TN rain is October–November–December from the North-East (NE) monsoon** (winds that have picked up moisture over the Bay).

**2016:** all-India **SW monsoon normal**, agri output good — **TN** had one of the **worst droughts of the century**, **~65% rainfall deficit** (class: if NE rain is 100 cm, that year ~**35 cm**). Farmers then protested at **Jantar Mantar** (half-clad, dead rats in the mouth, skeletal remains of suicide victims). Class point: urban India often does not even *count* this as a social problem.

## 7. Climate Change and Agriculture Cut Both Ways (GEO-07-07)

### 7.1 Greenhouse, then the Earth

A parked car / a glass-walled **greenhouse** (or a **polyhouse**) lets heat in and slows its escape → inside warmer than outside. **Greenhouse gases (GHGs)** — **carbon dioxide (CO₂)**, **methane (CH₄)**, **nitrous oxide (N₂O)**, **sulphur hexafluoride (SF₆)**, water vapour — do something similar to Earth. More GHGs → more trapped heat → **global warming**.

**Physics (class, no LaTeX):** a **hot** body radiates at a **short** wavelength; a **cooler** body at a **longer** wavelength. The Sun is extremely hot → **short-wave** insolation crosses the air and **heats the land**. The land is cooler → **long-wave** radiation heats the air above. Warm air expands, rises → **low pressure** → winds (high → low) → moisture → rain. Change temperature → change pressure → change wind → change precipitation = **climate change**.

**Climate change** = change in **temperature, wind and precipitation** of an area **over a period of time**.

It *has* been **natural** (glacial / interglacial cycles): change in **axial tilt**, change in the **elliptical orbit**, **sunspot** cycles. **Present-day speed** is **anthropogenic** (human GHG rise).

### 7.2 Who emits, and why the tropics are hit harder

Class shares (order-of-magnitude, for Mains colour):

| Source | Share of GHGs (class) |
|:---|:---|
| Transport | ~**13%** |
| Industry | ~**21%** |
| **Agriculture + land-use change** (**UN FAO**) | **~18%** |

Any climate shift hits **agri more than most other sectors**. The hit is **more pronounced in the tropics** than in the temperate zone:

1. **More people** (eastern India, tropical China, South-East Asia) → more food demand.
2. Tropics already farm **~12 months** (kharif + rabi). Temperate Canada may have **~8 months** under snow; **+2 °C** can *lengthen* that season (snow 4 months → 2). Tropics get **heatwaves**, not a longer season.
3. Tropical countries are **poorer** — fewer drought-resistant / flood-tolerant seeds, less irrigation, almost no climate-controlled farming. A ₹100/kg onion shock hits the poor, not a Delhi rich household.

Faulty agri practices also *cause* the GHGs. Two big ones in this lecture: **flooded paddy** and **livestock**. Third: **stubble burning**.

## 8. Flooded Paddy vs Direct Seeding of Rice (GEO-07-08)

India has the **largest area** under paddy and is the **largest rice producer** — so it is also a **top methane source** from rice (*The Hindu*, 23 July 2026). Class: paddy fields ≈ **10% of world CH₄**.

**The problem is not rice. The problem is the transplantation method.**

### 8.1 Transplantation (the common method)

1. Sow seed in a **nursery** (raised bed in one corner).
2. **Plough** and **flood** the main field; **puddle** (break soil aggregates to fines). **Water-holding capacity is inversely proportional to grain size** — sand drains, clay holds.
3. After ~**30 days**, seedlings ~**15 cm** → workers transplant **two seedlings per hill** in rows.
4. Field stays flooded. Rice makes **aerenchyma** and can live **waterlogged ~7–8 days**. **Weeds cannot**. Water = **natural herbicide**.
5. Dead weeds decompose. Standing water = **no oxygen** → **anaerobic** bacteria → **CH₄**.

Also: heavy **labour** (Punjab / HR / western UP rice depends on migrants from eastern UP, Bihar, Bengal — workers are scarce even at high wages); **25–30 irrigations**; class already gave **~5,000 litres of water per kg of Punjab rice**; **groundwater** collapse.

**System of Rice Intensification (SRI)** is **not** DSR — class: a hybrid (very early, two-leaf transplant). Do not mix them in Prelims.

### 8.2 Direct Seeding of Rice (DSR)

Seed drill on a tractor puts seed **straight into the main field**. No nursery, no transplant labour.

**Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD):** never flood. Keep the field wet. Clay **swells when wet, cracks when dry** — cracks = time for the next irrigation. **~15 irrigations** instead of 25–30. Crop **matures 7–10 days earlier**. **No standing water → no transplant-style methane pulse.**

**Kharif 2026:** Punjab DSR area **+36% to >4 lakh ha** — **El Niño** year, June–July rain deficit, plus a government push (new seed varieties in a later “seeds” lecture).

**Challenge that keeps DSR unpopular:** **weeds**.

## 9. Livestock Methane and “Non-Veg Milk” (GEO-07-09)

Cattle release **more methane than paddy fields** (class). Four-chamber stomach; **enteric fermentation** (anaerobic digestion; class named the gut bacteria). Most CH₄ leaves as **belching**, not as dung.

Why is a “natural” gut process counted as **anthropogenic**?

1. **Numbers:** **artificial insemination** and veterinary care → more cattle than a natural herd.
2. **Diet:** high-protein / hard-to-digest feed raises milk *and* methane.

Milk depends on **breed + diet**.

| Animal (class) | Daily milk (order of magnitude) |
|:---|:---|
| Indigenous (**Gir**, **Sahiwal**) | ~**3.5 L** even for a good animal |
| Cross-bred (India) | ~**7–8 L** |
| **Jersey / Brown Swiss** in India | ~**13–14 L** |
| Same exotic types in **Israel / US** | up to ~**35–36 L** |

Gap is largely **feed**. **“Non-veg milk”** (news when **USAID** pressure to open India’s dairy market): US / Israeli farms mix **intestines** and **blood meal** (dried blood) into feed. India: **~8 crore** small/marginal farmers with **1–2 backyard animals** vs US mega-herds (scale + price shock). Hindu ritual use of milk is a second political block if the cow was fed meat.

## 10. Stubble Burning (GEO-07-10)

**October–November smog** in Delhi is *partly* **paddy stubble** from Punjab / HR / western UP. Class will **not** die on the year-round share. Article cited: stubble ≈ **14% of Delhi–NCR PM2.5 (Particulate Matter 2.5)** *across the year* — **small for the year, large in those two months**.

### 10.1 Why the farmer burns

This belt grows **rice as a commercial crop** (basmati for export; non-basmati almost entirely lifted by the **Food Corporation of India (FCI)** / state agencies — not eaten locally). **Wheat** is the staple. **~90%** of paddy and wheat is cut by a **combine harvester** (harvest + thresh) — it takes only the **ear**, leaves **hard, silica-rich non-basmati stems**. Transplanting put **2–3 seedlings per hill**, so the stubble is a clump.

**Window:** rice harvested ~**1 November** → wheat must be **drilled by ~15 November** (after that, cold kills germination) = **~15 days**. A normal seed drill **cannot** push through standing stubble. Migrant labour has already gone home.

Burning is free, instant, and **sanitises** the field (monsoon fungi, pests, reptiles). **Myth:** ash adds nutrients. Class: some nutrients are added, **more are destroyed** → **net loss**.

### 10.2 Why winter makes it a Delhi problem

In winter the Sun is toward **Capricorn** → land cold → air above **dense and sinking**. Summer burning would loft the plume. Winter burning parks **oxides of carbon / nitrogen / sulphur + particulate** in the **breathing layer**. Calm winter air compounds it.

### 10.3 Solutions that work only if the calendar allows

**Pusa bio-decomposer** (**Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI) / Pusa**): capsules → spray → stubble becomes manure in **~30 days**. **Delhi** has used it with success. **Punjab / HR** usually have only **~15 days** — the capsule is too slow.

Why not transplant earlier and harvest by mid-October?

- **2001 Punjab:** **8 hours free farm power** → farmers tried **two rice crops a year** in a **semi-arid** state that cannot grow even one rice crop on rainfall alone → **groundwater crash**.
- **2009:** Punjab and HR laws — **no paddy transplant before 15 June ± 5 days** (held through **2024**). That late start → late harvest → no 30-day decomposer window.

**Variety trap**

| Variety | Who | Duration | Yield (class) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pusa-44** | **IARI** under **Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR)**, ~**1993–94** | **150–160 days** (~**130 days after** a 30-day nursery) | up to **~6,000 kg/ha** |
| **PR-121, 122, 126, 128** | **Punjab Agricultural University (PAU), Ludhiana** | e.g. **PR-128 ≈ 120 days** | a bit less (~**4,500–5,000 kg/ha**) but **~40 days saved** |

Transplant **20 June** + Pusa-44 → harvest **~1 November** → **15-day** wheat window. Swap in **PR-128** → theoretically **~55 days** — enough for Pusa decomposer **or** a **55–60 day moong (green gram)** crop. Many farmers **take the moong**, then still transplant PR rice, and the rice–wheat window **collapses again**. Others stay on Pusa-44. **2025:** Punjab tried to **ban Pusa-44** and refused procurement; farmers brought seed from HR; **High Court** restored the variety.

**Happy Seeder / Super Seeder:** cuts stubble, lays it as mulch, drills wheat in one pass. **50–80% capital subsidy** (higher for cooperatives in the class example). Still unpopular: needs a **high-horsepower tractor**, **slow**, **more diesel** — subsidy is one-time, **operating cost** is every season.

### 10.4 What actually reduces fires

Lectures do not move farmers. **Haryana has controlled burning better than Punjab** by **paying**:

- Class example: if cutting stubble costs **₹1,500/acre**, pay **₹1,500/acre** for *not* burning → farmer still nets a gain versus fire
- Village that goes from **>80%** burning last year to **zero** this year: **₹1 lakh** to the **gram panchayat** for welfare

Then **give the straw a price**: manure; **second-generation ethanol** (residue, not cereal — contrast first-generation food-to-fuel); **paper** (class: **Trident** mill near **Bathinda**); biopower. **Haryana** puts **balers** at block / village level so transport is cheap.

**Enforce:** fines that are actually **collected** (pre-election write-offs kill the signal). Officers were blocked at village gates → **satellite geotagging**. Farmers adapted: **International Forum for Environment, Sustainability and Technology (IFEST) Stubble Burning Status Report 2025** — fires shifted to **late afternoon / evening**, when the monitoring satellite is not overhead. A claimed **~90% drop** can be a **sensor gap**, not a behaviour change.

**Long-term:** change the **cropping pattern**. **Punjab and Haryana are not made for rice.** Grow what soil and climate can carry without free power and a June-15 law.

**Environment as tragedy of the commons / free-rider:** Delhi blames Punjab in October; Punjab asks why Delhi runs **ACs** for **7–8 months** on a grid that is **>70% coal**, and why it will not use public transport. Blame is cheap; nobody owns the clean-up.
