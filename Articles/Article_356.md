# Article 356 — Provisions in Case of Failure of Constitutional Machinery in States (President's Rule)

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

## Text (Simplified)

1. If the President, on receipt of a report from the Governor of a State or **otherwise**, is satisfied that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution, the President may by Proclamation:
   * (a) Assume to himself all or any of the functions of the Government of the State and all or any of the powers vested in or exercisable by the Governor.
   * (b) Declare that the powers of the Legislature of the State shall be exercisable by or under the authority of **Parliament**.
   * (c) Make incidental and consequential provisions, including suspending in whole or in part the operation of any provisions of this Constitution relating to any body or authority in the State (except High Courts).
2. Any such Proclamation may be revoked or varied by a subsequent Proclamation.
3. Every Proclamation shall cease to operate at the expiration of **two months** unless approved by resolutions of both Houses of Parliament (by **Simple Majority**).
4. If approved, it continues for **six months** at a time, up to a maximum period of **three years** (subject to 44th Amendment conditions for extensions beyond one year: National Emergency in operation + EC certificate that elections cannot be held).

## Key Points for UPSC
* **Grounds for Imposition:** 
  1. *Article 356:* Failure of constitutional machinery (Governor's report or 'otherwise').
  2. *Article 365:* Failure of State to comply with, or to give effect to, any directions given by the Union Executive.
* **High Court Powers Protected:** The President **cannot** assume the powers of the State High Court or suspend any constitutional provisions relating to High Courts (Article 356(1) proviso).
* **Parliamentary Approval & Majority:**
  * Must be approved within **2 months** by both Houses by a **Simple Majority** (contrast with Article 352 which requires Special Majority within 1 month).
* **Landmark Case — S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994):**
  * Proclamation under Article 356 is subject to **Judicial Review**.
  * The State Legislative Assembly should **not be dissolved immediately** upon proclamation; it can only be dissolved **after Parliament approves** the proclamation. Until then, it can only be kept under **suspended animation**.
  * Floor test on the floor of the House is the **sole constitutional test** to determine majority support.
  * Secularism and Federalism are part of the **Basic Structure** of the Constitution.
* <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**One Nation One Election (ONOE) Misuse Trap:** Holding a fallen state assembly in perpetual suspended animation under Article 356 until the next synchronized national election date directly violates the Bommai guidelines, as President's Rule cannot be used as an administrative tool for electoral calendar alignment.</span>
