# Article 111: Assent to Bills & Presidential Veto Powers

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Executive & Legislature - Legislative Powers of President, Part V Chapter 2]`
> **Last Updated:** 2026-08-17
> **Related Notes:** [Polity_Lec2_AbheyKumar_President.md](../GS_Polity_VR_Notes/Polity_Lec2_AbheyKumar_President.md)

### Active Recall Questions

<details><summary>What are the three constitutional choices of the President when an Ordinary Bill is presented for assent under Article 111?</summary>

1. **Declare Assent:** Bill becomes an Act (law).
2. **Withhold Assent (Absolute Veto):** Bill dies immediately and permanently. (Exercised on advice of CoM — e.g., *PEPSU Appropriation Bill 1954*, *MP Salaries Bill 1991*).
3. **Return for Reconsideration (Suspensive Veto):** Bill is sent back with/without presidential recommendations.
</details>

<details><summary>What happens if Parliament passes a returned Bill again?</summary>

- If the Bill is passed again by both Houses (with or without amendment) by a **Simple Majority**, the President **"shall not withhold assent therefrom"** (assent becomes mandatory).
- Example: *MP Prevention of Disqualification Bill, 2006* returned by Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam, re-passed by Parliament, and subsequently assented.
</details>

<details><summary>Can the President return a Money Bill for reconsideration under Article 111?</summary>

- <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**NO (Article 111 Proviso):** The proviso to Article 111 explicitly states that the President may return the bill *"if it is not a Money Bill"*. The President has **NO power of suspensive veto over Money Bills**. For a Money Bill, the President can only either give Assent or Withhold Assent.</span>
</details>

<details><summary>Does the President have any veto power over Constitutional Amendment (CA) Bills?</summary>

- **NO:** Under the **24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971** amending Article 368(2), when a CA Bill is presented, the President **"shall give his assent"**. The President cannot withhold, return, or pocket a CA Bill.
</details>

<details><summary>What is a "Pocket Veto" and where does it originate?</summary>

- Article 111 does NOT prescribe any time limit within which the President must declare his decision.
- By keeping the bill pending indefinitely on his desk without declaring assent, withholding assent, or returning it, the President exercises an informal **Pocket Veto** (e.g., Zail Singh and the *Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986*).
</details>
