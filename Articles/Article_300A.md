# Article 300A — Right to Property (Constitutional Right)

> **Part:** XII — Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits
> **Subject:** Persons not to be deprived of property save by authority of law

## Text
No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law.

## Key Points
- **Originally:** Right to Property was a **Fundamental Right** under Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31.
- **44th Amendment Act, 1978** (Janata Party Government): Converted Right to Property from a **Fundamental Right** to a **Constitutional Right** under Art. 300A.
- This **reduced its status** — it is no longer enforceable under Art. 32 (right to constitutional remedies) but can be enforced under Art. 226 (HC writ jurisdiction).
- **Significance for Socialism:** The existence of Right to Property (even as a constitutional right) proves India is NOT a Marxian-socialist state (which requires NO private property).
- India's Democratic Socialism is **flexible** — allows private property while aiming for equitable distribution through [Art. 39(b)](Article_39b.md) and [Art. 39(c)](Article_39c.md).

## Historical Context
- Indira Gandhi's government had issues with Right to Property as a FR because it obstructed land reform legislation.
- The Janata Party government (post-Emergency) amended it to balance individual rights with state's welfare objectives.
