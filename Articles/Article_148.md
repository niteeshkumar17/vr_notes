# Article 148 — Comptroller and Auditor-General of India

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

## Text & Constitutional Provisions (Simplified)

1. **Establishment & Appointment:** There shall be a Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG) who shall be appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**.
2. **Tenure & Removal:** The CAG shall only be removed from office in **like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court** (i.e. under Article 124(4) on proved misbehaviour or incapacity via special majority in both Houses of Parliament).
3. **Oath:** The CAG takes an oath before the President in accordance with the **Third Schedule**.
4. **Post-Retirement Ineligibility:** The CAG is **not eligible for further office** either under the Government of India or under the Government of any State after retirement (guarantees constitutional independence).
5. **Administrative Expenses:** The administrative expenses of the CAG's office, including all salaries, allowances, and pensions, shall be **charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India** (not subject to parliamentary vote).

## Key Points for UPSC Prelims & Mains
* **Guardian of Public Purse:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar termed the CAG as *"the most important officer under the Constitution of India"* and one of the four bulwarks of the democratic system (alongside SC, UPSC, and ECI).
* **Term of Office:** 6 years or up to 65 years of age, whichever is earlier (prescribed by CAG's DPC Act, 1971).
* **Salary & Conditions of Service:** Determined by Parliament by law (equal to that of a Supreme Court judge) and cannot be varied to CAG's disadvantage after appointment.
* **Audit Mandate:** Audits all receipts and expenditures from the Consolidated Fund of India and each State/UT with Legislative Assembly, Contingency Funds, and Public Accounts.
