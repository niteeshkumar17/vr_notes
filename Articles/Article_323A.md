# Article 323A — Administrative Tribunals

> **Part XIV-A: Tribunals (Inserted by 42nd Amendment Act, 1976)**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

## Constitutional Provisions & Scope

* **Article 323A:** Empowers **Parliament ONLY** to establish administrative tribunals for adjudication of disputes relating to recruitment and conditions of service of public servants (Union, State, local authorities, public corporations).
* **Key Features:**
  * Can be established ONLY by Parliament (not State Legislatures).
  * Only ONE central administrative tribunal (CAT) and state administrative tribunals (SATs) can be set up under Art 323A.

## Article 323A vs Article 323B Distinction

| Parameter | Article 323A | Article 323B |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Subject Matter** | Public Service matters ONLY | Specific matters (Taxation, Foreign Exchange, Industrial/Labour, Land Reforms, Ceiling on Property, Elections, Foodstuffs) |
| **Establishing Authority** | Established **ONLY by Parliament** | Established by **Parliament OR State Legislatures** (within legislative competence) |
| **Hierarchy** | Single hierarchy of tribunals | Multiple tribunals can be created |

## Land Mark Judgments & Recent Legislation
* **L. Chandra Kumar Case (1997):** SC ruled that power of judicial review of High Courts under Art 226/227 and Supreme Court under Art 32 is part of **Basic Structure**. Decisions of tribunals are subject to appeal before Division Bench of High Court.
* <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 & 2026 Bill:** SC invalidated key provisions of 2021 Act for violating separation of powers. Parliament passed **Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026** establishing the **National Tribunals Commission (NTC)** (HQ: New Delhi, Chairperson: Retd SC Judge/HC CJ + 4 Members: 2 Judicial, 2 Technical) for uniform selection and oversight.</span>
