# Article 23 — Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings and Forced Labour

> **Part:** III — Fundamental Rights (Right against Exploitation)
> **Subject:** Prohibition of traffic in human beings and begar and other similar forms of forced labour

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

## Text

(1) Traffic in human beings and begar and other similar forms of forced labour are prohibited and any contravention of this provision shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law.
(2) Nothing in this article shall prevent the State from imposing compulsory service for public purposes, and in imposing such service the State shall not make any discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste or class or any of them.

## Key Points & Constitutional Scope
* **Available Against Both State and Private Individuals:** Unlike many fundamental rights that operate only against State action, Article 23 protects individuals against both the State and private entities.
* **Positive Injunction on State:** Imposes an obligation on the State to eradicate practices of human trafficking, *begar*, bonded labour, and forced labour.
* **Exceptions [Clause (2)]:** The State can impose compulsory public service (e.g., military conscription or disaster response), provided no discrimination is made based *only* on religion, race, caste, or class (gender is not listed in Clause 2, allowing gender-specific compulsory services if rational).

## Landmark Judicial Precedents & Evolution
* **People's Union for Democratic Rights (PUDR) v. Union of India (1982) [Asiad Workers Case]:**
  * Supreme Court held that "force" under Article 23 is not restricted to physical violence or legal coercion.
  * **Economic Compulsion:** When poverty, hunger, or destitution forces a worker to accept remuneration below the statutory minimum wage, it constitutes **"forced labour"** within the meaning of Article 23.
* **Sanjit Roy v. State of Rajasthan (1983):**
  * SC struck down provisions of the *Rajasthan Famine Relief Works Employees Act* that allowed paying famine-affected workers less than the minimum wage.
  * Held that the State cannot take advantage of the helpless condition of citizens struck by drought or famine to extract labour below statutory minimum wages.
* **<span style="color: #e53e3e;">Rural Work & Minimum Wages Scrutiny (Aug 2026):</span>** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">In the MGNREGA / VB-G RAM G Act hearing (Aug 2026), the Supreme Court examined the argument that rural work provided below statutory minimum wage floors violates Article 23, reaffirming that economic vulnerability cannot be leveraged by the State to dilute wage entitlements.</span>
* **<span style="color: #e53e3e;">Delink from Minimum Wages Act, 1948 (Sep 2026 CA):</span>** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">MGNREGA notified wages were delinked from the Minimum Wages Act, 1948 from 2009, so scheme wages need not equal the statutory minimum — the *Sanjit Roy* / *PUDR* Article 23 floor is the constitutional argument against that gap.</span>
