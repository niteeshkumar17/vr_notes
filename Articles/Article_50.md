# Article 50 — Separation of Judiciary from Executive

> **Part IV — Directive Principles of State Policy**

## Text

**50. Separation of judiciary from executive.**

The State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.

---

## Key Points

*   **Nature:** This is a **Directive Principle of State Policy (DPSP)** — non-justiciable, meaning it cannot be enforced through courts, but it is fundamental in governance.
*   **Significance:** This is the **only explicit reference to Separation of Powers** in the Indian Constitution.
*   **Purpose:** Ensures that the judiciary operates independently from the executive, particularly at the lower court level where magistrates historically had both executive and judicial functions.
*   **Implementation:** The Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC, now BNSS) provisions for separation of judicial and executive magistrates implement this directive.
*   **Current Status:** Separation has been largely achieved — Judicial Magistrates function under the High Court, while Executive Magistrates function under the State Government (District Administration).
