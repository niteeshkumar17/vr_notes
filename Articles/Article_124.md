## Text (Key Clauses)

**124. Establishment and constitution of Supreme Court.**

(1) There shall be a Supreme Court of India consisting of a Chief Justice of India and, until Parliament by law prescribes a larger number, of not more than seven other Judges.

(2) Every Judge of the Supreme Court shall be appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal after consultation with such of the Judges of the Supreme Court and of the High Courts in the States as the President may deem necessary for the purpose and shall hold office until he attains the age of sixty-five years:

Provided that in the case of appointment of a Judge other than the Chief Justice, the Chief Justice of India shall always be consulted.

---

## Key Points

*   **Original sanctioned strength:** CJI + 7 = 8 judges (1950).
*   Parliament has power to **increase** the number of judges by law (Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956).
*   **Current strength (2026):** CJI + 33 = 34 judges (proposed to increase to 38).
*   **Appointment:** By the President, after consultation with CJI and other SC/HC judges → evolved into the **Collegium System** (Three Judges Cases: 1981, 1993, 1998).
*   **NJAC (National Judicial Appointments Commission)** — established by 99th Amendment + NJAC Act 2014, struck down by SC in 2015 as unconstitutional (violated judicial independence).
*   **Retirement age:** 65 years for SC judges; 62 years for HC judges.
*   **Qualifications:** Must be a citizen of India + (a) Judge of HC for 5 years, or (b) Advocate of HC for 10 years, or (c) Distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President.
*   <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Judges Cases Matrix (Evolution of Collegium):**
    *   **First Judges Case (1981 — *S.P. Gupta*):** Executive primacy; 'Consultation' does not mean concurrence.
    *   **Second Judges Case (1993 — *SCAORA*):** Judicial primacy established; collective opinion of CJI + 2 senior-most judges; 'Consultation' = Concurrence.
    *   **Third Judges Case (1998 — *Special Reference 1 of 1998*):** Expanded to 5-member collegium (CJI + 4 senior-most SC judges) for SC appointments and transfers.
    *   **Fourth Judges Case (2015 — *NJAC Case*):** 99th CAA 2014 & NJAC Act struck down (4:1) for violating Judicial Independence as part of the Basic Structure.</span>
*   <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Transparency & Accountability Jurisprudence (Modern Debates):**
    *   **Justice Ujjal Bhuyan's Observation (2026):** Openness in the collegium process is essential to strengthen public confidence and ensure merit as the governing principle.
    *   ***CPIO vs Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019):*** 5-Judge Bench held the Office of the CJI is a "Public Authority" under Section 2(h) of the RTI Act, 2005; judicial independence is not a shield against transparency.
    *   ***MediaOne Judgment (2023):*** SC held sealed-cover secrecy is "antithetical to a transparent and accountable system", highlighting the need to avoid opacity in judicial appointments.
    *   ***Umadevi Doctrine (2006):*** SC ruled that public appointments under Articles 14 & 16 must follow open, transparent procedures rather than backdoor methods.
    *   **Global Models:** UK Judicial Appointments Commission (publicly advertised vacancies & structured interviews) and South Africa Judicial Service Commission (public nominations & televised interviews).</span>
