# Article 4: Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 to provide for the amendment of the First and Fourth Schedules

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Constitution - Part I Union & Territory, Amendment, Schedule I & IV]`
> **Last Updated:** 2026-08-17
> **Related Notes:** [Indian_Polity_Constitution.md — Lecture 05](../GS_Polity_VR_Notes/Indian_Polity_Constitution.md)
> **Related Articles:** [Article 2](Article_2.md) | [Article 3](Article_3.md) | [Article 368](Article_368.md)

### Active Recall Questions

<details><summary>What does Article 4(1) provide?</summary>

- Any law made under Art 2 and Art 3 is to provide for **amendments to Schedule I and Schedule IV** and any incidental, supplemental, and consequential matters.
- **Schedule I** = Names of all States and UTs.
- **Schedule IV** = Number of Rajya Sabha seats allocated to each State.
- Whenever Art 2 or 3 is used → Schedule I and IV are **automatically amended**.
</details>

<details><summary>What is the significance of Article 4(2)?</summary>

- **Art 4(2) states:** Any aforesaid law (under Art 2/3) is **NOT to be deemed as an amendment to the Constitution under Article 368**.
- "Amendment under Art 368" = political phrase for **Special Majority**.
- This confirms: Laws under Art 2 & 3 are passed by **simple majority**, NOT special majority.
</details>

<details><summary>What is the "Protection of Article 4" rule?</summary>

- **Key Rule:** Whenever Art 2 and 3 are in action → protection of Art 4 applies → **Simple majority** suffices.
- If Art 2 & 3 are **NOT in action** → protection of Art 4 does NOT apply → **Special majority** is needed.
- **Example:** If Parliament wants to change RS seats for UP **without** using Art 2/3 (no state formation/alteration) → Art 4 protection absent → **Special majority** required to amend Schedule IV directly.
</details>
