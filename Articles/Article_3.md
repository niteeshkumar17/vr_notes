# Article 3: Formation of new States and alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing States

> **Tags:** `[GS-2: Constitution - Reorganization of States, Federalism]`
> **Last Updated:** 2026-08-13
> **Related Current Affairs:** [[2026-08-13_Current_Affairs]] (Kerala Name Change)

### Active Recall Questions

<details><summary>What does Article 3 of the Constitution deal with?</summary>

- Article 3 empowers the Parliament to:
  1. Form a new State by separation of territory from any State or by uniting two or more States.
  2. Increase the area of any State.
  3. Diminish the area of any State.
  4. Alter the boundaries of any State.
  5. Alter the name of any State.
</details>

<details><summary>What is the procedure for altering the name of a State under Article 3?</summary>

- A Bill contemplating such a change can be introduced in the Parliament **only with the prior recommendation of the President**.
- Before recommending the Bill, the President must refer the same to the **State Legislature concerned** for expressing its views within a specified period.
- The President (or Parliament) is **not bound** by the views of the State Legislature.
- The Bill is passed by a **simple majority** in Parliament.
- <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Current Affair (Aug 2026):** For example, the Kerala Assembly passed a resolution in 2024 to change its name to *Keralam*. Subsequently, the Union government introduced and the Rajya Sabha passed the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 to effect this change.</span>
</details>
