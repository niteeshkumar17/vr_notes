# Article 41 — DPSP: Right to Work, to Education and to Public Assistance in Certain Cases

> **Part:** IV — Directive Principles of State Policy
> **Subject:** Right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement

<summary>Show Answer</summary>

## Text

The State shall, within the limits of its economic capacity and development, make effective provision for securing the right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement, and in other cases of undeserved want.

## Key Points & Conceptual Dimensions
* **Socialistic Directive Principle:** Directs the State to establish social safety nets and welfare measures for vulnerable segments.
* **Qualifying Clause — "Limits of Economic Capacity":**
  * Unlike Part III Fundamental Rights which are justiciable and enforceable, the Right to Work under Article 41 is explicitly conditioned upon the State's available financial resources and economic development.
  * Therefore, the State cannot be compelled through a writ of mandamus to provide universal employment immediately.
* **Statutory Embodiment of Article 41:**
  * Schemes like **MGNREGA (2005)** and its successor **VB-G RAM G Act** serve as statutory operationalisations of the constitutional aspiration contained in Article 41.
  * Social security pensions (IGNOAPS), maternity benefits, and disability pensions derive constitutional legitimacy from Article 41.

## Inter-Relationship with Fundamental Rights (Part III vs. Part IV)
* **Article 21 vs. Article 41:** While Article 21 guarantees the right to live with human dignity (which includes livelihood as held in *Olga Tellis*), the positive obligation to guarantee employment is structured under Article 41 as a progressive developmental goal.
* **<span style="color: #e53e3e;">Supreme Court Distinction (Aug 2026):</span>** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">In the MGNREGA / VB-G RAM G Act proceedings (Aug 2026), the Supreme Court (Justice Joymalya Bagchi Bench) observed that the Constitution does not make the Right to Work an absolute Fundamental Right; it remains a democratic aspiration under Part IV by which the State formulates graded compensatory employment policies.</span>
* **<span style="color: #e53e3e;">Doctrine of Non-retrogression (Sep 2026 CA):</span>** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">*The Hindu* Economic Notes (1 Sep 2026) invoked *Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India*: once the State has enacted progressive welfare legislation (here MGNREGA as a statutory right to work), it cannot deliberately undo that floor. Authors use this against replacing a demand-driven guarantee with VB-G RAM G.</span>
