## 1. Where the chapter starts (HIS-VED-01)

Scholars are **not unanimous** over the events of the **few centuries after the decline of the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)**.

**Vedic Age (class bracket):** **1500 BC – 600 BC**.

**Max Müller** (19th-century German Indologist): by **1200 BC**, some **tribal pastoral wanderers** appeared in the **upper Ganga plains**. They composed **hymns (prayers)** in **Sanskrit** and called themselves **arya** — **noble** (decent, dignified).

**Why 1500 and 1200 both sit on the page:** **1500 BC** is the **base** of the Vedic bracket. **Painted Grey Ware** starts **1200 BC**. Pottery they used **before 1200 BC is unknown**.

**Rig Vedic Age:** **1500 BC – 1000 BC**. Earliest hymns were later collected in books called **Veda**. The earliest of those books is the **Rig Veda**, so the earliest period is the **Rig Vedic Age**. This is **history** in the class sense: written records we can read. (Stone Age file: history starts with the Vedic Age.)

**Context only (class: do not write the politics into the answer):** Hindus call ancestors *arya putra*; **Iran** is read as the land of *arya*; European race-politics (Hitler; a US prison gang calling itself Aryan Brotherhood) reused the word. Shared basic words and a similar pantheon (thunder, air, fire, water) are why scholars think the speakers once lived together. **Where** they lived is the fight.

## 4. Rig Veda: five tribes, Das, Dasyu (HIS-VED-04)

The **Rig Veda** says **five original tribes** of Aryans **wandered together** and were collectively called **Panchajana** (*panch* = five, *jana* = people).

**Notebook spelling: Panchajanya.** That is also the name of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) magazine he used as a hook. Dictated word is **Panchajana**.

They regularly fought people who were:

| Term | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Avrata** | Do **not** follow the **rules (vrata)** of the Aryans. Vrata = a long list of rules / commitment, not “skipping a meal.” |
| **Akratu** | Do **not** perform the **rituals (kratu)** of the Aryans. |

| Outcome | Word |
|:---|:---|
| Defeated and enslaved | **Dasa** (slave) |
| Kept fighting | **Dasyu** (bandit / robber). Modern echo he gave: *dasyu sundari* for Phoolan Devi. |

**Trap:** **Dasa was one of the original Aryan tribes**, not a ready-made word for “slave.” The Rig Veda mentions an Aryan king **Divodasa**. Other Aryans later enslaved the Dasas, and **then** Dasa became a synonym for slave (class: the way a neutral word turns into an insult). **Dasyu was always** the word for **enemies**.

Aryan commanders took pride in **dasyu-hatya** (killing of Dasyus) and then took the title **Trasadasyu** (killer of Dasyus; *tras* later tied to beating).

**Identification is a great debate.** Sapta Sindhu had **Saraswati as the main river**, and scholars disagree on which river that is.

**Notebook wrote “tributary of the Indus” for Max Müller.** The audio dictated **mythical river**. Recall **mythical**.

The Rig Veda is a **book of prayers**, not a constitution. “Women in Sabha and Vidatha” is an inference from those prayers. **Samiti and Gana: absence of a mention is not a ban.** Modern reading (Sabha = broad, Samiti = elite) is **our** reading; the text never draws the chart.

**Why the Rajan is weak.** Tribal pastoral society: **surplus and taxation were low**, so he could not keep a large workforce / bureaucracy. He depended on the **approval of the tribal assemblies**. Tax reached him **through** those assemblies.

| **Bhaga** | **War tribute** or **loot of war**. |

**Only two officers**

| Officer | Job |
|:---|:---|
| **Purohita** (priest) | Rituals for the welfare of the **whole jana**. The Rajan presides **only as the jana’s representative**. |
| **Senani** (commander) | **No standing / permanent army.** Men were recruited **in wartime** from **members of the jana** (volunteers). Senani **organises** them for war. |

In this **wandering** age **land was not property**. Main wealth = **cattle**. Main task of the Rajan = protect the cows of the jana. Therefore he is also **Gopa** (protector of cattle).

## 7. Dasarajna (HIS-VED-07)

**Dasarajna Yuddha** — **war of ten kings**.

Mentioned in the **seventh mandala** of the Rig Veda. Fought on the banks of the **Parushni (Ravi)**.

**Sudas**, king of the **Tritsu–Bharata** clan, defeated an alliance of **more than ten** kings of other tribes and clans, including **Puru, Yadu, Druhyu, Turvasha**.

**Later Vedic Age:** descendants of Sudas are called **Kuru**. They fight among themselves. That war is **Kurukshetra**, also called the **Mahabharata**. Class: scholars treat the Mahabharata war as a **Later Vedic** war. The **exact year** is not locked; older = harder to date.

## 8. Rig Vedic economy (HIS-VED-08)

Rig Vedic Aryans were **tribal pastoral wanderers**. **Cattle-keeping** was the **main** activity. **Agriculture** was **secondary**.

That economy produced **very little wealth and surplus**, so **no dominant class** could appear, and society stayed **egalitarian**. (The society *chapter* is next class.)

**Main metal = copper.** The Rig Veda calls copper **ayas**.

**Cattle = main wealth**, so the **cow** is the most important animal. The Rig Veda calls the cow **aghnya** — **not to be killed** (*ghnya* = violence / killing).

**Horse** is the **second** most important animal: movement and warfare. Elite warriors owned **ox- and horse-drawn chariots**. They were called **maharathi**.

**Words built on *gau* / *go***

| Word | Class meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Gopa** | King — protector of cattle |
| **Gavishti** | Battle — fought **for** cattle. Also **gav-eshana** = **search** for cows. |
| **Gavyuti** | A **unit of distance**. Later taken as the distance at which a **cowbell** is heard (he added “later 12,000 feet” — the unit, not a Rig Vedic survey). |
| **Godhuli** | **Evening** — returning cows raise dust that shows above the trees. |
| **Gomant** | A **wealthy** person (many cattle). |
| **Duhitri** | **Daughter** — the one who **milks**. Boys wander with the herd; in the evening the women milk. |
| **Gotra** | **Family**. In the wandering age people slept **where the cows were tied**. Same cowshed = same gotra. Later an exogamous extended family (same gotra must not marry). |

**Goghna** (guest): two readings in class — a guest given milk, butter and ghee, **or** a guest for whom a cow is killed. If an answer says Vedic people ate beef, class said take the name **D. N. Jha**. That fight belongs to **society**, next class. Do not turn it into a one-line “they ate beef / they did not.”
