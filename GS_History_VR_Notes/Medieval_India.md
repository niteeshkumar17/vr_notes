## 1. Arab Invasion (Arabic Speakers)

*   **"Arab"** = **linguistic category** (Arabic speakers), NOT a race/geography. Just like "Aryan" = Indo-European language speakers.
*   Arabs = speakers of Arabic from the **Umayyad Caliphate** (capital: **Damascus**, Syria).
*   Arabs were always **free men** (not slaves).

### 712 CE — Muhammad bin Qasim
*   Defeated **King Dahir of Sindh**.
*   **First to impose Jaziya** in India (poll tax paid by non-Muslims for **exemption from military service**).
*   **Converted Jats** of the region — first reference of **conversion** in India.

### Source
*   **Chachnama** — written in **Arabic** (original author unknown / lost).
*   Later translated to **Farsi (Persian)** by **Ali Kufi** → renamed **Fathnama**.
*   Now both versions are attributed to Ali Kufi.

---

## 2. Turkish Invasion (Persian Speakers)

*   **"Turkish"** = **Persian speakers** (Farsi) — NOT free men; they were **slaves** (Mamluks/Ghulams).
*   Persian was NOT the language of the Quran → Persian speakers were **later converts** to Islam → became slaves.

### Ghaznavids (Capital: Ghazna/Ghazni)

| Person | Key Facts |
|--------|-----------|
| **Alptigin** | **Founder** of Ghaznavid Empire; had no son; had a favourite **slave** |
| **Subuktigin** | Alptigin's slave → succeeded him; **first Turkish invader** to enter India (failed invasion); he is a **slave** and a **Turk** (Persian-speaking) |
| **Sultan Mahmud** (997–1050 CE) | Son of Subuktigin; **first Sultan** of the dynasty (free man, though from slave family); **1000–1001 CE**: defeated Hindu Shahi ruler **Jayapal** (first invasion); **1010 CE**: captured **Ghor** (turning point — best **horses** + **iron ore** for swords); **1024 CE**: invaded **Mathura**; **1026 CE**: invaded **Somnath** (1000 years ago — significant anniversary); **1030 CE**: captured **Lahore** (made it second capital); court was the **centre of Persian culture** |
| **Sultan Masud** (1030–40) | Son of Mahmud; captured **Punjab** region |

### Five Court Scholars of Mahmud (Centre of Persian Culture)

| # | Scholar | Work | Language | Significance |
|---|---------|------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | **Abul Fazl Baihaki** | **Tarikh-i-Baihaki** | Farsi | Court historian; **only eyewitness account** of Mathura & Somnath invasions |
| 2 | **Firdausi** | **Shahnama** | Farsi | First true Persian book ever written; called the **"Mahabharata of Afghanistan"** |
| 3 | **Al-Hujwiri** | **Kashf-al-Mahjub** | Farsi | **World's first Sufi literature** |
| 4 | **Ibn Sina** | **Book of Healing** (Latin translation) | Arabic/Latin | **First successful cataract operation** in the world; polymath |
| 5 | **Al-Biruni** | **Kitab-al-Hind** | Arabic | Uzbek scholar **kidnapped by Mahmud** (chained in court); wrote in Arabic (not Farsi) as protest; great astronomer — calculated distance of earth to moon/sun; also wrote on palmistry |

> *Al-Biruni's Kidnapping*: Mahmud met him at a Karam Sarai (medieval restaurant) in Uzbekistan → impressed by his Farsi → kidnapped him in a sack → chained him in court → Al-Biruni wrote all books in Arabic (rebellion).

## 3. Ghorids — From Ghaznavid Feudatories to Independent Kingdom

*   **Ghor** was under Ghaznavid control through **two brothers** (feudatories).
*   These brothers **revolted** → burned Ghazna → established the **Ghorid dynasty**.
*   *Parallel*: Just as Cholas once worked for Pallavas → similarly Ghorids worked for Ghaznavids.

| Person | Role | Capital | Direction |
|--------|------|---------|-----------|
| **Ghiyasuddin Firoz** (elder) | Co-ruler | **Ghor** | Moved **left** (away from India → into Iran) — not important for India |
| **Shihabuddin Muhammad (= Muhammad Ghori)** (younger) | Co-ruler | **Ghazna** (burned city) | Moved **right** (towards India) — **most important** |

*   Mother made them promise: **never fight each other** → two separate thrones.
*   **Key Difference**: **Mahmud Ghazni** came to India to **loot** (invader); **Muhammad Ghori** came to **settle** (permanent revenue system — brother forced him eastward).

### Muhammad Ghori → No Son → Army of Slaves + Free Men

*   **Muizzi slaves** = Ghori's personal slaves (named after him).
*   **Khalji free men** also joined (attracted by promise of wealth).
*   *Key Rule*: Whenever you see **"Khalji"** → the person is a **free man** (never a slave).

## 4. Muhammad Ghori — Three Phases of Indian Invasion

### Phase I: Capturing the Frontier (1191–1192 CE)

*   Captured **fort of Tabarhind (Bhatinda)** — the **last frontier fort** of the **Chahamanas (Chauhans)** under **Prithviraj III (Prithvi the Third)**.
*   **1191 CE — 1st Battle of Tarain**: Ghori **lost** → his army had never seen **elephants** before → horses panicked and ran.
*   **1192 CE — 2nd Battle of Tarain**: Ghori **won** → took **Delhi, Mathura, Ujjain, Banaras, Gwalior**.

### Phase II: Feudatory System (Post-1192)

*   Ghori **did not kill Indian rulers** — made them his **feudatories**.
*   **Prithviraj III was NOT killed** — given **Ajmer** to rule from.
*   **Evidence**: A **gold coin** discovered:
    *   One side: **Shiva's Nandi** + name of **Prithvi** in Sanskrit.
    *   Other side: Written **"Sri Mahasamanta Muhammad"** in Sanskrit.
    *   → Proves Prithvi was working as **feudatory** of Muhammad Ghori.
*   Later, Ghori **replaced Prithvi** with his brother **Hari Singh** (dispute over revenue sharing).
*   **1194 CE — Battle of Chandawar**: Ghori defeated **Jaichand** (last powerful ruler of North India).

### Phase III: Control of Gangetic Plains

*   Ghori takes over **entire Gangetic plains**.
*   **Pushed Indian rulers to the fringes**.
*   **Hari Singh** was replaced by **Prithvi's son Govind** → given **Ranthambore** (last Chauhan outpost → Ranthambore dynasty).

## 7. Sultan Iltutmish (1211–1236) — The Real Founder of the Delhi Sultanate (HIS-MED-09)

> Ten points — this is the single most examinable list of the Slave Dynasty.

| # | What he did | Why it matters |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | Took **formal permission (investiture) to become SULTAN** from the **Abbasid Caliph Al-Mustansir** | He is no longer merely a *ghazi* or a governor — he is a **legally recognised Sultan**. Islam now enters India **legally** → India becomes **Dar-al-Islam** ("land of Islam", where Islamic law runs) → hence Iltutmish, not Aibak, is the **real founder of the Delhi Sultanate** |
| **2** | Issued the **Tanka** — the **silver coin** | Once you are Sultan, you issue coin. First Sultanate silver currency |
| **3** | **Completed the Qutub Minar** | Kept Aibak's **dedication to Bakhtiyar Kaki** unchanged |
| **4** | Created the post of **Chief Religious Minister** and appointed a **Sufi — Bahauddin Zakariya** (Suhrawardi) to it | A Sufi religious minister ⇒ the state now formally adheres to **Sharia** |
| **5** | Formally **extended Dar-al-Islam** | The legal-religious basis of the Sultanate |
| **6** | Created **two administrative posts**: **Wazir** (Prime Minister) and **Ariz-i-Mamalik** (Defence Minister) | The first ministries of the Sultanate |
| **7** | Created **two systems**: **(a) IQTA** — land assigned as **salary/revenue assignment**; **(b) an audit system — Naqib** | Iqta is the backbone of Sultanate finance (see section 14). Audit was built in from day one |
| **8** | Bought his own corps of slaves — the **CHAHALGANI** (*Turkan-i-Chihalgani*, "The Forty") | The **first military elite** of the Sultanate — and the force that will later make and unmake Sultans |
| **9** | Appointed **Razia as Sultan-in-absentia** whenever he left Delhi | Unprecedented — a woman formally on the throne of Delhi |
| **10** | Brought the **Gupta iron pillar from Udayagiri (Madhya Pradesh)** and installed it **in front of the Qutub Minar** | It still stands in the Qutub complex |

*   **Source for the whole reign:** **Minhaj-us-Siraj Juzjani** — **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** (Persian). *Tabaqat* = biography; *Nasir* = Nasiruddin Mahmud, the ruler under whom he wrote.

### 7.1 Reading Medieval Names, Titles and Book-Names — The Cue System (HIS-MED-10)

> UPSC medieval questions are overwhelmingly **book–author** questions. Even if you don't know the book, the *word pattern* gives the answer away.

| Cue in the word | What it tells you | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **bin** | "son of" | **Muhammad bin Qasim** = Muhammad, son of Qasim |
| **binte** | "daughter of" | Azman **binte** Afzal |
| **al- / -i** (place) | "of that place" | **Al-Biruni** = the man from Beruni |
| **Tarikh** | history — **Sultanate** period | *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi* |
| **Tawarikh** | history — **Mughal** period | *Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh* |
| **Tuzuk** | **Turkic** (not Persian) | *Tuzuk-i-Baburi* |
| **-nama** | **Persian (Farsi)** | *Shahnama*, *Akbarnama* |
| Hard, difficult-to-pronounce words | usually **Arabic** | *Kashf-al-Mahjub* |
| Title **"Mian"** | conferred by **non-Mughals / Arabic-speaking patrons** | **Mian Tansen** — *UPSC has asked who gave Tansen the title "Mian"; the trick was that three options were Mughals* |
| Title **"Mirza"** | **Mughal** | Mirza Man Singh |

## 8. Razia (1236–1240) (HIS-MED-12)

*   **First ruler of the Sultanate to issue a GOLD COIN — the *dinar***.
*   On her coins she called herself **"JALAL" (Jalalat-ud-din)** — *she never called herself Razia*. We know Jalal = Razia **only because of Minhaj-us-Siraj's book**.
*   **Wore male attire** and **sat openly on the throne**. *(Flagged in class as a UPSC 2026 question.)*
*   **First Islamic religious revolt in India — the Revolt of Nur Turk.** The orthodox **ulama** could not accept a woman on the throne; she crushed the revolt.
*   **Razia vs the Chahalgani** — she realised the real threat was not the orthodox clergy but her father's forty slaves, who had become too powerful:
    *   **Removed the Chahalgani** from important posts and gave those posts to **non-Chahalgani** men.
    *   **Bought her own set of slaves** — deliberately **NOT** from Central Asia or the Middle East (they would be kin to the Chahalgani), but from **Africa**.
    *   These African slaves appear in the sources under a chain of names: **Africans → Ethiopians → Abyssinians → Habshis → Siddis**. Their descendants are counted today as **Siddis** in the Census — found in **Gujarat, Bengal (mostly Muslim)** and **Karnataka (mostly Christian)**.
*   **The end:** The Chahalgani revolted under **Altunia**, who **imprisoned Razia**. Razia then played her best card — she **offered him marriage**; they married and became one team. Together they marched on Delhi, but the Chahalgani **intercepted and killed them both just short of Delhi, at Kaithal (Haryana), in 1240**. Her original mausoleum still stands beside the Kaithal highway.

## 9. Ulugh Khan / Sultan Balban (1266–1287) (HIS-MED-13)

> Balban was **himself a Chahalgani** and the **Naib** of Nasiruddin Mahmud. Everything he did follows from that one fact: a Chahalgani who took the throne cannot afford another Chahalgani above or beside him.

| # | Measure | Explanation |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Abolished the post of Naib** (Deputy Sultan) | Only a Chahalgani could become Naib — so the post itself had to go |
| **2** | **POLICY OF BLOOD** | Killed the other Chahalgani and anyone who could be a probable threat to the throne |
| **3** | **POLICY OF IRON** | The **Mongols** were the standing external problem → he answered it by **consolidating** the Sultanate (creating the departments — see section 13) |
| **4** | **DIVINE THEORY OF KINGSHIP** | The first Sultan to claim divinity. Consequences below |

**Elements of the Divine Theory of Kingship:**

*   **Fixed a law of succession** for the first time — note that a fixed hereditary succession law is **not** an Islamic principle.
*   Called himself **ZIL-I-ALLAH** — *zil* = shadow (Persian), *i* = of, *Allah* = God → **"Shadow of God on Earth"**. Only **two** rulers in Indian history used this: **Balban** and **Akbar** (as *Zil-i-Ilahi*).
*   Introduced **court etiquette** for approaching a divine king: **SIJDA** (prostration, forehead to the ground) and **PAIBOS** (kissing the Sultan's feet).
*   Introduced the **Iranian New Year festival — NAVROZ**.
*   Claimed to be the **great-grandson of AFRASIYAB**, a mythical hero of Iranian legend.

*   **Source:** **Fatawa-i-Jahandari** by **Zia-ud-din Barani** — who therefore wrote **two** books: *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi* and *Fatawa-i-Jahandari*.

## 10. The Four Pillars of the Sultanate (HIS-MED-14)

> Just as a democracy has four pillars, the Sultanate rests on four.

| # | Pillar | Root word | Who they were |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | **SULTAN** | *sult* = **wielder of the sword** | The analytical point is not the etymology — it is that **anyone could become Sultan**, because **Islam had no caste hierarchy** |
| **2** | **ULAMA** | *alim* ← *ilm* = **knowledge** | The intellectual class that **interprets Sharia** — Maulana, Qazi, Sadr-us-Sudur |
| **3** | **UMARA** | *amir* | The **bureaucracy** |
| **4** | **KHUT** | — | The **local landed chiefs** — the **Indian** pillar. A tiny minority of outsiders could not rule India without them. Called **Thakur** in inscriptions; **Amir Khusrau later coined the word "ZAMINDAR"** for them |

**How Sanskrit inscriptions describe the newcomers** (pillars 1–3, who came from outside):

| Sanskrit term | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Mlechha** | outsider, impure |
| **Turushka** | Turk (also used for "slave") |
| **Pahlava** | Persian |

## 11. What Changed with the Advent of Islamic Rule in India? (HIS-MED-15)

> A big Mains-type question. Answer in four heads: **economic, social, scientific-technological, political-administrative**.

| Head | Change |
|:---|:---|
| **1. Economic** | The invaders came from the **Arabian peninsula, Central Asia and the Middle East** → **overland trade routes opened** → trade grew → **urbanization**; new demand (dates, carpets, paper) created **workshops = KARKHANAS** |
| **2. Social** | **Conversion — welcomed by many**, especially **lower castes and untouchables**, because Islam had no caste hierarchy. It was **not forced**; it followed **occupational and economic logic** (which is why whole occupational groups converted together). Plus **language and cultural exchange** |
| **3. Science & Technology** | **(i) Manufacturing:** paper, carpets, soap (from the 12th century). **(ii) Metallurgy:** wrought iron + silver (to resist oxidation) → **WOOTZ steel**. **(iii) Building:** **mortar and lime** (*chuna*) enter India for the first time → makes the **true dome** possible. **(iv) Irrigation:** the **Persian wheel** |
| **4. Political & Administrative** | **Feudal → NON-FEUDAL, centralized state.** The **iqta is not a feudal grant** and the **iqtadar is not a bhogika**. Administratively, a full structure of **departments (diwans)** appears. **This single shift is why Early Medieval ends and Medieval begins with the Sultanate** |

> **Class aside — what a "masjid" actually is:** *Masjid* means a **congregation standing shoulder to shoulder in prayer** — Islam does not require a **building**; wherever people stand and pray together is a masjid. Related vocabulary from the same digression: Prophet Muhammad's migration from **Mecca** to **Yathrib** = *hijra* (start of the Islamic calendar); Yathrib became **Madinat-un-Nabi** → **Madina**; those who welcomed him = **Ansari** ("welcomers"); those who kept faith through everything = **Siddiq** (the first being Abu Bakr); *Rasool* = the one who brought a holy book, *Nabi* = prophet.

## 12. Water Management across 1,500 Years — South vs North India (HIS-MED-16)

> **Why the South is different:** the South's advantage is the coast; its **disadvantage is fresh water** (saline coast + old, hard crystalline rock = poor groundwater). Fresh water therefore becomes the **most precious resource** — and it is deliberately kept **out of the king's hands** (a corrupt or casteist Raja would ruin everyone). Instead it is controlled by the **temple** (tanks) and by **Brahmin assemblies** (canals) — which is exactly why **temples became economic centres / proto-banks and temple towns grew in the South first**.

### 12.1 South India

| # | Structure | Who controlled it | Names in the sources |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Water tanks** | the **temple** | **Eri, Keri, Tataka, Kulam** — a place-name ending in *-kulam* or *-eri* usually means a town that grew around a tank |
| **2** | **Canals** | **Brahmin villages** (*agraharam*) — a **committee**, never one man | **Kal, Vaykkal, Aruvaykkal** — e.g. in Thanjavur the agraharams control Brihadeshwara, which controls the **Grand Anicut** |
| **3** | **"Wells"** | — | **Kinaru / Kanvai** — but the South has no true wells: the hard old rock means these are really **catchment / percolation pits** holding still water |
| **4** | **PICOTAH** | — | The **lever-lift**, which came **from the Arabs** (who had the same desert water problem) through the South Indian trade contact |
| **5** | **Dams** | — | The defining part of a dam is its **sluice gate**: **horizontal flap → Tumpu / Madagu / Kalingu** (used on the sloping **Coromandel** side — Tamil Nadu, Andhra, Odisha, where pressure opens the flap); **vertical shutter → Kumizhi** (used on the **Malabar** side) |

> **Geography decides technology:** the slope of the Coromandel gives you enough pressure for a flap-type sluice; the Malabar does not, so it needs a shutter. The same lesson repeats across Indian water history.

### 12.2 North India (HIS-MED-17)

| # | Structure | Detail |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Caste wells** | The North had **plenty of water — so it made water casteist**. Separate wells for Brahmins, Kayasthas and others |
| **2** | **Reservoirs — Tadaga** | For genuinely dry regions: Rajasthan, Gujarat, Rann of Kutch |
| **3** | **Dykes — Kulya** | Embankment channels |
| **4** | **Step-wells — Vav / Vapi / Baoli** | Reserved for **royalty and for gods** — e.g. **Rani ki Vav**, **Agrasen ki Baoli** |
| **5** | **Irrigational wells** | Developed in **three stages** — see below |

#### The Three Stages of the Irrigational Well

| Stage | Period | Name | Lifting power |
|:---:|:---:|:---|:---|
| **S1** | **7th–9th century** (Early Medieval) | **NORIA** | **Men** pull the water |
| **S2** | **9th–11th century** (just before the Turks) | **GHATI-YANTRA** | A **lever**, pulled by an **animal** |
| **S3** | **11th–13th century** (arrival → establishment of the Sultanate) | **Arhat / Rahat / Dhenkli**, best known as **ARAGHATTA** (the Persian wheel) | **Pulley + animal** |

*   In the **araghatta**, a horse or bullock is tied to a wheel and the leather bucket on the other side is called the **CHARAS** — made of leather by the **CHARANS**, an untouchable community of Rajasthan.
*   The same Charans held an important wartime job in the Sultanate: carrying water in leather bottles to quench the army's thirst.

> **The irony worth remembering:** the community whose hands Hindu society would not accept water from was the community that **made the buckets that lifted everybody's water**.

## 13. The Central Departments — Diwans (HIS-MED-18)

> "Consolidation" is not a vague word. It means exactly this: **Balban created the departments**, and later Sultans only **added** to his list. *Diwan* = department.

| Created by | Post / Department | Portfolio (modern equivalent) | Goes to |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Iltutmish** | the post of **SULTAN** itself (+ Wazir, Ariz-i-Mamalik) | — | — |
| **Bahram Shah** | **NAIB** (Deputy Sultan) | — | *abolished by Balban* |
| **Balban** | **Diwan-i-Wizarat** | PMO / finance | **Umara** |
| **Balban** | **Diwan-i-Arz** | Defence | **Umara** |
| **Balban** | **Diwan-i-Insha** | Correspondence, *farman* (Information & Broadcasting) | **Umara** |
| **Balban** | **Diwan-i-Risalat** | Foreign affairs — i.e. **expansion of Dar-al-Islam** | **Ulama** |
| **Balban** | **Sadr-us-Sudur** *(no "diwan" prefix)* | Chief religious officer | **Ulama** |
| **Balban** | **Qazi-ul-Quzat** *(no "diwan" prefix)* | Chief Justice **+ Education** | **Ulama** |
| **Alauddin Khalji** (1296–1316) | **Diwan-i-Riyasat** *(under Wizarat)* | **Commerce** | — |
| **Alauddin Khalji** | **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj** *(under Wizarat)* | **Income tax / arrears** | — |
| **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (1325–51) | **Diwan-i-Kohi** *(under Riyasat)* | **Agriculture** — gave **pre-harvest loans** called **SONDHAR** | — |
| **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** (1351–88) | **Diwan-i-Bandagan** | Department for **slaves** | — |
| **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** | **Diwan-i-Khairat** | **Charity** | — |
| **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** | **Diwan-i-Istihqaq** | **Pensions** | — |

> **Why three departments went to the Ulama:** to run **Risalat** (spreading Islam), **Sadr-us-Sudur** (religion) or **Qazi-ul-Quzat** (justice and education) you must know **Sharia, Quran and Islamic law** — so those three necessarily go to the **Ulama**, while the other three go to the **Umara**. Three each = **division of power with checks and balances**. *That* is what "consolidation" means.
>
> **These are ALL the departments the Sultanate ever created**, from Iltutmish to the Lodis. There is no other department to learn.

| Type | Meaning | Hereditary? | Taxable? |

> **UPSC definition trap:** *Waqf* is an **endowment** — **not** a religious *grant*, **not** a religious *charity*, **not** an extension of land. In law its **owner is God**.

### 14.2 Khalji period (1290–1320): Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316) — expansion

*   **Abolished the iqta.** Khalsa + Iqta + Madad are melted into **one single core area** — his **political core** — and everything in it is taxed:

| Tax | On what | Rate |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **KHARAJ** | Produce (land revenue) | **50%** |
| **GHARAI** | Houses | house tax |
| **CHARAI** | Goats, sheep, milch animals | grazing tax |

*   Because Madad is now part of the core, **WAQF also became taxable** — **Alauddin is the only Sultan of Delhi ever to tax the waqf** (including Hajj offices). *This is the single most examinable exception of the topic.*
*   **Outside the core:** he expanded from Delhi to **Madurai** (Malik Kafur **defeated** — deliberately not killed — **Jatavarman Pandya**), taking Gujarat, Andhra, Telangana; **not Kashmir, not Bengal**. Defeated rulers **kept their kingdoms and paid tribute**.
*   That whole tribute belt was treated as **one giant district (SIQQ)**, which was impossible to administer — so it was broken into **HAZARI** (packets of 1,000 units) → **SADI** (100 units) → each with a tribute collector, the **AMIR-I-SADAH**, sent by Alauddin. *(Hence place-names like Hazaribagh.)*
*   **Net effect:** a **centralized core** plus a **decentralized, quasi-feudal tributary periphery**.

### 14.3 Evolution of the Landed System (HIS-MED-20)

| Ruler / dynasty | Crown land | Assignment | Grant | Waqf |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---:|
| **Iltutmish & Balban** | Khalsa | **Iqta** *(non-feudal)* ✔ | Madad | non-taxable |
| **Alauddin Khalji** | Khalsa (enlarged core) | *iqta abolished* | Siqq (tribute belt) | **TAXED** ⚠ |
| **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** | Khalsa only (kills rulers, takes the land) | — | — | non-taxed |
| **Firoz Shah Tughlaq & Sayyids** | Khalsa | **Bhog** — iqta became **hereditary**, i.e. it started behaving as **feudal** property | — | non-taxable |
| **Lodis** | **Khalisa** *(non-feudal)* | **Iqta** *(non-feudal)* ✔ | **Madad-i-Maash** | non-taxable |

> **The question UPSC can ask:** *when did the Sultanate have a proper iqta system?* Only twice — under the **Slave Dynasty** and under the **Lodis** (✔ above). In between, it was either abolished (Alauddin, MbT) or had degenerated into hereditary, feudal *bhog* (Firoz Shah, Sayyids).

## 16. Land Revenue System — MASAHAT (HIS-MED-22)

> **Masahat** = the Persian term for the **land revenue system**. *UPSC has asked directly: "What is Masahat?"* All the administration above exists for exactly one purpose — collecting this revenue.

| # | Ruler(s) | Method | Share | Mode | Machinery / innovations |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Iltutmish & Balban** | **Crop-sharing method** — simply continued the old **Hindu** system | **1/3 of gross produce** (*ushr*) | share of the standing crop | Village officials inspect the crop before harvest and take the state's share |
| **2** | **Alauddin Khalji** | **Measurement method** — the **most scientific** of the three | **50% — kharaj** | **in kind only** (grain, not money) | **(a)** unit of measurement: the **BISWA** (a sub-unit of the bigha, still used in western UP and Rajasthan); **(b)** two **granaries** — **Delhi** and **Jhain** (Rajasthan); **(c)** to let people buy that grain he minted **fractional copper coins** of the **JITAL** (½, ¼, ⅙, ⅛) called **GHANI** |
| **3** | **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** | **Tax farming method** | **1/3 of gross produce** | **cash only** | The land goes to the **highest bidder** — the **khut**, hence **KHOTI** — who keeps a fixed **10% commission** for collecting |

*   **Why the ghani matters today:** the *chavanni* and *athanni* were ghani coins (withdrawn from circulation around 2005), and "**Kachchi Ghani**" mustard oil still carries **Alauddin Khalji's measurement word**.
*   **Link to Modern India:** the **auction of revenue collection to the highest bidder** is *not* a British invention. The **term** *ijaradari* comes from **Warren Hastings**, but the **concept is as old as Firoz Shah Tughlaq**.

## 17. The Khaljis (1290–1320) — Expansion (HIS-MED-23)

> **One-word cue for the whole dynasty: EXPANSION.** Whatever the question — political expansion, expansion on land, expansion in monuments, expansion of cities — the answer word is *expansion*.
>
> **Second cue: "Khalji" always means a FREE MAN**, never a slave. So with the Khaljis, the **slave elite is thrown out of Indian administration** for the first time. **Expansion + removal of the slaves = the "KHALJI REVOLUTION".**

### 17.1 Jalaluddin Khalji (1290–96)

*   Founder of the dynasty — he was the staff officer of the 3-year-old Kayumars and killed him to take the throne.
*   A revolution implies fights — his reign is a **period of revolts**:

| # | Revolt | Nature |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Sidi Maula** | **Religious** |
| **2** | **Khusrau Khan** *(as named in class)* | **Political** |
| **3** | **Ali Gurshasp** | The successful one — **he killed Jalaluddin Khalji** and took the throne as **Alauddin Khalji** |

> *Cross-check for accuracy:* standard textbooks name the political revolt of Jalaluddin's reign as that of **Malik Chhajju** (the displaced governor of Kara); **Khusrau Khan's** coup belongs to **1320**, against **Mubarak Shah Khalji**, and is what ends the dynasty. Keep both names in mind if a statement-based question turns on the date.

*   **Ali Gurshasp was Jalaluddin's own nephew** (and son-in-law) — he killed him at Kara and changed his name to **Alauddin Khalji**.

### 17.2 Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316)

| # | Achievement | Detail |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Defeated the Mongols decisively** — threw them out | The **most successful Sultan of Delhi against the Mongols** |
| **2** | Brought **new military technology** to India | — |
| **3** | Introduced the **best breeds of horses** | Created **horse-breeding centres — PAIGAH**; started the **branding system (DAGH)** and the **descriptive roll (CHEHRA)**; introduced **DO-ASPA** — two horses per soldier as a rule; also created a **central postal system** |
| **4** | **Market reforms and price regulation** | The famous experiment — it **ran for about 25 years** |

> **Standing exam line:** *after Balban, the most successful Sultan of Delhi is Alauddin Khalji.*

## UPSC Cues & PYQ Connections — Delhi Sultanate (Lecture A4)

*   **Iltutmish, not Aibak**, is the **real founder** of the Delhi Sultanate (Abbasid investiture → Dar-al-Islam).
*   **Firsts:** first silver **tanka** (Iltutmish) · first **gold dinar** (Razia) · first **Islamic religious revolt** — Nur Turk (Razia) · first **Mongol invasion** (Bahram Shah) · first **spy system** (Balban) · first **fixed law of succession** (Balban).
*   **Book–author pairs:** *Taj-ul-Maasir* — Hasan Nizami · *Tabaqat-i-Nasiri* — Minhaj-us-Siraj Juzjani · *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi* **and** *Fatawa-i-Jahandari* — Zia-ud-din Barani.
*   **Definition questions:** *Masahat* = land revenue system · *Waqf* = religious **endowment** · *Iqta* = **non-feudal** revenue assignment.
*   **The one exception to memorise:** **Alauddin Khalji is the only Sultan who taxed the waqf.**
*   **Proper iqta system existed only under the Slave Dynasty and the Lodis.**
*   **Zil-i-Allah** was used by only two rulers in Indian history — **Balban** and **Akbar** (*Zil-i-Ilahi*).
*   **Sanskrit was a working language of the Sultanate's administration** (Balban's two karkuns) even though **Persian** was the court language.
*   **Title cue:** "Mian" ≠ Mughal (asked for **Mian Tansen**); "Mirza" = Mughal.
*   **Revenue-farming to the highest bidder** begins with **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**, not Warren Hastings.
