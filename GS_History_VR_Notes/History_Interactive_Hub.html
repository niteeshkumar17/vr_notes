Key Highlights:

  • Aurangzeb's death in 1707 triggered an immediate war of succession between his 3 sons: Muazzam (63), Azam (54), and Kam Baksh (40).
  • Muazzam defeated Azam at the Battle of Jajau (1707) and Kam Baksh at Hyderabad (1709).
  • Muazzam ascended the throne as Bahadur Shah I, marking the start of the Later Mughal era.

Political Transformation & Cause-Effect Arrows (1707–1761)
1. Death of Aurangzeb (1707) Trigger
Death of AZB at Ahmednagar ➔ Power vacuum in Delhi ➔ Muazzam wins War of Succession (Bahadur Shah I) ➔ Release of Shahuji (1707) to destabilize Marathas.
2. Rise of Kingmaker Nobles & Zulfiqar Khan (1712) Noble Dominance
Zulfiqar Khan kills 3 rival brothers under elephants ➔ Jahandar Shah made Emperor ➔ Zulfiqar becomes PM ➔ Abolishes Jaziya in Gujarat to build independent army.
3. Sayyid Brothers & Maratha Coalition (1713–1719) Imperial Execution
Sayyid Brothers use Farrukh Siyar to execute Zulfiqar Khan ➔ Golden Farman to EIC (1717) ➔ Balaji Vishwanath proposes Dual Governance ➔ Farrukh Siyar executed by Sayyid Brothers & Marathas (1719).
4. Foreign Invasions & Total Disintegration (1739–1761) Mughal Collapse
Muhammad Shah Rangeela neglects state ➔ Nadir Shah Battle of Karnal (1739), loots Peacock Throne ➔ Successor states break away ➔ 3rd Battle of Panipat (1761) halts Marathas and sets stage for Sikh rise in Punjab.
Key Exam Takeaway
UPSC Mains & Prelims Focus

Structural Analysis:

  • The shift from sovereign emperors to noble kingmakers (Zulfiqar Khan, Sayyid Brothers, Nizam-ul-Mulk) was the chief structural flaw of 18th-century Mughals.
  • Maratha intervention in Delhi politics (1719) established Marathas as the paramount de facto power of the subcontinent until 1761.
