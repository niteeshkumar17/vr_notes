## 1. Chalcolithic Age (HIS-IVC-01)

**Dates (class):** **4th millennium BC → 2nd millennium BC.**

**When it starts:** **substantial** use of copper tools — **not** the first copper object. Metal appears in the **Neolithic** already. Class analogy: we are not in an “AI age” because a computer was invented; an age starts when the thing is **in substantial use**.

By the **mid-4th millennium BC**, various **Neolithic** villages / cultures started using substantial copper tools and **transitioned** to the Chalcolithic Age.

In this age they **greatly improved agriculture** to produce **enough surplus** to support **high-volume long-distance trade**. That grew **market centres** and centres of **industrial production**.

**Industry** here is **secondary production** — cloth, pots, *chattai* — **not** modern factories. Class: when the British came they ended India’s **rural industries**.

This is the **beginning of urbanization**, where the **earliest cities** appeared. Class example: **Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)**.

### Bronze Age is not a separate age

**Worldwide**, the **developed phase** of the Chalcolithic Age is called the **Bronze Age**. It is **not** a separate age.

**Bronze** = alloy of **copper + tin**.

**India lock:** availability of **tin was limited**. Main source = mines of the **Afghanistan** region. Therefore a **true Bronze Age never appeared in India**. In the Indian Chalcolithic, **bronze tools and artifacts existed but were limited**.

**Stone tools continued** (class: they continue even today — *silbatta*).

| Age | Tools | Other |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Chalcolithic** (4th mill. BC – 2nd mill. BC) | **Copper** tools + **stone** tools | Trade and industry substantial; **urbanization** in **some** places (IVC) |

### Iron Age (parked)

Iron Age **starts somewhere in the 2nd millennium BC**. Class: **do not** take a deep interest yet — features come with **Later Vedic**. Some anthropologists end it with the **Mauryas**; that is not your job here.

**Chalcolithic does not end** when this chapter heading ends. **IVC** and **Rig Vedic** still sit inside it.

## 4. Four names (HIS-IVC-03)

**Harappa is a city / site. IVC is the civilization.** They are not two different cultures.

| Name | Why | Class status |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Harappan Civilization** | First discovered site = **Harappa**, on the **Ravi**, Sahiwal district, **West Punjab, Pakistan**. Discovered by **Daya Ram Sahni**, **1920/21**. A new site is compared with **Harappa’s features**. | **Most scientific / technical** name. Archaeology names a culture from the **first** discovered site (Oldowan ← Olduvai). |
| **Indus Valley Civilization** | Majority of **early** sites were found on the **Indus river system**. | **Most used** name. Sir uses this in class. |
| **Sindhu–Saraswati Civilization** | Majority of sites prospered on the **Indus** and **Ghaggar–Hakra**. Indus = **Sindhu**. **Ghaggar–Hakra** is identified as **Saraswati**. | **UPSC uses “Saraswati.”** Debate of which river = Vedic chapter. |
| **Meluha** | **Mesopotamian** records (Iraq), **3rd millennium BC**: traders of Meluha came from the **east** with **cotton textiles and beads**. Description matches IVC traders. | **Could have been** their contemporary name. **Guess — no guarantee.** |

**Ghaggar–Hakra (class, not a Vedic essay):** two monsoon streams (north Rajasthan / Ghaggar, Sindh / Hakra) look separate because the middle is dry. Satellite: **one palaeo-channel**. Old channels of **Sutlej** and **Yamuna** once fed it → an enormous river. **Tectonic uplift / flood** created a watershed: Sutlej now joins the **Indus**, Yamuna the **Ganga**, Ghaggar–Hakra **declined**. Cultural memory at Allahabad / Prayag: Yamuna “brings Saraswati’s water” = **river capture**. For Prelims, **Saraswati** is the word UPSC used.

Sites now run **Iran → Uttar Pradesh (UP)** and **Afghanistan → Maharashtra**. So “Indus Valley” is geographically thin; **Harappan** is the feature-name.

## 5. Geography — map secondary, features primary (HIS-IVC-04)

**Prelims lock:** a site on the Indus that is **contemporary** but **lacks Harappan features** is **not** Harappan. A site **far away** with the **core features** **is**. **Map is secondary; features are primary.** Map **changes** when a qualifying site is found.

Core features class listed (citadel, boundary wall, beads, seals, mother goddess, Harappan script, grid planning, sewage, covered drains). Sites are **not replicas** of each other; the **core** is the same.

### Cardinal sites (class)

| | Site | Where (class) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **West** | **Sutkagendor** | **Balochistan–Iran** border; still in **Pakistan** |
| **North** (older textbook) | **Manda** | **Jammu**, Jammu and Kashmir |
| **East** | **Alamgirpur** | **Meerut** district, **western UP**, **between Yamuna and Ganga** |
| **South** | **Daimabad** | **Ahmednagar** district, **Maharashtra** |

**Ganga lock:** **no** Harappan site **on the Ganga banks** or **east of the Ganga**. Alamgirpur is **west of Ganga**, after crossing the **Yamuna**.

**Northernmost now:** **Shortughai** (northern **Afghanistan**) — **trading outpost** built by IVC traders. It has the **features**, so the map moved; **Manda is no longer northernmost**. The surrounding region has a **different** culture (**BMAC**); Shortughai is Harappan **because of features**.

### Two surviving ports

A naval-trade civilization would have had **hundreds** of ports; most were wiped. **Two** remain because **tectonic uplift** lifted them — **today they are not on the coast**.

1. **Sukta Koh** (*koh* = mountain) near **Sutkagendor** / **Gwadar** (class: China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) place-name only).  
2. **Lothal** — mouth of the **Sabarmati**, **Ahmedabad** district, **Gujarat**. **Artificial dockyard** (basin filled through one gate so waves do not smash boats while loading). Class: some say “swimming pool”; a pool on the coast is a weak story — the find has dockyard features.

## 6. Theories of origin (HIS-IVC-05)

### Mesopotamian origin — **archaeology does not support**

Some scholars (class: **do not write names**; spoken: Mackay / Wheeler) said IVC **urbanization** was **migration of Mesopotamian people** into the Indus region. **Mesopotamian elite** lived in the **cities**; **indigenous commoners** lived in neighbouring **villages**. “Proof”: **citadel + fortification wall** (Mesopotamia urbanised earlier).

### Indigenous origin — **archaeology supports**

**Majority** of scholars: the developments needed for urbanization happened **where the cities are found** (Indus region) **for over 1,000 years**. That early layer is **proto-urbanization** (early / undeveloped urban features). It became the **basis** of Mature Harappan cities.

Harappan **city** (green) matches the **layer below it** (red) **more** than it matches Mesopotamian cities. Borrowing a citadel idea ≠ becoming Mesopotamian — class: Netflix / Apple in the house does not make you American.

**Lock:** urbanization of IVC was **gradual, successive, and indigenous**.

**Proto-** = early / undeveloped (proto-nationalism after 1857; proto-human before *Homo sapiens*). A highway *kasba* with a few pukka shops linking villages = proto-urban, not a city.

## 7. Timeline of phases (HIS-IVC-06)

| Phase | Also called | Dates | Age |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **1. Food-producing** | **Mehrgarh** phase | **8th mill. BC → 4th mill. BC** | **Neolithic** |
| **2. Early Harappan** | Pre-Harappan / **regional cultures** / proto-urban | **3300–2600 BC** | Chalcolithic |
| **3. Mature Harappan** | **Integration** / urban phase / **IVC** | **2600–1900 BC** | Chalcolithic |
| **4. Late Harappan** | Disintegration / **deurbanization** | **1900–1300 BC** | Chalcolithic |

### Food-producing / Mehrgarh

From the **8th millennium BC**, many **Neolithic** villages appeared in **north-western India** and built an **agrarian base** (food production) to support a **large population**. For millennia the main change is **villages growing**.

**Mehrgarh** = **one of the earliest villages** developed in India. **Quetta, Balochistan**, **south of the Bolan Pass** (Suleiman Range). Class: Upinder Singh gives it **three pages** — remember the site.

### Early Harappan / regional Chalcolithic cultures (3300–2600 BC)

By the **mid-4th millennium BC** these villages used **substantial copper** → Chalcolithic. Surplus supported **high-volume trade**. Villages of a region built **trade networks** and, besides goods, exchanged **features** (pottery, tools, irrigation, crops, clothing, houses) → **regional Chalcolithic cultures**.

**Four main pre-Harappan regional cultures** (recognise on a map; class: no need to mug every village):

| Culture | Region (class) |
|:---|:---|
| **Amri–Nal** | **Sindh** and **Gujarat** |
| **Kot Diji** | Main **Indus** — northern Sindh and western Punjab (Multan belt) |
| **Sothi–Siswal** | **Haryana**, north **Rajasthan**, south **Punjab** — between present **Sutlej** and older **Saraswati**. Class: **Ropar** northern end of this culture, **Kalibangan** southern |
| **Damb Sadaat** | **Balochistan** |

**Proto-urban features already here:** straight roads, boundary wall, houses in a line, **baked bricks**, bead production, seal production. These become the **basis** of Mature urbanization → again: **gradual, successive, indigenous**.

### Mature Harappan / IVC (2600–1900 BC)

By **2600 BC** the regional cultures produced enough surplus for **high-volume long-distance trade**, exchanged **best features**, and **merged** into one **large, integrated, uniform, urban** civilization — **IVC**. Class analogy: metro / more communication → Delhi–Gurugram–Noida feel like one culture.
