## Geological Timeline & Epochs

### Eras (Based on Fossil Evidence)
| Era | Dominated By | Approx. Period |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Palaeozoic Era** (Old Life) | Fish (water creatures) | ~500 crore yrs ago |
| **Mesozoic Era** (Middle Life) | Reptiles / Dinosaurs | ~25 crore yrs ago |
| **Cenozoic Era** (New Life) | Mammals (lion, elephant, whale, monkeys, humans) | ~6.6 crore yrs ago (66 million yrs ago) |

> **Key Transitions:** Single-cell organism (LUCA — Last Universal Common Ancestor) → Cambrian Explosion → Permian-Triassic (Gigantic) Extinction → Cretaceous-Paleogene (Big) Extinction → Mammals dominate → Primates → Homo sapiens (~2 lakh years ago)

### Climatic Ages (Based on Rock/Sediment Evidence)
- Cambrian → Jurassic → Triassic → Pliocene → Oligocene → **Pleistocene** → **Holocene**
- 19th century classification: Primary (Fish) → Secondary (Dinosaurs) → Tertiary (Mammals) → **Quaternary (Humans)**

### Pleistocene Epoch (25 lakh years ago → 10,000 BC)
- Earth entered **cycles of ice ages** (glaciation) and **warm periods** (inter-glaciation)
- Riss glaciation and Würm glaciation were the last two ice ages
- This epoch is characterized by the **cycle of ice ages**
- Human evolution and stone tool usage occurred during this epoch

### Holocene Epoch (10,000 BC → Present)
- **Last ice age ended** → Earth entered a warming period
- "Hol" = Recent (Greek/Hindi); "Holocene" = Recent warming
- **Agriculture began** because of the warming of earth (warming came first → agriculture followed)
- Transition from **hunter-gatherer** to **food producer**

### Anthropocene Epoch (Proposed)
Some scholars suggest that since the **19th century (Industrial Revolution)**, warming of earth is **unnatural** and climatic changes are **mainly due to human actions**.
- *Anthropo* = Human → "Human-dominated epoch"
- ⚠️ **Not universally accepted** — still a proposal, not in formal academics

> **Fascinating fact:** If Earth's entire history were compressed into one year:
> - Humans appeared on **31 December at 11:50 PM** (10 minutes of the year)
> - Agriculture appeared at **11:59:40 PM** (20 seconds of evolution!)

## Palaeolithic Age (Stone Age → Anthropology)

> The **formation and use of stone tools** by humans marks the beginning of the Palaeolithic Age.

**Critical distinction:** Accidental use of stone ≠ Palaeolithic Age. **Conscious making** of uniform tools, keeping them for later use = true tool-making = Palaeolithic Age.

- If any animal achieves conscious tool-making, their Palaeolithic Age would start
- Humans are at least **25 lakh years ahead** of all other animals in this respect

### Stratigraphy & Relative Dating
- If you dig up earth, **lower layers = older** (called **relative dating** or **stratigraphy**)
- Eras determined by **fossils/bones**; climatic ages determined by **rocks/sediments**

---

### 1. Lower Palaeolithic Age (25 lakh years ago – 1.5 lakh years ago)

The **oldest stone tools** made by humans are found at the **lowest level** in archaeology; therefore it was named **Lower Palaeolithic Age**.

> This is the longest period: ~23.5 lakh years out of 25 lakh years total

#### Tool Tradition 1: Oldowan Tools
- **First discovered in:** Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania, Eastern Africa
- These were **pebble tools** made by the process of **flaking** and used mainly for **chopping**
- Therefore also called **chopper-chopping tools**
- **Process:** Take a pebble (stone rounded by water action) → break into core + flake → discard flake → use core edges for chopping
- **Chopping** = cutting by force (like a helicopter/chopper cuts air)
- Used primarily for: breaking animal bones (for marrow), cutting skin
- ~99% diet was vegetarian, only ~1% non-veg
- **Human species associated:** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Homo Habilis**</span> (first tool user)

#### In India — Oldowan/Soanian Tools
- Earliest Oldowan tools discovered on the **banks of Soan River** (tributary of Sutlej), **Potwar Plateau, West Punjab, Pakistan**
- Therefore called **Soanian Tools**
- Soan River tools dated to ~20 lakh years ago (Rawat, Pakistan)
- ⚠️ **No Homo Habilis fossil is found in India**

#### Tool Tradition 2: Acheulean Tools
- The **second major tool tradition** of Lower Palaeolithic Age
- Named after **Saint-Acheul, France** (first discovery site)
- These tools were **triangular hand axes** used for various purposes like chopping, hammering, scraping, boring, etc.
- Major improvement over Oldowan — hammer/axe-like action reduces jerk to head
- **Human species associated:** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Homo Erectus**</span> (first to walk straight)

#### Acheulean Tools in India
- **Majority of Lower Palaeolithic sites in India have Acheulean tools**
- Also called **Madrasian Industry** (after sites near Madras/Chennai)
- **Key Indian Sites:**
  - Didwana, Singi Talav (Rajasthan)
  - Belan Valley (Allahabad, UP)
  - Paisra, Munger (Bihar)
  - Bhimbetka (Raisen, MP)
  - Adamgarh (Narmadapuram/Hoshangabad, MP)
  - Sabarmati Valley (Ahmedabad, Gujarat)
  - Hunsgi (Karnataka)
  - **Attirampakkam, Pallavaram** (Tamil Nadu) — very famous
  - Kuliana (Odisha)

#### Oldest Human Fossil in India
- **Skull of Homo Erectus**, belonging to **6 lakh years ago**
- Discovered in **1982** at **Hathnora** (Sehore, MP), in **Narmada Valley**
- Named **Narmada Man** or **Narmada Human**
- Discovered by **Arun Sonakia**

> **Why so few fossils in India?** Indian soil is generally **acidic** → bones decompose. Also, frequent river movements expose and scatter remains. Animals may have taken bones. Tools found in great numbers, but fossils are scarce.

---

### 2. Middle Palaeolithic Age (1.5 lakh years ago – 45,000 years ago)

The **strata (depositional layer)** found above the deposits of Lower Palaeolithic Age is identified as **Middle Palaeolithic Age**.

#### Tool Tradition: Mousterian Tools
- Named after **Le Moustier**, a site in France
- Prepared by the process of **bifacial flaking** (**Levallois technique**)
  - Take a stone disc → hammer from both sides → entire tool has a sharp edge
- Used mainly as **scrapers, points, triangles**, etc.
- **Human species associated:** <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Homo Neanderthalensis (Neanderthal man)**</span>

#### Mousterian Tools in India
- First discovered in **Nevasa** (near Pune / Ahmednagar, Maharashtra)
- Therefore called **Nevasian Tools**
- ⚠️ **No Neanderthal fossil is discovered in India so far**

#### Neanderthals & Evidence of Religion
Sites of Neanderthal man are the **earliest to give evidence of religion**, including:
- **Respectful burial of dead**
- Burial in a **specific pattern** (e.g., feet southwards)
- **Grave goods** (objects kept with the dead body)
- **Food kept in non-habitational caves**

#### Emergence of Homo Sapiens
- **Homo Sapiens appeared either at the end of Lower Palaeolithic Age or in Middle Palaeolithic Age**
- *Sapien* comes from the Sanskrit word *Sapna* (dream/think) — "the one who thinks/dreams"
- Homo Sapiens are the species that create art, imagination, novels, paintings
- Only Homo Sapiens are **superstitious** and **religious** — sign of intelligence (obsessive pattern tracking)

---

### 3. Upper Palaeolithic Age (45,000 years ago – 10,000 BC)

#### Key Characteristics
- **All other human species went extinct**; only **Homo Sapiens survived**
- This age **coincided with the last phase of Pleistocene Epoch** (last ice age)

#### Tool Tradition: Parallel Blades & Bone Tools
- Major tools: **Parallel blades** — sophisticated, fragile, indicates better hand control
- For the **first time**, **substantial bone tools** were used
  - Scoops, sharp hunting knives (double-edged), kitchen knives (single-edged with blunt side)
  - Crisscross grip patterns — consciously designed
  - Example: **Kurnool Caves, Andhra Pradesh**

#### Art & Cave Paintings
- For the **first time**, **art forms were made by humans** — especially **carvings and paintings on cave walls**
- **Key Sites:**
  - **Bhimbetka** — Raisen, MP (local legend: Bheem used to sit here → Bheem-Bethak → Bhimbetka)
  - **Adamgarh** — Narmadapuram (Hoshangabad), MP
  - **Lakhudiyar** — Almora, Uttarakhand

## Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age) — 10,000 BC to 8,000 BC

The **end of Pleistocene Epoch** (last ice age) and the **beginning of Holocene Epoch** (warming of earth) marks the beginning of the **transition of humans from hunter-gatherers towards food producers**. This **transitionary phase** is called the **Middle Stone Age** or **Mesolithic Age**.

> Mesolithic = Epi-Palaeolithic (peak of Palaeolithic) + Proto-Neolithic (earliest Neolithic)

### Key Features

#### Targeted Hunting of Smaller Animals
- In the beginning, humans started **targeted hunting-gathering of relatively smaller animals**
- Ice age mammals (mammoths) had gone extinct; ice sheets melted; river streams appeared
- Earlier mammoth-hunting strategies (chasing, pit traps, cliff drives) didn't work for small, fast animals
- **Solution:** Compound tools with stone tips on sticks → **microliths**

#### Tool Tradition: Microliths
- **Microliths** are **small stone tools** (1–4 cm) used mainly as part of **compound/composite tools**
- Tied to tops of sticks as arrowheads, spear tips
- Some had **back-to-back** binding — when removed, creates a bigger wound
- Shape of arrowheads same even today — only material has changed

#### Domestication & Surplus
- **Domestication of plants and animals began** in Mesolithic Age
  - ⚠️ Agriculture & livestock are **features of Neolithic Age**, but domestication **began** here
- **Surplus appeared for the first time** (although **irregular and unreliable**)
- **Pottery appeared** in some Mesolithic cultures
  - ⚠️ Pottery **appeared** in Mesolithic (exception) | Pottery is a **feature** of Neolithic (rule)

> **Terminology:** Microlith = small stone tool | Macrolith = large stone tool | **Megalith** = large stones kept on graves (e.g., Taj Mahal, Humayun's Tomb, Sanchi Stupa, Rajghat)

#### Key Indian Sites
- **Belan Valley** (Allahabad, UP) — Lekhania, Morana Pahad
- **Sarai Nahar Rai** (Pratapgarh, UP)
- **Lahuradev** (UP)

## Neolithic Age (New Stone Age) — 8th millennium BC to 4th millennium BC

### Definition
The practice of **agriculture and livestock as main activity** marks the beginning of Neolithic Age.

### Key Characteristics

#### Settled Life & Villages
- Due to agriculture, humans started living **sedentary life (settled life)** in **villages** (houses built near agricultural fields)
- Although hunting-gathering continued, it became a **secondary activity**

#### Tool Tradition: Celts
- Main tools were **Celts** (C-E-L-T-S)
- Celts were **polished, finished, symmetric, and precision tools**
  - Axes, chisels (asymmetric — to produce fire), grinding stones (*silbatta*)
- **Microliths further evolved** and became **symmetric**
- These are the works of **specialized tool-makers** (part-time hunter-gatherers or full-time craftsmen)

#### Neolithic Revolution
- Due to **agricultural surplus and settled life**, population **greatly expanded**
- Due to large surplus, a **substantial part of population was now free from the obligation of food production**
- They took up **other professions**: tool-making, pottery, cloth making, masonry, etc.
- This led to **division of labour, specialization**, etc., greatly **improving the efficiency of society**
- Due to these **sudden and fundamental changes**, humans became the **prime species** who now **control all other species of plants and animals**
- Therefore it is now known as the **Neolithic Revolution**

> **Evolution vs Revolution:** If change is fundamental but slow = **Evolution**. If change is fundamental and sudden = **Revolution**. Neolithic Revolution = sudden & fundamental.

## Human Speciation Summary

| Species | Key Feature | Associated Tools |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Australopithecus** | First to practice **bipedalism** (walk on two feet) | — |
| **Homo Habilis** | First **tool user** | Oldowan (chopper-chopping) tools |
| **Homo Erectus** | First to **walk straight** | Acheulean (triangular hand axes) |
| **Homo Neanderthalensis** | Mainly in Europe; earliest **evidence of religion** | Mousterian (Levallois technique) |
| **Homo Sapiens** | First to **think/dream**; art, imagination, religion | Parallel blades, bone tools, microliths, celts |

> **Key clarification:** Monkeys are NOT our ancestors — monkeys are our **cousins**. We and monkeys share a **common ancestor**. Homo Erectus is a true ancestor. Neanderthals (Europe) and Denisovans (Asia) interbred with Homo Sapiens.

## Key Concepts for UPSC

### Uncanny Valley Theory
- Explains why we fear **human ghosts** but not animal ghosts
- During evolution, different human species (Homo Erectus, Neanderthals) **killed each other**
- Faces that are **~95% human** (almost-but-not-quite) trigger fear
- This evolutionary memory persists as our fear of "uncanny" faces

### Selfish Gene & Survival
- Selfish mothers' children survived → selfish gene passed on
- Egoistic humans survived → ego is evolutionary
- Non-egoistic humans could not survive in hunter-gatherer age
- **All our weaknesses come from evolution**

### Why Monkeys Didn't Evolve Further
- We did NOT evolve from modern monkeys
- Modern monkeys and humans **evolved from a common ancestor**
- "Monkeys are our cousins, not our grandparents"
