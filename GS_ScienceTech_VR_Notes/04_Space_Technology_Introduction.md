## 2. Biotechnology preview (later block) (ST-04-02)

After outer space, class will jump to the opposite extreme — **molecular** sizes.

**Biotechnology** is not a literal “biology + technology.” Functionally: **all the tools and techniques** through which humans change the **rate and direction of evolution** of a living organism — and can even cause **extinction**. Class: that is “playing God / nature.”

**Evolution** (class): gradual and **accumulative** changes towards **better adaptability** to the environment. Everything evolves — living, non-living, culture, language, even the Civil Services Examination.

**Cotton → bollworms → BT cotton**

- Cotton’s damaging pests = **bollworms** (larvae of a moth). They feed on cotton **bolls** and cut productivity. (Audio: “ball bombs” / “bonbons” = bollworms.)
- Given centuries, cotton *might* evolve resistance. We do not wait.
- **BT cotton** is the biotech shortcut: **gradual → quick**. Class: **BT here is not the word “biotechnology.”** (Sheet did not expand the letters; do not invent a full form until next class.)
- Knock-on: cotton becomes non-edible to the pest → bollworm numbers fall → birds that eat them are hit. One gene edit moves a **food web**.

**CA hooks for later:** India–United States trade deal and **genetically modified (GM)** crops; **GM mustard**; **messenger Ribonucleic Acid (mRNA)** cancer therapy; **ethanol** from molasses / maize / rice (productivity of feedstock).

## 4. What “space” means — Karman Line and the Outer Space Treaty (ST-04-03, ST-04-04)

**Space** (generic) = any **three-dimensional unoccupied** area. **Space technology** always means **outer space**.

Walk up from Earth’s curvature. Imaginary boundary at **100 km** = **Kármán / Karman Line**, proposed by **Theodor Karman** (class: Hungarian physicist — no biography needed).

| Domain | Where | Activities | Sovereignty |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Aeronautical space** | **Below** the line | Needs **air** — aircraft | **Yes.** Airspace belongs to the state underneath. Class joke: BrahMos “evening walk” over Pakistan. |
| **Outer / astronautical / astronomical space** | **Beyond** the line | Spacecraft, satellites | **No** sovereign claim. Common property of humankind. **No military base** on Moon / Mars / an asteroid. |

**100 km is not rigid.** **National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)** / United States treats it as **80 km**. Lies “largely in that zone.”

**United Nations Outer Space Treaty (OST), 1967** — Cold War, fear of space militarisation. Class: **non-binding / non-enforceable** — moral pressure. That is why militarisation is creeping back.

## 5. What a satellite is — and the three application families (ST-04-05)

**Generic satellite** = any entity whose **identity is derived around another body**. NCR cities are satellites of Delhi. Moon is Earth’s satellite; Earth is the Sun’s. **Microsatellite Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA)** in biology uses the same word with no orbit.

**Here:** **artificial / application satellites**, **majorly** around Earth — not absolutely. **Chandrayaan** orbits the Moon; **Mangalyaan** orbited Mars; **Shukrayaan** would orbit Venus.

Value is not “a new application terrestrial tech cannot do.” Value is **coverage** → **cheaper** service. Higher altitude → bigger footprint.

| Family | What it does | Class applications |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Communication** | Links | **70–75%** of use = **TV broadcast**. Also **tele-education**, **tele-medicine** (Siachen soldier guided from a city), **satellite-based internet services (SBIS)** — **Starlink** most numerous / successful. |
| **Remote sensing (RS)** / **Earth Observation Satellite (EOS)** | Sense from a distance | Forestry, agriculture (this year’s GS-I), geology / minerals, disaster management, weather, **military surveillance** + **surgical-strike precision**. |
| **Navigation** | **Position and timing** in real time | The blue dot + “you will be there by…”. Essay hook: tech making humans loners. Class: siblings in **Uttar Pradesh (UP)** guided onto a half-built bridge. |

**Trap:** an **EOS is necessarily** a remote-sensing satellite. A remote-sensing satellite is **not necessarily** Earth-sensing — **Chandrayaan** remote-senses the **Moon**.

## 6. Why 100 km — orbital velocity, fuel, collision avoidance (ST-04-06)

**Orbit** = the path. Each orbit needs a **unique orbital velocity**. More or less → you leave that orbit.

Sheet: **~30 km** holds **99.99%** of Earth’s atmosphere. Audio: **under the Karman Line** sits **nearly 100%** of the atmosphere (gravity glues it). **International Space Station (ISS)** class altitude: **~400 km**.

**Thought experiment:** plant a satellite at **30 km** vs **400 km**, both given the “right” speed.

- **30 km:** air **resists** → speed drops → only fix is **burn fuel continuously** → tank empty in **minutes**. You do not spend billions for minutes. **No satellite stays in orbit powered by fuel.**
- **Beyond atmosphere:** give the velocity **once**. **Inertia** keeps it going unless an external force acts.

That is **why** outer space is defined where it is: **beyond Karman, motion is not fuel-powered.**

**Satellites still carry fuel** — for three jobs only:

1. **Acquire initial velocity**
2. **Change orbit** — **orbit raising** (higher) / **orbit lowering** (lower). Today’s GISAT: **transfer orbit** = temporary; must be raised.
3. **Collision Avoidance Manoeuvre (CAM)** — dead objects at **25–30,000 km/h**, millions of them. Satellite must “dodge.” Fuel for CAMs.

Fuel quantity is a **strong determinant of lifespan**. First mistake after dry tanks is the last. World (including India) is working on **on-orbit refuelling** — not operational in class’s “at the moment.”

## 7. History — balloons, sounding rockets, Space Race 1.0 (ST-04-07)

Astronomy is as old as humankind. **Space technology** is ~a century old.

**1930 (sheet and class):** United States sent **hot-air balloons** — first military surveillance (height = coverage), then **scientific instruments** for **vertical profiling** (layer-by-layer: thermometer, pH, carbon dioxide (CO₂), magnetic field, particles). **Two limits:** very **slow**; ceiling ~**25 km** (outer pressure falls, balloon bursts / drops). Not outer space — but it posed the problem.

**Sounding rockets** — faster + higher; still carry instruments for **atmospheric investigation**. **“Sounding”** is figurative: a **naval** term (sound waves for **underwater** vertical profiling). Not because the rocket uses sound.

**India / ISRO** example is **later than Sputnik**, not before it. Class named the **Rohini** series (regular; **do not confuse** with **Rohini satellites**). First Indian sounding-rocket launch: **21 November 1963** (Nike-Apache from **Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS)**, Thiruvananthapuram). First fully Indian **Rohini RH-75: 20 November 1967**.

### Update — 16 September 2026 (climatology L11)

**Why Thumba:** it sits near the **magnetic equator** (advantages for sounding-rocket launch — S&T class). Magnetic equator itself = Rizwan Sir geomagnetism (`07_Geomagnetism.md`). Climatology L11 only named TERLS as the third-equator example. **No extra Day-1.** Dates above stay this file.

**Space Race 1.0** = **military hostility + geopolitical antagonism + high optics** (whatever goes up is visible). Tool of **upmanship**, not humanity. Expect a Mains “**then vs now**” question.

| Who / when | What (class) |
|:---|:---|
| **Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR), 1957** | **Sputnik** — first artificial satellite. US President: “just one small ball in the air.” Optics = nationalism. |

**Prelims trap (class wrote a four-option question):** valuable because of (1) **zero gravity** (2) **weightlessness** (3) **high-frequency radiation**.

| Claim | Verdict |
|:---|:---|
| **Zero gravity** | **Wrong.** If gravity were zero the station would **escape** Earth. Gravity is what **binds** it. You get **microgravity** — very small, **not zero**. Gravity falls with distance. |
| **Weightlessness** | **Correct.** Station is in **continuous free fall**. Weight is felt as the **recoil** of a surface. No platform “pushing back” → no weight. ISS has been “falling” for **over two decades** without hitting Earth because **orbital velocity** makes the **curve of fall parallel to Earth’s curve**. |
| **High-frequency radiation** (ultraviolet (UV), X-rays, …) | **Correct.** Outside the atmosphere those frequencies are **accessible**. |

**Orbital-velocity recap (Newton’s tower):** throw harder → land farther; give the **specific** velocity → you keep “falling around” the planet. Velocity is a **vector**; gravity still pulls; the path is curved **because** of gravity.

## 11. Outer-space warfare (ST-09-02)

Once one side could put up a satellite, the other side started learning to knock it down. The technology is not exotic: turn a missile **vertical**. Lower satellites sit a **few hundred kilometres** up. Missiles already fly **thousands of kilometres**.

| Kill | What it is |
|:---|:---|
| **Hard kill** | **Kinetic** hit. The missile is an **anti-satellite (ASAT)** weapon. Creates a cloud of fragments that can kill satellites you never meant to touch. Kept for **deterrence** |
| **Soft kill** | **Hack the communication.** Order a small turn or a small shift. A satellite aimed at one country can be made to miss Earth entirely |

**Who has shown the hard kill (class order, no extra years):** the United States developed it after the Soviet satellite; Russia then showed it could hit too; **China in 2007** hit one of its own; **India in 2019**, **Mission Shakti**, hit one of its own in a **lower** orbit.

India’s answer to the “irresponsible debris” charge: (1) the right to build **deterrence**; (2) the hit was **low**, so fragments **settle through the atmosphere** and do not stay as debris. A BBC line that India was becoming a **major** debris source is answered with his comparison in §16. He called it the pot and the kettle.

**Moon militarised.** US and China trade the charge. The fear: a base on the Moon that can hit satellites at will. “Who controls the Moon controls the world” is the claim behind grabbing a patch — and behind “the Moon is ours.”

**Tourism and colonisation** sit on the same board (figure above). Tourism is already low-Earth-orbit; lunar and Martian tourism are the later step. People pay **20, 30, 40, 50, even 60 million dollars** for **one seat** and **a few minutes**. That revenue is an argument for funding the missions the examiner thinks have no application. Colonisation is the far one: **SpaceX** says it is working to colonise **Mars**.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** On this board **OSM / OSW / OSC** are mining, warfare, colonisation. **“OST” on the sheet is tourism.** In Lecture 10, **OST** is the **Outer Space Treaty, 1967**. Do not swap them. **Mission Shakti = 2019**, low orbit, India’s own satellite. **China’s test = 2007.** Hard kill is deterrence because the fragments are indiscriminate. Soft kill is a hack, not a missile.</span>

## 12. From two national stations to one shared platform (ST-09-03)

Lecture 10: US **Skylab**, USSR **Mir**, each alone, was the foolish first chapter. As the Cold War cooled they agreed coexistence would produce more. He dated that shared project from **1975**, completed **2000**: the **International Space Station (ISS)**.

| Fact | Class |
|:---|:---|
| What it is | An **orbital platform** for sustained human presence. Functionally a **laboratory in space**. **Largest man-made object** in space, on the scale of a **football field** |
| How it was built | **Module by module** (a module is any component with a **defined function** — the word is not space-only). **Docking** = functional fitting. Two modules in different orbits and speeds must be brought to one orbit, relative speed near **zero**, then joined. Controlled from Earth |

## 13. What a station is used for (ST-09-04, ST-09-05)

Biggest slice: **human physiology** (functional systems — heart, digestion, nerves, hormones, and the rest). Even a lazy body works against **gravity** all day: heart, brain, blood flow, skin elasticity, muscle tone, bone mineral. **Microgravity** challenges every one of those, which is why the station is a disease laboratory. If a Prelims list offers seven body systems, do not strike them out — someone is studying them.

| Line | Class |
|:---|:---|
| **Muscle degeneration** | Returning crews cannot walk unaided. Muscle memory is lost; rehabilitation follows. The accelerated loss is a window on **muscular dystrophy / muscular atrophy**. Gene therapy exists: **one injection**, which he priced at **₹18 crore**, and it used to be the world’s costliest |
| **Ageing** | Muscle loss is also an ageing question. If the paper says the station is used for **ageing research**, that is **correct**. **Wing Commander Shubhanshu Shukla**, **Axiom-4**, worked on this |
| **Neurodegeneration** | **Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s**, and the like |
| **Biological clock** | **Endocrine** system, **circadian** rhythm. On Earth the body tunes itself to one sunrise and one sunset. On the station: **16 sunrises and 16 sunsets a day** |
| **And the rest** | He added **cancer** as one more human-system study, not a closed list |

**Model organisms** — not every human question can be run on humans. It started with **Laika**. Since then: dog, cat, chimpanzee, tortoise, and very commonly **mice** (body plan close to ours, small, easy to breed in numbers). **Plants:** Shukla took household seeds, including **methi**, to germinate back on Earth and look for change. **Insects:** cockroach; **fruit fly (Drosophila)** — a favourite for **genetics**, **not less than a dozen Nobel Prizes**, called the **Cinderella of genetics**. Fruit flies are a likely **Gaganyaan** passenger. **Microbes:** bacteria, fungi, viruses — including **antibiotic resistance**.

**Material science.** Structure decides function, and **gravity helps decide structure**. In microgravity a material takes a **new structure**, so new properties, so new applications. Recent Indian example: **Bhubaneswar-based Serendipity Space** — drug / pharmacological synthesis in microgravity; molecules may fold differently. **3D printing:** particles layered by a program; low gravity means **less compaction**. **Bioprinting:** the layered particles are **living cells** → a **tissue** (ear, nose, drug tests). Printing a whole **organ** is the aim, **not** something happening now. Sustaining that condition on Earth would be costly; up there the microgravity is the point.

**Fluid mechanics.** Flow changes (water floats as globules; you catch them). One use he gave: a better **fuel-injection / ignition** system, because how fast fuel is injected decides the engine. Peristalsis still moves a swallowed drink; that was an aside.

**Astronomy.** **Astrophotography** from the **Cupola** (glass walls; no PM2.5, PM10, or haze). Sensors **outside** the station see **high-frequency radiation** (ultraviolet, X-rays) that the atmosphere **blankets** before it reaches Earth. Same radiation point as Lecture 10.

**Atmosphere, sometimes.** **Transient Luminous Events (TLE)** — very short, a **fraction of a second**. Ordinary lightning is the luminous event **under** the cloud, seen from the ground. Pilots above the cloud reported a **halo** plus **branched tentacles** like a jellyfish, on the **upper** side, rarely visible from Earth. **ELVES** (he spelled E-L-V-E-S) = the halo. **Sprite** = the branches. In **2018** the **European Space Agency** put a camera on the station and photographed them. So a station can do atmospheric science, not as its everyday job.

### Diplomacy — the station as geopolitics

Space technology in general, and a space station in particular, are **as much a manifestation of global geopolitics as they are a factor shaping it**. What happens on Earth shows up in space, and space developments steer Earth.

1. **Russia** has occasionally **threatened to leave** the ISS, then stayed: building its own is expensive, it is already at war, and the ISS is near the end of its life anyway.
2. **China and Russia** are collaborating on stations **around Earth and around the Moon**. The lunar one is **ILRS** (International Lunar Research Station) — Lecture 10.
3. **NASA** has offered ISRO **assistance / a probable collaboration** for the upcoming **BAS**.
4. **India**, on the contrary, wishes to **ally with Russia** for the same station.
5. **China** is extending the **Silk Road** initiative into outer space: **Tiangong** visits at a **competitive price** for the **Global South**. He named **Pakistan** as the obvious first beneficiary.
6. The **European Space Agency (ESA)** also wants to cooperate with India on **BAS**.

News he tied to point 6: an **International Space Summit in Paris**; the item he showed was dated **9 September** — ESA exploring cooperation on **human spaceflight, lunar, and Venus** missions. He had also said, just before the BRICS meet in India, that a Prime Minister had come from that Paris summit and ESA had said it was willing to use BAS for research.

## 14. Space debris — what it is, and why it is exploding (ST-09-06)

It is no longer two space powers. Nearly **18** nations are space-faring, and private companies are pushing satellites in great numbers.

**Space debris / space junk** = **man-made defunct objects**. He spelled the pronunciation as “duh-BREE”. Singular and plural are the same word.

| Count | Class |
|:---|:---|
| Functional satellites now | **About 15,000** |
| Of those, Starlink | He said **over 10,000**, so the rest of the world is about **5,000**. He also said **8,000** Starlink already lifted. Sheet locks **15,000** and the **42,000** plan, not the 8,000-versus-10,000 split |
| Larger than **10 cm** | **Over 33,000**. This is the size that can be **monitored from Earth**. It understates the problem |
| Smaller than **10 cm** | **Millions** |
| Speed | Sheet: **20 / 25 / 50 thousand km/h**. He also said **25,000–30,000 km/h**. Faster than a bullet. A **ball bearing** at that speed can kill a working satellite |

Lifespans he used as examples: **8, 10, 20 years**. Then the satellite is functionally dead and **stays up**.

**What the junk is**

1. **Dead satellites and their components** — parts come off and travel separately.
2. **Upper stages of launch vehicles.** Lower stages fall into the ocean. The upper stage delivers the satellite and then **keeps orbiting**, with no job. ISRO’s partial answer is the **PSLV Orbital Experimental Module (POEM)**: scientific instruments on that upper stage, so after delivery it still returns data (radiation, or a robotic arm whose flex you test from Earth). **POEM does not reduce the debris. It reduces the guilt.**
3. **Objects humans add by accident**, mostly on a **spacewalk**. The suit is radioprotective; the gloves kill fine grip; wrenches and screws slip; you cannot chase them.

**Why the pile is exploding now**

1. **More agencies.** State agencies are the smaller worry. **Private** companies are the large one. In India, even though the opening is late: **over 400** registered, **over 1,000** lined up. A firm on the scale of SpaceX has an economic weight above the **GDP of most countries**.
2. **New services.** **Satellite-based internet.** **Starlink** (SpaceX): full package **over 42,000** (he first said 48,000, then corrected). **OneWeb** (European): **more than 1,000**. **Project Kuiper** (Amazon): **more than 1,000**. Sheet also writes **Guowang** beside them; he did not expand the name. And **orbital data centres**: servers in orbit, because ground data centres eat **power and water**. In orbit the power is **solar**, and the heat leaves by **radiative cooling**. SpaceX’s projection he quoted: **not less than a million** satellites. **Pixxel** is working towards **India’s first** orbital data centre, “by this year.” **Agnikul Cosmos** is another Indian effort. *The Hindu*, **April 2026**: that million-satellite layer, and experts’ worry. With the Starlink craft already up, he said **a satellite drops on an average every day**. One night last September, about **1:30**, Delhi and the National Capital Region saw a fiery streak — a **Starlink** re-entry, not a meteor.

## 15. Kessler, removal, and where the junk goes (ST-09-07)

**Kessler**, an American astrophysicist, in the **1980s**: put up satellites this way and **one collision** starts an **uncontrollable cascade**. The number of pieces **keeps rising even if launches stop**, because the pieces already up hit each other and multiply. **Number matters more than size** — the ball bearing is enough.

That statement (**Kessler’s syndrome**) is less a definition of the mess than a **hint at the solution**. “Send fewer” is not available: space technology worldwide is **not even 20% saturated** (phones and television are near saturation; space is not). The solution is **remove more**: **Space Debris Removal (SDR)**.

Identify a dead object moving at tens of thousands of kilometres an hour, catch it, and take it away. Wishful, and hard.

| Challenge | Class |
|:---|:---|
| **1. Cost** | A cleanup mission is immensely expensive. Nothing forces a state to pay. Five new satellites will look cheaper than a cleanup |
| **2. The tools** | **Magnetic pull** (fly a strong magnet through a dense patch). **Net or sail** (he said China is developing this: shoot a net, or run a sheet into the stream). **Robotic claw / arm** for **larger** pieces. **Laser ablation**: go very close and **vaporise** — **India and Japan** are collaborating. Every one of these needs **AI pattern recognition**, or you will grab a **live** satellite |
| **3. A weapon** | The same arm or laser can take an enemy’s **working** satellite. This is the space-warfare problem again, and one reading of “weapons in space” |
| **4. Who must** | **No law** regulates **space traffic**. The answer he wants in a Mains copy: the world needs an **enforceable** law that makes removal a **common but differentiated responsibility** — collective, **in proportion to what you contributed**. Bhutan is not sent the whole bill. Once it is a legal duty, removal can become a **service you sell** |

**Mission Embrace**, flown on **Vikram-1** (**Skyroot Aerospace** — the private orbital launcher from Lecture 10). A satellite with arms, carrying **its own** debris block, to practise **throw and catch**. A question “Mission Embrace is related to” = **debris-removal technology**. The company name in the audio did not land cleanly.

**After you have caught it**

Maximum congestion is in the orbits just outside the **Karman line**, a few hundred kilometres up: **low Earth orbit (LEO)**.

| Orbit | What you do with a dead satellite |
|:---|:---|
| **LEO** | **Pull it down.** It falls through the atmosphere and **burns by friction**. Not every craft burns equally well. **Japan** has flown a **wooden** satellite, **LignoSat** (sheet spelling), so the body itself is the firewood |
| **35,786 km** | The farthest orbit in which we park Earth satellites (Lecture 10). Hauling one all the way down is **not viable**. **Push it out beyond 35,786 km** and leave it circling where no working satellite sits. That outer path is the **graveyard orbit** |

## Prelims traps (class)

- **Karman = 100 km**; **NASA / US ≈ 80 km** — not a brick wall.
- **OST 1967 is non-binding.** No sovereign claim; no mili-base on celestial bodies — **moral**, not a police force.
- **EOS ⊂ remote sensing**; Chandrayaan is RS, **not** an Earth-observation sat.
- Satellites do **not** stay up by burning fuel. Fuel = start + raise/lower + **CAM**.
- **Three** sats at **35,786 km** (equilateral, same orbit) → global cover.
- Space station ≠ **zero gravity**. **Microgravity + weightlessness + radiation.**
- **Rohini sounding rockets ≠ Rohini satellites.**
- **BT** in BT cotton **≠** the word biotechnology.
- **India is not in the space race** (Sarabhai Doctrine) even if a **2040** human-Moon goal exists.
