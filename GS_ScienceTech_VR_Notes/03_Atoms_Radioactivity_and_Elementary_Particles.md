## 1. Atom → Nucleus → What Is “Elementary”? (ST-03-01, ST-03-02)

High-school story: the atom was the smallest indivisible bit. It is not. An atom has a **nucleus** (protons + neutrons) and **electrons** in a cloud / **orbitals** around it.

**Elementary particle** = not made of anything smaller; indivisible building block. **Protons and neutrons are not elementary** — each is three **quarks**. **Electrons are elementary** (lepton family, not the quark family).

**~118 elements** discovered. **Identity of an element = number of protons = atomic number (Z).** Electrons do not: strip an electron from hydrogen and it is still hydrogen (now charged). **Sodium chloride (NaCl):** sodium donates an electron to chlorine — both stay Na and Cl.

Periodic table is ordered by **increasing Z**.

| Particle | Charge / mass (class) | Where | Elementary? | Discovery (class / sheet) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Proton** | Positive; in nucleus | Nucleus | **No** — 3 quarks (**2 up + 1 down**), held by **gluons** | **Ernest Rutherford** |
| **Neutron** | Uncharged; mass **slightly larger** than a proton | Nucleus | **No** — 3 quarks (**2 down + 1 up**) | **James Chadwick** |
| **Electron** | Negative; **negligible mass** | Orbitals around nucleus | **Yes** — **lepton** | **J.J. Thomson** |

Almost **all mass** sits in the nucleus. Nucleus held by the **strong nuclear force** — **strongest of the four fundamental forces** — which overcomes **electrostatic repulsion** between protons.

**Gluons** = force-carrying “tiny bundles of energy” (same family as **photons** → later: **bosons**). They keep quarks together and give the strong force that holds the nucleus.

## 2. Notation, Isotopes, Hydrogen and Carbon (ST-03-01)

**Z** = protons. **Mass number A** = protons + neutrons.

Standard symbol: \(^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}\) (example \(^{12}_{6}\mathrm{C}\)).

**Isotopes** = same element (same **Z**) , different neutron number → different **A**. **Similar chemical properties** (same electrons); **nuclear properties can differ**.

**Hydrogen**

| Isotope | Notation (sheet) | Notes (class) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Protium** | \(^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}\) | Ordinary; non-radioactive; makes \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) |
| **Deuterium** | \(^{2}_{1}\mathrm{H}\) | Extra neutron; \(\mathrm{D_2O}\) = **heavy water** (needed in **nuclear reactors**, not as drinking water) |
| **Tritium** | \(^{3}_{1}\mathrm{H}\) | **Scarce** on Earth; \(\mathrm{T_2O}\); **radioactive** |

**Carbon:** **C-12** non-radioactive / stable; **C-14** radioactive — used for **carbon dating**.

## 3. Radioactivity, α / β / γ, Half-Life (ST-03-03, ST-03-04)

**Radioactivity:** spontaneous emission of energetic **radiation** (gamma) or energetic **particles** (alpha, beta) from the nucleus of an **unstable** atom, to become more stable. Spontaneous = the species that emits α cannot be made to emit β instead. If the daughter is still unstable, the chain continues.

### 3.1 What is emitted

| | What it is | Charge | Nucleus change (class) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Alpha (α)** | **2 protons + 2 neutrons** = **helium nucleus** = \(\mathrm{He}^{2+}\) (“doubly charged helium”) | Positive | \(^{A}_{Z}X \xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y\) — **new element** |
| **Beta (β⁻)** | **Fast-moving electron.** Inside: \(n \rightarrow p + e^{-}\); proton **stays** in the nucleus | Negative | \(^{A}_{Z}X \xrightarrow{\beta^{-}} {}^{A}_{Z+1}Y\) — **A unchanged, Z + 1** |
| **Gamma (γ)** | **Energy only** (not a particle in this scheme) | — | No change of Z or A in the class picture |

**Penetrating power:** **γ > β > α**. Class: α exposure is the *least* dangerous of the three in this ranking; **gamma is highly dangerous** (largest penetrating capacity).

Beta electron is **not** an orbital electron — it is born in the nucleus from neutron decay.

### 3.2 Half-life

Time in which the radioactive substance falls to **half** its quantity. Measures **rate of decay**. **Every radioactive isotope has its own half-life.** You **cannot alter** it.

**Prelims trap:** **twice the half-life is not the “full life.”** After one half-life you have ½; after two you have ¼ — not zero.

**Uses class drilled**

1. **Chernobyl (1986), USSR — now Ukraine.** Biggest nuclear-reactor disaster in class’s telling. City evacuated; **~40 years on, still abandoned**; unfit for **thousands of years** because isotopes there have **long half-lives**. Half-life is how you **predict** that.
2. **Technetium-99m (Tc-99m)** — medical diagnosis. Half-life **~6 hours**: long enough to inject / ingest a **tiny** dose, map organs / tumours in 2-D or 3-D, then decay to insignificant activity so it does not sit in tissue.
3. **Carbon dating** — age of **dead organic** matter. Living things keep the environmental **C-12 : C-14** ratio (plants fix carbon; animals get it from the food chain). After death, **intake stops**, **C-14 keeps decaying**. Half-life of C-14 **~5,000 years** (class: “around 5,000 something”; sheet **5k yrs**). Ratio mismatch → age. **Bodies are slightly radioactive** while alive because of C-14.

## 4. Elementary-Particle Map (ST-03-05, ST-03-06)

Elementary particles = building blocks; everything else is combinations of them.

Two big families:

**Fermions** = **matter** particles (all ordinary matter). Two types:

| Quarks (6) | Leptons (6) |
|:---|:---|
| Up, Down, Top, Bottom, **Strange**, **Charm** | **Charged (−1):** electron, muon, tau |
| | **Neutral:** electron neutrino, muon neutrino, tau neutrino |

**Bosons** = **force-carrying** particles (“tiny bundles of energy”). Four, matching the **four fundamental forces**:

| Boson | Force |
|:---|:---|
| **Gluon** | Strong nuclear |
| **Photon** | Electromagnetic |
| **W and Z bosons** | Weak nuclear |
| **Graviton** | Gravitational |

A fundamental force exists only if energy / a force-carrier is **exchanged**.

**Quarks**

- Building blocks of protons, neutrons, etc.
- **Only** particles in nature with **fractional charge** (class: ±⅓, ±⅔).
- Can feel **all four** fundamental forces.

**Leptons** feel **weak**, **gravity**, and **electromagnetic if charged**. They **do not** feel the **strong** nuclear force.

**Hadrons** = quark combinations (proton, neutron, …). **Not elementary.**

Class also named **pion** as another quark combination — do not drill unless asked.

**Higgs boson** sits **outside** this four-force table until later: **CERN Large Hadron Collider (LHC), 2012** — **“God particle”** because it (via the **Higgs field**) **gives particles mass**.

## 5. Neutrinos — “Ghost Particles” (ST-03-07)

Lepton family. **Tiny (not massless)** mass, **not yet quantified** in class; **electrically neutral**; **rarely interact** with matter. **Second most abundant** particles in the universe after **photons**.

Because they barely interact, they keep information from **origin of the universe**, neutron stars, nuclear events. They **cannot be contained** — class: in principle useful even for spotting **hidden underground nuclear activity**. Hard to detect.

**India-based Neutrino Observatory (INO):** **Theni district, Tamil Nadu** — **Bodi West Hills**. Tunnel **~1.2 km** deep so only neutrinos arrive; other radiation is absorbed. **IceCube** neutrino observatory = **Antarctica**, **not** India’s.

## 6. Bosons, S.N. Bose, States of Matter (ST-03-08)

**Boson** is named for **Satyendra Nath Bose (S.N. Bose)** — **not J.C. Bose**. Contemporary of **Einstein**; they built **Bose–Einstein statistics**. His work helped later discovery of bosons; he did not “find” the particles himself.

**States of matter (class upgrade from Class 10’s three):**

| State | When |
|:---|:---|
| Solid, liquid, gas | Everyday |
| **Plasma** | Extremely high T — electrons knocked off → **ionised gas** (core of the **Sun** / stars). **Not** Bose’s discovery |
| **Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC)** | Another state associated with Bose–Einstein work |

### Particle and anti-particle (class closed here)

Every particle has a counterpart: proton ↔ antiproton, neutron ↔ antineutron, electron ↔ **positron**, neutrino ↔ antineutrino. Same mass, opposite charge.

When a particle meets its antiparticle they convert to **energy as photons**. A high-energy photon can make a **particle–antiparticle pair**. Class: if antimatter could be made commercially it would be an extreme energy source; **CERN LHC** has made only tiny amounts.

## UPSC Cues

- **Z = protons = identity.** Electrons can change without changing the element.
- **p = uud, n = udd**; gluons; **e⁻ = lepton / elementary**. Discoverers: Rutherford / Chadwick / Thomson.
- Isotopes: same Z, different A; **D₂O = heavy water (reactors)**; tritium scarce + radioactive; **C-14 dating**.
- α = He nucleus, **A−4, Z−2**; β⁻ = nuclear electron, **Z+1, A same**; γ = energy; penetration **γ > β > α**.
- Half-life: **2 × half-life ≠ gone**. **Tc-99m ~6 h**; **C-14 ~5,000 y**; Chernobyl **1986 / Ukraine** still empty because of long half-lives.
- Fermions vs bosons; quarks feel **all four** forces; leptons **no strong** force; hadrons **not** elementary.
- **INO = Theni / Bodi West Hills / 1.2 km**; IceCube = Antarctica. Higgs **2012 / God particle / mass**. **Boson ≠ J.C. Bose**. Particle + antiparticle → photons; electron’s antiparticle = **positron**.
