## Part B: Public Health — Key Vocabulary

### Immunity
- **Definition:** Ability of the body to fight against **infection or diseases**
- Role of **White Blood Cells (WBC)** — specifically **B-cell lymphocytes**

### Pathogen / Pathogenic Microbe
- **Disease-causing microorganisms**, including bacteria and virus
- Can also include fungi and protozoa

### Antimicrobial Medicine
- Used to **prevent growth & multiplication** OR **kill** pathogenic microbes
- **Antibiotic** = Anti-**bacterial** medicine → prevents growth/multiplication or kills **bacteria**
  - ⚠️ **Antibiotics do NOT act against viruses**
- **Antiviral drugs** = Anti-**viral** medicine → acts against viruses
  - Needed specifically because antibiotics are ineffective against viruses

### Inflammation
- When there is **injury or infection** to the body → triggers **immune response**
- Body increases blood flow to the affected area → brings more WBCs to fight infection
- **4 signs of inflammation:** Redness, Swelling, Heat, Pain
- Even **fever** is a form of inflammation — body raises temperature to make it difficult for bacteria/virus to multiply
  - ⚠️ Too high fever can damage the body → need to control with antipyretics

### Antipyretic
- **Reduces body temperature** / **reduces/controls fever**

### Analgesic
- **Reduces pain** / acts as **pain reliever**

### Paracetamol — The Dual-Action Drug
- Acts as **BOTH** antipyretic AND analgesic
- ⚠️ **Paracetamol is NOT an antimicrobial medicine**
- Cannot act against any pathogenic microbe (neither bacteria nor virus)
- Only manages symptoms (fever + pain), does NOT treat the infection itself

## Part C: Virus — Definition & Structure

### Definition
> **Virus = Obligate Intracellular Parasite**

Each word carries a specific meaning:

| Word | Meaning | Implication |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Obligate** | Compulsory / Mandatory | Virus **MUST** use a living host cell — cannot complete life cycle without it |
| **Intracellular** | Inside/Within the cell | Virus infects & multiplies **inside** a living cell |
| **Parasite** | Organism dependent on another for life processes | Virus is completely dependent on host cell for reproduction |

- Virus **infects and replicates** inside a living cell called its **host cell**
- **All viruses are parasites** — correct statement for Prelims
- Host cell types: **Plant cell, Animal cell, Bacterial cell, Fungal cell**

### Virus is Acellular
- Virus **lacks any cellular structure** → called **acellular** (without cell)
- This is why we say "bacterial cell" but NEVER "viral cell"
- No nucleus, no organelles, no ribosomes

### Composition of Virus — Nucleocapsid

```
Nucleocapsid = Nucleic Acid + Capsid
                    |              |
          Genetic Material    Protein Coat
          (Viral Genome)    (Protects genome
                             from environment)
```

- **Nucleic acid / Genetic material / Viral genome** — Complete set of biological instructions (software) to make new copies of the virus; contains **genetic code** → genes encoding viral proteins
- **Capsid** — Protein coat/covering that **protects** the genetic material from the outside environment

### DNA vs RNA Virus

| Feature | DNA Virus | RNA Virus |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Genetic Material | Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) | Ribonucleic Acid (RNA) |
| Structure | Double-stranded DNA | Single/double-stranded RNA |
| Examples | HPV, HBV | SARS-CoV-2, Dengue, Nipah, Ebola, Influenza, HIV |

#### Critical Prelims Rule
> **A virus can have EITHER DNA OR RNA as its genome — NEVER BOTH**

#### All Living Systems vs Virus — Genome Rule
| | Plants | Animals | Bacteria | Fungi | **Virus** |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Genetic Material | DNA only | DNA only | DNA only | DNA only | **DNA OR RNA** (never both) |

- In ALL other living systems, the genome is **always only DNA**
- Only viruses can have RNA as their genetic material

## Part D: Virus Tropism (Host Cell Specificity)

- Virus develops **specific preference** for a particular host cell
- Not every virus infects every type of cell
- **Virus tropism** = host cell specificity
- Example: **Polio virus** → infects **only human cells** (not plants, not other animals)

### Examples of Virus Tropism
| Virus | Target Host Cell | Disease |
|:---|:---|:---|
| SARS-CoV-2 | Human lung cells (ACE-2 receptor) | COVID-19 / Severe Pneumonia |
| Hepatitis B | Human liver cells | Liver cancer |
| Polio virus | Human motor neurons | Paralytic polio |
| HPV | Human genital cells | Genital cancer |

## Part E: DNA Virus Examples

### 1. Human Papilloma Virus (HPV)
- Causes **genital cancer** in **both males AND females** — Prelims trap
  - Example: **Cervix cancer** in females
- **Mode of transmission:** Sexual transmission
- **Remains asymptomatic** — person may not show symptoms but can transmit
- **HPV Vaccine:**
  - **Gardasil** — a type of **Subunit / Peptide vaccine** (protein-based)
  - Injects only **virus protein fragment** (NOT full virus) → therefore "subunit"
  - Current India drive: Females aged **9-11 years** (before sexual maturity)
  - Can be given to males too (developed countries already do this)
  - India's current focus: Females first, males later

### 2. Hepatitis B Virus (HBV)
- <span style="color: #e53e3e;">**UPSC Trap Alert (MST-028):** Hepatitis B is a **DNA virus**. (In contrast, Hepatitis A, C, D, and E are RNA viruses).</span>
- Infects **only liver cells** — best example of virus tropism / host specificity
- Can cause **liver cancer** (virus-induced cancer)
- **Preventable** using **HBV vaccine**
- **Remains asymptomatic for many years** → major threat
  - Person unknowingly transmits infection for years
  - May suddenly develop liver cancer symptoms
  - Prelims statement: "Number of HBV cases can be more than HIV" — **CORRECT** (because HBV is asymptomatic for years, HIV shows symptoms within months)

#### Modes of Transmission (Blood-borne virus):
1. Contaminated blood transfusion
2. Unsterilized medical instruments / infected needles
3. Sexual transmission
4. Infected mother → child transmission during pregnancy
5. **Tattooing** (dual risk: both HBV and HIV)

#### Prelims Statement: "HBV transmission is similar to HIV" — **CORRECT**
- All modes of HBV transmission are also applicable to HIV
- Examiner can club both topics in one statement

## Part G: Virus-Host Cell Interaction — SARS-CoV-2 Case Study

### Mechanism of Infection

```
Infected person → Coughing/Sneezing → Respiratory droplets
                                           |
                                    Inhalation by another person
                                           |
                                    Nasal cavity (nasal mucosa)
                                           |
                                    Trachea (windpipe)
                                           |
                                    Lungs → Infection → Inflammation
                                           |
                                    PNEUMONIA (infection + inflammation of lungs)
                                           |
                                    Reduced oxygenation of lungs
                                           |
                                    Shortness of breath / Breathlessness
```

### Virus Attachment Protein (VAP) and Cell Surface Receptor

| Component | In SARS-CoV-2 Context |
|:---|:---|
| **Virus Attachment Protein (VAP)** | **Spike protein** of SARS-CoV-2 |
| **Cell Surface Receptor** | **ACE-2 receptor** on human lung cells |

- Spike protein contains **Receptor Binding Domain (RBD)**
- RBD interacts with ACE-2 receptor → allows virus to **attach, enter, and multiply** in lung cells
- ACE-2 receptors act as **gateway** for virus attachment and entry

### If Virus Succeeds (No Immunity):
1. Virus infects host cell
2. Increased **viral replication** → new viral copies
3. Increased **viral load** (concentration of virus in blood)
4. **Severe pneumonia** → dip in oxygen saturation → breathlessness
5. Rush to hospital for **oxygen therapy**

### Why Adults Were More Severely Affected Than Infants (COVID-19)?

| | Infants & Children | Adults |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Lungs | **Immature & developing** | **Fully developed & mature** |
| ACE-2 receptors | **Fewer in number** | **More in number** |
| Virus sticking | **Loose / weak** | **Strong** |
| Pneumonia severity | Mild / spared | **Severe** |

- This is **NOT about immunity** — adults have stronger immunity
- It's about **ACE-2 receptor expression** on lung cells
- Immature lungs = fewer ACE-2 receptors = virus cannot stick strongly
- This was a **blessing in disguise** for infants and children during COVID-19

## Part H: Antibody-Mediated Immunity

### How Antibodies Protect Us

```
Human body → WBC (White Blood Cells)
                  |
          B-cell lymphocytes
                  |
          Secrete ANTIBODIES (immunoproteins)
                  |
          Circulate in the blood
                  |
          Find & MASK the spike protein of virus
                  |
          CUT OFF virus-host cell interaction
                  |
          Virus CANNOT attach, enter, or multiply
                  |
          Viral load does NOT increase
                  |
          No severe pneumonia
                  |
          Virus forced to LEAVE the body
                  |
          Infection CLEARED
```

- **Antibodies** = Defensive/immunoproteins circulating in blood
- Secreted by **B-cell lymphocytes** (a type of WBC)
- Antibodies **block virus attachment and entry** by masking the spike protein
- This is called **Antibody-Mediated Immunity**
- If spike protein is overwhelmed by antibodies → interaction with ACE-2 receptor is cut off → virus becomes **unsuccessful**

### Victor vs Victim
| Scenario | Victor | Victim |
|:---|:---|:---|
| No antibodies | Virus | Human body |
| Antibodies present | **Human body** | **Virus** |

## Part I: Virus Metabolism — Living or Non-Living?

### Respiration (Metabolism)

> **Respiration** = Biochemical process that breaks down food into energy

| Type | Condition | Energy Produced |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Aerobic** | In presence of O2 | Significant/considerable amount |
| **Anaerobic** | In absence of O2 | Less amount |

#### Does Virus Have Respiration?
> **NO.** There is **absence of respiration (metabolism) in a virus** — neither aerobic NOR anaerobic

- Virus cannot produce its own energy source
- This is why virus is considered **non-living**

#### Does Bacteria Have Respiration?
> **YES.** Bacteria has **BOTH** aerobic AND anaerobic respiration

- Bacteria can convert food into energy → shows metabolism → is living

### Reproduction

| Organism | Can Reproduce? | How? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Virus** | Yes, BUT only inside a living host cell | Uses host cell machinery; no independent reproduction |
| **Bacteria** | Yes, independently | **Binary fission** (asexual reproduction) — cell divides into 2 equal halves |

- **Binary fission** = natural cloning (next generation cells are genetically identical to parent = clones)

### Conclusion: Virus = Transition Phase

> **Virus is regarded as at the transition phase or at the border of living and non-living**

| Feature | Living? | Non-Living? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| No respiration/metabolism | | Yes |
| Reproduction (inside host cell) | Yes | |
| **Verdict** | **Transition between living & non-living** | |

## Part J: Bacteria vs Virus — 7-Point Comparison

| # | Feature | Bacteria | Virus |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Cellular location** | **Extracellular** microbe (outside host cell) | **Intracellular** microbe (inside host cell) |
| 2 | **Reproduction** | Self-reproduction using **binary fission** (asexual) — no host cell needed | Always replicates/reproduces **inside a living host cell** |
| 3 | **Genome** | Always **DNA only** | Either **DNA or RNA** (but NOT both) |
| 4 | **Respiration (Metabolism)** | Present — both **aerobic & anaerobic** | **Absent** — neither aerobic nor anaerobic |
| 5 | **Ribosomes** | **Present** — for protein synthesis | **Absent** (acellular) — uses host cell ribosomes for viral protein synthesis |
| 6 | **Culture medium** | Can use **both living & non-living** (synthetic/artificial) medium | Culture medium is **always living** medium — cannot use synthetic medium |
| 7 | **Drug sensitivity** | Sensitive to **antibiotics** | **Not sensitive to antibiotics** — requires **antiviral drugs** |

### Key Differences to Remember:
- **Bacteria** = has cellular structure, independent metabolism, self-reproducing, DNA-only genome
- **Virus** = acellular, no metabolism, dependent on host cell, can have DNA or RNA
- **Ribosomes** = protein factories of the cell → present in bacteria, absent in virus
- Virus uses **host cell's ribosomes** to make its own proteins
