## 1. The link is the dendritic cell (ST-11-01)

Innate immunity and adaptive immunity both protect the body. They do not work in isolation. The **communication link** is the **dendritic cell**. It belongs to **innate** immunity.

It stays in **constant immunosurveillance**. If a bacterium or a virus enters, the cell **processes and presents the foreign antigen** to the **helper T-cell**, which belongs to **adaptive** immunity. Because it presents the antigen, it is also called an **antigen-presenting cell**.

The helper T-cell then secretes **cytokines** into the blood. Lecture 2 already called cytokines the radar signal. They activate two arms:

- the **B-cell**, which secretes **antibodies**
- the **cytotoxic T-cell**

That is the united fight. The regulatory T-cell, which stops these same cells from attacking healthy tissue, stays Lecture 2.

## 2. Antibodies also work on bacteria (ST-11-02)

Lecture 2 stopped at antibodies **blocking a virus**. The same antibodies act on bacteria in two ways.

A bacterium is wrapped in a **cell wall**. The antibody **breaks that wall**. He called the breaking **opsonisation**. Phagocytosis, already the neutrophil’s job in Lecture 2, comes **after** the wall is broken. Engulfing is easier once the wall is gone. His picture was a potato that has to be boiled before it can be swallowed.

Some bacteria also pour **toxins** into the blood. The toxin travels and can damage the **liver, kidney, and lungs**, up to multiple-organ failure. Antibodies **neutralise** those toxins and protect the organs.

<span style="color: #e53e3e;">**Prelims trap:** Opsonisation is the **breaking of the bacterial cell wall**. Phagocytosis is the **engulfing**, and it is the **neutrophil** that does it. Do not swap the two words. Antibodies are not limited to viruses.</span>

## 3. Which diseases are worth a vaccine (ST-11-03)

A disease is **self-limiting**, which he also called **self-remitting**, when the body’s own immunity clears it. Common cold: mild fever, sore throat, headache, and the person is back in two or three days. It does not carry severity, a hospital bed, or death.

A **life-threatening** disease carries **virulence**, his word for severity. That produces **morbidity** (illness, and the loss of the body’s productivity) and **mortality** (the death rate: deaths per thousand, the pair of the birth rate). Such a disease can raise infant mortality and maternal mortality, and it pulls **life expectancy** down. He put India’s life expectancy at **around 70 years**. The Human Development Index line of about **71**, and the three components of that index, are the 30 September governance class. Do not merge the two figures.

**Communicable** means the disease spreads, person to person, or through a **vector**. Dengue is his vector case: the mosquito. **Non-communicable** stays with the person. Blood pressure, which he called hypertension, is the example. A gene may still reach the next generation. Person-to-person spread does not.

He used the fertility rate as a demography aside, not as this lecture’s spine. He said India’s rate is **below 2**, and he said **1.9**. Replacement level, he said, is **2.1**. Below that, the population graph declines, and he put a possible Indian decline at **2050**. It is not the same in every State. The survey figure of **2.0**, and the geography window, stay those notes. **1.9** and **2050** are this class. Do not average them.

The intervention he wants against a spreading life-threatening disease is a **vaccine**. A vaccine **prevents**. It is not a cure given after the person is already ill. Pulse polio, with millions of children taking the oral drops together, is his picture of that prevention, inside what he called universal immunisation. The programme’s target and year stay the Social Issues note.

## 5. Control, elimination, eradication (ST-11-05)

The road map has three rungs. The goal is the third. The tool that decides it is the vaccine.

**Control** is what a country does while the burden is still high. It **breaks the chain of transmission**, prevents the spread, and cuts the number of new cases, which is the disease burden. His three measures: a **face mask**, **hand hygiene** (washing with soap and water), and **vaccines**. COVID is the picture.

**Elimination** means **no new active case** is reported from a **specific region or territory**.

**Polio in India** is his elimination case.

- The **last case** was in **2011**, from **West Bengal**.
- The World Health Organization’s rule, as he stated it: **three consecutive years** with no new active case, then the country is certified free.
- India received **polio-free status in 2014**.
- **Pakistan and Afghanistan** still report active cases. They are still **polio-endemic**: **live poliovirus is still circulating** there. Eradication has not been reached.

**Eradication** is **global elimination**: no new active case from **any part of the world**. **Smallpox, 1980**, achieved with the smallpox vaccine, is the example. One new case anywhere and the word cannot be used. Polio can be called eradicated only when Pakistan and Afghanistan have also eliminated it.

The Social Issues note says polio was eradicated in 2014 and that the virus remains in **Pakistan and Bangladesh**. This class splits the words, and it names **Afghanistan**, not Bangladesh. Keep both locks. Do not silently replace either.

## 6. What sits inside a vaccine (ST-11-06)

The **active ingredient** is the **antigen**: the protein of the virus or the bacterium, the memory of the invader. It triggers an immune response and builds memory cells. A **live** virus cannot be the ingredient. It would cause the disease. It has to be turned into a **vaccine virus**.

Three ways the antigen can arrive:

| Form | What it is | Does it multiply? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Whole pathogen**, weakened or **attenuated** | Not dead. A safer version of the same virus or bacterium | **Yes.** Replicating vaccine virus. It multiplies without serious disease |
| **Whole pathogen, inactivated** | Dead. The antigen is still intact, so it can still trigger the response | **No.** Non-replicating |
| **Fragment** | Only a piece. He called this a **subunit** vaccine. The spike protein, and the human-papilloma protein already in Lecture 1, are this family | The fragment itself is not a live pathogen |
| **Blueprint** | The genetic code that tells the person’s own cell to **make** the protein | The cell makes the antigen inside the body |

He also said the antigen may be a protein or a **sugar**, or the whole organism. The key line: every vaccine contains the active component, or the blueprint for making it.

Development is **long and complicated**. He said about **10 years**. The sheet writes **10–15 years**. Keep both. The stages:

1. **Pre-clinical trial**, in **laboratory animals**. Look for toxicity: very high fever, a blood clot, a heart-attack risk. **80 per cent** of candidates never cross this. **Less than 20 per cent** reach people.
2. **Clinical trial**, in human volunteers, on **informed consent**. Dose is hit and trial: 10 virus particles may not trigger a response, 1,000 may be toxic, and the body may need 10,000, so the same vaccine is split into a **double or triple dose**. The **route** is the same kind of trial. An **oral** vaccine needs **more doses** than an **injectable** one. Oral polio, from **0 to 5 years**, with boosters, is that fact.
3. The trial also has to ask whether the vaccine can be given to a person who already has **cancer, HIV, or an autoimmune disease**.

The 40 per cent and 60 per cent cuts in infant mortality, and the later 80-against-20 comparison, are **illustrations of how a trial is read**. They are not the published efficacy of a named vaccine.

A public-health **emergency** can shorten the path. COVID took about **18 months**, against the ordinary decade. Africa, he said, did not have the expertise, so the continent looked to the **United States and Europe**, and each of those looked after its own people first. That was the **vaccine divide**, or **vaccine inequity**. India had the expertise, and companies such as the **Serum Institute of India**.

**Trust** sits on top of the data. Two forwarded cases of a clot or a heart attack, which may belong to the person’s own condition, become **vaccine hesitancy**. People then refuse later vaccines as well, and control and elimination stall. He treated a claim that a World Health Organization committee had openly declared the COVID vaccine a cause of **myocarditis** as **misinformation**. He said there is **no concrete medical evidence** of that concern, and that the **Indian Council of Medical Research** and an **institute of medical sciences in New Delhi** have said so. He also placed resistance, misinformation, and a fear of **sterilisation** in **Pakistan and Afghanistan**. The sentence produced the word coronavirus. The disease he had just called still endemic there is **polio**.

## 7. Safety first, then efficacy (ST-11-07)

Two parameters decide whether a vaccine is introduced. **Safety is more important than efficacy.** He would not compromise even **1 per cent** on safety.

**Safety** means two things.

- No **excessive inflammatory reaction**: very high fever, blood clot, heart-attack risk. A vaccine that does this cannot go into the population.
- No **vaccine-derived infection**. The oral polio vaccine contains vaccine virus. If that virus itself paralyses the muscles of the hands and legs, the vaccine has caused the disability it was meant to prevent.

**Efficacy** is whether it prevents **mortality** and **severity**, so the vaccinated person is not hospitalised. It is measured by comparing the vaccinated with a **placebo** group. The placebo is **not** an empty “unvaccinated” label. Those people are injected with **saline or glucose**, so they believe they were vaccinated. If they know they were not, the stress itself lowers immunity and the comparison is spoiled. His illustration: **80 per cent** of the placebo group infected, **20 per cent** of the vaccinated. Again an illustration.

He separated the two judgements. If infant mortality from a disease falls from **80 per cent** to **50 per cent**, the **30-point** gain can be enough to introduce the vaccine. Safety is not scored that way.

When the **benefits far outweigh the risk**, the vaccine goes to the vulnerable population. A needle in the arm muscle can leave **redness, swelling, a mild fever, and uneasiness**. The word that landed as “retinitis” is that redness. It sits inside immunotolerance and is gone in **24 to 48 hours**. It is not life-threatening.

## 8. How it works: Covaxin, and the two responses (ST-11-08)

The principle of immunisation is the answer to “how does a vaccine work?”. His example is the COVID vaccine **Covaxin**: **inactivated, non-replicating SARS-CoV-2**. Dead, and still carrying the antigen.

The injection is the **first exposure** to that foreign antigen. The response is the **primary immune response**. The body processes and presents the antigen and builds **immunological memory**: **antigen-specific lymphocytes**, both **B-cells** (humoral) and **T-cells** (cell-mediated). He also called these **memory cells**.

When the **live** virus later enters, its spike protein meets that memory. Within a few hours the memory cells pour out a high concentration of **anti-spike antibodies**. They circulate for many months and **block attachment**. That protection is **vaccine-induced immunity**.

The graph has **antibody concentration** on the vertical axis and **time in weeks** on the horizontal. The sheet marks 2, 4, 6, and 8.

| | Primary | Secondary |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Who | The **non-vaccinated**. No memory. The virus is a surprise attack | The **vaccinated**. Memory is already built |
| Speed | **Delayed.** Longer, while the antigen is processed | **Immediate**, within hours |
| Strength | **Weak** in potency. A low concentration, then it dips | **Strong and specific.** High concentration, circulating for many months |
| Life | **Short** | **Long-term and stable** |
| Result | The virus can take the body over: hospital, and in some cases death | Protection against the life-threatening disease after vaccination |

He dictated the two expansions. **Secondary immune response** is the strong, specific response on the **second or a later exposure** to the **same** foreign antigen, using activated lymphocytes or memory cells. **Primary immune response** is the response on the **first** exposure. It is weak, and it is short. The short form **SIR** on this sheet means the secondary response. It is not the Special Intensive Revision of the electoral rolls.

**What a vaccine is expected to do, and where that fails.**

1. The ideal is **long-lived** immunity, so the person is not vaccinated for that disease every year. **Influenza**, and he put **swine flu** in the bracket, is the deviation: **short-lived** immunity and an **annual** dose. Mutation, which he called genetic variability, changes the protein. A new variant needs the vaccine **updated** with that variant. He would prefer a **reprogrammable** vaccine, because the memory has to be updated when the protein changes. Antigenic variation of an RNA virus is already Lecture 2. This class adds the annual dose.
2. **HIV / AIDS** and **hepatitis C** still have **no effective vaccine**. Mutation is so frequent, and the virus expresses so many different proteins while it replicates, that the vaccine cannot be updated fast enough. This is why hepatitis C is in both tables: no vaccine, and the reason is the speed of the change. The treatment for hepatitis C remains the earlier line.
3. The **ideal** role is to prevent **both infection and disease**. The **necessary** role is to prevent **disease**. A new variant can still **infect** a vaccinated person, because it **escapes** the antibody. That infection need not become virulence, a hospital stay, or death. **Cell-mediated immunity, the T-cells**, still holds it. COVID is the example.
