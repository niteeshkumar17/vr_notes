## 1. A tribe is a forest people with a culture of its own (SOC-04-01)

Tribes sit **outside** the Hindu caste order. In this class, **tribe** and **Scheduled Tribe (ST)** are the same word. They live in the forest, so they are **geographically isolated**, and isolation is why the culture is their own. **Culture**, as he insisted, includes dress, food, language, religious practices, customs, art, architecture, dance and music.

**Population:** about **8.6 per cent**. **Types:** about **705** (the count itself is Lecture 1). His proof of diversity is that each tribe has its own culture: a different language (he treated that as about 705 languages — a classroom illustration, not a linguistic census), a different occupation, and a different religion.

| Occupation he named | Where |
|:---|:---|
| Pastoral | animal rearing; the **Toda** example is below |
| Shifting agriculture | Northeast |
| Hunting and gathering | Andaman and Nicobar; some southern tribes |

Religion, in his examples: nature, animals, spirits, ancestors.

**Anthropologists** study tribes. **Sociologists** study outsiders. Diversity made a single definition hard. Later, anthropologists listed features that keep turning up, and those features are the definition.

| | **Tribal** | **Outsider** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Manner | Shy of outsiders | In contact |
| Place | Geographically isolated | Mainstream |
| Change | Resistant. Happy as they are | Development is the point |
| Attachment | Forest, and their religion | — |
| Work | **No division of labour.** Everyone does the same work, and every work. Homogeneous. Knowledge does not accumulate. Stagnant | **Strong** division of labour, and then a division inside that (eye surgeon, neurosurgeon). Knowledge keeps growing. Society becomes complex |
| Religion | **Belief** (cannot be proved or argued) + **practice** (dance an hour around the tree; Tuesday at the Hanuman temple) | Belief + practice + **philosophy**: the mind asks a question and answers it (who am I, why born, what happens to the soul). He named Charvaka, Mimamsa, Nyaya, Shankaracharya, Ramanuja |

**1965.** The Government of India, on the **Lokur Committee** (he spelled it out as L-O-K-U-R; the audio first heard “local”), officially defined ST and gave **five criteria**. A community that meets them can be listed.

1. Geographical isolation  
2. Shyness  
3. A distinct culture (the isolation is the cause)  
4. Economically backward — no division of labour, so they survive and do not progress; resistance to change keeps them there  
5. **Primitive traits** — traits modern societies have dropped. This is the one he starred  

Primitive, in his examples: shifting agriculture, hunting and gathering, snake-catching, polyandry, polygamy.

**Status follows the community, and it follows the traits.** The Supreme Court, as he stated it: a tribe does **not** lose ST status on conversion to any religion, because a tribe is a tribe by **primitive traits**, not by a religion. Archery and Ghoomar can continue after a Bhil becomes Hindu, and the status continues. He said about **90 per cent** of Garo, Khasi, Jaintia, Naga and Kuki have become Christian and still hold ST status. One person dropping the traits is not enough. The status goes only when the **whole community** has lost them.

The Scheduled Caste contrast stays on Lecture 3: conversion to Christianity or Islam loses SC reservation. OBC reservation can still be used. That rule does not cross over to ST.

## 3. Isolate, assimilate, or integrate (SOC-04-03)

Development and the unique culture pull against each other. Develop them, and contact with outsiders dissolves the culture. Isolate them, and there is no development. Tribes themselves do not want change. The state still has to develop them, or the rest of the world moves and they stay backward. The debate is **how**, not whether.

| Approach | What it does | Who used it |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Isolation** | A boundary. Outsiders stay out. Culture is kept. They stay backward, inbreeding rises, population falls. Also kept tribes out of the national movement | **British, from 1909.** Tribal areas became **excluded** and **partially excluded**. India does **not** follow this after independence |
| **Assimilation** | Culture 2 drops its language, food and dress and becomes culture 1. Development is gained. Uniqueness is lost | Two outsider camps, below |
| **Integration** | Keep the culture **and** develop them. Slow. The meaning of development is different from the outsider meaning | **Nehru.** He also called it the **Panchsheel** policy **toward tribes**. That is not the foreign-policy Panchsheel |

**Christian missionaries** were allowed into the excluded areas and are strong in the Northeast and the east. Conversion is **instant**. Schools and hospitals come with it, which is why, in his line, Northeastern students speak strong English.

**Hindu right-wing groups** call tribes **vanvasi**: Hindus who stayed in the forest, backward Hindus, not a separate people. The shared practices he named are nature worship, ancestor worship and animal worship. The gap is Vishnu and Mahesh (Shiva). They were on the way to becoming fully Hindu when British isolation cut that contact. From **2014**, with those groups in power, the process restarted: temples of Vishnu and Mahesh in the forest, and schools that teach Sanskrit, the Mahabharata and the Ramayana. Becoming Hindu, in his contrast with Christianity, is **slow**. There is no instant conversion. The cost he named is the same as any assimilation: the unique culture goes.

**Integration, as he actually taught it** (the five printed points were on a slide he did not read out):

- Socio-economic development **and** cultural preservation.  
- Development is **slow**, and it is **different**. Income rises **while they stay in the forest**. Leave the forest and the culture goes. **Minor forest produce** is that income.  
- Social development means health and education, still inside the habitat.  
- The state takes active steps to keep the culture: tribes make their **own customary laws**. He will **not** impose a **Uniform Civil Code** on them. His example: property to the youngest daughter, even though that is not gender equality.  
- A **right to live in the forest** is part of this policy. That right is the 2006 Act in the next sections.  
- He said there are **six** areas where these customary laws run, and he pointed at the Sixth Schedule. The Fifth Schedule and the Sixth Schedule are the **next class**. Do not build that chapter from the word “six”.

## 4. From the Indian Forest Act to joint management (SOC-04-04)

**Minor forest produce (MFP):** anything that can be collected from the forest **except timber**. His list: tendu leaves, sal leaves, honey, lac, guar gum, betel nut, medicinal plants and herbs (ashwagandha, cinnamon), drumstick. Outsiders buy these cheap. The **Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA)** declares an **MSP** so the produce cannot be bought below that floor. He glossed MSP once as “minimum selling price”. Other notes expand the same letters as Minimum Support Price. Training tribes to **process** the produce themselves is the next rise in income.

**Indian Forest Act.** First passed **1865**, amended **1927**, so the class calls it the **Indian Forest Act, 1927**. From 1865 the forest is the legal property of the state (then the British government). The state preserves, conserves and manages it, through the **Indian Forest Service**. It can ban shifting agriculture, ban tendu collection, and close a reserved forest. Tribes who had treated the forest as theirs live **at the mercy of the state**, and can be evicted in the name of preservation.

**After independence the Act continued.** Forest became a **State** subject, so State governments were the new owners. He contrasted this with land reform for peasants: a great deal was done for them, and nothing of that kind for tribes. The **National Forest Policy, 1956** made **development more important than tribal rights**. Displacement for a project did not even require a second thought.

**Chipko, from 1970,** in **Uttarakhand** (the notebook’s UK). Forest had been handed to a sports company for bats. Women could no longer collect deadwood to cook. People hugged the trees. The Uttar Pradesh government withdrew the order. He called it the **first environmental movement in India**, and a tribal movement. In India, environmental movements are led by the **poor and by tribes** — an “empty stomach” movement — because they are hit first. His present example: the **Sundarbans**, which he described as the delta of the Brahmaputra, going under as the sea rises, so fishing and tribal communities there move first. In the West, he said, elites lead the same kind of talk.

**1976.** The **42nd Constitutional Amendment** moved forest to the **Concurrent List**. Centre and States can both legislate.

**1980. Forest (Conservation) Act.** A State cannot divert forest for a project without the Centre’s permission. He called this a direct result of Chipko.

**National Forest Policy and joint forest management.** The notebook writes **1988**. The audio says **1998**. Lock the page: **1988**, and remember that the recording heard 1998. Joint forest management means the State **and** the tribes preserve and conserve together. The State’s role is not dropped. Full transfer of that job to the tribes waits for 2006.

## 5. Forest Rights Act, 2006 (SOC-04-05)

Full name he dictated: **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006**. Short name: Forest Rights Act. It corrects the historic injustice of 1865 and 1927. It is the statutory form of the right to live in the forest.

A **forest dweller** lives in the forest, depends on it entirely, and has lived there for a **minimum of 75 years**. He applied that clock to **both** ST and non-ST dwellers.

Three rights:

| Right | Whose | What |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Land** | Individual family | That family becomes the **legal owner** of the patch it lives on. Before 2006 the legal owner was the State |
| **Use** | Individual, in his split | Collect and **sell** MFP (anything except timber). Also the use of **community resources** — a lake, a grazing ground, a cave. The State **remains the owner** of those resources and **cannot deny** the use. It cannot notify “no grazing from tomorrow” |
| **Community** | Joint, all who live there | **Preserve, conserve and manage** the forest, including its biodiversity (flora and fauna). Decisions, including cutting, need the tribes’ permission |

He was explicit that land and use are individual, and community rights are joint. The “use of community resources” still sits inside use rights, with the State keeping ownership.

**Who runs it.** State governments, under the coordination of **MoTA**. Not the Ministry of Environment and Forest. States have implemented it **poorly**, from fear of losing the forest, and they **delay**.

**Where it runs.** Every kind of forest: reserved, protected, village, national park, wildlife sanctuary. He attributed to the Supreme Court the line that anything which meets the **dictionary meaning** of forest is a forest for this Act.

## 6. Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (SOC-04-06)

Of **705** STs, **75** are **Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTG)**. Every PVTG is an ST. Most STs are not PVTGs. They are the tribes who are more backward than the other tribes. He dictated **four** criteria, all of which must be met. A mark on the page that looks like a “9” is not a second list.

1. **Pre-agricultural** technology — mostly hunting and gathering. His examples: Jarawa, Sentinelese, Shompen, Kalbelia.  
2. **Population continuously declining.** This is the starred criterion. Cause: they have **isolated themselves**, and inbreeding follows. India’s policy is not isolation. Some tribes still practise it.  
3. **Extremely low literacy**, because the isolation keeps schools out.  
4. A **subsistence** economy, which he tied to the first criterion.

His population sketch, which adds up to all 75 and leaves no middle band: **12** PVTGs above **50,000**, still declining; **63** below **1,000**, “alarmingly” declining. **Sentinelese:** **30 to 40** people left. He said many PVTGs may soon be gone. The word he used was “extant”; he meant they may disappear.

The old name was **Primitive Tribal Groups**. He tied that name to a **1973** commission, and the rename to **2006**, because “primitive” was the word being dropped. He pointed at unread handout pages for the rest of that history.

**PM JANMAN, 2023.** He said “Jan Man Yojana” and did not expand the letters. MoTA is the nodal ministry. He said **nine** ministries work under it, and that it provides **11** basic infrastructure facilities in PVTG habitats. He named housing, drinking water, electricity (he added solar), telecom, health care, and roads. Do not invent the other five facilities, or the names of the nine ministries. The scheme runs only where PVTGs live: **18 States and one Union Territory, Andaman and Nicobar**. **Punjab, Haryana and Delhi** have **no** ST population, so neither the list nor the scheme is there.

## 7. Land alienation, health, and where he stopped (SOC-04-07)

**Land alienation** is the biggest problem. Alienation, in his sociology, is the feeling of being cut off. In psychology he called the same feeling depression. It happens when tribes are **displaced without being asked**, and then **not properly rehabilitated**. A hill they worship, or a forest they know, is not replaced by a desert plot. The push is mines and dams and power projects. Tribes sit on the minerals. He named the **Chota Nagpur** plateau.

Three responses:

1. **Forest areas — the Forest Rights Act, 2006.** Consultation is mandatory before acquisition or displacement. In his telling, tribal land in a forest cannot be acquired without the tribes’ permission.  
2. **Non-forest areas where tribes live — the land-acquisition law of 2013** (he said LAR / LARR). Acquisition must come with **rehabilitation and compensation**. He then added that the same rehabilitation-and-compensation law also applies **inside** a forest, **after** the tribes have said yes under the Forest Rights Act. The notebook’s shorthand is “non-forest → LARR”. The audio is the fuller sequence: forest consent first, then rehabilitation.  
3. **Supreme Court, 2013: Odisha Mining Corporation versus the Ministry of Environment and Forest.** **Niyamgiri** hills in the Eastern Ghats of Odisha, bauxite for aluminium. The tribes worship the hill as **Niyam Raja**. The corporation moved to acquire. Tribes, with NGOs, went to court on the Forest Rights Act. The Court was for the tribes and called it a **green referendum**: **every Gram Sabha** votes, and **one “no”** stops the acquisition. He said that after 2013, tribal rights sit **above** development, which reverses the 1956 policy, and that land alienation has **reduced**. He also said States still find **loopholes** in the Act and in the ruling. An earlier internal-security class still treated Niyamgiri as live in 2024. Both lines stay.

The **7 September** newspaper note is the other reading: MoTA told a power company that the Act’s text has **no** clause requiring Gram Sabha consent for forest clearance. Do not collapse that clip into this ruling, or this ruling into that clip.

**Health.** Habitat is shrinking: heat dries lakes and rivers, fire pushes people out, food and water run short, so the state has to supply both. Concerns he listed: malnourishment, low immunity, vaccines not taken, and health facilities hard to reach because the places are remote. A recent example he did not name: **25 children** of one tribe died after drinking dirty water.

**Sickle-cell anaemia** is a genetic disorder from a defective gene, passed parent to child. Of **100** patients, **95** are tribes, because of inbreeding. The **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare** has a mission to **eliminate** it by **2047**, by promoting **outbreeding**: a couple should not marry if both carry the disorder. He treated gene editing as a path that brings its own side effects.

### 8. Schools, then a better price for forest produce (SOC-05-01, SOC-05-02)

Educational backwardness is geographical isolation: the school is too far. Primary, in this class, is class **1 to 8**. Secondary is class **9 to 12**. Primary education is free and compulsory, so the Ministry of Tribal Affairs opened **ashram schools** in tribal areas for class **1 to 8**. These are not Ministry of Education schools.

**Eklavya Model Residential Schools (EMRS)** are the secondary answer. A residential school means a hostel, because the children cannot travel in from a remote hamlet.

**Economic backwardness** has the same cause, isolation. The Panchsheel line from Lecture 4 still holds: raise income inside the forest. Minimum support price for minor forest produce is already that lecture. The new scheme is **Van Dhan Yojana**, which he also called **Janjati Vikas Yojana**.

- Self-help groups of tribes. One group has **30 to 40** members. They collect together, so the pile is larger. His example: cloves, then clove oil.
- Every **10 to 15** groups feed one **Van Dhan enterprise**, which processes and markets.
- The Ministry of Tribal Affairs does the processing and the marketing at first, and trains the groups to take both over.

### 9. PM-JUGA for the villages that are not PVTG (SOC-05-02)

The fifth problem is poor infrastructure. In **2024** the Ministry of Tribal Affairs launched **PM-JUGA**. He did not expand the letters. It does for ordinary tribal villages what PM JANMAN does for PVTG habitats.

A **tribal village** has a majority ST population, and at least **500** ST people. The facilities he named: housing, drinking water, electricity, telecom, roads. Livelihood is a **tribal homestay**, so a visitor pays to stay and see the life. Health is a **mobile health unit** (a clinic on wheels, because people will not travel to a centre) and **sickle-cell** diagnostic and screening centres. The disease itself, and the 2047 mission, stay on Lecture 4.

He closed the development argument here. After Niyamgiri, **2013**, displacement and rehabilitation are no longer the live debate. The live debate is socio-economic: education, health, and income. A 2025 mains question that still frames tribal development as only displacement and rehabilitation should be answered in that direction. A 2026 GS-II line he wrote down — tribal policy is grounded in **equity** — means unequals are treated unequally: ST reservation in elections, government jobs, and higher education. The notebook’s word is “justice”. The sentence he spoke was equity-oriented governance.

On a 2025 sustainable-development question, his opinion was that the long run helps the poor and the tribes, because they are hit first by climate damage (he used the Western Ghats). Renewable energy is costly, so they cannot buy the transition themselves. India’s line at the climate conferences is that the countries which industrialised first should cut, and should pay. India does not drop the environmental goal.

### 10. Fifth Schedule and Sixth Schedule (SOC-05-03, SOC-05-04)

The Constitution has **12** Schedules. A tribal area may be under the Fifth, under the Sixth, or under neither. The Sixth can be created only in **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram**. A scheduled area in Gujarat can only be Fifth. A scheduled area in Mizoram can only be Sixth. Assam cannot be given a Fifth Schedule area. **Ladakh** is asking for the Sixth. That needs a constitutional amendment adding a place to those four. He also said the government will not do it: Ladakh touches Pakistan and China, and autonomy there would increase China’s influence.

| | **Fifth** | **Sixth** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Article | **244(1)** | **244(2)** |
| Exam name | **Scheduled areas** | **Tribal areas** |
| Who decides | The **President**, meaning the Union government | A district inhabited by one tribe, in those four States |
| Size | Minimum a **block** (partial). Maximum a **district** (full). Also: economically and socially backward, majority tribal | Generally one district. A pocket of a second tribe inside it is a **tribal autonomous region** |
| Who runs it | The **State government**, under ordinary Central and State laws | A **district council**, or a **regional council** for the pocket |
| Council | **Tribal Advisory Council** in every Fifth Schedule area. It advises the Governor. He cannot make the rules without consulting it | **30** members: **26** elected for **5** years, **4** nominated by the Governor |
| Law | Modern statute | **Customary** law: forest, land transfer, police, marriage, divorce, inheritance, adoption. Own lower courts. Tax, toll, fees |
| Land | Cannot be sold to an **outsider**. Outsider means not domiciled in that area, not “someone from another State’s scheduled area” | The council’s own land law |
| How many now | **10** States, and more can be added. He did not name the ten | **10** areas, all inside the four States |

He counted the ten Sixth Schedule areas as three in Assam (**Karbi Anglong**, the North Cachar hills he called by their present name only in passing, and **Bodoland**), three in Meghalaya (**Garo, Khasi, Jaintia** — he treated the State as three districts), three in Mizoram (**Chakma, Lai, Mara**), and one covering almost all of Tripura. **Bodoland** is the exception on size: four districts, and a council of **46**, not 30. The 2003 accord’s 46, and the 2020 jump in seats, stay on the 28 August note. This class did not teach that jump.

Why only those four States: at independence the Northeast was still Assam, the tribes there had not assimilated, and many would rather have left India than drop their customs. Autonomy was the bargain that kept them in, and that brought insurgency down. He said there is no single cleaner reason.

He first said adding a State to the Sixth needs a **special majority**. Later he said amending the Fifth or the Sixth Schedule is a **simple** amendment, and that an area can be taken off the Sixth by a simple majority. Both sentences are his. Do not collapse them.

### 11. PESA brings the panchayat into the Fifth (SOC-05-05)

India was a two-tier Union and State until **1992**. The **73rd** Amendment created Panchayati Raj for rural areas. The **74th** created urban local bodies. He described the rural ladder as Gram Sabha, a **five-member** Gram Panchayat in each village, one block panchayat, and a zila parishad. A block is a group of villages. It is a rural unit.

Those amendments **created the structure and did not assign the powers**. The State decides what to hand down. That handing-down is decentralisation. Article 244(2) is different: the Constitution itself lists the council’s powers. The Fifth Schedule had no such bodies. The 73rd Amendment **exempted** scheduled areas.

The **Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)** — he said “PISA” — extends the **73rd** Amendment to Fifth Schedule areas. He started to include the 74th and then cut it. Do not put urban local bodies inside PESA. The Act itself defines some powers of the Gram Sabha and the panchayat, in **Section 4**, clauses **(d), (e), (i), (j), (k), (l) and (m)**. He did not read the clauses out. The aim is the Sixth Schedule’s idea of local control, done through the Gram Sabha, in the Fifth. It does **not** put the Fifth on par with the Sixth. Sixth Schedule autonomy is in the Constitution. PESA is a statute.

### 12. Denotified, nomadic, semi-nomadic (SOC-05-06)

After STs he opened a further vulnerable section: **DNTs**. Three groups sit under the label. **Denotified** tribes, **nomadic** tribes, and **semi-nomadic** tribes (they move, less often).

The British could not accept that a people might simply not settle. The **Criminal Tribes Act, 1871** notified some nomadic tribes as **born criminals**, so a trial was unnecessary. Independent India **repealed** the Act and **denotified** them. Some of those communities were later listed as SC, some as ST, some as OBC. Some are none of the three. A denotified community is not automatically an ST. The Lokur five criteria are still what makes an ST. About **1,235** communities are denotified, nomadic, or semi-nomadic. About **500** of them are the denotified ones.

Their work, as he described it: begging, acrobatics, devotional songs, tents, special oils, exotic animals. Nothing fixed, and nothing that pays, until they settle.

**SEED, 2022.** He glossed it as the social and economic empowerment of DNTs. The **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment** runs it, not Tribal Affairs. Tribal Affairs keeps STs and PVTGs. Social Justice keeps SCs, OBCs, manual scavengers, persons with disabilities, transgender persons, the elderly, and DNTs. Four parts:

1. Permanent houses, through **PM Awas Yojana**, so they settle.  
2. Livelihood and self-employment, including semi-skilled work.  
3. Coaching for competitive examinations, so those who are already SC, ST or OBC can actually use the reservation.  
4. Health cover of **₹5 lakh per family**, and they are automatic beneficiaries of **Ayushman Bharat**.

Tribes and Naxalism, in one line he parked: alienation after forceful displacement is why some pick up arms. He treated that link as largely eased after 2013. The movement itself is internal security.

A 2021-style question on **tribal knowledge** uses Lecture 4’s comparison and adds three words from this sitting. Tribal knowledge is simple, sensory, and used to **conserve** nature. Outsider knowledge is philosophical, goes past the senses, keeps changing because of the division of labour, and is used to **control** nature.

### Lecture 5 — locks

1. Ashram schools are class **1 to 8**. EMRS is class **6 to 12**, ST only, one per tribal block of majority plus **20,000**. **499** exist. **250** more are wanted. Central funding became **100 per cent** in **2018–19**. The scheme dates from **1997**.  
2. Van Dhan groups are **30 to 40** people. **10 to 15** groups make one enterprise. PM-JUGA is **2024**, for a tribal village of majority plus **500**, and it is the non-PVTG twin of JANMAN.  
3. Fifth Schedule is **244(1)**, scheduled areas, State administration, land not for outsiders, a Tribal Advisory Council. Sixth is **244(2)**, tribal areas, only four States, a council of **30** (**26 + 4**). Bodoland’s council in this class is **46**.  
4. PESA, **1996**, extends the **73rd** Amendment into scheduled areas and lists specific Gram Sabha powers. It does not copy Article 244(2).  
5. The Criminal Tribes Act is **1871**. About **1,235** DNT communities, about **500** denotified. SEED is **2022**, Social Justice, **₹5 lakh** a family.
