## 1. Births, deaths, and a survey that is not a census (SOC-05-07)

Population trends are whether the number is rising or falling, and how fast. The tools are **birth rate**, **death rate**, and **migration**. If births exceed deaths, the population rises. A wide gap means it rises fast. Western populations, in his line, rise by immigration, because local births do not.

**Birth rate** is babies born per **1,000** people. **Death rate** is deaths per **1,000**. From **National Family Health Survey (NFHS) 5**, for **2019–21**:

| | Figure |
|:---|:---|
| Birth rate | **17.6** |
| Death rate | **6.0** |
| Total fertility rate | **2.0** |

He once called the death rate “six per cent”. The definition he had just given is per thousand. Lock **6.0 per 1,000**. The gap is why India’s population is still rising.

**NFHS** is a survey by the **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare**, repeated about every **four or five years**. A census asks everyone. A survey asks a selection, costs less, finishes sooner, and gives an approximate picture. NFHS-5 is **2019–21**. He said **NFHS-6**, for **2023–24**, is almost the same on these three numbers, because births fall slowly, deaths stay flat, and the fertility rate is barely moving. The survey also collects infant mortality, maternal mortality, nourishment, and sex ratio. Every figure below that he did not date separately is the NFHS-5 figure.

**Total fertility rate (TFR)** is the average number of children born to a woman in a country. India’s is **2.0**. He first called **2** the ideal. He then said the extra **0.1**, making **2.1**, accounts for accidental deaths and infant mortality, and that **2.1** keeps the population stable. India, at 2.0, is below that. The geography class’s replacement level of 2.1 stays on that note. This 2.0 is a survey reading, not a second definition.

Births, once they start falling, keep falling. He called the fall stubborn. Deaths fall with medicine and then level off, because the health system has already done the easy saving. Prosperity pushes a household toward lifestyle and away from more children.

## 2. How India grew, then the five phases (SOC-05-07)

| Years | What he locked |
|:---|:---|
| **1901–1921** | Very slow. Growth about **5.4 per cent**. Births high and deaths high, so the net is small |
| **1921–1951** | Growth about **47 per cent**. Births still high. Deaths start falling: hospitals, **vaccines**, **antibiotics** |

| **1981–2021** | On the page, under the words “demographic dividend”, with a star. He did not teach the dividend |

**Stages of demographic transition.** Transition means the change in those two rates.

| Phase | Rates | Who, in this class |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | Both high. Population stationary | Underdeveloped countries |
| **2** Early expansion | Births high, deaths low. Population starts rising | **Sub-Saharan Africa** (south of the Sahara; he set North Africa — Algeria, Tunisia, Morocco, Egypt — apart as more developed) |
| **3** Late expansion | Births still high, and they have **started to fall** | **India**, in the model he spoke |
| **4** | Births almost equal deaths | **China** |
| **5** | Births **below** deaths. Negative growth. Many old people, few children, easy **immigration** so someone does the work | Japan, South Korea, the United States, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, and the Western countries as a group |

Southern States inside India are moving faster than northern States. Northern birth rates are falling slowly.

## 3. Why the explosion happened, and why the South is nervous (SOC-05-07)

The explosion is **1951–1981**. Two reasons, and he stopped on the second.

**Poverty raises the birth rate.** A poor household thinks more children means more income, and that income will lift the house. The work those children get is unskilled, so the income never arrives, and the births have already happened. His evidence: northern States have low per capita income, depend on agriculture, and a TFR of **3.1**. Southern States industrialised after **1947**, have a higher per capita income, and a TFR of **1.8**. He slipped once and called both TFRs low. The pair he stated with the incomes is **3.1 and 1.8**.

From the **1970s**, southern State governments also ran population-control measures, and he said those worked. By **2026** the population gap is wide. Lok Sabha seats follow population. A new census and a delimitation would, in the southern argument, take a large block of seats away and punish the States that controlled births. Parties could then win India in the north alone. The southern request he reported: do not delimit until that gap narrows.

**Lack of awareness** is the second reason: people do not know the gains from family planning, or how contraceptives work. That discussion is the next class.
