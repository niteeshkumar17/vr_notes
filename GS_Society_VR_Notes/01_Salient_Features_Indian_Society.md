## 2. West = modern; India = traditional (SOC-01-01)

| | West | India |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Driver | **Science** → **rational** / logical thinking | **Religion** |
| Process | Underwent **modernisation** | **Such modernisation never happened** |

**-ization** = a **process**.

**West before the 14th century:** traditional; Church answered origin-of-humans (Adam and Eve), day-and-night, etc.

**Then three revolutions (class chain):**

1. **Scientific Revolution (SR)** — Galileo, Newton, Darwin; experimental **proof**; people trust science more than Church. Religion replaced by science.  
2. **Industrial Revolution (IR)** — factories / machines → surplus goods → **colonies** as markets. New ideology: **capitalism** (life-goal = **wealth**). Class: this is why the West is “advanced”; India stays largely **agrarian**.  
3. **French Revolution (FR)** — **liberty** (speech, trade, work). By the **18th century**, Europe is **modern**.

India did **not** have this SR → IR → FR sequence. That is the background for every Social Issues answer.

## 3. History of evolution of Indian society (SOC-01-02)

Three phases. Religions **born in India:** Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism. **Islam** comes from outside. **Modernity** also comes from outside (British).

### I — Ancient (from ~**1500 BC**)

- **Hinduism** — first text **Rig Veda** → **Varna**, later **caste**.  
- **Jainism** and **Buddhism** — new **philosophies** (sets of ideas); close to Hindu thought, not identical.  
- Each religion brings **art and culture** (temples; North vs South temple profile; Buddhist **stupa**).  
- Sheet sequence (do not invent extra kings): Rig Vedic Period (RVP) → Later Vedic Period (LVP) → Pre-Mauryan → Mauryan → Post-Mauryan → Gupta → Post-Gupta. **Caste peaks in the Gupta age**; Kalidasa sits here as the literature name.

### II — Medieval (from ~**1000 AD**)

- **Islam** rules a long stretch (class: about **700–800 years**). Art: **domes, minars, mosques**.  
- **Sikhism** — hybrid of Islam and Hinduism. **Philosophy** closer to Hindu; **art** closer to Islamic (**gurdwara** looks nearer a mosque than a temple).  
- Religions so far: **four**, then **five** with Sikhism.

### III — Modern (British)

British bring **modern institutions** to prolong rule: **law, bureaucracy, judiciary, democracy, parliamentary system**; **railways, printing press**; the idea of India as a **nation**.

**Lock:** British rule **could not modernise India**. Indians **never modernised**.

Before independence: modern institutions run by the British (modern people) in a modern way. After they leave: same institutions occupied by **traditional** Indians (religion / caste).

**Therefore:** **modern institutions are run with traditional values.** Example: election is a modern idea; voting in India still runs on **religion and caste**.

**Fourth salient feature (repeat):** Indian society is **half modern, half traditional** → **chaos and confusion**. Unlike the West, which is **completely modern** (class later: “largely” modern — do not treat the West as a perfect 100%).

## 4. Salient features (SOC-01-03, SOC-01-04)

### 4.1 Diversity (unlike the West = **homogeneous**)

Class France example: one language, one main religion (Christianity). India is the opposite.

| Kind | Class lock |
|:---|:---|
| **Religious** | **Seven** main religions (the five above + **Christians** and **Parsis**). Each has its own **practices** = **customs** (marriage, divorce, inheritance, festivals). |
| **Customary diversity** | Because of religious diversity. |
| **Linguistic** | States formed on language → challenge of **regionalism**. Religious diversity → challenge of **communalism**. |
| **Caste** | About **4,000–5,000** caste groups. |
| **Ethnic** | Common **cultural** identity. Culture includes **dress, food, language, art, music, dance, festivals, customs**. Same cultural group = same **ethnicity**. Examples: Bengali / Bihari / Rajasthani. **Main marker in India = language** (Bengali, Telugu, Tamil…). **Race** is also a marker: **North vs South**; **Northeast vs mainland**. |
| **Tribal** | About **705** types of tribes. |

### 4.2 Unity in diversity

Diverse societies have **high** conflict risk; homogeneous ones have **less**. India is still united.

**Reasons (class order):**

1. **Tolerance** (mature) toward other cultures — evidence: **co-existence** of religions.  
2. **State** is given the job of unity / harmony — **punish those who spread hatred**.  
3. **Common citizenship** + **Constitution** → a sense of being Indian (Hindu / Muslim / Bihari / tribe / caste still **Citizen of India**).  
4. **Freedom of movement** (settle, trade, travel — including the Northeast).

Maintaining unity is **not** the West’s typical problem in this contrast.

### 4.3 Continuity from past to present

Customs (religious), **caste / untouchability / child marriage** continue. Class word: we have **sustained** our culture. **Tradition** = practices that used to happen in the past.

**Reasons:**

1. India is **yet to undergo** modernisation.  
2. State is **pro-religion** (promotes religion) — **Kanwar Yatra**, **Kumbh** as class examples. West does not promote religion this way. Promoting religion **indirectly** keeps religious customs alive.  
3. **Joint family** — grandparents pass traditional values to the next generation. West = **nuclear** families.  
4. **Indian villages = closed systems** — not undergoing social change. **Reservoirs** / preservers of culture. Class image: **nuclear bunkers** against the British “modernisation bomb.” **Resistant to change** because **self-sufficient** (needs met **inside** the village → no exposure). Class: about **70%** of India’s population lives in villages.

Unlike the West, which **lost** continuity by modernising.

**Answer drop:** *India is diverse yet united (tolerance + State + common citizenship + movement); it is traditional because villages, joint family and a pro-religion State kept past customs alive; institutions are modern, people are not.*

## 5. Caste — occupation map (SOC-01-05)

Caste groups are **mainly identified by occupation**. Same occupation can still mean **different rituals / lifestyle / names** because of **geography**. That is why **4,000–5,000** groups make caste **complex**.

Study by **six occupational buckets**, then map onto **Varna** (only the **upper** three like to wear a Varna badge).

| # | Bucket | Varna claim / later label | Class examples (expand state codes) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Priestly** → Brahmins | **Brahmin** | **Rajasthan:** Gaur, **Saraswat**. **Uttar Pradesh (UP):** **Saryupari** (orthodox teachers; surnames Mishra, Dwivedi, Trivedi, Chaturvedi, Pandey) vs **Tyagi** (**land owners**, western UP). **Bihar:** **Maithil** (Jha) vs **Bhumihar** (land owners). Tamil Nadu: **Iyer / Iyengar** (not found in Rajasthan). Kashmiri Pandits / Bengali / Odia Brahmins: class says some eat **non-vegetarian** food — lifestyle can change with region. |
| 2 | **Traditional warriors** → Kshatriya | **Kshatriya**; also **zamindars** | **Jats** (Haryana, Rajasthan); **Marathas** (Shivaji lineage); **Thakurs** (UP); **Rajputs** (Rajasthan); **Tomars** (Madhya Pradesh). |
| 3 | **Baniya** (traders) | **Vaishya** | Rajasthan: **Agarwal, Maheshwari**. Punjab: **Arora / Khatri** cluster (class also listed Chopra, Khanna, Kapoor, Mehra as this Punjab trader set). |
| 4 | **Peasant / agrarian** | No Varna claim they like; **Other Backward Classes (OBC)** with #5 | **Highest population.** **Zamindar (upper caste)** takes **rent** from **tenants**; tenants + **agricultural labourers** = **peasants** (work the land they do not own). **Yadavs** (UP, Bihar); Jats (also peasant in this list); **Kunbi Marathas** = peasant (**non-Kunbi** Marathas claim Kshatriya / Shivaji); **Reddys** (Andhra Pradesh); **Vanniyars** (Tamil Nadu). |
| 5 | **Artisans** (craft) | **OBC** with #4 | **Lohar** (blacksmith); **Sonar / Soni** (goldsmith); **Kumhar** (potter); **Nai** (barber); **Mali / Saini** (gardener — not every Saini; class: Mali in UP / Rajasthan, also Maharashtra); **Nishad** (boat-makers, UP); **Maurya** (sculptors / culture-related work). |
| 6 | **Untouchables** | **Scheduled Castes (SC)**; **stigma of untouchability**. **Not** in the four-Varna list. | Work treated as **impure** because it touches **human / animal waste**: faeces, urine, **sweat**, **dead body**. **Manual scavenging / sweepers** — **Bhangi**, **Mazhabi**. Cremation; **Dhobi** (washerman — sweat); butcher; leather — **Chamar** (class: Kanpur); cobbler — sheet **Valmiki**. |

**Upper caste (UC)** = 1–3. **Lower caste (LC)** = 4–6. Inside LC: **4–5 = OBC** (manual / agricultural labour — **not** “impure”); **6 = SC** (impure work). **Varna identity is claimed by UC**; LC’s **new** identities are **OBC** and **SC**.

**Land reform (after independence):** tenants become **land owners**; old **zamindars** (mostly traditional warriors, some priestly land-owners) **lose land** → migrate to **urban** jobs → drop traditional occupation. **Peasant + artisan** stay more **rural** and agrarian. UC are **no longer** the village landowners in this story.

**Gotra** = name of an **ancestor**; same gotra = same blood → **no same-gotra marriage**. **Surname** can be title (Chaudhary; Brahmin Mishra / Dwivedi), father’s name (South), **gotra** (Jat / Maratha tendency), caste, sub-caste, or **village** (Jat Sikh — class: **Moosa** as a village name, as in Sidhu Moosewala).

## 6. Features of caste — why it still lives (SOC-01-06)

**Mains equation (class):**  
**Caste = (flexible — occupation / secular status) + (rigid — birth / ritual status).**  
**Rigidity + flexibility allowed caste to survive.**

1. **Hierarchy** → **social inequality** (UC vs LC).  
2. **Occupation fixed by birth** → traditionally **no change of occupation** → no **mobility**.  
3. **However, flexibility** — change of occupation, **especially in urban areas**. Caste **dissociates** from its main identifier (occupation). Pressures: **urbanisation, migration, change in the economy** (agrarian → industry / **service sector** = new occupations), **reservation** (LC change occupation by using the quota). If caste had stayed fully rigid, class says it would have **collapsed**.  
4. **Ritual status** = caste **by birth**, **fixed** (a Dalit may become an IAS officer and still **dies a Dalit**; no law lets you change caste). **Secular status** = occupation, **flexible**.  
5. **Endogamy** (same-caste marriage) — **most defining** feature. Practised in **rural and urban** areas. Occupation rule **diluted**; endogamy **has not**. This keeps caste **alive**.  
6. **Restrictions on LC** — especially untouchables: cannot use UC **community resources** (wells, ponds, bathing ghats, cremation grounds); **no inter-dining**; historically **no education**; **no temple entry**. Result: **social exclusion** → LC labelled **socially and educationally backward** → Constitution gives **reservation**. **Reservation in India is because of the caste system.**  
7. **Pure / impure** — **pure / sacred** = things connected with **God**; **cow** and cow products (class: urine, dung, milk). **Impure** = human waste, animal waste **except cow**, dead bodies, **alcohol** and **non-veg**. **Impurity transfers by touch; purity does not.** Pure must be protected from impure → **stigma of untouchability** (body treated as impure). Temple is pure, so an untouchable entering would make it impure — that is the class logic for the ban.

**OBC vs SC in one line:** OBC = **manual labour**; SC = **impure** work.

## 7. Varna vs caste; British census; Sanskritization (SOC-01-06)

| Varna | Caste |
|:---|:---|
| Theory in the **Rig Veda** | About **4,000–5,000** groups |
| **Four**: Brahmin (priest), Kshatriya (warrior), Vaishya (trader), Shudra (**servant** of the other three) | Customs differ even when occupation is the same; **geography** varies caste |
| **Division of labour** — class: work is **different**, not ranked as high/low in this theory | **Pure / impure** and **untouchability** |
| **No** concept of pure/impure; **no** untouchability | Has both |

**Who linked Varna to caste?** **The British** — not the Rig Vedic world as a census table.

| Year | Class lock |
|:---|:---|
| **1871** | First **caste census** — list of castes + population of each (ask people their caste). Shock: **4–5 thousand** names. |
| **1881** | Try to **classify caste into the four Varnas**. |
| **1891** | Top **three Varnas = UC**; last Varna = **LC**. Formal UC/LC stamp. **Government jobs** to **UC**. |
| After 1891 | LC **caste associations** → **petition** British for **UC** status (so they can get those jobs). British **do not** grant UC. |
| **1931** | **Last** caste census of that series. Still **never** giving them UC status. |
| **1941** | Class: **no** census — **no resources** (war). |
| After independence | Government decides **not** to do a caste census: Constitution aims at a **caste-less** society; asking caste would keep **caste consciousness** alive. Ordinary census continues; **caste not asked**. |
| **2011** | A caste count was attempted; people often returned **surname as caste** → **invalid** data (class: the list ballooned). |
| **2026** | Government **will** conduct a caste census **with a list** of castes to choose from (class: more valid). Do not invent a month. |

**Sanskritization** (two keywords: **desire** + **imitate**):

LC want **UC status**. They claim ancestors from UC and **copy UC practices** (stop **alcohol** and **non-veg**). Examples: **Yadavs** as **Yaduvanshi** (descendants of Krishna → Kshatriya); **Vishwakarma** artisans as descendants of Lord Vishwakarma → Brahmin; **Kushwaha** peasants (Bihar) as descendants of **Kusha** (Rama’s son) → Kshatriya; **Kurmi** (Bihar) / **Patel** (Gujarat) peasants claiming old Kshatriya status. Associations: **Yadav Mahasabha**, Kurmi / Kushwaha sabhas.

British reply in class: UC is about **occupation**, not lifestyle. They changed **rituals**, not **occupation** → **never got UC**. If they had, class says the **OBC movement** would not have been needed.
