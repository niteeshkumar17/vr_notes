## 1. Contraceptives, and why 67 per cent is not 90 (SOC-06-01)

**67 per cent** of the eligible population in India uses contraceptives. In Western countries the figure is about **90 per cent**. People know the methods and still hold back, because the awareness is incomplete. The fear is of **side effects**, and of surgery. Men in particular will not accept vasectomy: the fear is that they will lose masculine characteristics. He called that misinformation.

| Method | What he locked |
|:---|:---|
| **Oral** | The woman takes one tablet a day. While she is taking it, she does not conceive. He compared it to **Mala-D** |
| **Injectable** | Either partner can take it. He said a monthly injection, and also that the couple does not conceive for the **six months** it covers. He did not settle one schedule |
| **Intra-uterine contraceptive device (IUCD)** | Also written **RUCD** on the sheet. A **T-shaped copper** device, placed in the uterus by a minor procedure. While it is in, the couple cannot conceive. **Best**, because it is **reversible** and, in his words, **100 per cent safe**. Remove it six years later and a second child is still possible |
| **Tubectomy** and **vasectomy** | Tubectomy is **nasbandi**, in women. Vasectomy is in men. The sheet marks both **not reversible**. He first said a reversal window of two or three years, then withdrew it: wait six years and it cannot be undone. Lock **irreversible** |

Religions that discourage contraceptives have a high fertility rate. His case was **Islam**, with a total fertility rate of **3.1**, because the religion discourages the methods. Asked whether people in India follow religion or science, he answered **religion**. The northern-state **3.1** on the previous note is a different sentence. Do not swap the two.

## 2. Empowerment, marriage age, and a son (SOC-06-02)

**Empowerment** means not depending on anyone: self-dependent, so she can take her own decisions. Women in India, in his line, are not empowered. A girl is denied proper nourishment, education, and skill, and she is married early. She then depends financially on the husband. He takes the household decisions. That dominance he called **private patriarchy** (*patri* = male). Reproductive decisions sit with him too: how many children, the gap, how many boys, whether a contraceptive is used. She cannot refuse, because the money is his.

The fertility pattern that follows:

- **Working** women have a **low** total fertility rate. After two children she can refuse, because she also has her work.
- Women who are **not working** have a **high** rate.
- Women educated **till class 12** have a **low** rate. They know what a small family is worth, and they know the contraceptives.

**Early marriage raises the fertility rate for girls only.** A boy’s age at marriage does not. A woman has a fixed reproductive span. He put the start at about **14** and the end at about **42–45**. The ovaries produce a fixed number of eggs for life. After **35**, number and quality fall sharply, and sperm and egg stop meeting. A man, after puberty, can reproduce through life. Marry at **16** and the reproductive life is long. Marry at **30** and it is short. The first girl has the higher chance of more children.

**Parsis** are the late-marriage case. Their fertility rate is **1.0**. Girls marry at an average age of **30**, because a Parsi girl is expected to marry a Parsi boy, the community is small, and a match inside it takes time.

**Hindus**, in this class, have a high fertility rate because the preference for a **male child** is strong. A son is treated as compulsory, so births continue until one arrives. Muslims, on his split, are the contraceptive case above, not the son-preference case.

## 3. Infant deaths, and the schemes that answer them (SOC-06-03)

A high **infant mortality rate (IMR)** raises the birth rate. Parents in a high-IMR country believe their children may not survive. They go for several births in a short span, as **insurance** if some die. That raises the fertility rate. His grandmother had seven children on that calculation, and all seven lived. The government wants both IMR and the **maternal mortality rate (MMR)** down.

Definitions he dictated. If someone says the IMR is **30**, that means: of **1,000** children born alive, **30** die within one year. **30 is his example, not an Indian figure he locked.** MMR is mothers who die in childbirth, per **1 lakh** deliveries. India’s, he said, is about **100**: 100 mothers die for every 1 lakh deliveries. The sheet writes the same **100 per 1,00,000**.

Two instruments. Vaccines are free, and uptake was still low: people are not fully aware, they fear side effects, and he called that a **cultural lag**. Third-world uptake of new medical technology is low. He used the COVID vaccine as the picture. Institutional delivery is the other instrument. Poverty sends the birth home, because a hospital visit costs money even when the delivery itself is free. Home delivery, in a country weak on sanitation, kills mother and child by **infection**.

**Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP).** The target is **90 per cent** of eligible children and mothers, not 100. **90 per cent is herd immunity**: the disease does not spread. The sheet dates the programme **1989**. The audio did not dictate the year. In **2014** the government went into mission mode because uptake was still low: **Mission Indradhanush**, to intensify the UIP. The sheet adds awareness, cold storage, and indigenous production under that mission, and writes **NFHS-5 → 77%**. He did not say, in clear English, what the 77 counts. Do not invent the indicator. **Polio**, on the sheet, was eradicated in **2014**. The spoken point is that the vaccine still has to be given, because polio remains in **Pakistan and Bangladesh** and can come back in.

**Human papillomavirus (HPV)**, added to the UIP in **2026** on the sheet. It causes **cervical cancer**. The cervix is the tube between the vagina and the uterus. **Herpes, syphilis, and HIV** need unprotected sex. HPV can pass by **skin-to-skin contact even when the sex is protected**. The sheet limits the girls’ doses to ages **10–14**. The audio did not lock that band in English.

**Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)** and **Janani Shishu Suraksha Yojana (JSSY)**. The sheet dates them **2005** and **2011**. The audio did not dictate the years. Both are the **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare**, not the Ministry of Women and Child Development.

| | JSY | JSSY |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **What moves** | A **direct benefit transfer** into the **woman’s** account, as compensation for delivery-related expenses | No transfer. The care itself is free |
| **Mother** | The cash | Free delivery. Free hospitalisation after the birth: **3 days** for a normal delivery, **7 days** for a **C-section**. Free medicines and supplements. Free food, **for the mother only**. Free ambulance |
| **Child** | — | Free treatment, free referral care, and a free ambulance, each **for one month** |

He muddled which scheme carries the cash, then corrected it: the transfer is **JSY**. JSSY is the free-care scheme. Together they have worked. **97 per cent** of deliveries in India are now institutional: 97 of every 100 births are in a hospital or a clinic. The 4 September note’s point, that the rush into hospitals outran safety and staff, stays there.

**Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana** is the Women and Child Development scheme, for women who earn a **daily wage**. Rest after delivery is lost wages, so they go back to work at once. The compensation: **₹5,000** for the first child. If there is another child and that child is a **girl**, **₹6,000** more. If the next child is not a girl, nothing further. He pointed at a handout page and was not sure whether it was page 7 or page 16.

**Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan** is Health again. **Surakshit** is the word he wanted remembered. The purpose is universal **antenatal** care: care of the mother **before** the birth, while she is pregnant. He also called it **prenatal**. Check-ups and sonography. **Diabetes** and **hypertension** make a pregnancy high-risk, so those mothers are checked repeatedly and then delivered safely. **Postnatal** care is health care **after** the birth. He said he had not given that scheme the time it needed.

Three field workers, and they are not the same job:

- **Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA)**. A woman, at least **10th-pass**, **over 18**, **one in every village**. She is **not a nurse**. She generates awareness: the small family, contraceptives (she can say she has used them and had no side effects, so the village trusts her), vaccines, government schemes, and the nutrition of mother and child. She takes a pregnant woman to hospital and is paid an incentive **per delivery**.
- **Auxiliary nurse midwife**: a **trained nurse** who conducts the delivery.
- **Anganwadi** worker: nutrition and early education for children aged **0 to 6**, and only that age band.

## 4. What a large population does, besides eat (SOC-06-04)

Resources are limited, so a rising population produces conflict. The shortage he named is **water, land, and energy**, especially **land**. **Food is not on that list.** The **Green Revolution** ended the food shortage.

**Poverty reproduces itself.** A poor family has four children, two girls and two boys. They do not get nutrition or education, so cognitive ability stays poor, the work they find is semi-skilled or unskilled, the income stays low, and the poverty is still there. One poor household becomes four. Poor people, he said, multiply fast. He also called this a **vicious cycle**.

**Culture of poverty** is a different claim. People are poor not for lack of jobs, but because of **values**, and those values are inherited, generation to generation. His list: **religion**; a **fatalistic** attitude (everything is fate, fixed by God, so there is no point working to change it); **no reading habit**; **instant gratification** (money goes to short-term wants, including alcohol, and not to education, health, or nutrition); **domestic violence**. In that household the woman is the steadier earner. The man is not regular at work. A prosperous house, by contrast, passes on hard work, reading, and long-term spending on the child’s nutrition and education. The rich reproduce the rich, and they **multiply wealth**. The poor multiply **people**. Over time that is **inequality**: the rich get richer, the poor get poorer.

He added a southern water fight. Southern rivers are not perennial. Their flow depends on the monsoon, so there is not enough water for every State that wants it. Northern rivers, in the contrast he was drawing, carry enough that the fight is less desperate.

The last impact is that inequality itself, widening as wealth multiplies on one side and population on the other.

## 5. Three ages, and a window that closes (SOC-06-05)

Population age structure is the share in each band.

| Band | Who | What happens to the share |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **I · over 64** | Old | He locked India’s share at **8 per cent**, and said this share **rises** |
| **II · 16 to 64** | Young, and the **working** age. The highest share | Generates the income. Falls later, because these people become old |
| **III · under 16** | Children | Falls, because the birth rate is already falling |

The sheet’s other two percentages are not legible as a pair that adds to 100 with the 8. Do not lock a 57 or a 31. The audio locked the **8**, the rank order, and the direction of each arrow.

The working-age band supports the other two. Its income buys goods and services. Consumption is what fuels **GDP**. A country with a high share in that band therefore sees fast GDP growth. That phenomenon is the **demographic dividend**.

It is a **lifetime** opportunity, and it sits inside a **window**. Fast growth has to happen while the window is open. If the window closes, the chance of growing that fast is **lost forever**. A country that misses it can never again grow as fast as it could have during the window. The close arrives when the old share is high: the young have aged, and fewer children are being born. Old people **save**. They do not spend. One phone lasts five years. A young buyer replaces a phone every year or two.

He said India’s window is **“30 to 2050”**, and that **after 2050** the old share rises. The sheet writes a span that reads **2015–2050**. The geography class’s window is about **2052–2055**. Three sentences, three homes. Do not collapse them. When this window closes he also used the phrase **demographic winter**: consumption falls, as animals eat through a summer and then do not eat through a winter. **Japan and South Korea** are already in that winter. Births keep falling, the young grow old, the old do not die, and life expectancy is high. In Japan people still work until **80 or 85**. They may still not spend. A young person can plan ten years ahead. An old person cannot know how long the work will last, and that uncertainty holds the money back.

Western growth, with the window already shut, is **1 or 2 per cent**. India, inside the dividend, is growing at **7 to 8 per cent**. After the close, India would also be at 1 or 2 per cent. The West became rich, and lifted per capita income, **before** its window shut, so the old there can still consume. India has to do the same. **India must become rich before it gets old.** Old, in that sentence, means the window has closed.

Predicting India after 2050 is difficult, because it depends on how fast the fertility rate keeps falling. It is about **2.0** now. If the fall is steep, the government wants it held at **2.1**, so that Japan’s situation does not arrive. **2.1** is the ideal he restated. The definition stays on the geography note.

How the State uses the window:

1. The population **must be skilled**. Without income beyond basic food and clothes, the consumption that lifts GDP does not happen. He pointed at a skill mission and did not open its machinery.
2. **Incentivise the private sector to create formal jobs.** Formal means a **permanent** job and a **fixed salary**. Informal means a contract, no permanency, and no fixed pay. An IT-company engineer and an HDFC Bank manager are formal. A sales agent, paid by the product, is informal. People with a fixed permanent salary spend more. The incentive can be a tax concession, or land for the business, on the condition that the factory does not keep people informal.
3. **Raise women’s participation in the workforce.** Two earners in the house means a double income, and a double income means more consumption.

## 6. Force, then awareness (SOC-06-06)

**Direct** control is also **punitive**: the State uses **forcible** methods.

- **China, 1980:** the **one-child** policy. The first child is the last. There is no choice. The geography class dates that policy to **1979**, and the previous Social Issues class to the **1980s**. This sitting said **1980**. All three stay.
- **India, 1976**, during the **Emergency**: forced tubectomy and forced vasectomy. People hated it. The government changed. The new government understood it would not repeat the method. India **left** forcible control and moved to **preventive** control.

Preventive control is awareness: the benefits of a small family, so the decision is an informed one. The awareness that exists is mainly **urban**. Villages need **ASHA**, one woman in each village, as above.

In **2000** India declared a **National Population Policy**. It states that the government will **never** adopt punitive measures. Only preventive ones.

A student raised Rajasthan: in panchayat elections, a person with more than two children cannot contest. He wavered, then placed **incentives and disincentives** under preventive, indirect control. They are not the one-child column. He was not sure of the example. Do not promote it to a locked statute.

**China did control the birth rate**, drastically, after the one-child policy. The bill came later.

- China is **ageing fast**. Average age **37**. India’s is **26**.
- China’s dividend window will close **sooner**. He put India’s close at **50**, and said that if India had adopted a one-child policy the window would close at **35 or 40**. Those are the numbers he spoke. Do not turn them into extra calendar years.
- China will soon face a **shortage of young labour**. That is dangerous because the economy is **manufacturing**-based and needs a constant supply of labour. He mentioned **artificial intelligence** as a possible substitute and did not develop it.
- The birth rate keeps **falling**, even though the government now pays people to have a second child and a third. He dated a **two-child** policy to **2000** and a **three-child** policy to **2020**. The two-child year was not a clean sentence. Once a birth rate starts falling, it keeps falling. Before the **1980s**, Chinese couples tended to have two or three children. The one-child rule left a **surplus income**. They built a lifestyle. A lifestyle becomes a habit, and a habit does not reverse because a government offers a cheque for a second child.

## 7. Girls per thousand boys (SOC-06-07)

**Sex ratio**, from **NFHS-5**: number of **women per 1,000 males**. India has **1,020** women per 1,000 men.

**Child sex ratio**: girls aged **0 to 6** per 1,000 boys aged 0 to 6. About **952**. So 952 girls per 1,000 boys in that band.

By nature, a girl’s chance of survival is **higher** than a boy’s, so a natural child sex ratio should show **more girls**. India’s shows more boys. The conclusion he drew: **more girls die in infancy than boys**. He later guessed that the female **XX** chromosome carries more immune-system genes, then said he had not read it and would check. Do not lock the chromosome line. The survival claim he was sure of is the one above.

Two reasons girls die.

1. **Nutrition** is withheld from the girl, ages 0 to 6. The household treats it as wasted money, because she will leave for another house at marriage. Less food, higher mortality.
2. **Sex-selective abortion**, which he also called **female foeticide**.

The practice is sharpest in **Punjab, Haryana, and Delhi**. Those three have a **skewed** child sex ratio: very few girls against the boys. Punjab and Haryana became rich with the **Green Revolution** of **1970**. Richer families want **fewer** children, two or three at most, and they still want a **son to inherit the land**. In **1970** an **ultrasound** machine arrived. It was for **genetic abnormality**: if the test showed one, the parents could abort. It was expensive, so only the rich could buy it. It also showed the **sex**. A girl was aborted.

The household rules he spelled out. If the first child is a boy, a second girl is acceptable and they stop. If the first is a girl, the second **must** be a boy, which means a sex test and an abortion if it is another girl. They will not go on to a third. The same pattern, he said, is strong in the **urban middle class**, and that is Delhi’s version: a prosperous lifestyle; a first boy means they do not have a second child at all; a first girl means the second is made to be a boy.

The child sex ratio in Punjab and Haryana fell **as low as 700**. In **1990** the government passed the **PCPNDT Act**. He expanded it as **pre-conception, pre-natal determination test**. It **banned the use of an ultrasound machine for sex determination in a clinic**. Asking the doctor the sex of the child is a **criminal offence**. The machines then got small. Farmers imported them and ran the test **at home**. The Act did not stop the practice.

A very low ratio in a few States produces **trafficking**. Girls are bought by those rich farmers, treated as a **commodity**, and the male members of the household sexually exploit them, because a purchased girl is something the market sold them.

The answer he described is awareness, especially in villages: why a girl should be born, that what a boy can do a girl can do, celebrate the birth, and send her to school. The scheme name on the audio is **“baby bachao”**, repeated. That is **Beti Bachao Beti Padhao**. Awareness by itself does **not** produce social change in India, and especially not in villages, which he called closed. Enrolment has to reach **secondary** school, classes **9 to 12**. After class 8 a rural girl is taken out for housework and marriage.

Why the early marriage, beyond fertility. She is a **burden**. Send her to the other house as soon as possible, and let that house feed her. There is no point spending the family’s resources on a girl. At the wedding, the **girl’s father** pays. She is a financial liability. A son is an **asset**: he stays, he supports the parents in old age, the family land stays safe because nobody will try to take it, and a son is what makes a household count as Hindu in the traditional mind he was describing. Land inheritance is the strong rural reason.

Two questions he answered, and one he left.

- **Southern States will close the dividend window sooner than northern States.** He agreed. Southern fertility is **1.8** and already falling fast, so the young share shrinks and today’s adults age quickly. The North, at **3.1**, is at an earlier stage and still has time. Both rates are the previous class’s pair.
- **Women’s empowerment is the key to population control.** It is a **very important** key. It is **not the only** key.
- **Why urban fertility is lower than rural**, and the rural-versus-urban child sex ratio, are on the sheet as questions. The urban answer is not in clear English. What he had already taught, and what applies to the city, is work, schooling to class 12, and the middle-class rule that a first son ends the family.
