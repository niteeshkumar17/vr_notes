## 1. How the two Houses are made (POL-07-01)

| | **Rajya Sabha** | **Lok Sabha (LS)** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Total now | **245** = **233 elected + 12 nominated** | **543 elected**. **No nominated** members now (old Anglo-Indian nominated seats gone) |
| Who elects | **Elected** Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs) of **States + Union Territories (UTs) that have a legislature** — class: **Delhi, Puducherry, Jammu and Kashmir** | **Direct** election from **territorial constituencies** |
| Method | **Proportional Representation (PR) + Single Transferable Vote (STV)** | **First Past the Post (FPTP)** = **maximum** votes. **Not** a majority. Class: election of **popularity**, not majority |
| Label | Council of States — **indirect** people | **Popular House** — **direct** people |
| Seat list | **Fourth Schedule** (233) | **Parliament by law** — **RPA 1950**. Changes with population / new states. Has moved since 1950 |

**Trap:** both Houses represent **the same people**. LS = direct; RS = people of the States via MLAs. Do not write “RS does not represent the people.”

**PR here ≠ Presidential PR.** President: vote **value** ∝ State **population**. RS: all **403** Uttar Pradesh (UP) MLAs elect UP’s **31** RS seats — **no extra vote-value**. PR+STV is used because the electoral college must pick **more than one** member (top-five FPTP would collapse if everyone first-preferences the same name). Class will not drill the arithmetic in the room.

**Class numbers (do not invent more):** Delhi **7 LS / 3 RS**. UP **80 LS / 31 RS**. Tamil Nadu **39 LS**.

### Why two RPAs? (class prompt — do not invent the split)

Both Acts together regulate **LS / Assembly / Council / RS** elections. **Only statute named in the GS-II syllabus** (*salient features of the Representation of the People Act*). Class hint after the 2003 ballot change: **1950 has the seats; 1951 has the process.** Confirm from the **index pages** yourself.

## 2. Open ballot, *Kuldip Nayar*, anti-defection, NOTA (POL-07-02)

| | RS election | LS election |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Ballot | **Open** (only such election in India) | **Secret** — **free and fair** (no force) |
| Since | Originally **secret**. **Open from 2003** (RPA **1951**) | Always secret |

**Open** = a party **observer** may **see** the MLA’s preference sheet. The MLA is still free to vote as they like. The **system** will not punish them.

***Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India, 2006*** (class: renowned journalist; now deceased). Challenge: open ballot kills free voting. Court / government logic class stressed: **secret** RS polls hid **cross-voting** and **corruption**; **indirect** election + party representation was being gamed.

**Anti-Defection Law (ADL)** applies only to a **proceeding of the House**. President / Vice-President / RS **elections are not House proceedings** → **ADL does not apply**. Same logic: **None of the Above (NOTA)** is for **direct** free voters only — **no NOTA** in President / VP / RS elections.

## 3. Continuous House vs dissolvable House (POL-07-03)

| | RS | LS |
|:---|:---|:---|
| House term | **None** — **continuous House** | **5 years**, then **auto-dissolved** |
| Members | **6 years**; **1/3 retire every 2 years** | Whole House goes together |
| Age (class) | **Same RS since 1952** — **74 years** old | This is the **18th LS**, about **2 years** old. **17** LS have come and gone |

**Early dissolution — 1971:** after 1952–57–62–67 the next poll was due **1972**. Indira Gandhi called it a year early on a “good vibe” (*India is Indira…*). **Second-highest** majority ever; beaten only by **1984**.

**Extension beyond 5 years** (only during **National Emergency**):

1. Emergency proclaimed in the country **or any part**.
2. **Parliament by law**.
3. **≤ 1 year at a time** (3 / 6 / 9 months fine; not more than 12). Can repeat while Emergency lasts.
4. When Emergency is **revoked**, leftover extension **cannot go beyond 6 months** (time to hold a poll).

**1971 cycle:** next poll due **1976** → extended to **1977**, approved again to **1978** but the **second year was not used** — election held in **1977**.

**Bye-election** (any House, any vacancy): winner gets only the **leftover** term, not a fresh 5 / 6 years. Class: a bye-election today → new LS member sits till **2029**.

## 4. RS is federal — minus / plus / equal (POL-07-04)

**Why an RS at all?** **Federalism** = **division of power** between **two layers** (Centre and States; names differ abroad). Centre’s Union-list law hits every State → States need a House in the conversation. **Seventh Schedule** is the map.

Do **not** write “RS is useless / less useful.” Visibility (LS elections, government formation, money) ≠ relevance.

### Minus (LS stronger) — finance + administration

1. **Make / break government** — Confidence Motion (CM), No-Confidence Motion (NCM), Censure Motion, Adjournment Motion. **Collective responsibility to LS** (parliamentary principle 2 — Lecture 4 / Article **75(3)**). **United States of America (USA) House is also direct-elected** and still cannot make/break the executive — so “direct election” is **not** the reason. Reason: **parliamentary democracy**.
2. **Money Bills** — introduce **only in LS**. RS may **recommend within 14 days**; LS may **override**. Reason: **no taxation without representation** — people’s money → **House of the People**.

### Plus (RS only / RS unique)

- **Article 249** (national-interest State-list law) and **Article 312** (new All India Service) — **RS resolutions**. Class: **249 used zero times**. **312:** only **Indian Forest Service** created after Independence (Indian Administrative Service / Indian Police Service inherited from the British). Put this **later** in an answer — rare.
- **Removal of the Vice-President** — introduced in RS (Lecture 5: effective in RS, simple in LS).
- **National Emergency / President’s Rule / Financial Emergency** normally need **both Houses**. If **LS is dissolved**, **RS alone** can approve. **LS alone can never** — RS is always there. **Significance of a continuous House:** it **assists continuity of administration**. Class: President’s Rule when an LS poll is due.

### Equal — legislation + deliberation

- **Ordinary Bills, Financial Bills, Constitution Amendment Bills (CABs)** need **both Houses**.
- **Deadlock** on ordinary / financial → **joint sitting, Article 108** (detail next class). Class: **three** joint sittings in ~80 years. LS is roughly **double** RS, so LS would dominate the room — **not** an argument that RS is useless.
- **No joint sitting for Article 368.** Both Houses sit **separately**. One House’s no = dead.

**42nd / 44th story (class):** Emergency **42nd** = **mini-Constitution** (strengthen the executive). Janata **45th Constitutional Amendment Bill** → became **44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978**. RS still Congress-controlled; it **diluted** the rollback; LS had to **accept RS changes** or lose the Bill. **House of Elders** = **experience / link to the past** (2/3 still sitting), **not** because members are older. **Temperance** vs LS “change everything in one election.”

**Numbering trap:** CAB numbers and CAA numbers are **two pipelines**. Class: **106** CAAs so far. This year’s Budget-session **131st CAB** (delimitation / **850** seats) **failed** — that bill number is used up; next bill **132** would become Act **107** if it passed.

**Deliberation:** both have Question Hour, Zero Hour, debates. **LS: Question Hour then Zero Hour. RS inverted** (class: RS switched ~**10 years** ago to get more Zero-Hour time). **Starred** = oral in the House; **unstarred** = written papers. Zero Hour = on the spot; Question Hour needs advance notice.

## 5. Committees — RS is inside Parliament’s work (POL-07-05)

**Estimates Committee** = **LS only** (not a “Parliament” committee in class’s sense).

| Body | Size (class) | Split |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** | **22** | **15 LS + 7 RS** |
| **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)** | **22** | **15 LS + 7 RS** |
| **Department-Related Standing Committee (DRSC)** | **31** each; class **24** DRSCs cover the ministries | **21 LS + 10 RS** |
| **Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC)** | **31** (this LS’s first JPC — current affairs; class did not name the subject) | **21 LS + 10 RS** |

**PAC:** examines the **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)** audit of government accounts (report to Parliament **through the President**). Overspend / underspend / fraud. **COPU:** same idea for **public sector undertakings**. **DRSCs:** schemes, policy, and the **budget** of their departments. Class news hook: **DoPT** DRSC chair **Brij Lal** on **Civil Services Aptitude Test (CSAT)** — read the paper, not only daily-news videos.

A parliamentary committee **cannot run without RS members**. Many **ministers** sit in RS (class: **S. Jaishankar**). Indirect election is a **window for talent** that may not win an FPTP popularity contest. **About one-third** of India’s PMs have come from RS.

**PMs from RS (class four):** **Indira Gandhi**, **I.K. Gujral**, **H.D. Deve Gowda**, **Dr Manmohan Singh**. Sheet listed the first three + Manmohan. **Narendra Modi = 15th PM** → 4 of the previous 14.

**Line to hold:** **RS is not a secondary House; it is the second House.** (State **Legislative Councils** *are* secondary to Assemblies — later topic.)

## 6. Motion vs resolution; three LS special powers (POL-07-06)

**Motion** = a **proposal to transact business** (*prastav*). Introduction of a bill, a discussion, anything. It **moves** through stages.

**Resolution** = a **motion that is voted** — a House **decision**.

**Every resolution is a motion; every motion is not a resolution.** Raghav Chadha / gig workers (Lecture 5) = motion to **discuss**, no vote.

Make-or-break tools sit on **Article 75(3)** (collective responsibility to LS). Procedure lives in the **Rules of Procedure of the Lok Sabha**, not the Constitution.

**Three rules to remember (gap of 7):**

| Rule | What |
|:---|:---|
| **184** | General **motions** |
| **191** | **Voting** on motions |
| **198** | **NCM** specifically |

**CM** and **Censure** are **not** in the rules — **convention**. Adjournment has its **own** block: **Rules 56–63** (**8** rules).

**Three special powers of LS** (class count):

1. Make / break (CM / NCM / Censure / Adjournment).
2. Money Bills — introduce + override.
3. **Discontinue National Emergency** — **special sitting of LS** (sessions class next).

## 7. NCM vs Censure Motion (POL-07-07)

| | **No-Confidence Motion** | **Censure Motion** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Home | **Rule 198** | Convention under **184 + 191**. Word *censure* = **criticise** — not in the rules |
| Against | **Entire Council of Ministers (CoM)** only | **One / group / entire CoM** |
| Point | **Want of confidence** of LS. **Reason not required** (“This House does not have confidence in the CoM” is enough) | An **act, failure to act (omission), or policy**. **Reason required** |
| Leave | **Leave of the House** — **≥ 50 members** stand to **introduce** (passage is still **simple majority**) | **No leave**. Speaker + **Leader of the House** + **Business Advisory Committee (BAC)** slot the discussion — government can **delay** |
| If it passes | Entire CoM must go. **PM’s resignation is enough** (pivot). **Never passed** in independent India. **1999:** Vajpayee resigned after a **confidence** test, not after an NCM *passed*. Last NCM **introduced 2023** (BJP **303** — class: it was never going to pass) | Official record + **political** hit. **Never passed** against a minister. If it ever did, an NCM would follow |

**NCM clock (Rule 198):** notice to the **Speaker’s office**. **Before 10:00** (sitting starts **11:00**) → taken up **the same day** (Speaker picks the hour). **After 10:00** → **next working day**. After leave: dispose within **≤ 10 days**. Marshal counts those standing.

**Why bring a motion that will fail?** Debate. Class: 2023 NCM; recent proposal to **remove the Speaker** — not to win the numbers, to put **partisan chair** on the record. **Seven bills in 36 minutes** is the opposite of that democracy.

## 8. Adjournment Motion (POL-07-08)

**Adjournment** (everyday) = Speaker ends a **sitting** (lunch, 18:00). **Adjournment Motion** = a **member** proposes to **stop today’s listed work** to discuss an **urgent matter of great public importance**.

- **Leave ≥ 50.**
- After discussion the House **votes**. If passed → **rest of the day adjourned**.
- **Element of censure:** majority slaps the **government’s agenda**. Same reason it exists **only in LS**, **not in RS**.

## 9. Presiding officers — both Houses (POL-07-09)

| | LS | RS |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Presiding | **Speaker** | **Chairman** (Vice-President) |
| Deputy | **Deputy Speaker** | **Deputy Chairman** |
| Panel if both absent | **Panel of Chairpersons ≤ 10** (Speaker appoints) | **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons ≤ 6** (Chairman appoints) |

Deputy **works only when the chair is empty**. Otherwise they are an ordinary member.

Chair **does not participate** like a member (no ordinary speech / motion / vote) except a **casting vote** on a **tie**.

They **regulate**: who speaks, how long, *baith jaiye*. **Interpret** the Constitution and the **rules** for the sitting — that interpretation is written down for later Speakers. **Discipline:** suspend a member for at most **the end of the session**. **Face of the House** (not of the government), inside and outside — President’s message (e.g. return of a bill) arrives **to the chair**; a result is official when the chair **announces** it.

## 10. Three special powers of the LS Speaker (POL-07-10)

Same count as LS’s three special powers:

1. **Certify a Money Bill.** Certificate is **final** for the House to **proceed**. **Final ≠ unreviewable in court.** Class: even an SC judgment can be overruled by a larger bench.
2. **Preside at a joint sitting:** **Speaker → Deputy Speaker → Deputy Chairman of RS** (**not** the RS Chairman) → a **member decided by the Houses**.
3. **Article 94 proviso:** when LS is **dissolved**, the Speaker **does not vacate** until **immediately before the first meeting** of the next LS. **Institutional continuity** — 17th → 18th LS. One person, no law-making; members are gone; the **face** stays. Vacates **automatically** the night before the new House meets.

### Speaker Pro Tem (convention)

**Member-elect** ≠ member until the **oath**. Chicken-and-egg: need a Speaker to oath members; need members to elect a Speaker.

**President** administers the oath to **one** member-elect and names them **Speaker Pro Tem** (temporary). That person (1) oaths the rest (540+ , days) and (2) conducts the election of the Speaker. Class: House then chose **Om Birla**. Pro Tem’s job ends when the new Speaker sits.

**Article 93:** House shall choose **one** Speaker and **one** Deputy Speaker **as soon as possible**. **No clock.** **No Deputy Speaker** for the **entire 17th LS + 2 years of the 18th** = **7 years**. Government hides behind *asap*; class: **spirit** of the article + **convention** (Speaker = ruling; Deputy = **opposition**) + democracy. **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)** pending.

**Homework split:** **Absence** of S + DyS + panel ≠ **vacancy** (death / resignation / removal). **Panel is appointed by the Speaker** — if the Speaker’s office is **vacant**, the panel has **no role**. Answers differ; you find them.
