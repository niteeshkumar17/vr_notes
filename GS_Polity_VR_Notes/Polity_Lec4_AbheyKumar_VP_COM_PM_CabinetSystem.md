## 1. Convention: President's Right to Encourage & Warn (Continued from Lecture 3)

- **Convention from England**, observed in India also
- President has the right to **encourage** or **warn** the Council of Ministers
- Only for discharge of **executive functions** — not other matters
- **Practically does nothing** — but when the President speaks, people listen
- Presidents are very **polite and docile** in their demeanour
- It is purely a **conventional practice**, not written anywhere

## 2. Caretaker Government — Detailed

### Definition & Nature
- A **temporary government** that runs during the gap between resignation of PM and appointment of new PM
- **Not a constitutional term** — purely conventional
- Lasts only a few days to weeks
- Exists because the **parliamentary system mandates** there must always be a CoM (UNR Rao v. Indira Gandhi, 1971)

### What Can a Caretaker Government Do?
| ✅ Should Do | ❌ Should NOT Do |
|:---|:---|
| Decisions **necessary for day-to-day administration** | **Major policy decisions** |
| Routine governance & continuity | New policy initiatives |

### President's Discretionary Power in Caretaker Period
- Both terms ("major policy" and "day-to-day decision") are **highly subjective**
- **President decides** which decisions are necessary and which are not — with **discretion**
- President **cannot take a 6th decision** on their own — can only filter from CoM's advice
- **Discretion is in NOT taking action**, not in taking independent action
- Example: If CoM advises 5 things, President can decide: Advice 1 & 2 = necessary → action; Advice 3, 4, 5 = not necessary → wait for next PM

> **Key Statement:** "In case of a caretaker government, since it is not expected to take major policy decisions, only necessary decisions for day-to-day administration, it is the President who decides which decisions are necessary or not."

### Not Cast in Rigid Mode — Only Restricted Mode
- What a caretaker government can/cannot do is **not fixed**
- It depends entirely on **circumstances** and **President's discretion**
- **1999 ABV Example:** Vajpayee resigned in April 1999 (lost majority); Kargil war happened in May → caretaker PM had to fight a war → major decision but **necessary** → President supported, Sonia Gandhi rallied behind PM
- Books may say "can do this, cannot do that" — but remember it's always **"may"**

### 3 Situations When CoM is Dissolved
1. **PM Resigns** → pivot gone, CoM dissolved → same PM becomes caretaker
2. **PM Dies** → pivot gone, CoM dissolved → senior-most cabinet minister becomes caretaker (convention since 1964, Nehru's death)
3. **Lok Sabha Dissolved** → mother of CoM died → same PM becomes caretaker

### Convention for Caretaker PM (when PM dies)
- **1964 (First Instance):** Nehru died → Congress still had majority → party needed time to choose new leader
- President appointed the **senior-most cabinet minister** → became convention
- Managed entirely by **conventions** — nothing written

## 3. R. Venkataraman's Emergency Lamp Analogy

- R. Venkataraman was VP (1982–87) → President (1987–92) → 10 years at the top
- In his farewell speech, he described the **role of the President** as that of an **"emergency lamp"**:
  - When political power supply is proper (clear majority) → nobody bothers about the President
  - When political power supply is disrupted (no clear majority / hung parliament) → President's role becomes critical
  - The role **ceases** the moment a PM emerges with parliamentary majority
- **1989 election** during his tenure was the first time Centre saw no clear majority → President became crucial
- Tamil Nadu Governor example: all over news during hung assembly → invisible after clear majority emerged

## 4. Vice President of India

### Overview
- Second in line after the President
- Functions: **Acting President** (when vacancy arises) OR **Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha**
- **As Vice President, there is virtually no work** — nothing to discuss

### Election of VP
| Feature | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| **Elected by** | **All members of Parliament** (both elected AND nominated) |
| **Contrast with President** | President = only elected members of Parliament + State Legislatures |
| **Method** | Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV) — same as President |
| **Rationale** | VP is just a backup for President's office — no need to involve states |

### Removal of VP
- **Parliament removes** (Parliament elects, Parliament removes)
- **Key condition:** Proposal to remove must be **introduced in Rajya Sabha only** (since VP is Chairman of RS)
- If RS passes → goes to LS → if LS also passes → VP stands removed
- Majority requirements will be covered in Parliament chapter

### Timing of Election & Vacancy
| Situation | Rule |
|:---|:---|
| **Regular election** | Must be held **before completion of term** |
| **Vacancy (death/resignation/removal)** | Election must be held **as soon as possible** |
| **Unique feature** | **No fixed time limit** (unlike President's 6 months rule) — only office where this is possible |

## 5. Council of Ministers (CoM) — Structure & Appointment

### What is "Government" — 3 Levels of Meaning
| Level | Meaning | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Broadest** | Legislature + Executive + Judiciary | "Government of India" |
| **Narrow** | Executive only | Administrative machinery |
| **Narrowest** | Council of Ministers | "Modi Sarkar" / "Modi Government" |

### Appointment Chain
```
President --(?discretion)--> appoints PM ---(PM's advice)---> President appoints Ministers
```
- **President appoints PM** → discretion may or may not exist (depends on majority clarity)
- **President appoints other ministers** on the **advice of PM** → no discretion here
- **× = No discretion** | **? = May or may not** | **A = On advice**

### Individual Responsibility of Ministers
- **Minister is responsible to the President** (objectively/formally — President appointed, President removes)
- **Practically responsible to the PM** (because President removes on PM's advice)
- Both statements are correct — depends on context (objective vs subjective/practical)
- **Responsibility** means: for your action/inaction, who takes action against you?
- Ultimate meaning: **Who can remove the minister?** → President (on PM's advice) → hence responsible to both

### Collective Responsibility of CoM
- **Collectively responsible to Lok Sabha** (Article 75(3))
- They came to power because of **majority in Lok Sabha** → will lose power by **losing majority in Lok Sabha**
- If Lok Sabha is not an option in MCQ → next most suitable answer = **Parliament** (not PM, since PM is part of CoM)
- **"Mark the most suitable answer"** — first instruction in UPSC exam

## 6. Hierarchy within Council of Ministers

### 3 Ranks of Ministers (Currently)

| Rank | Description | Number (Current ~) | Cabinet Meeting |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Cabinet Ministers** | Senior leaders, important portfolios, manage ministry **independently** | ~29 | **Regular members** |
| **MoS (Independent Charge)** | Manage their own ministry independently but **not part of cabinet** | ~5 | **May be invited** when their ministry is discussed |
| **MoS (Minister of State)** | Work **under** a Cabinet Minister — assistants/lieutenants | ~34 | **Not invited** (their boss is already there) |

> **Current total CoM strength = ~69** (29 Cabinet + 5 MoS-IC + 34 MoS + 1 PM = ~69 but 5 MoS-IC are also MoS for something → overlap counted once)

### Key Points About Cabinet Ministers
- Manage their ministries **independently** — PM is NOT their boss
- PM's role = **coordination and leadership** only
- Day-to-day decisions in Ministry of Finance → Finance Minister decides (PM may not even know)
- Cross-ministry decisions (e.g., Operation Sindoor) → need coordination → PM leads

### Key Points About MoS (Independent Charge)
- Also manage their ministry independently → very similar to Cabinet Minister
- **Not part of Cabinet** → PM decided to keep them out of the core team
- Example: Dr. Jitendra Singh → MoS (IC) for Science & Technology + MoS for DoPT
- **May be invited** to cabinet meetings only when their ministry's issue is being discussed → consulted → then leave

### Key Points About MoS (without IC)
- Work under Cabinet Ministers — assist them with large ministries
- Example: MoS Finance assists Nirmala Sitharaman
- Work allocation decided by the **Cabinet Minister** they serve under
- **Not invited** to cabinet meetings — their boss (Cabinet Minister) is already present

### Deputy Ministers (Historical — No Longer Appointed)
- Used to exist as assistants → lollipop for alliance-building
- **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** → imposed **15% of Lok Sabha strength** cap on total CoM size
- Deputy Minister post stopped being used — redundant since MoS already exists
- **Deputy CM** = not a constitutional post (CM is constitutional; Deputy CM, Senior Minister, etc. = internal nomenclature)

### Constitutional Position on CoM
- Constitution only says: **"There shall be a Council of Ministers"**
- Doesn't mention Cabinet Minister, MoS, MoS-IC, Deputy Minister etc.
- **All nomenclature is internal** to the government — Constitution doesn't care
- Key rule: Total CoM **cannot exceed 15% of Lok Sabha strength** (91st CAA, 2003)

## 7. Cabinet — The Real Decision-Making Body

### Cabinet vs CoM Relationship
```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│         Council of Ministers (~69)     │
│                                      │
│    ┌──────────────────────┐          │
│    │   CABINET (~30)      │          │
│    │   (PM + 29 Cabinet   │   MoS,   │
│    │    Ministers)         │   MoS-IC │
│    └──────────────────────┘          │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Cabinet ≠ Council — Key Differences
| Feature | Council of Ministers | Cabinet |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Size** | ~69 | ~30 |
| **Constitutional mention** | Article 74, 75 | **Only in Article 352** (added by 44th CAA, 1978) |
| **Meetings (per 5-year term)** | **Zero** (practically never meets) | **Weekly** (sometimes more than once/week) |
| **Decision-making** | Does nothing — a formal/legal entity | **All real decisions are taken here** |

### Cabinet in the Constitution — A Special Story
| Timeline | Status |
|:---|:---|
| **1947 (Pre-Constitution)** | Cabinet existed (Nehru had a cabinet of 13) — word was "Cabinet" not "Council" |
| **1950 (Constitution enacted)** | Cabinet word **NOT in Constitution** — but Cabinet still functioned |
| **1978 (44th CAA)** | Cabinet word **added** — but only in Article 352 (National Emergency) |
| **State Constitutions (today)** | Cabinet word **still not in state articles** — but every state has a cabinet |

> **Insight:** Cabinet has always existed, with or without constitutional mention. It is so fundamental that it preceded the Constitution itself.

### Why a Separate Cabinet? (Reasons)
1. **Ease of conducting business** — smaller body = easier to hold meetings, conduct government work
2. **Ease of decision-making** — lesser divergence of opinion in 30 than in 69 (but not one person → avoids dictatorship)
3. **Maintenance of secrecy** — fewer people = better secret-keeping (first rule of secrecy: don't tell anyone)
4. **High workload** — government's workload is massive
5. **Not every decision requires all ministers** — e.g., JS appointment doesn't need 69 people

### Decision Flow in Government
```
Cabinet Committee takes decision
    → treated as Cabinet's decision
        → treated as Council's decision
            → presented as President's decision
```
- What citizens see: **"President is pleased to appoint..."**
- What actually happens: A cabinet committee decided it far away from the President
- **President does not even know** who was appointed — only their name is used
- **Council does nothing** — from now on, even Council is a myth (just like President)

## 8. Cabinet Committees

### Types
| Type | Purpose |
|:---|:---|
| **Standing Committees** | Long-term purpose — can go beyond one government |
| **Ad Hoc Committees** | Short-term, specific purpose — once purpose is over, committee dissolved |

### Current Status
- **8 Standing Committees** (was 12, then 10 under Modi — now 8)
- **0 Ad Hoc Committees** currently
- Numbers can change — not critical to memorize exact count

### Most Important Committee: Appointments Committee of Cabinet (ACC)
- **Members:** Only **2** — PM + Home Minister
- **Function:** All appointments of **Joint Secretary and above** in Government of India
- JS+ level = **mid-career** (~17-19 years of service) → point where you enter **policy road**
- Before JS level: you're a worker/implementer (800 district collectors)
- From JS level: **policy input, monitoring, evaluation** → work closely with Cabinet → hence Cabinet decides appointments

### GoS (Group of Secretaries)
- JS and above (after ~17 years of service) start taking part in **policy decisions**

## 9. Transaction of Business Rules (TOBR) & Allocation of Business Rules (AOBR)

### Constitutional Basis: Article 77(3)
> *"The President shall make rules for the convenient transaction of the business of the government and the allocation among the ministers of that business."*

### Two Sets of Rules
| Rules | Content |
|:---|:---|
| **Transaction of Business Rules (TOBR)** | How decisions are taken — committees, process, decision-making machinery |
| **Allocation of Business Rules (AOBR)** | Who does what work — which minister handles which ministry |

- All cabinet committee details are in **TOBR**
- Who made these rules? **Cabinet** (not President, not Council — President's name is used but Cabinet decides)

## 10. Prime Minister — Leader of the System

### PM Comes First
- PM appointed **before** Council → Council created on PM's advice
- **Council cannot exist without PM** → if PM vacates office (resignation/death) → Council **automatically dissolved**
- **PM is the pivot** which holds the Council in power/office → pivot gone = Council gone

### PM as Residual Legatee of the Government
- **Residual Legatee** = all leftover responsibilities are delegated to the PM
- PM allocates portfolios to ~70 ministers → after allocation, some work is **left over** (known and unknown)
- All leftover work = PM's responsibility
- **Even allocated work** → PM is also responsible for it
  - If Nirmala Sitharaman's policy fails → history won't remember her → **"Modi did it"**
  - Nehru did it, Indira Gandhi did it, Vajpayee ji did it → PM = face of government
- **PM = face of the government, name of the government**
- Elections fought in PM's name: "Ghar ghar Modi, har har Modi" — not "Ghar ghar Gadkari"

### Primus Inter Pares vs Inter Stellas Lunar Minoris

| Phrase | Meaning | Source |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Primus Inter Pares** | First among equals | Traditional description — PM is leader but all ministers are at par |
| **Inter Stellas Lunar Minoris** | Moon among lesser stars | William Harcourt (UK MP) — PM is special, not just first among equals |

- **Primus Inter Pares:** PM is not boss → Cabinet ministers manage independently → PM's role = coordination + leadership → "they swim together and they sink together"
- **Inter Stellas Lunar Minoris:** Harcourt argued PM has special status → PM is like the bright moon among lesser stars
- **Teacher's Critique:** Highly unscientific — moon is NOT a star → comparing apples to oranges → don't be a fan of fancy phrases → focus on real arguments

## 11. PM Form of Government vs Cabinet Form of Government

### PM Form of Government (Not a Constitutional/Legal Term)

#### When Does It Emerge?
- **Single party** wins **majority** in lower house elections
- Party led by an **undisputed** and **charismatic** leader
- Party members know: "we won because of THIS leader" → subservient nature
- People know it, party knows it, **the person also knows it**
- CoM/Cabinet reduced to **"shadows of the PM"** — no real debate/discussion

#### Advantages
1. **Stability** — government is stable with clear majority
2. **Timely decisions** (NOT "fast" — fast is actually a negative)
3. **Strong/Bold decisions** — e.g., Article 370 abrogation → only this kind of government could do it

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
1. **Fundamental principle of parliamentary democracy (collective decision-making & collective responsibility) may be undermined** — always use **probabilistic language** ("may be")
2. **Kitchen Cabinet / Coterie** can emerge (see below)
3. **Influence may be more by political interest and less by public interest** — one person deciding → easier to be influenced
4. **Higher chances of misuse of power** — power concentrated without proportional accountability

#### Phases of Indian Politics
| Period | Nature |
|:---|:---|
| **1947–1964** | Nehru era — strong PM form |
| **1966–1984** | Indira Gandhi era — PM form (except 1977–79) |
| **1989–2014** | Coalition era — Cabinet form |
| **2014–present** | Strong PM form again |

### Cabinet Form of Government

#### When Does It Emerge?
- **Coalition era** — no single party majority
- Even charismatic PM (e.g., Vajpayee 1999) cannot dominate because coalition partners resist
- **Cabinet is more powerful** than PM — able to control the PM collectively

#### Merits
1. **Collective decision-making** — debate and discussion happen
2. **Kitchen cabinet is less likely** to emerge

#### Demerits
1. **Less stability** — coalition may break
2. **Decisions may be delayed** — e.g., Dr. Manmohan Singh's government → "policy paralysis" (decisions not just delayed but government stopped taking decisions because every coalition partner objected)

## 12. Kitchen Cabinet / Coterie

### Definition
- **Extra-constitutional power centres** that exercise power **without any accountability towards Parliament**
- People **outside the constitutional system** who can influence PM and get decisions in their favour

### How It Works
- In PM form of government → easier to influence **one person** (PM) than convince entire cabinet
- Route 1 (through PM) is easier than Route 2 (through Cabinet)
- These people may have personal connections (friendship, business, school ties, political favours)
- PM decides → Cabinet just agrees (shadows) → decision made

### Problems
1. **Power without accountability** → recipe for disaster in democracy
2. Not accountable to Parliament → cannot be questioned
3. Decision may be good or bad — **the problem is the process, not the outcome**
4. Even if they take a good decision → if process allows unaccountable power → future misuse is inevitable

### Terminology
| Term | Formality |
|:---|:---|
| **Kitchen Cabinet** | Informal/popular phrase — widely used but too casual |
| **Coterie** | More formal/technical term — preferred in academic writing |

## 13. Shadow Cabinet (UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand)

### Definition
- **Does NOT exist in India** — exists in countries with **two-party political systems**
- In UK: opposition party **behaves as a cabinet** — mirrors the ruling government's structure

### Structure
| Ruling Side | Opposition Side |
|:---|:---|
| PM | **Leader of Opposition (LOP)** = "PM in waiting" |
| Minister of Home Affairs | Shadow Minister A |
| Minister of Finance | Shadow Minister B |
| Minister of Defence | Shadow Minister C |

### Purpose — Two Arguments
1. **Channelized effort by opposition** → each shadow minister studies one specific ministry's policies, schemes, budget, expenditure → systematic scrutiny of every ministry (not just big ones like Defence/Finance)
2. **Training of portfolio** → if voted to power, **cabinet is ready** → quick transition → "Cabinet in waiting"

### Why Not in India?
1. **India is NOT a two-party system** — opposition is fractured
2. India is moving towards **two-alliance system** (NDA vs INDIA) but not stabilized yet
3. Parties switch alliances between elections
4. With India's diversity, unlikely to become two-party → maybe two-alliance system eventually

### Answer Writing: Opinion-Based Questions (Critical Skill)
**Framework for Balanced Answers (Madhya Path — Buddha's Middle Path):**

**If Supporting the Proposal:**
1. State the positives (reasons for support)
2. Acknowledge challenges that come as "free gift" with positives
3. Recommend solutions to handle those challenges

**If Opposing the Proposal:**
1. State the negatives (reasons for opposition)
2. Acknowledge the positives you'd be letting go
3. Suggest reforms in the existing system to achieve those positives without the proposal

> **Key Insight:** "The most important word in 'middle path' is not 'middle' — it is 'PATH'." Just listing plus/minus is not an answer. You must show a path forward.

- **Never make blanket statements** — especially when criticizing
- **Always use probabilistic language** — "may be undermined," "higher chances," "less likely"
- **Criticize on principles**, not on outcomes — e.g., collective decision-making principle being violated
- **Write positives first** — shows positive frame of mind (negatives come naturally; finding good things requires effort)

## 14. Caretaker Government — Consolidated Summary

### Why Caretaker Government Exists
- **Parliamentary system requires** there must always be a CoM
- **UNR Rao v. Indira Gandhi (1971):** SC held there must always be a CoM
- Without CoM → President would have to take decisions alone → presidential system → unconstitutional
- Managed **only by conventions** — nothing written

### Who Becomes Caretaker PM?
| Situation | Caretaker PM |
|:---|:---|
| PM resigns | Same PM |
| Lok Sabha dissolved | Same PM |
| PM dies | Senior-most Cabinet Minister (convention since 1964) |

### Caretaker Government Restrictions
- Should only take decisions **necessary for day-to-day administration**
- Should NOT take **major policy decisions** — both terms subjective
- **President decides** what is necessary → discretionary power
- **Not cast in rigid mode** — only in **restricted mode**
