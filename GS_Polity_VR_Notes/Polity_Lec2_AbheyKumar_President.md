## 1. Office of Profit Under the Government (OOP)

### Origin — Separation of Powers (SoP)
- **Legislature controls Executive** → Legislature must NOT come under Executive's control
- Members of Parliament should **not hold any office of profit under the Government**
- "Under the Government" = under the Executive → **Executive posts are offices of profit**
- **Rule of Thumb:** Is the post an executive post? → If yes, it is an Office of Profit

### Key Clarification — Is the President an Office of Profit?
- **Yes.** President = Head of the Executive → biggest executive office in India
- Art 59(2): President "shall not hold any **other** office of profit" → The word **"other"** proves the President's office is itself an OOP
- This distinction is critical:
  - **For candidates (Art 58):** Shall not hold **any** office of profit
  - **For sitting President (Art 59):** Shall not hold **any other** office of profit

> **Key Insight:** If exceptions are given (Art 58), it means the offices ARE OOP. Otherwise, why would exceptions be needed?

### Session Start at Parliament
- **11:00 AM** — Session begins → **Question Hour** (Q&A)
- **12:00 PM** — Zero Hour (also Q&A)
- Most parliamentary time = Executive accountability (asking questions), NOT law-making
- Legislature's primary function = **keeping a check on the Executive** (accountability)
- Law-making is one part of this larger accountability function

## 2. Eligibility for President — Article 58

### Basic Qualifications:
1. Citizen of India
2. Minimum age: **35 years**
3. Eligible to become a member of Lok Sabha
4. Should not hold any **office of profit under the Government**

### Four Exceptions to OOP Rule (Art 58):
| # | Exception | Logic |
|---|-----------|-------|
| 1 | **President** (sitting) | Re-election allowed; already holds OOP but exempted |
| 2 | **Vice President** | Like a promotion; VP is also executive but exempted |
| 3 | **Governor** of any State | Similar executive head; allowed to become President |
| 4 | **Any Minister** (Central or State) | Any minister of any level is exempted |

> **Trap:** Exception is given BECAUSE they ARE offices of profit. Exception ≠ "Not OOP." Exception = "OOP but allowed anyway."
> This exception is **ONLY for the election of President** — not for any other purpose.

### Application Examples:
- **Can PM Narendra Modi contest for President?** → **Yes.** He is a Minister (PM), exempted under Art 58.
- **Can Rahul Gandhi (LoP) contest?** → **Yes.** He is NOT executive; just a member of Parliament. No issue at all. If he wins, his Lok Sabha seat **auto-vacates** on taking oath as President (Condition under Art 59, not eligibility).

## 3. Conditions of Office of President — Article 59

> **Eligibility vs. Conditions:** Eligibility = to contest the election. Conditions = after becoming President.

### Key Conditions (Art 59):
1. **Shall not hold any other office of profit** (Clause 2) → Proves President's own office IS an OOP
2. **Shall not be a member of any legislature** — Parliament or State Legislature
   - If an MP/MLA wins presidential election → seat **auto-vacates** on taking oath (not resignation, not disqualification — **automatic**)

## 5. Election of President — Articles 54 & 55

### 5A. Electoral College of President (Art 54)

**Members of Electoral College:**
- **Elected** MPs (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)
- **Elected** MLAs of States

> **Critical Keywords:**
> - **"Elected"** — Nominated members of RS/Puducherry Assembly do NOT vote (no link to "people of India")
> - **MLCs excluded** — Not elected by the people (elected by special groups — teachers, graduates, etc.)
> - Only **MLAs** from state legislatures are included (not MLCs)

### Union Territories & Electoral College:

| UT | Participates? | How? |
|----|---------------|------|
| **Delhi** | ✅ Yes | 70th Constitutional Amendment Act, **1992** |
| **Puducherry** | ✅ Yes | 70th CAA, 1992 |
| **J&K** | ❌ No (as of 2022 election) | Constitution says only "Delhi & Puducherry"; J&K became UT in 2019 but not added to Art 54 |

- **Original Constitution** said "States" only → UTs didn't exist (UT concept came in **1956** — reorganization)
- Delhi got assembly in **1992** → 70th Amendment added Delhi + Puducherry to Art 54
- J&K: Assembly was dissolved (President's Rule) during 2022 election → no MLAs anyway
- **2027 election:** J&K now has MLAs, but Constitution still doesn't include J&K → **amendment needed** or make J&K a state again, or SC judgment

> **Possible Solutions for J&K (2027):**
> 1. Amend Constitution → add J&K to Art 54
> 2. Make J&K a state again (Omar Abdullah's demand) → automatic inclusion
> 3. SC judgment → spirit of Constitution, representatives of people should vote

### 5B. Proportional Representation (PR) System

**Two components:**
1. **System** = Proportional Representation
2. **Method** = Single Transferable Vote (STV)

#### Why "Proportional"?
- Representatives (MPs + MLAs) represent **different populations**
- An MLA representing 200 people should have more vote value than one representing 100
- Vote value is **in proportion to population represented**

#### First Assumption: MPs and MLAs are Equal as Groups
- Both groups represent the **same people of India** (largely)
- Exception: Chandigarh has an MP but no MLA → not exactly same people
- But **fair assumption** → no better alternative
- Also justified because President has **dual role**: Head of the Union (MPs participate) + Head of State/Federal Union (MLAs participate)

### 5C. Value of Vote of MLA

**Formula:**
```
Value of Vote of 1 MLA = Population of State / (No. of Elected MLAs × 1000)
```

- **Population** = as per **1971 Census** (frozen until first census after 2026)
- ÷ 1000 → simplifies calculation; doesn't change proportion
- Originally: last available census was used (1951 → 1961 → 1971)
- **Frozen at 1971** → to prevent rewarding states with high population growth (poor governance) and punishing states with good family planning (good governance)
  - North India = faster population growth → value would keep increasing
  - South India + NE = controlled population → value would stagnate
  - Freezing = fairness to southern & NE states

> **Until when frozen?** → Until **first census AFTER 2026** (not "until 2026")
> - Census 2021 started late (COVID), reference date = **1 March 2027**
> - So next year's (2027) election will STILL use **1971 census** (data not available yet)
> - Delimitation dispute (Vijay et al.) = related but different issue

#### Real Values (2022 Election):
| State | MLA Vote Value | Notes |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| **UP** (highest) | **208** | 403 MLAs |
| **Sikkim** (lowest) | **7** | 32 MLAs |
| **Jharkhand = Tamil Nadu** | Equal | Ratio works out same |

> **Key Questions & Answers:**
> - Two MLAs from **same state** → Equal value? **Yes** (assumption within state)
> - Two MLAs from **different states** → Always unequal? **Not necessarily** (may be equal, e.g. JH = TN)
> - Total value of all 32 Sikkim MLAs (32 × 7 = 224) ≈ Value of 1 UP MLA (208)

### 5D. Value of Vote of MP

**Assumption:** All elected MPs are **equal** (within Parliament → same house → same people of India → no discrimination)

**Formula:**
```
Value of Vote of 1 MP = Total Value of Votes of All Elected MLAs / No. of Elected MPs
```

- Total MLA votes ≈ 5,43,000 → Total Electoral College value ≈ 10,86,000
- MP value (2022 election) = **700**

**Why indirect method?**
- Cannot calculate MP value directly from population → would make MPs unequal
- First calculate all MLA values → sum them → equate to total MP value → divide equally
- This ensures: Total MLA votes = Total MP votes (both groups equal in aggregate)

> **MP value is always higher than MLA value of same state** → MP constituency is larger (more population) than MLA constituency

### 5E. Single Transferable Vote (STV) — The Method

Also called: **Preferential Voting System / Order of Choice System**

#### How It Works:
1. **Ballot Paper:** Candidates listed (A, B, C, D...) → Voters write preference numbers (1st, 2nd, 3rd...)
2. **Secret Ballot** — like all elections (exception: Rajya Sabha election = open ballot)
3. Preferences written in **any Indian language** (must write, no thumb impression)
4. **Minimum preferences:** 1 → **Maximum:** Number of candidates (not mandatory to exhaust all)

#### Winning Condition:
- **Majority of valid votes polled** → i.e., >50% of actual votes cast (not total members)
- If 100 members, 5 don't vote → need majority of 95 (i.e., 48+)

#### Counting Process:
1. **Round 1:** Count **first preferences** only
   - Value of ballot = value of that voter's vote (e.g., UP MLA = 208, not 1)
   - If someone gets majority → **Winner declared**
2. **If no majority → Round 2:**
   - **Least preferred** candidate **eliminated**
   - Their ballots reopened → look at **next available preference**
   - Transfer vote value to next preferred candidate still in race
   - If no next preference available → ballot set aside
3. **Repeat** until:
   - Someone achieves majority, OR
   - Only **one candidate left** (wins even without majority)

#### STV Example (Simplified: 100 members, value = 1 each):
```
Round 1:  A=40, B=30, C=20, D=10 → No majority (need 51+)
          D eliminated → 10 ballots reopened → 6 to A, 2 to B, 2 to C
Round 2:  A=46, B=32, C=22 → Still no majority
          C eliminated → 22 ballots reopened → transfer by next preference
Round 3:  Continue until majority or 1 candidate left
```

#### Why STV?
- Elects the **most preferred** candidate (not just plurality winner)
- Unlike FPTP (Lok Sabha): candidate with 40% can win even if 60% hate them
- In STV: preferences ensure broader acceptability

#### Historical Facts:
| Event | Year | Details |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Only 2nd round of counting** | **1969** | V.V. Giri (Varahagiri Venkata Giri) became President |
| **Elected unopposed** | **1977** | Neelam Sanjiva Reddy — no other candidate |
| All other elections | — | Winner declared in 1st round itself |

## 6. Timing of Election — Article 62

### Principle: There is a term → Two possibilities

**Term of President = 5 years**

| Scenario | When is election held? |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Term completed** | Election held **BEFORE** completion of term |
| **Term NOT completed (Vacancy)** | Election held **AFTER** vacancy, **within 6 months** |

### New President takes oath on the **next day** of outgoing President
- Unlike PM (PM takes oath before the outgoing PM's term ends)

### Vacancy = only 3 things:
1. **Death** (e.g., Zakir Hussain — 1969, Fakhruddin Ali Ahmad)
2. **Resignation**
3. **Removal** (Impeachment / Court order)

> Nothing else can happen. Either complete the term or one of these three. Be convinced — no fourth possibility.

---

## 7. During Vacancy — Acting President

### Succession Order:
| Priority | Who? | Role |
|----------|------|------|
| 1st | **Vice President** | Acting President (NOT President) |
| 2nd | **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** | If VP also unavailable |
| 3rd | **Senior-most Judge of SC** | If CJI also unavailable (37 judges available) |

### Vice President's Dual Role:
- **Primary purpose:** Backup for President (that's why the office was created)
- **Secondary (routine):** Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha
  - **Ex-officio** = "by virtue of the office" → whoever is VP automatically gets RS chairmanship
  - No separate appointment needed; if VP changes, RS Chairman changes automatically
- VP salary: Gets salary as **Chairman of RS** (no separate VP salary); when acting as President → gets **President's salary**
- When VP acts as President → **Deputy Chairman of RS** handles RS

### Historical Usage of Backup:
- Only **2 Presidents died in office** (Zakir Hussain 1969, Fakhruddin Ali Ahmad)
- Total backup usage ≈ **1 year** out of ~80 years → "very useless backup"
- 1969: Justice **M. Hidayatullah** (CJI) was Acting President briefly (V.V. Giri had resigned as VP to contest)

## 8. Disputes on Election — Article 71

### Key Provisions:
1. **Only Supreme Court** can hear and dispose disputes (highest office → highest court; High Courts have NO role)
2. Applies to both **President and Vice President** elections

### If SC Invalidates the Election:
- All decisions taken and **completed prior** to invalidation → **NOT invalid for this reason alone**
- Invalidation is **prospective** (from now onwards), NOT retrospective
  - **Principle:** What happened, happened. Now it won't happen.
  - Same principle as triple talaq invalidation, any law struck down, etc.
  - Exception: In rare judicial cases, retrospective application possible

### Cannot Dispute on Ground of:
- **Vacancy in Electoral College** — some MLA/MP died/resigned → doesn't matter
- President is the highest office → can't remain vacant for such operational issues
- Election will happen regardless

### Who Can Dispute?
- **Principle (applies to ALL elections):** Only **interested parties** can dispute
  - = **Voters** of that election + **Candidates** of that election
  - For President: Members of **Electoral College** + Candidates
  - Random citizen CANNOT challenge (you didn't vote directly)
  - Even PM's Lok Sabha election → only voters of that constituency + candidates of that constituency

### Governing Law:
- **Presidential and Vice Presidential Elections Act, 1952**
  - "Act" (capital A) followed by year = statute/legislative act of Parliament

## 10. Assent to Bills & Veto Powers — Article 111

### Journey of a Bill:
```
Bill (Proposal of Law)
  → Parliament (Both Houses pass it)
    → President (Assent)
      → Act (Law)
        → Notified in Gazette (Rajpatra)
          → Enforceable
```

- **Gazette (Rajpatra)** = official mode of government communication
  - Contains: notifications, orders, names of gazetted officers, Acts
  - Unless notified in Gazette → Act exists but is NOT enforceable

### Veto = Power of Chief Executive to prevent enactment of legislation, either temporarily or permanently
- Not a word in the Constitution — a scholarly/academic term

---

### 10A. Types of Bills (Classification by Subject Matter)

| Type | Definition | Key Points |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **Ordinary Bill** | Bill that is NOT money, NOT financial, NOT constitutional amendment | Defined by exclusion; "ordinary things have no definition" |
| **Money Bill** | Bill dealing **ONLY** with money matters | Keyword: **ONLY** money. Defined in Art 110 |
| **Financial Bill** | Bill dealing with money **AND** non-money matters | Money + Ordinary combined |
| **Constitutional Amendment (CA) Bill** | Bill that amends Constitution **under Article 368** | Keyword: **under Art 368**. Ordinary bills can also amend Constitution (e.g., Art 3 — rename state) |

> **Critical Distinctions:**
> - **Money Bill** = category, not an actual bill name. Actual bills: Appropriation Bill (expenditure), Finance Bill (revenue/taxation)
> - **Budget = 2 Money Bills:** Appropriation Bill + Finance Bill
> - **Appropriation** = allocation of funds for spending (not just "expenditure")
> - **Finance Bill ≠ Financial Bill** — Finance Bill = revenue/tax proposal (money bill); Financial Bill = a category (money + non-money)
> - "Money bill" and "financial bill" are **categories** → no bill becomes "Money Act" or "Financial Act"

### 10B. President's Three Choices (Art 111)

When a bill is presented for assent, President has **3 choices** (first time):

| Choice | Action | Effect | Type of Veto |
|--------|--------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | **Declare Assent** | Bill → Act | No veto |
| 2 | **Withhold Assent** | Bill **dies** — comes to an end permanently | **Absolute Veto** |
| 3 | **Return for Reconsideration** | Bill sent back to Parliament with/without recommendations | **Suspensive Veto** |

---

### 10C. Absolute Veto (Withholding Assent)

**What it means:**
- President says **NO** → Bill is **dead**. Permanently finished. No revival.
- "Withholding **assent**" (NOT "withholding the bill") — Assent is President's to withhold; Bill is Parliament's
- **No = No.** Move on. Make a new proposal.

**Four-Point Framework:**

| Aspect | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| **What does it do?** | Brings the bill to a **permanent end** |
| **On whose advice?** | **Advice of Council of Ministers** (President doesn't act on own here) |
| **Which bills?** | Ordinary ✅ Money ✅ Financial ✅ CA Bill ❌ |
| **Examples** | PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954 (Rajendra Prasad) + Salaries/Allowances/Pension of MP Amendment Bill, 1991 (R. Venkataraman) |

> **Major Misconception Debunked:**
> - Many books/sources wrongly say "money bills cannot be rejected"
> - **BOTH examples of absolute veto in India are MONEY BILLS:**
>   - PEPSU Appropriation Bill (1954) = expenditure bill = **money bill**
>   - MP Salaries Bill (1991) = salary/allowance = **money bill**
> - **Money bills CAN be rejected.** Generally President gives assent (because COM recommended the bill in the first place), but rejection is legally possible.

**Why generally assent to money bills?**
- Money bill requires **President's recommendation** to introduce (Art 110)
- President's recommendation = Council of Ministers' recommendation
- Same COM unlikely to later advise rejection
- But: Government can change, private member's bill possible, etc.

---

### 10D. Suspensive Veto (Return for Reconsideration)

**What it means:**
- President returns bill to Parliament with recommendations/suggestions
- Parliament may accept, modify, or ignore recommendations
- If Parliament passes bill **second time** (with or without changes) → President **SHALL declare assent** (no choice)
- Effect: **Delay** + opportunity for reconsideration, discourse, media attention

**Four-Point Framework:**

| Aspect | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| **What does it do?** | **Delays** enactment; cannot permanently kill the bill |
| **On whose advice?** | **Own discretion** (understood, not written) — President's own suggestions/wisdom |
| **Which bills?** | Ordinary ✅ Financial ✅ Money ❌ (can't delay money) CA Bill ❌ |
| **Example** | MP Prevention of Disqualification Amendment Bill, **2006** (Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam returned; Parliament re-passed without changes → Kalam gave assent) |

> **2006 Case — Sonia Gandhi & NAC:**
> - NAC (National Advisory Council) chairperson = Sonia Gandhi (MP)
> - Allegation: NAC chair = Office of Profit → Sonia would be disqualified as MP
> - Congress govt passed bill adding NAC chair to exempted list → Dr. Kalam returned it
> - Parliament re-passed same bill without changing a single comma → Kalam had to give assent
> - Significance: Kalam's principled stand got recorded in history; discourse happened

**Why no suspensive veto on Money Bills?**
- Money bills deal with government's immediate financial needs → delay not allowed
- President must decide: either give assent or withhold (reject) — no return option

---

### 10E. Pocket Veto (No Time Limit to Choose)

- Constitution gives **no time limit** for President to make a choice
- If President simply **does not choose** (neither assent, nor withhold, nor return) → bill stays in "pocket"
- **Not written in Constitution** — arises from the absence of a time limit
- Effectively: bill **neither dies nor becomes law** — indefinite limbo

> **To be discussed in Lecture 3** along with Mercy Powers and Constitutional Position of President.

---

### 10F. Veto Powers — Applicability Summary Table

| Veto Type | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill | Financial Bill | CA Bill (Art 368) |
|-----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Absolute** (Withhold Assent) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ (shall assent — 24th CAA, 1971) |
| **Suspensive** (Return) | ✅ | ❌ (no delay) | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Pocket** (No time limit) | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |

> **For CA Bills:** President **shall declare assent** — no choice, no veto. Written in Constitution after **24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971** (earlier it was convention/practice, now mandated written rule).
> **For Money Bills:** Can reject (absolute) but cannot delay (no suspensive/pocket).
