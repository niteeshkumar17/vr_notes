## 1. Pocket Veto — Detailed (Continuation from Lecture 2)

### Recap: How Pocket Veto Arises
- Article 111 gives President **3 choices** (Assent / Withhold / Return) but provides **no time limit** to choose
- If President simply **does not choose** for an indefinite period → bill lies on the table → **Pocket Veto**
- **Not a power to do something** — it is a **power to do nothing**

### Pocket Veto = Discretionary Power
- Since it is **not written** in the Constitution, nobody can advise the President to exercise it
- The President is **thinking** which option to choose → discretion of the President
- Applies only to **Ordinary and Financial Bills** (not Money Bills, not CA Bills)
- Both **Suspensive Veto** and **Pocket Veto** are discretionary powers of the President

### The Only Example: Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986
| Detail | Fact |
|--------|------|
| **Bill** | Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill |
| **Government** | Rajiv Gandhi (PM) — 424 seats, youngest PM ever |
| **President** | Giani Zail Singh |
| **Year Introduced** | 1986 |
| **Controversial Provision** | Government could legally intercept any postal communication |
| **What Happened** | President kept the bill on the table indefinitely — did nothing |
| **Duration** | ~4 years (1986–1990) |
| **Resolution** | President R. Venkataraman (new President) **returned** the bill in 1990 on advice of PM Chandrashekhar |
| **Parliament's Response** | Never re-passed the bill — it disappeared |

> **Key Insight:** Technically, the final act was a **Suspensive Veto** (return), but the case is cited as the example of **Pocket Veto** because the bill was kept idle for 4 years. Pocket veto has been observed only **once** in Indian history.

### Background Context
- 1984 election: Congress won **424 seats** (highest ever majority, even more than present BJP's 240)
- Rajiv Gandhi: Youngest PM, enormous power
- Giani Zail Singh: Old-school, loyal to Indira Gandhi, differences with Rajiv after her assassination
- Bill was widely criticized as an attack on citizens' privacy

### Important Distinction: Parliamentary Bills vs State Bills
- SC ruling on pocket veto for **Governor on state bills** = **different** issue (SC said no pocket veto for Governor)
- This case is about **Parliamentary bill coming to President** — completely separate

## 2. Mercy Powers / Clemency Powers — Article 72 & 161

### Constitutional Framework
| Article | Authority | Scope |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| **Article 72** | **President** | Union law offences + Court Martial + **Death sentences** (even under State law) |
| **Article 161** | **Governor** | State law offences only |

> **Critical Terminology:** Call them **Mercy Powers** or **Clemency Powers** — NOT "Pardoning Powers" (because pardon is only ONE of the powers). Constitution itself never uses the heading "Pardoning Powers."

### Types of Mercy Powers

| Power | What Changes? | Effect | Example |
|-------|--------------|--------|---------|
| **Pardon** | Conviction + Sentence | **Both removed** — person treated as if crime never committed | Rarely used in India; very common in USA (Joe Biden pardoned Hunter Biden, preemptive pardons) |
| **Commutation** | **Nature** of sentence | Substitutes harsher punishment with a lighter one | Death → Life imprisonment |
| **Remission** | **Quantum** of sentence | Reduces the period without changing character | Life → 20 years (then released) |
| **Respite** | Nature **temporarily** | Temporary relaxation of the nature of punishment | Rigorous → Simple imprisonment for 4 weeks (due to injury/pregnancy) |
| **Reprieve** | **Delays execution** | Nothing changed — same punishment, just later | Execution delayed by 3 weeks to allow mercy petition filing |
| **Suspension** | Indefinite delay | Courts suspend sentences on appeal — **not exercised by President/Governor** | Higher court suspends lower court's sentence during appeal |

> **Suspension** is written in Art 72 but practically used only by **courts**, not by President/Governor. It gets covered by reprieve for the executive.

### Nature of Sentence (Severity Hierarchy — Highest to Lowest)
1. **Capital Punishment** (Death penalty — "hanged by the neck until death")
2. **Imprisonment**
   - **Rigorous Imprisonment (RI)** — hard physical labour required
   - **Simple Imprisonment (SI)** — no hard labour
3. **Fine** (monetary penalty — amount = quantum)
4. **Community Service** (NEW — introduced under BNS/BNSS)
   - e.g., 50 hours at old age home — for petty offences
   - Philosophy: Reform over revenge

> **Life imprisonment = Life** → person dies in jail. NOT 14 years or 20 years. There is NO such thing as "life imprisonment = 14 years."

### Key Mapping: Which Power Changes What?

| What Changes | Power Used |
|-------------|-----------|
| Conviction removed + Sentence removed | **Pardon** |
| Nature changed (harsher → lighter) | **Commutation** |
| Quantum reduced (amount/years) | **Remission** |
| Nature changed temporarily | **Respite** |
| Execution delayed (nothing changes) | **Reprieve** |

### President vs Governor — Key Differences

| Aspect | President (Art 72) | Governor (Art 161) |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Law** | Union law offences | State law offences |
| **Territory** | Anywhere in India | Within the State |
| **Court Martial** | ✅ Yes (Supreme Commander) | ❌ No |
| **Death Penalty** | ✅ Can pardon (even under State law) | ❌ **Cannot pardon** death sentence |
| **Commute Death** | ✅ | ✅ (can commute death → life, but cannot pardon) |

> **UPSC Trap:** "Governor cannot pardon" does NOT mean Governor has NO mercy powers. Governor can **commute**, **remit**, **respite**, **reprieve**. Governor cannot **pardon** — which is only ONE power. Governor specifically cannot pardon death sentence but can commute it.

### Successive Application of Mercy Power
- **Yes**, mercy power can be applied successively (one after another)
- Example: Death → Life imprisonment (commutation by President) → Life → 20 years (remission by State/President)
- Same person, different powers applied at different times

### Pardon — The Highest Form of Mercy (Detailed)

**What Pardon Does:**
- Removes **conviction** AND **sentence**
- Also removes **all other disqualifications** attached to conviction
- Places the person in the **same legal position as before committing the crime** (innocent)
- e.g., A convict barred from contesting elections → after pardon, can contest again

**Timeline of Legal Status:**
```
Birth → [Innocent] → Crime → [Accused] → Trial → [Convict] → Pardon → [Innocent Again]
```

**When Can Pardon Be Granted?**
- ✅ After trial (typical)
- ✅ During trial (trial stops)
- ✅ Before trial (no trial starts — **Preemptive Pardon**)
- ❌ Before crime (would be license to commit crime)

**Preemptive Pardon (USA Practice):**
- Very popular in USA — Presidents pardon people before trial/charge
- Example: Joe Biden pardoned Hunter Biden, his sister, brother-in-law on his **last day in office** (4:45 PM before leaving at 5 PM)
- Protects from prosecution for **past actions** only, not future crimes
- Not practiced in India — in our system, President acts on COM advice (not absolute power like USA's Presidential system)

### Why Do Mercy Powers Exist?

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Tradition/Legacy** | Kings historically had power of mercy ("Maharaj, maaf kijiye") — philosophy continued |
| **Philosophy** | State should have a bigger heart; reform over revenge |
| **Humanitarianism** | Courts examine only law + facts; cannot consider humanitarian factors (small children, special circumstances) |
| **Correcting Errors** | Even SC can err — executive provides a final safety valve |
| **Executive Implementation** | Court gives order; executive implements it — executive can say "for these reasons, I won't fully implement" |

### Nature of Mercy Power — Key Constitutional Points

| Statement | True/False | Explanation |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Mercy power is an **Act of Grace** | ✅ True | Executive's compassion, not convict's right |
| Convict has **Right to Hearing** | ❌ False | No right to hearing — executive may give, but convict cannot demand |
| It is a **Judicial Function** of President | ❌ False | President does not examine evidence, call witnesses, follow judicial procedure |
| It is a **Judicial Power** of President | ✅ True | The power is judicial in nature but exercised as **executive** |
| President overrides Supreme Court | ❌ False | President only modifies **implementation** of court order, not the judgment itself |
| President is superior to SC | ❌ False | SC remains highest court of justice; President is not a court |

> **Key Distinction:** A power of the President may be **judicial in nature** (mercy) or **legislative in nature** (ordinance), but the President always **functions as executive**.

### Judicial Review of Mercy Power
- **Yes**, judicial review is possible — but courts examine only **whether the decision was malafide (bad faith)**
- Courts do NOT re-examine evidence or guilt (that's already established)
- **Malafide** = bad faith, wrong intention (e.g., pardoning because person is minister's daughter)
- **Bona fide** = good faith, genuine reason (e.g., India signed international treaty against that punishment)
- **ICOM/MHA** handles mercy petitions (delegated from President to Ministry of Home Affairs)
- **Shatrughan Chauhan case:** SC directed that mercy petitions must be decided **expeditiously** — unexplained delay of 20+ years = mental torture → SC itself commuted death to life in bulk
- No specific time limit, but "timely" decision expected

## 3. Constitutional Position of President

### The Foundation: Article 53
- **Text:** "Executive power of the Union shall be vested in the President, who shall exercise it either directly or through officers subordinate to him"
- **Constitution says:** President can act directly
- **Reality:** President acts on aid and advice of Council of Ministers

### Article 74 — Aid & Advice of Council of Ministers

**Article 74(1)** (as amended by **42nd CAA, 1976**):
> "There shall be a Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister to aid and advise the President, who **shall** act according to such advice."

- **Original provision** (pre-1976): Only said "to aid and advise the President" — NO "shall act according to advice"
- **42nd Amendment (1976)** added: "who shall act according to such advice" → made it **constitutionally binding**

**Proviso to Article 74(1)** (inserted by **44th CAA, 1978**):
> "The President may require the Council of Ministers to **reconsider** their advice, either generally or otherwise, and the President **shall** act according to the advice tendered after such reconsideration."

- This is a **totally new power** (inserted, not substituted) — didn't exist before 1978
- President can ask COM to reconsider **once** → after reconsideration, must act on the advice
- This is a **discretionary power** of the President (though not written as discretionary)

### Art 74(1) Proviso ≠ Suspensive Veto (Important Distinction)

| Feature | Suspensive Veto (Art 111) | Art 74(1) Proviso |
|---------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **What is sent back?** | A **Bill** | **Advice** |
| **Sent back to?** | **Parliament** | **Council of Ministers** |
| **Relationship** | Executive ↔ Legislature | Within the Executive team |
| **Second time** | President **declares assent** | President **acts** on advice |

### Supreme Court Judgments on Position of President

| Case | Year | Key Ruling |
|------|------|-----------|
| **Ram Jawaya v. State of Punjab** | 1955 | India follows parliamentary system → President should work on advice of COM |
| **Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab** | 1974 | President cannot exercise executive power against or without COM advice (most important — remember this one) |
| **U.N.R. Rao v. Indira Gandhi** | 1971 | "There **shall be** a Council of Ministers" = always, even when LS dissolved (caretaker government) |

### Court's Two Key Conclusions (Shamsher Singh):
1. President **cannot** exercise executive functions **without** the advice of COM
2. President **cannot** exercise executive functions **against** the advice of COM
- All President can do: ask COM to **reconsider once** → after that, must act

### There Shall **Always** Be a Council of Ministers
- Art 74(1) begins: "There **shall be** a Council of Ministers..."
- "Shall be" = always, at all times
- Even when Lok Sabha is **dissolved** → **Caretaker Government** exists
- **U.N.R. Rao v. Indira Gandhi (1971)** confirmed this
- Example: 2024 — Modi resigned after election → became caretaker PM → took oath again as new PM after ~5 days. In between = caretaker COM existed.

### Three Principles of Parliamentary System

| # | Parliamentary System (India) | Presidential System (USA) |
|---|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **1st (Primary)** | Head of State is **Nominal** (just a name) | Head of State is **Real** |
| **2nd** | Real executive power with **Council of Ministers** (headed by PM) | — |
| **3rd** | COM is collectively responsible to **Legislature** (Lok Sabha) | Not responsible to Legislature |
| **Corollary** | Members of COM **derived from** Legislature | Members **cannot be** from Legislature |

> **Duality of Heads:** Parliamentary system has TWO heads — **Head of State** (President = de jure, formal, legal) and **Head of Government** (PM = de facto, practical, real). In Presidential system (USA), both = same person (Trump is both Head of State & Head of Government).

### Ambedkar's Comparison: President of India ≈ Crown of England

| Aspect | President of India | Crown (King/Queen) of England |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Status | Head of State | Head of State |
| Real power | No (with COM) | No (with PM & Cabinet) |
| Ambedkar's words | **"More or less a ceremonial office"** | "They **reign** but do not **rule**" |

> **Critical Phrase:** Ambedkar said "**more or less** ceremonial" — NOT "ceremonial." The words "more or less" imply that while 99.9% ceremonial, there exists 0.1% discretionary space. This is what makes India's President a **Constitutional Head**, not a **Figurehead**.

### Figurehead vs Constitutional Head

| Feature | Figurehead | Constitutional Head |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Office of** | Respect, dignity, ceremony | Respect, dignity, ceremony |
| **Real authority** | ❌ **None whatsoever** | Generally ❌, but **sometimes ✅** |
| **Acts on advice** | **Always** — no exceptions | **Generally** — but not in extraordinary circumstances |
| **Exceptional circumstances** | Still no authority | Has **own authority** (discretionary) |
| **Example** | King of England | **President of India** |

> **Conclusion:** India's President is a **Constitutional Head** (not a mere figurehead) because discretionary powers exist, even if implicit and rare.

### President's Evolved Role
- Over the years, President has evolved into the role of **friend, philosopher, and guide** to the government
- President is **apolitical** (detached from politics) → can give unbiased advice
- COM has the mandate of the people but is politically motivated (next election concerns)
- President can give objective, non-partisan advice because of distance from electoral politics
- In practical terms, the **President is the real advisor** to COM (not the other way around)
  - COM's "advice" is actually a **decision** (binding on President)
  - President's suggestions/returns are actual **advice** (not binding on COM)

## 4. Discretionary Powers of President

### Key Characteristics of Presidential Discretion
- **NOT explicitly mentioned** in the Constitution — they are **implicit** (understood)
- The word "**discretion**" does NOT exist in any provision for President
- Contrast: For **Governor**, "discretion" is explicitly written (Art 163) — at least 3-4 times
- **Same Constitution makers** used "discretion" for Governor but NOT for President → **intentional** omission
- President's discretion is **not intentional** but **situational** — arises from extraordinary circumstances
- Governor's discretion is **intentionally** provided

### List of Discretionary Powers (Discussed So Far — 7 points, 8th onwards in next lecture)

| # | Discretionary Power | Article/Provision | Why Discretionary? |
|---|--------------------|--------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Suspensive Veto** (Return of Bill) | Art 111 | President's own suggestions — not on COM advice |
| 2 | **Pocket Veto** (Inaction on Bill) | Not written — emerges from absence of time limit | Unwritten power; no advice possible for something not written |
| 3 | **Reconsider Advice** (Art 74 Proviso) | Art 74(1) Proviso (44th CAA, 1978) | President asks COM to rethink — obviously on own initiative |
| 4 | **Seek Information** from PM (Art 78) | Art 78(b) | If President wants information from PM, PM must furnish — President asks on own |
| 5 | **Summon Parliament** (if 6 months lapsed) | Art 85 | If COM hasn't advised summons and 6 months gap exceeds → President protects Constitution by summoning on own |
| 6 | **Dissolution of Lok Sabha** (minority situation) | Art 85 | COM advice to dissolve is binding **only when COM has majority**; if minority, President decides independently |
| 7 | **Appoint PM in Hung Parliament** | Convention | No party/coalition has majority → President decides who gets first chance (convention: largest pre-poll alliance leader) |

### Hung Parliament — President's Most Important Duty

**President's Primary Real Duty:** Provide India a **stable government**
- If majority clear → appoint leader of majority party → done
- If **Hung Parliament** (no party has majority):
  1. Try coalition government
  2. If no coalition has clear majority → President's discretion:
     - Call leader of **largest pre-poll alliance** first
     - **Pre-poll** (not post-poll) because pre-poll alliances are more **ideological** and likely **stable**
     - **Largest** because highest probability of proving majority
  3. That leader becomes PM → must prove majority on floor of House
  4. This practice → repeated → becomes **convention**

> **Convention Formation:** 1st time: President thinks and decides → 2nd time: follows precedent → 3rd time: media predicts outcome → Convention established. Conventions derive force from being **followed over time** — breaking them invites massive criticism even though not legally enforceable.
