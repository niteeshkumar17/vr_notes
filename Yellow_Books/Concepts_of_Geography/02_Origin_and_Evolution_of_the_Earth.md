## 2. Shape of the Earth (GEO-01 extras)

- **Eratosthenes** (2nd century BC): early accurate size (Fundamentals already: he coined *geography*).
- Diameters match class: equatorial **~12,756 km**, polar **~12,714 km** (book also **7,900 miles** for polar).
- Rotation → bulge at equator, flatten at poles.
- Book term: **oblate ellipsoid**, commonly called **geoid** (“Earth-like”). Class sequence is **oblate spheroid → geoid**. Same exam idea: not a perfect sphere; treat as sphere for most sums.

### Evidence of sphericity (not in class — learn this list)

1. **Circumnavigation** — Magellan’s crew **1519–1522**; no “edge.”
2. **Circular horizon** — expands as you go higher.
3. **Ship:** mast first on approach; hull first to vanish on departure. On a flat Earth the whole ship would pop in/out at once.
4. **Sunrise / sunset** at different longitudes (Earth rotates **west → east**; east sees sunrise first).
5. **Lunar eclipse** — Earth’s shadow on the Moon is **circular**.
6. Other planets/Moon/Sun look circular in a telescope (analogy).
7. Equal poles in a line on curved ground: middle pole sits slightly **higher**.
8. **Aerial / satellite** photos show the curved limb.

## 3. Rotation and revolution (GEO-01 / seasonality)

| Motion | Book | Effect |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Rotation** | Spin on axis, ~**24 hours** (**solar day**) | Day / night; daily temperature; winds and currents that move heat |
| **Revolution** | Around the Sun, ~**365.242 days** | Seasons (with tilt). **Leap year** every four years (29 Feb) for the extra fraction |
| Distance | Average **93 million miles** = **1 astronomical unit (AU)** | — |

**Axis:** **23.5°** from the **normal** to the orbital plane = **66.5°** to the **ecliptic** (orbital plane). Class Theia already locked **23.5°**.

**Circle of illumination:** great circle, day vs night. Tilt → day length varies with latitude and season. If the axis were **vertical**, day and night would be **equal everywhere all year**.

**Three reasons the book gives for why rotation matters:** (1) geographic grid; (2) measure of time; (3) physical processes and life.

**Dawn / twilight (extra):** dawn = sky lighting just **before** sunrise (reds/oranges). Twilight = Sun still **below** the horizon but sky lit by **scattered** sunlight (before sunrise and after sunset). Class already: twilight **lengthens** with latitude (DST logic).

### Perihelion and aphelion (extra — seasons are **not** from this)

| | When (book) | Distance |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Perihelion** (closest) | **2–4 January** (~14 days after December solstice) | **147.1 million km** (91.4 million mi) |
| **Aphelion** (farthest) | **3–6 July** (~14 days after June solstice) | **152.1 million km** (94.5 million mi) |

Elliptical orbit → small distance change; book: **minimal** effect on seasons. **2013 PYQ trap:** variations of day length are **not** “elliptical revolution” alone.

### Solstices and equinoxes (book dates)

| Event | Date (book) | Overhead / pole |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **June solstice** (NH summer) | ~**21 June** (also 20–22) | Tropic of **Cancer**; Arctic **24 h** day; Antarctic **0 h** |
| **December solstice** (NH winter) | ~**21–22 December** | Tropic of **Capricorn**; Arctic **0 h**; Antarctic **24 h** |
| **Vernal equinox** | ~**20–21 March** | Equator; Sun crosses celestial equator **S → N**; NH spring |
| **Autumnal equinox** | ~**22–23 September** | Equator; Sun crosses **N → S**; NH autumn |

Equator: **12 h** day **every** day (class). Arctic Circle: Sun **does not set** ~21 June; **does not rise** ~22 December.

### Five reasons for seasons (book list — 2013 key)

1. Revolution around the Sun  
2. Daily rotation  
3. Tilted axis  
4. **Parallelism** — axis keeps pointing the same way (**Polaris**) all year  
5. **Sphericity** (different angles of incidence)

**Do not pick** “elliptical orbit” as the cause of seasonal day-length change.

## 4. Location of places (mostly class)

- Rotation W→E gives two natural ends: **North and South Poles** → grid of **parallels** and **meridians**.
- Degrees, then **60 minutes**, **60 seconds** (angular; extra vs class).
- **Parallels at 1°:** 89 N + 89 S + equator = **179**. Extra.
- Meridians: **semi-circles**, **equal length**, meet at poles, cut the equator at **right angles**. Prime Meridian through **Greenwich Observatory**.
- **1° latitude ≈ 111 km (69 miles).** **1° longitude** shrinks toward the poles; at **60°** ≈ **55.5 km** (class ≈ **56 km**).

## 5. Standard time and IDL (GEO-01-02 extras)

- 360° / 24 h → **15° = 1 hour**; **1° = 4 minutes**. Earth W→E: **east = later** (add); **west = earlier** (subtract).  
  **Ignore** any line that says time *falls* as you go east — the same chapter’s worked sums use the class rule.
- Standard meridians usually multiples of **15°** or **7°30′**.
- **Indian Standard Time (IST):** **82°30′ E**, book: through **Mirzapur**. IST = **GMT + 5 hours 30 minutes** because \(82.5 \times 4 = 330\) minutes. (Class had 82½° E and the 7½° rule; **Mirzapur** is the extra name.)
- Wide countries: several meridians. Book records: **France 12** time zones (overseas territories), **USA 11**. Class assignment was to list multi-zone countries — these two numbers are the book’s.

**Worked example (book):** Greenwich **7:30 PM**; find local time at **112.5° W**.  
\(112.5 / 15 = 7.5\) hours west → 7:30 PM − 7 h 30 min = **12:00 noon**.

**IDL:** ~**180°**. Clock is **12 hours** from Greenwich either way. Line **bends** (Aleutians etc.).  
Class rule (keep this, not a muddled “eastward = Wednesday” sentence):

| Travel across the IDL | Calendar |
|:---|:---|
| **East → West** (towards Asia / “add a day” on the book diagram) | **Lose / skip** a calendar day |
| **West → East** (towards the Americas / “subtract a day”) | **Gain / repeat** a calendar day |
